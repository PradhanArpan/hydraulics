Course Objectives: To introduce the students to various hydraulic engineering problems such as open channel flows and hydraulic machines. At the completion of the course, the student should be able to relate the theory and practice of problems in hydraulic engineering.
Prerequisites: Engineering Physics, Engineering Mathematics, Introduction to Fluid Mechanics
Units
Teaching Hours
Unit-1: Boundary Layer Theory
Boundary Layer Analysis - Assumption and concept of boundary layer theory. Boundary-layer thickness, displacement, momentum and energy thickness, laminar and turbulent boundary layers on a flat plate; Laminar sub-layer, smooth and rough boundaries. Local and average friction coefficients. Separation and Control.
Practicals-
1. Calibration of V-notch
2. Calibration of Rectangular Notch.
3. Calibration of Ogee weir
4. Calibration of Broad crested weir.
5. Calibration of Venturi meter and Orifice meter.
6. Determination of hydraulic coefficient of a vertical orifice
06
Unit-2: Open Channel Flow
Introduction: Comparison between open channel flow and pipe flow, geometrical parameters of a channel, classification of open channels, classification of open channel flow, Velocity Distribution of channel section.
Uniform Flow: Continuity Equation, Energy Equation and Momentum Equation, Characteristics of uniform flow, Chezy's formula, Manning's formula. Factors affecting Manning's Roughness Coefficient, Most economical section of channel, Computation of Uniform flow, Normal depth. Computation of economical section and bed roughness analytically as well as by using software.
Non-Uniform Flow: Specific energy, Specific energy curve, critical flow, discharge curve, Specific force, Specific depth, and Critical depth. Channel Transitions. Hydraulic Jump - Theory of hydraulic jump, Elements and characteristics of hydraulic jump in a rectangular Channel, length and height of jump, location of jump, Types, applications and location of hydraulic jump.
Measurement of Discharge and Velocity - Venturi Flume, Standing Wave Flume, Parshall Flume, Broad Crested Weir. Gradually Varied Flow - Dynamic Equation of Gradually Varied Flow, Classification of channel bottom slopes, Classification of surface profile, Characteristics of surface profile. Computation of water surface profile.
10
Unit-3: Impact of Jets
The force exerted by the jet on Stationary and Moving Plates and Vanes: Flat, Inclined and Curved.
Force exerted on a series of radial vanes - Flat and Curved.
Experiments - 
Determination of Darcy's friction factor for a straight pipe
10
Unit-4: Hydraulic Machines
Turbines: Introduction to hydraulic machines, Classification of turbines, impulse and reaction turbines. Design features, The efficiency of turbines, operating and main characteristic curves.
Hydraulic Pumps: Introduction, Classification of pumps: centrifugal and reciprocating pumps, pumps in series and parallel, The efficiency of the pumps, characteristic curves.
Experiments - 
Demonstration of the working of turbines (Pelton, Francis, Kaplan)
11
Unit-5: Computational Fluid Dynamics
Basic Equations of fluid dynamics, Grid generation. Basics of modelling using OpenFOAM and ANSYS-Fluent. 3D Modelling of Open Channel Flow (Uniform/ Non-Uniform) on OpenFOAM and ANSYS-Fluent. Comparison of Experimental data with different CFD Models.
Basic Equations of fluid dynamics, Grid generation. Basics of modelling using HEC-RAS. 3D Modelling of Open Channel Flow (Uniform/ Non-Uniform) on HEC-RAS. Comparison of Experimental data with different CFD Models.
Experiments - 
1. Create models with initial and boundary conditions for open channel flows
2. 3D Modelling and Validation of Velocity and Discharge using different CFD Models
08

21.  MINI-PROJECT TOPICS
Each group (3–4 students) takes one open-channel problem through three stages. Stage I (CIA I) solves it analytically; Stage II (CIA III) obtains laboratory, field or HEC-RAS data for the same problem; Stage III (Overall CIA) models it in HEC-RAS 2D (groups wanting a 3D model may use OpenFOAM through the SimFlow interface) and validates the model against Stages I and II, linking Units II and V with laboratory experiments 3, 4, 9 and 10.
Sl. No.
Project Topic
Focus
Stage I (CIA I)
Stage II (CIA III)
Stage III (Overall CIA)
1
Uniform flow in a rectangular laboratory flume
Manning's equation; normal depth
Compute normal depth and Manning's n for a design discharge and bed slope.
Measure depth at three discharges in the flume; back-calculate n.
HEC-RAS 2D model of the flume; validate depth and mean velocity; report % error.
2
Most economical trapezoidal section for an irrigation canal
Economical section; uniform flow
Derive the economical section for a given discharge and slope; compare with a rectangular section.
Build a HEC-RAS model of the canal reach for both sections.
2D velocity distribution for both sections (3D in SimFlow optional); compare with Stages I and II.
3
Hydraulic jump in a rectangular channel
Sequent depth; energy loss
Compute sequent depth, jump length and energy loss for three Froude numbers.
Create a jump in the flume below a sluice gate; measure y1, y2 and jump length.
HEC-RAS 2D (full momentum) model of the jump; compare location and sequent depth with Stages I and II.
4
Flow over a broad-crested weir
Critical depth; discharge measurement
Derive the theoretical head-discharge relation.
Use Exp 4 readings to obtain Cd and the rating curve.
HEC-RAS 2D model with a weir structure; compare water surface and Cd with laboratory values.
5
Flow over an ogee spillway crest
Head-discharge; non-uniform flow
Set up the crest geometry and theoretical head-discharge relation.
Use Exp 3 readings to obtain Cd against head.
HEC-RAS 2D head-discharge for the spillway; 3D crest pressures in SimFlow (optional).
6
Venturi / Parshall flume as a critical-depth meter
Channel transition; critical flow
Compute discharge from upstream head; check free-flow conditions.
Measure heads and discharge in the flume or venturi set-up.
HEC-RAS 2D model of the flume; compare computed, measured and simulated discharge.
7
Backwater (M1) profile behind a weir
Gradually varied flow
Compute the M1 profile by the direct step method.
Measure the water-surface profile upstream of a weir in the flume.
HEC-RAS 1D and 2D models of the reach; compare backwater length and depths.
8
Drawdown (M2) profile at a free overfall
Gradually varied flow; critical depth
Compute the M2 profile and brink depth.
Measure brink depth and the upstream profile in the flume.
HEC-RAS 2D model of the overfall; compare brink depth and profile.
9
Flow over a hump and through a width contraction
Specific energy; choking
Compute water-surface change and the choking condition.
Test a hump or contraction in the flume for two discharges.
HEC-RAS 2D model of sub-critical and choked cases; compare with Stages I and II.
10
Estimation of Manning's n for different bed roughness
Roughness coefficient
Review n values and expected depths for smooth and rough beds.
Flume tests with two bed roughnesses; back-calculate n.
Calibrate Manning's n in HEC-RAS 2D; compare simulated and measured depths.
11
Flow in a compound channel (main channel + floodplain)
Divided channel method
Compute discharge capacity for three flow depths.
Build a HEC-RAS cross-section model of the compound channel.
HEC-RAS 2D model of the compound section; examine the velocity field at the interface.
12
Hydraulic capacity of a road culvert
