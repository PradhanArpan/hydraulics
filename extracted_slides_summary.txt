--- Slide 35 ---
One, Two and Three Dimensional Flows 
 The terms one, two or three dimensional flow refers to the number of space coordinate required to describe a flow. 
 It appears that any physical flow is generally three-dimensional. 
 But these are difficult to calculate and thus simplification is necessary. This is achieved by ignoring changes to flow in any of the directions, thus reducing the complexity. 
 It may be possible to reduce a three-dimensional problem into a two-dimensional  or even an one-dimensional problem.

--- Slide 36 ---
One, Two and Three Dimensional Flows 
 One Dimensional Flow: 
 If the flow parameters (such as velocity, pressure, depth etc.) at a given instant in time only vary in the one direction; 
 In the direction of flow and not across the cross-section 
 Across the cross-section and not along the flow

--- Slide 37 ---
One, Two and Three Dimensional Flows 
 Two Dimensional Flow: 
 If it can be assumed that the flow parameters vary in the direction of flow and in one other direction at right angles to the flow.

--- Slide 38 ---
One, Two and Three Dimensional Flows 
 Three Dimensional Flow: 
 If the flow parameters vary in all three directions of flow

--- Slide 8 ---
Impulse turbine 
 Impulse Turbine is a turbine which is driven by the Impulse of Water. 
 In case of an impulse turbine water from the dam flows through a pipeline. 
 After passing a guide mechanism it comes out through a nozzle. 
 All the Energy of water is converted into Kinetic Energy. 
 Water in the form of a jet from nozzle impinges on the buckets fixed to the outer periphery of a running wheel. 
 After coming out of buckets water flows with a low velocity. 
 The Pressure of water both at Entering and Leaving the buckets is Atmospheric.

--- Slide 9 ---
reaction turbine 
 A Reaction Turbine is a turbine into which water enters with High Pressure and flows over Curved Blades. 
 A part of Pressure Head is converted into Kinetic Energy and the remaining part remains as Pressure Head. 
 The Reaction Pressure is responsible for the motion of the Runner. 
 The Difference of Pressure between Inlet and Outlet point is known as Reaction Pressure. 
 The entire Water Passage remains totally Enclosed.

--- Slide 10 ---
Impulse and reaction turbine

--- Slide 11 ---
Impulse and reaction turbine 
 1. Conversion of Fluid Energy 
 Impulse turbine: Fluid energy is converted into KE by a Nozzle 
 Reaction Turbine: Fluid energy is partly transformed into KE, before it enters the runner of the turbine. 
 2. Changes in Pressure and Velocity 
 Impulse Turbine: Pressure remains same 
 Reaction Turbine: Pressure and Velocity changes while passing through runner 
 3. Admittance of Water over the Wheel 
 Impulse Turbine: Enters a part or whole of the wheel circumference 
 Reaction Turbine: Water enters over the wheel circumference

--- Slide 12 ---
Impulse and reaction turbine 
 4. Installation 
 Impulse Turbine: Above the Tail Race, No Draft Tubes 
 Reaction Turbine: Above or below Tail Race, Draft tubes required. 
 5. Flow Regulation 
 Impulse Turbine: By a Needle valve 
 Reaction Turbine: Guide vane assembly

What is computational fluid dynamics? 
 Computational fluid dynamics (CFD) is the science of predicting fluid flow, heat transfer, mass transfer, chemical reactions, and related phenomena by solving the mathematical equations which govern these processes using a numerical process. 
 The result of CFD Analyses is relevant engineering data used in 
 Detailed Product Development. 
 Conceptual Studies of New Designs. 
 Troubleshooting. 
 Redesign. 
 CFD analysis Compliments Testing and Experimentation. 
 Reduces the total effort required in the laboratory.

--- Slide 5 ---
Why USE computational fluid dynamics? 
 Numerical simulations of fluid flow (will) enable 
 architects to design comfortable and safe living environments 
 designers of vehicles to improve the aerodynamic characteristics 
 chemical engineers to maximize the yield from their equipment 
 petroleum engineers to devise optimal oil recovery strategies 
 surgeons to cure arterial diseases (computational hemodynamics) 
 meteorologists to forecast the weather and warn of natural disasters 
 safety experts to reduce health risks from radiation and other hazards 
 military organizations to develop weapons and estimate the damage

Computational Modeling – GRID – Generation Process 
 The process of grid-generation for complex geometries normally involves following steps: 
 Decompose the problem domain into a set of sub-domains (blocks) 
 In each block, generate the requisite grid. Typical sequence of operations would be, 
 Generate edge-grid 
 (i.e. divide the edges of a surface in desired number of one dimensional elements). 
 Using the edge grids, generate the grid on the block-surfaces. 
 Use surface grids as input to generate volume mesh. 
 Check mesh quality, and modify the mesh as required.

 The flow in a closed conduit is not necessarily a pipe flow

--- Slide 6 ---
PIPE FLOW & Open channel flow – Comparison 
 PIPE FLOW 
 The Hydraulic Gradient Line (HGL) is the sum of the Elevation and the Pressure Head (connecting the water surfaces in piezometers). 
 The Energy Gradient Line (EGL) is the sum of the HGL and Velocity Head. 
 The amount of Energy Loss when the liquid flows from section 1 to section 2 is indicated by hL. 
 OPEN CHANNEL FLOW 
 The Hydraulic Gradient Line (HGL) corresponds to the Water Surface Line (WSL);  where it subjected to only Atmospheric Pressure which is commonly referred  to as the Zero Pressure Reference. 
 The Energy Gradient Line (EGL) is the sum of the HGL and Velocity Head. 
 The amount of Energy Loss when the liquid flows from section 1 to section 2 is indicated by hL. 
 For uniform flow, the drop in the EGL is equal to the drop in the channel bed.

 Surface Roughness 
 The surface roughness is represented by the size and shape of the grains of the material forming the wetted perimeter and producing a retarding effect on the flow. This is often considered the only factor in selecting a roughness coefficient, but it is actually just one of several major factors. 
 Generally speaking, Fine Grains result in a relatively low value of n and Coarse Grains, in a high value of n. 
 In Alluvial Streams where the material is fine in grain, such as sand clay, loam, or silt, ,the retarding effect is much less than where the material is coarse such as gravel; or boulders.

--- Slide 39 ---
Factors affecting manning’s n 
 Channel Irregularity 
 Channel irregularity comprises irregularities in wetted perimeter and variations in cross section, size, and shape along the channel length. 
 In natural channels, such irregularities are usually introduced by the presence of sand bars, sand waves, ridges and depressions, and holes and humps on the channel bed. 
 These irregularities definitely introduce roughness in addition to that caused by surface roughness and other factors. 
 A Gradual and Uniform change in cross section, size, and shape will not appreciably affect the value of n, but abrupt changed or alternation of small and large sections necessitates the use of a large value of n.

 Silting and Scouring 
 Silting may change a very irregular channel into a comparatively uniform one and decrease n. 
 Scouring on the other hand may do the reverse and increase n. 
 However, the dominant effect of silting will depend on the nature of the material deposited. Uneven deposits such as sand bars and sand waves will increase the 'roughness’. 
 The amount of uniformity of scouring will depend on the material forming the wetted perimeter. Thus, a sandy or gravelly bed will be eroded more uniformly than a clay bed.

 Size and Shape of Channel 
 There is no definite evidence about the size and shape of a channel as an important factor affecting the value of n. An increase in hydraulic radius may either increase or decrease n, depending on the condition of the channel ( as happens in a multi stage channels) .

 Stage and Discharge 
 The n value in most streams decreases with increase in stage and in discharge. 
 When the water is shallow, the irregularities of the channel bottom are exposed and their effects become pronounced. 
 If the bed and banks of a channel are equally smooth and regular and the bottom  slope is uniform, the value of n may remain almost the same at all so a constant is usually assumed in the flow computation. This happens mostly in artificial channels.

 Owing to the seasonal growth of aquatic plants, grass, weeds, willow, and trees in the channel or on the banks, the value of n may increase in the growing season and diminish in the dormant season. 
 Suspended Material and Bed Load 
 The suspended material and the bed load, whether moving or not moving, would consume energy and cause head loss or increase the apparent channel roughness.

 Product of mass and acceleration of the flowing fluid and acts in the direction opposite to the direction of acceleration.
