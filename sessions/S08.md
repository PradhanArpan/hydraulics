> [!note] Core Concept
> When a boundary layer encounters an **adverse pressure gradient** ($\frac{dp}{dx} > 0$, where pressure increases and velocity decreases downstream), fluid particles near the wall—already slowed by viscous friction—are rapidly brought to rest and forced to reverse direction. This causes the boundary layer to detach from the solid surface, termed **boundary layer separation**.

## 1. Mechanics of Separation
Along a surface:
1. **Favorable Pressure Gradient ($\frac{dp}{dx} < 0$):** Pressure drops downstream; flow accelerates; velocity profile is full and stable; separation cannot occur.
2. **Zero Pressure Gradient ($\frac{dp}{dx} = 0$):** Flow over flat plates; shear diminishes gradually; no separation.
3. **Adverse Pressure Gradient ($\frac{dp}{dx} > 0$):** Pressure rises downstream (e.g., expanding diffusers, downstream side of bridge piers, curved spillway crests). Kinetic energy of near-wall particles is exhausted, leading to:
   - At point of separation: $\left(\frac{\partial u}{\partial y}\right)_{y=0} = 0 \implies \tau_0 = 0$
   - Downstream of separation: $\left(\frac{\partial u}{\partial y}\right)_{y=0} < 0$, producing reverse backflow and large turbulent eddies (wake).

## 2. Consequences of Separation
- Massive increase in **form (pressure) drag**.
- Stalling and loss of lift on hydraulic hydrofoils and turbine blades.
- Vortex shedding causing structural vibrations in bridge piers and chimney stacks.
- Cavitation in zones of detached low-pressure eddies.

### Example 8.1: Verification of Boundary Layer Separation
**Problem:** A velocity profile in a diverging channel is given by:
$$\frac{u}{U} = 2\left(\frac{y}{\delta}\right) - 4\left(\frac{y}{\delta}\right)^2 + \left(\frac{y}{\delta}\right)^3$$
Determine whether the flow is attached, on the verge of separation, or separated.

**Solution:**
Differentiating with respect to $y$:
$$\frac{\partial u}{\partial y} = U \left[\frac{2}{\delta} - \frac{8y}{\delta^2} + \frac{3y^2}{\delta^3}\right]$$
Evaluating at the solid wall ($y = 0$):
$$\left(\frac{\partial u}{\partial y}\right)_{y=0} = U \cdot \frac{2}{\delta} = \frac{2U}{\delta} > 0$$
Since the velocity gradient at the wall is positive, wall shear stress $\tau_0 > 0$. Therefore, the flow is **fully attached** and not separated.

### Key Takeaways
- Separation condition: $\left(\frac{\partial u}{\partial y}\right)_{y=0} = 0 \implies \tau_0 = 0$.
- Requires an adverse pressure gradient ($\frac{dp}{dx} > 0$).
- Causes large wake, high pressure drag, vortex-induced vibrations, and cavitation risk.
