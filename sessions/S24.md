> [!note] Core Concept
> In reaction turbines (Francis) and centrifugal pumps, fluid moves radially or diagonally across curved vanes mounted on a rotating runner. Because the tangential speed $u = \omega r$ varies from inlet to exit, linear momentum is replaced by the **Conservation of Angular Momentum**, yielding **Euler's Turbine Equation**.

## 1. Angular Momentum & Euler's Torque Equation
Let water enter a runner at radius $r_1$ with whirl velocity $V_{w1}$ and exit at radius $r_2$ with whirl velocity $V_{w2}$:
1. Angular momentum entering runner per second:
   $$\Omega_{in} = \rho Q (V_{w1} r_1)$$
2. Angular momentum exiting runner per second:
   $$\Omega_{out} = \rho Q (V_{w2} r_2)$$
3. By the moment of momentum theorem, torque $T$ exerted on the runner is:
   $$T = \Omega_{in} - \Omega_{out} = \rho Q (V_{w1} r_1 \pm V_{w2} r_2)$$
   *(Minus sign when whirl velocities at inlet and outlet are in the same direction).*

## 2. Work Done and Euler's Head
Multiplying torque $T$ by angular velocity $\omega$ gives work done per second:
$$W = T \omega = \rho Q (V_{w1} r_1 \omega \pm V_{w2} r_2 \omega) = \rho Q (V_{w1} u_1 \pm V_{w2} u_2)$$
Where $u_1 = \omega r_1$ and $u_2 = \omega r_2$ are the peripheral blade speeds at inlet and outlet.

The **Euler Head ($H_e$)**—the energy transferred per unit weight of fluid—is:
$$H_e = \frac{W}{\rho g Q} = \frac{V_{w1} u_1 \pm V_{w2} u_2}{g}$$

### Example 24.1: Torque and Power of an Inward Flow Runner
**Problem:** An inward radial flow runner has an outer diameter $D_1 = 0.80 \text{ m}$ and inner diameter $D_2 = 0.40 \text{ m}$. It rotates at $N = 600 \text{ rpm}$. Water enters at $Q = 0.50 \text{ m}^3/\text{s}$ with inlet whirl velocity $V_{w1} = 18.0 \text{ m/s}$ and discharges radially at exit with zero whirl ($V_{w2} = 0$). Calculate:
1. Blade peripheral velocities $u_1$ and $u_2$.
2. Torque $T$ exerted on the runner.
3. Power developed in kilowatts.
4. Euler head $H_e$.

**Solution:**
1. **Peripheral Blade Speeds:**
   $$\omega = \frac{2\pi N}{60} = \frac{2\pi \times 600}{60} = 62.83 \text{ rad/s}$$
   $$u_1 = \omega \frac{D_1}{2} = 62.83 \times 0.40 = 25.13 \text{ m/s}$$
   $$u_2 = \omega \frac{D_2}{2} = 62.83 \times 0.20 = 12.57 \text{ m/s}$$
2. **Torque $T$ on Runner:**
   Since $V_{w2} = 0$:
   $$T = \rho Q V_{w1} r_1 = 1000 \times 0.50 \times 18.0 \times 0.40 = 3,600 \text{ N}\cdot\text{m}$$
3. **Power Developed:**
   $$P = T \omega = 3,600 \times 62.83 = 226,188 \text{ W} = 226.19 \text{ kW}$$
4. **Euler Head $H_e$:**
   $$H_e = \frac{V_{w1} u_1}{g} = \frac{18.0 \times 25.13}{9.81} = \frac{452.34}{9.81} = 46.11 \text{ m}$$

<!--tab:Summary-->
### Key Takeaways
- Moment of momentum: $T = \rho Q (V_{w1} r_1 - V_{w2} r_2)$.
- Euler's equation: $W = \rho Q (V_{w1} u_1 - V_{w2} u_2)$.
- Radial discharge ($V_{w2} = 0$) maximizes work extraction.
