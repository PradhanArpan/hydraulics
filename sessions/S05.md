> [!note] Core Concept
> In turbulent boundary layers, turbulent eddies induce rapid momentum exchange between slow fluid near the wall and high-speed fluid in the outer stream. This makes the velocity profile much fuller, drastically increases wall shear stress, and accelerates boundary layer growth rate to $\delta \propto x^{0.8}$.

## 1. Velocity Profile & 1/7th Power Law
For smooth plates at moderate Reynolds numbers ($5 \times 10^5 < Re_x < 10^7$), Prandtl proposed the **1/7th Power Law**:
$$\frac{u}{U_\infty} = \left(\frac{y}{\delta}\right)^{1/7}$$
Wall shear stress from Blasius pipe resistance empirical law:
$$\tau_0 = 0.0225 \rho U_\infty^2 \left(\frac{\nu}{U_\infty \delta}\right)^{1/4}$$

Using Von Kármán's integral momentum equation with $\theta = \frac{7}{72}\delta$:
$$\delta(x) = \frac{0.37 x}{Re_x^{1/5}} = \frac{0.37 x}{Re_x^{0.2}}$$
Local skin friction coefficient:
$$C_{fx} = \frac{0.0576}{Re_x^{0.2}} \quad \text{or} \quad \frac{0.059}{Re_x^{0.2}}$$
Total drag coefficient (assuming turbulent from leading edge):
$$C_D = \frac{0.072}{Re_L^{0.2}} \quad \text{to} \quad \frac{0.074}{Re_L^{0.2}}$$

### Example 5.1: Turbulent Boundary Layer Growth on a High-Speed Flume
**Problem:** Water ($ν = 1.0 \times 10^{-6} \text{ m}^2/\text{s}$) flows at $U = 6.0 \text{ m/s}$ past a plate $3.0 \text{ m}$ long. Assuming the boundary layer is tripped and turbulent from the leading edge, calculate:
1. Boundary layer thickness $\delta$ at $x = 3.0 \text{ m}$.
2. Wall shear stress $\tau_0$ at the trailing edge.

**Solution:**
1. **Trailing Edge Reynolds Number:**
   $$Re_L = \frac{6.0 \times 3.0}{1.0 \times 10^{-6}} = 1.80 \times 10^7$$
2. **Boundary Layer Thickness $\delta$:**
   $$\delta = \frac{0.37 L}{Re_L^{0.2}} = \frac{0.37 \times 3.0}{(1.80 \times 10^7)^{0.2}} = \frac{1.11}{28.527} = 0.0389 \text{ m} = 38.9 \text{ mm}$$
   *(Note: A laminar boundary layer at this distance would only be $\approx 3.5 \text{ mm}$ thick—turbulent layer is >10 times thicker!)*
3. **Wall Shear Stress $\tau_0$:**
   $$C_{fx} = \frac{0.0576}{(1.80 \times 10^7)^{0.2}} = \frac{0.0576}{28.527} = 2.019 \times 10^{-3}$$
   $$\tau_0 = C_{fx} \cdot \frac{1}{2}\rho U^2 = (2.019 \times 10^{-3}) \times 0.5 \times 1000 \times 36.0 = 36.35 \text{ N/m}^2$$

### Key Takeaways
- Velocity profile: $u/U = (y/\delta)^{1/7}$.
- Thickness growth: $\delta = 0.37 x / Re_x^{0.2} \propto x^{0.8}$.
- Local skin friction: $C_{fx} = 0.059 / Re_x^{0.2}$.
- High near-wall momentum gives turbulent boundary layers superior resistance to separation.
