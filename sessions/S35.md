> [!note] Core Concept
> A **Centrifugal Pump** is a reversed reaction turbine that absorbs mechanical energy from an electric motor to increase the pressure and elevation of a liquid. Liquid enters axially through the suction eye and is accelerated radially outward by curved rotating impeller vanes into a decelerating volute casing that converts dynamic velocity head into static pressure head.

## 1. Impeller Blade Curvature
1. **Backward Curved Vanes ($\beta_2 < 90^\circ$):**
   - Self-regulating power curve (power demand levels off at high discharges).
   - Higher manometric efficiency due to lower exit velocity and less kinetic energy dissipation in the casing.
   - **Universal standard for all commercial centrifugal pumps.**
2. **Radial Vanes ($\beta_2 = 90^\circ$):** Used in deep-well submersible slurry pumps.
3. **Forward Curved Vanes ($\beta_2 > 90^\circ$):** Overloading power curve; used only in low-speed air ventilation blowers.

## 2. Manometric Head & Efficiencies
1. **Total Manometric Head ($H_m$):** Total head imparted by pump to the water:
   $$H_m = h_s + h_d + h_{fs} + h_{fd} + \frac{V_d^2}{2g}$$
2. **Euler Head ($H_e$):** From Euler's pump equation (assuming radial entry at inlet eye, $V_{w1} = 0$):
   $$H_e = \frac{V_{w2} u_2}{g}$$
3. **Manometric Efficiency ($\eta_{mano}$):**
   $$\eta_{mano} = \frac{H_m}{H_e} = \frac{g H_m}{V_{w2} u_2}$$
4. **Minimum Starting Speed ($N_{min}$):**
   For flow to commence, the centrifugal head created by impeller rotation must overcome the static delivery head:
   $$\frac{u_2^2 - u_1^2}{2g} \ge H_m \implies N_{min} = \frac{120 \eta_{mano} V_{w2} D_2}{\pi (D_2^2 - D_1^2)}$$

### Example 35.1: Manometric Head and Efficiency Calculation
**Problem:** A centrifugal pump impeller has internal diameter $D_1 = 0.20 \text{ m}$ and external diameter $D_2 = 0.40 \text{ m}$. It rotates at $N = 1200 \text{ rpm}$. The impeller vanes are backward curved with exit angle $\beta_2 = 30^\circ$. The radial velocity of flow is constant at $V_{f2} = 2.50 \text{ m/s}$. Water enters the impeller eye radially. Calculate:
1. Impeller peripheral speeds $u_1$ and $u_2$.
2. Exit whirl velocity $V_{w2}$.
3. Euler head $H_e$.
4. Manometric head $H_m$ if manometric efficiency is $\eta_{mano} = 82\%$.

**Solution:**
1. **Peripheral Speeds:**
   $$u_1 = \frac{\pi D_1 N}{60} = \frac{\pi \times 0.20 \times 1200}{60} = 12.57 \text{ m/s}$$
   $$u_2 = \frac{\pi D_2 N}{60} = \frac{\pi \times 0.40 \times 1200}{60} = 25.13 \text{ m/s}$$
2. **Exit Velocity Triangle:**
   From the exit triangle:
   $$\tan\beta_2 = \frac{V_{f2}}{u_2 - V_{w2}} \implies u_2 - V_{w2} = \frac{V_{f2}}{\tan(30^\circ)} = \frac{2.50}{0.57735} = 4.33 \text{ m/s}$$
   $$V_{w2} = u_2 - 4.33 = 25.13 - 4.33 = 20.80 \text{ m/s}$$
3. **Euler Head $H_e$:**
   $$H_e = \frac{V_{w2} u_2}{g} = \frac{20.80 \times 25.13}{9.81} = \frac{522.70}{9.81} = 53.28 \text{ m}$$
4. **Manometric Head $H_m$:**
   $$H_m = \eta_{mano} \times H_e = 0.82 \times 53.28 = 43.69 \text{ m}$$

### Key Takeaways
- Centrifugal pump converts mechanical work into fluid pressure head.
- Backward curved vanes ($\beta_2 < 90^\circ$) provide optimal efficiency and non-overloading characteristics.
- Manometric head: $H_m = \eta_{mano} \frac{V_{w2} u_2}{g}$.
- Priming is required to purge air before startup.
