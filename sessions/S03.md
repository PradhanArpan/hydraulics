> [!note] Core Concept
> **Theodore von Kármán (1921)** derived an integral momentum equation by applying the principle of conservation of momentum to a control volume encompassing the boundary layer. It enables determination of boundary layer growth $\delta(x)$ and wall shear stress $\tau_0(x)$ without requiring exact analytical solution of the partial differential Navier-Stokes equations.

## 1. Derivation of the Momentum Integral Equation
Consider a control volume $ABCD$ of length $dx$, unit width, extending from the solid boundary ($y=0$) to a height $h > \delta$ in the free stream:
1. Mass entering face $AB$: $\dot{m}_{in} = \int_0^h \rho u \, dy$
2. Mass exiting face $CD$: $\dot{m}_{out} = \dot{m}_{in} + \frac{d}{dx}\left[\int_0^h \rho u \, dy\right] dx$
3. By mass conservation, fluid entering top face $BC$ moves at velocity $U_\infty$:
   $$\dot{m}_{top} = \dot{m}_{out} - \dot{m}_{in} = \frac{d}{dx}\left[\int_0^h \rho u \, dy\right] dx$$
4. Net momentum flux exiting the control volume:
   $$\Delta M = \frac{d}{dx}\left[\int_0^h \rho u^2 dy\right] dx - U_\infty \frac{d}{dx}\left[\int_0^h \rho u \, dy\right] dx$$
5. Net external forces acting on control volume (pressure force and bottom wall shear $\tau_0$):
   $$\sum F_x = -\tau_0 dx - h \frac{dp}{dx} dx$$

Equating $\sum F_x = \Delta M$ and using Euler's equation for the external stream ($-\frac{1}{\rho}\frac{dp}{dx} = U_\infty \frac{dU_\infty}{dx}$), we obtain the **General Von Kármán Momentum Integral Equation**:
$$\frac{\tau_0}{\rho U_\infty^2} = \frac{d\theta}{dx} + (2\theta + \delta^*)\frac{1}{U_\infty}\frac{dU_\infty}{dx}$$

For flow along a **flat plate with zero pressure gradient** ($\frac{dU_\infty}{dx} = 0$):
$$\frac{\tau_0}{\rho U_\infty^2} = \frac{d\theta}{dx} \implies \tau_0 = \rho U_\infty^2 \frac{d\theta}{dx}$$

### Example 3.1: Shear Stress from Von Kármán Equation
**Problem:** A laminar boundary layer has velocity distribution $\frac{u}{U} = \sin\left(\frac{\pi y}{2\delta}\right)$. Using Von Kármán's equation:
1. Find momentum thickness $\theta$ as a fraction of $\delta$.
2. Express wall shear stress $\tau_0$ in terms of $\frac{d\delta}{dx}$.

**Solution:**
1. **Momentum Thickness $\theta$:**
   $$\theta = \delta \int_0^1 \sin\left(\frac{\pi \eta}{2}\right) \left[1 - \sin\left(\frac{\pi \eta}{2}\right)\right] d\eta$$
   Evaluating the integrals:
   $$\int_0^1 \sin\left(\frac{\pi \eta}{2}\right) d\eta = \frac{2}{\pi}$$
   $$\int_0^1 \sin^2\left(\frac{\pi \eta}{2}\right) d\eta = \frac{1}{2}$$
   $$\theta = \delta \left(\frac{2}{\pi} - \frac{1}{2}\right) = \delta \left(\frac{4 - \pi}{2\pi}\right) \approx 0.1366 \delta$$
2. **Wall Shear Stress $\tau_0$:**
   $$\tau_0 = \rho U^2 \frac{d\theta}{dx} = 0.1366 \rho U^2 \frac{d\delta}{dx}$$

### Examination Questions
#### 5-Mark Questions
1. Derive Von Kármán's momentum integral equation for flow past a flat plate under zero pressure gradient.
   *Solution Outline:* Apply Reynolds transport theorem/momentum balance on control volume of length $dx$ and height $h > \delta$.
#### 10-Mark Questions
1. State the assumptions in Von Kármán's integral momentum equation. Use this equation to prove that for any velocity profile satisfying $\tau_0 = \mu \left(\frac{\partial u}{\partial y}\right)_{y=0}$, the boundary layer thickness grows as $\delta \propto \sqrt{x}$ in laminar flow.
   *Solution Outline:* Set $\tau_0 = \mu \frac{U}{\delta} C_1 = \rho U^2 C_2 \frac{d\delta}{dx}$, separate variables $\delta d\delta = \frac{\mu C_1}{\rho U C_2} dx$, integrate to get $\delta^2 \propto x \implies \delta \propto \sqrt{x}$.

### Key Takeaways
- General equation: $\frac{\tau_0}{\rho U^2} = \frac{d\theta}{dx} + (2\theta + \delta^*)\frac{1}{U}\frac{dU}{dx}$.
- Flat plate zero-gradient form: $\tau_0 = \rho U^2 \frac{d\theta}{dx}$.
- Fundamental link between boundary layer shear drag and momentum deficit thickness rate.
