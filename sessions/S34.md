> [!note] Core Concept
> Operating hydraulic turbines across varying electrical grid demands requires understanding their **Characteristic Curves** (Main, Operating, and Muschel curves). Furthermore, avoiding **Cavitation**—the formation and violent collapse of vapor bubbles in low-pressure zones—is vital to prevent catastrophic pitting erosion and vibration.

## 1. Characteristic Curves
1. **Main Characteristics (Constant Head Curves):** Plots of Unit Speed ($N_u$) vs Unit Discharge ($Q_u$), Unit Power ($P_u$), and Efficiency ($\eta$) under constant test head $H$.
2. **Operating Characteristics (Constant Speed Curves):** Plots of Discharge ($Q$) vs Head ($H$), Brake Power ($P$), and Efficiency ($\eta$) when the generator runs locked to grid frequency ($50 \text{ Hz} \implies N = \text{const}$).
3. **Muschel (Iso-Efficiency) Curves:** Topographical contours of constant efficiency plotted on a graph of Unit Speed vs Unit Discharge, identifying the "sweet spot" of optimal plant operation.

## 2. Cavitation in Hydraulic Turbines
When local static pressure drops below the saturation vapor pressure of water ($p \le p_v \approx 2.34 \text{ kPa}$ at $20^\circ\text{C}$), water boils, forming vapor cavities. As these bubbles are carried into higher-pressure zones, they collapse implosively in microseconds, generating localized micro-jets of up to $10,000 \text{ atmospheres}$ that gouge metal pits out of blade surfaces.

**Thoma's Cavitation Factor ($\sigma$):**
$$\sigma = \frac{H_{atm} - H_v - H_s}{H}$$
Where:
- $H_{atm}$: Atmospheric pressure head ($pprox 10.3 \text{ m}$).
- $H_v$: Saturation vapor pressure head ($pprox 0.25 \text{ m}$).
- $H_s$: Suction height of runner above tailrace level.
- $H$: Net available turbine head.

To prevent cavitation, the plant operating factor must strictly exceed the **critical cavitation factor** $\sigma_c$:
$$\sigma > \sigma_c$$
This sets the **maximum allowable turbine setting height $H_{s\_max}$**:
$$H_{s\_max} = H_{atm} - H_v - \sigma_c H$$

<!--tab:Worked examples-->
### Example 34.1: Critical Setting Height of a Francis Turbine
**Problem:** A Francis turbine operates under head $H = 45.0 \text{ m}$. Empirical correlations for its specific speed yield a critical cavitation factor $\sigma_c = 0.12$. Atmospheric pressure is $10.10 \text{ m}$ of water and vapor pressure is $0.30 \text{ m}$. Determine the maximum permissible elevation of the runner above the tailrace to guarantee safety against cavitation.

**Solution:**
Using Thoma's cavitation equation:
$$\sigma = \frac{H_{atm} - H_v - H_s}{H} > \sigma_c$$
Setting $\sigma = \sigma_c = 0.12$:
$$0.12 = \frac{10.10 - 0.30 - H_s}{45.0} = \frac{9.80 - H_s}{45.0}$$
$$9.80 - H_s = 0.12 \times 45.0 = 5.40 \text{ m}$$
$$H_s = 9.80 - 5.40 = 4.40 \text{ m}$$
The turbine runner must not be installed higher than **$4.40 \text{ m}$ above tailrace level**.

<!--tab:Summary-->
### Key Takeaways
- Cavitation occurs when $p \le p_v$; imploding vapor cavities cause pitting erosion.
- Thoma factor: $\sigma = \frac{H_{atm} - H_v - H_s}{H} > \sigma_c$.
- Maximum setting height: $H_{s\_max} = H_{atm} - H_v - \sigma_c H$.
