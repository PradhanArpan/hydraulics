> [!note] Economic Design Optimization Criterion
> A channel section is defined as **most economical** (or **hydraulically most efficient**) when the cost of construction—comprising excavation and perimeter lining—is minimized.
> By Chezy's ($Q = A C \sqrt{R S_0}$) and Manning's ($Q = \frac{1}{n} A R^{2/3} S_0^{1/2}$) equations, for a given discharge $Q$, longitudinal slope $S_0$, and boundary roughness, the required cross-sectional area $A$ is minimum when the **hydraulic radius $R = A/P$ is maximum**.
> Therefore, for a fixed flow area $A$, the most economical section is the one that **minimizes the wetted perimeter $P$**.

## 1. Most Economical Rectangular Section
For a rectangular channel of bed width $B$ and flow depth $y$:
$$A = B y \implies B = \frac{A}{y}$$
$$P = B + 2y = \frac{A}{y} + 2y$$
Differentiating wetted perimeter $P$ with respect to depth $y$ and setting $\frac{dP}{dy} = 0$:
$$\frac{dP}{dy} = -\frac{A}{y^2} + 2 = 0 \implies \frac{B y}{y^2} = 2 \implies \mathbf{B = 2y}$$
Hydraulic Radius:
$$R = \frac{A}{P} = \frac{(2y)y}{2y + 2y} = \frac{2y^2}{4y} = \mathbf{\frac{y}{2}}$$
Hydraulic Depth:
$$D = \frac{A}{T} = \frac{2y^2}{2y} = y$$

**The Three Cardinal Rules of the Best Rectangular Section:**
1. **Bed width is twice the depth:** $B = 2y$.
2. **Hydraulic radius is half the depth:** $R = y/2$.
3. **Inscribed Semicircle:** A semicircle drawn with center on the water surface and radius equal to depth $y$ is tangent to the channel bottom and both vertical sidewalls.

## 2. Most Economical Triangular Section
For a symmetrical V-shaped triangular flume with side slopes $1\text{V} : z\text{H}$ (semi-vertex angle $\theta$, such that $z = \tan \theta$):
$$A = z y^2 \implies y = \sqrt{\frac{A}{z}}$$
$$P = 2y\sqrt{1+z^2} = 2\sqrt{\frac{A}{z}}\sqrt{1+z^2} = 2\sqrt{A}\sqrt{\frac{1+z^2}{z}} = 2\sqrt{A}\left(z^{-1/2} + z^{1/2}\right)^{1/2}$$
Minimizing $P$ with respect to side slope $z$:
$$\frac{d}{dz}\left(\frac{1+z^2}{z}\right) = \frac{z(2z) - (1+z^2)(1)}{z^2} = \frac{z^2 - 1}{z^2} = 0 \implies z = 1.0$$
Since $z = \tan \theta = 1.0$:
$$\mathbf{\theta = 45^\circ \implies \text{Total Vertex Angle } 2\theta = 90^\circ}$$
Hydraulic Radius:
$$R = \frac{A}{P} = \frac{y^2}{2y\sqrt{1+1}} = \frac{y^2}{2\sqrt{2} y} = \mathbf{\frac{y}{2\sqrt{2}}}$$
*Rule:* The most efficient triangular channel has a **right-angled vertex ($90^\circ$)** with $45^\circ$ sloping sides.

## 3. Most Economical Trapezoidal Section (The Half-Hexagon)
For a trapezoidal section with bed width $B$, depth $y$, and side slopes $1\text{V} : m\text{H}$ (angle with horizontal $\theta$):
$$A = (B + my)y \implies B = \frac{A}{y} - my$$
$$P = B + 2y\sqrt{1+m^2} = \frac{A}{y} - my + 2y\sqrt{1+m^2}$$

### Condition 1: Minimizing $P$ with respect to Depth $y$ ($\frac{\partial P}{\partial y} = 0$)
$$\frac{\partial P}{\partial y} = -\frac{A}{y^2} - m + 2\sqrt{1+m^2} = 0 \implies \frac{B + my}{y} + m = 2\sqrt{1+m^2}$$
$$\frac{B + 2my}{2} = y\sqrt{1+m^2} \iff \mathbf{\frac{T}{2} = \text{Sloping Side Length}}$$
*Rule 1:* **Half of the top width equals the length of one sloping side.**
Hydraulic Radius:
$$R = \frac{A}{P} = \frac{(B + my)y}{B + 2y\sqrt{1+m^2}} = \frac{(B + my)y}{B + (B + 2my)} = \mathbf{\frac{y}{2}}$$
*Rule 2:* **Hydraulic radius is half the depth of flow ($R = y/2$).**

### Condition 2: Minimizing $P$ with respect to Side Slope $m$ ($\frac{\partial P}{\partial m} = 0$)
$$\frac{\partial P}{\partial m} = -y + 2y \frac{2m}{2\sqrt{1+m^2}} = 0 \implies \frac{2m}{\sqrt{1+m^2}} = 1 \implies 4m^2 = 1 + m^2$$
$$3m^2 = 1 \implies m = \frac{1}{\sqrt{3}} \iff \mathbf{\theta = 60^\circ}$$
*Rule 3:* **The side slope must make an angle of $60^\circ$ with the horizontal.**
*Rule 4:* The cross-section forms a **half-regular hexagon**, and a semicircle drawn from the center of the water surface is tangent to the bed and both side slopes!

## 4. Circular Conduit Running Partially Full
For a circular pipe of diameter $D$ subtending an angle $2\theta$ at the center:
- Flow Area: $A = \frac{D^2}{8} (2\theta - \sin 2\theta)$
- Wetted Perimeter: $P = \frac{D}{2} (2\theta) = D \theta$
- Hydraulic Radius: $R = \frac{A}{P} = \frac{D}{4} \left(1 - \frac{\sin 2\theta}{2\theta}\right)$

1. **Condition for Maximum Velocity ($V_{\text{max}}$):**
   Maximizing $R$: $\frac{dR}{d\theta} = 0 \implies 2\theta = 257^\circ 30' \implies \mathbf{y = 0.81 D}$.
   $$V_{\text{max}} = 1.14 \times V_{\text{full}}$$
2. **Condition for Maximum Discharge ($Q_{\text{max}}$):**
   Maximizing $A R^{2/3}$: $\frac{d(A R^{2/3})}{d\theta} = 0 \implies 2\theta = 308^\circ \implies \mathbf{y = 0.95 D}$.
   $$Q_{\text{max}} = 1.07 \times Q_{\text{full}}$$

> [!important] Engineering Paradox
> A circular conduit running partially full at $y = 0.95 D$ carries **$7\%$ more water** than when running completely full under gravity! This occurs because as the water level approaches the crown ($y \to D$), the wetted perimeter increases rapidly without adding much cross-sectional area, creating excessive wall friction that chokes velocity.

### Example 13.1: Maximum Discharge in a Rectangular Channel (Slide 11)
**Problem:** A rectangular channel of width $B = 4.0 \text{ m}$ is laid on a bed slope of $1 \text{ in } 1500$. Find the maximum discharge through the channel. Take Chezy's $C = 50$.

:::sol Detailed Step-by-Step Solution
For a rectangular channel to carry maximum discharge, it must be the most economical section:
1. **Optimal Depth:**
   $$B = 2y \implies y = \frac{B}{2} = \frac{4.0}{2} = \mathbf{2.0 \text{ m}}$$
2. **Hydraulic Elements:**
   - Area: $A = B y = 4.0 \times 2.0 = 8.0 \text{ m}^2$
   - Wetted Perimeter: $P = B + 2y = 4.0 + 2(2.0) = 8.0 \text{ m}$
   - Hydraulic Radius: $R = \frac{A}{P} = \frac{8.0}{8.0} = \mathbf{1.0 \text{ m}} = \frac{y}{2}$ (Verified)
3. **Discharge:**
   $$V = C \sqrt{R S_0} = 50 \times \sqrt{1.0 \times \frac{1}{1500}} = \frac{50}{\sqrt{1500}} = \frac{50}{38.73} = \mathbf{1.291 \text{ m/s}}$$
   $$Q_{\text{max}} = A V = 8.0 \times 1.291 = \mathbf{10.328 \text{ m}^3/\text{s}}$$
:::

### Example 13.2: Most Economical Rectangular Dimensions for Given Q (Slide 12)
**Problem:** A rectangular channel carries water at the rate of $400 \text{ L/s}$ ($0.40 \text{ m}^3/\text{s}$) when the bed slope is $1 \text{ in } 2000$. Find the most economical dimensions of the channel if Chezy's $C = 50$.

:::sol Detailed Step-by-Step Solution
1. **Conditions for Best Rectangular Section:**
   $$B = 2y, \quad A = 2y^2, \quad R = \frac{y}{2}$$
2. **Chezy Discharge Equation:**
   $$Q = A C \sqrt{R S_0} \implies 0.40 = (2y^2) \times 50 \times \sqrt{\frac{y}{2} \times \frac{1}{2000}}$$
   $$0.40 = 100 y^2 \times \frac{y^{1/2}}{\sqrt{4000}} = \frac{100}{63.245} y^{5/2} = 1.5811 y^{5/2}$$
   $$y^{5/2} = \frac{0.40}{1.5811} = 0.25298 \implies y = (0.25298)^{2/5} = \mathbf{0.577 \text{ m}}$$
3. **Channel Dimensions:**
   - Optimal Depth: $\mathbf{y = 0.577 \text{ m}}$
   - Optimal Bed Width: $B = 2y = 2 \times 0.577 = \mathbf{1.154 \text{ m}}$
:::

### Example 13.3: Increasing Discharge by Reshaping to Economical Section (Slide 13)
**Problem:** A rectangular channel $4.0 \text{ m}$ wide carries water at a depth of $1.5 \text{ m}$. The bed slope is $1 \text{ in } 1000$ and Chezy's $C = 55$. It is desired to increase the discharge to a maximum by changing the dimensions of the section while keeping the cross-sectional area, bed slope, and channel roughness constant. Find the new dimensions and the percentage increase in discharge.

:::sol Detailed Comparison Solution
1. **Original Existing Channel:**
   - Area: $A = 4.0 \times 1.5 = 6.0 \text{ m}^2$
   - Perimeter: $P_1 = 4.0 + 2(1.5) = 7.0 \text{ m}$
   - Hydraulic Radius: $R_1 = \frac{6.0}{7.0} = 0.8571 \text{ m}$
   - Original Discharge:
     $$Q_1 = A C \sqrt{R_1 S_0} = 6.0 \times 55 \times \sqrt{0.8571 \times 10^{-3}} = 330 \times 0.029277 = \mathbf{9.661 \text{ m}^3/\text{s}}$$
2. **New Most Economical Section (Same Area $A = 6.0 \text{ m}^2$):**
   - Condition: $B_2 = 2y_2 \implies A = 2y_2^2 = 6.0 \implies y_2^2 = 3.0$
   - New Depth: $y_2 = \sqrt{3.0} = \mathbf{1.732 \text{ m}}$
   - New Width: $B_2 = 2y_2 = 2 \times 1.732 = \mathbf{3.464 \text{ m}}$
   - New Perimeter: $P_2 = 3.464 + 2(1.732) = 6.928 \text{ m}$
   - New Hydraulic Radius: $R_2 = \frac{y_2}{2} = \frac{1.732}{2} = \mathbf{0.8660 \text{ m}}$
3. **New Discharge & Percentage Increase:**
   $$Q_2 = A C \sqrt{R_2 S_0} = 6.0 \times 55 \times \sqrt{0.8660 \times 10^{-3}} = 330 \times 0.029428 = \mathbf{9.711 \text{ m}^3/\text{s}}$$
   $$\text{Increase in Discharge} = Q_2 - Q_1 = 9.711 - 9.661 = \mathbf{0.050 \text{ m}^3/\text{s}} \quad (\mathbf{+0.52\%})$$
:::

### Example 13.4: Economical Trapezoidal Canal Dimensions & Discharge (Slide 14)
**Problem:** A trapezoidal channel has side slopes of $1\text{H} : 2\text{V}$ ($m = 0.5$) and bed slope $S_0 = 1 \text{ in } 1500$. The cross-sectional area is $40 \text{ m}^2$. Find the dimensions of the most economical section and determine its discharge taking Chezy's $C = 50$.

:::sol Detailed Step-by-Step Solution
1. **Economic Criterion for Trapezoidal Section:**
   $$\frac{T}{2} = \text{Sloping Side} \implies \frac{B + 2my}{2} = y\sqrt{1+m^2}$$
   Given $m = 0.5$: $\sqrt{1+m^2} = \sqrt{1 + 0.25} = \sqrt{1.25} = 1.1180$
   $$\frac{B + 2(0.5)y}{2} = 1.1180 y \implies B + y = 2.236 y \implies \mathbf{B = 1.236 y}$$
2. **Area Equation:**
   $$A = (B + my)y = (1.236y + 0.5y)y = 1.736 y^2 = 40.0 \text{ m}^2$$
   $$y^2 = \frac{40.0}{1.736} = 23.04 \implies \mathbf{y = 4.80 \text{ m}}$$
   $$B = 1.236 \times 4.80 = \mathbf{5.933 \text{ m}}$$
3. **Discharge Calculation:**
   - Hydraulic Radius: $R = \frac{y}{2} = \frac{4.80}{2} = \mathbf{2.40 \text{ m}}$
   - Discharge:
     $$Q = A C \sqrt{R S_0} = 40.0 \times 50 \times \sqrt{2.40 \times \frac{1}{1500}} = 2000 \times \sqrt{0.0016} = 2000 \times 0.040 = \mathbf{80.0 \text{ m}^3/\text{s}}$$
:::

### Example 13.5: Canal Design for Minimum Concrete Lining (Slide 15)
**Problem:** A trapezoidal channel with side slopes of $1\text{H} : 1\text{V}$ ($m = 1.0$) has to be designed to convey $Q = 10.0 \text{ m}^3/\text{s}$ at a velocity of $V = 2.0 \text{ m/s}$ so that the amount of concrete lining for the bed and sides is minimum. Calculate the area of lining required per metre length of canal.

:::sol Detailed Step-by-Step Solution
1. **Flow Area Required:**
   $$A = \frac{Q}{V} = \frac{10.0}{2.0} = \mathbf{5.0 \text{ m}^2}$$
2. **Minimum Lining Condition:**
   Minimum lining corresponds to minimum wetted perimeter $P$ (most economical section):
   $$\frac{B + 2my}{2} = y\sqrt{1+m^2} \implies \frac{B + 2(1.0)y}{2} = y\sqrt{1+1} = \sqrt{2} y = 1.4142 y$$
   $$B + 2y = 2.8284 y \implies \mathbf{B = 0.8284 y}$$
3. **Solve for Depth $y$:**
   $$A = (B + my)y = (0.8284y + 1.0y)y = 1.8284 y^2 = 5.0$$
   $$y^2 = \frac{5.0}{1.8284} = 2.7346 \implies \mathbf{y = 1.6537 \text{ m}}$$
   $$B = 0.8284 \times 1.6537 = \mathbf{1.370 \text{ m}}$$
4. **Wetted Perimeter & Lining Area per Metre:**
   $$P = B + 2y\sqrt{1+m^2} = 1.370 + 2(1.6537)\sqrt{2} = 1.370 + 4.677 = \mathbf{6.047 \text{ m}}$$
   $$\text{Lining Area per metre length} = P \times 1.0 \text{ m} = \mathbf{6.047 \text{ m}^2/\text{m}}$$
:::

### Example 13.6: Economic Feasibility — Lined vs. Unlined Canal (Slide 16)
**Problem:** A trapezoidal channel has side slopes of $1\text{H} : 1\text{V}$ ($m = 1.0$). It is required to discharge $Q = 13.75 \text{ m}^3/\text{s}$ with a bed gradient of $1 \text{ in } 1000$. If unlined, Chezy's $C = 44$; if lined with concrete, $C = 60$. The cost per $\text{m}^3$ of excavation is 4 times the cost per $\text{m}^2$ of lining. Both designs must be most efficient. Determine whether the lined or unlined canal is cheaper, and find its economical dimensions.

:::sol Economic Feasibility Analysis
Let cost per $\text{m}^2$ of lining be $k$. Then cost per $\text{m}^3$ of excavation is $4k$.
For both canals, most economical section with $m = 1.0$ gives:
$$B = 0.8284 y, \quad A = 1.8284 y^2, \quad R = \frac{y}{2}$$
$$Q = A C \sqrt{R S_0} \implies 13.75 = (1.8284 y^2) C \sqrt{\frac{y}{2} \times 0.001} = \frac{1.8284 \times \sqrt{0.0005}}{\sqrt{1}} C y^{5/2} = 0.040885 C y^{5/2}$$

1. **Option A: Unlined Canal ($C = 44$):**
   $$13.75 = 0.040885 \times 44 \times y_u^{5/2} = 1.7989 y_u^{5/2}$$
   $$y_u^{5/2} = \frac{13.75}{1.7989} = 7.6435 \implies \mathbf{y_u = 2.253 \text{ m}}$$
   $$A_u = 1.8284 (2.253)^2 = \mathbf{9.281 \text{ m}^2}$$
   Cost per metre length = Excavation only:
   $$\text{Cost}_u = A_u \times (4k) = 9.281 \times 4k = \mathbf{37.124 k}$$

2. **Option B: Lined Canal ($C = 60$):**
   $$13.75 = 0.040885 \times 60 \times y_L^{5/2} = 2.4531 y_L^{5/2}$$
   $$y_L^{5/2} = \frac{13.75}{2.4531} = 5.605 \implies \mathbf{y_L = 1.996 \text{ m}}$$
   $$B_L = 0.8284 \times 1.996 = \mathbf{1.653 \text{ m}}$$
   $$A_L = 1.8284 (1.996)^2 = \mathbf{7.284 \text{ m}^2}$$
   $$P_L = 2.8284 y_L = 2.8284 \times 1.996 = \mathbf{5.645 \text{ m}}$$
   Cost per metre length = Excavation + Lining:
   $$\text{Cost}_L = A_L \times (4k) + P_L \times k = (7.284 \times 4 + 5.645) k = (29.136 + 5.645) k = \mathbf{34.781 k}$$

3. **Conclusion & Decision:**
   Since $\text{Cost}_L (34.781 k) < \text{Cost}_u (37.124 k)$, the **lined canal is cheaper** (saves $6.3\%$ of total project cost)!
   - **Optimal Dimensions:** Bed width $\mathbf{B = 1.653 \text{ m}}$, Depth $\mathbf{y = 1.996 \text{ m}}$.
:::

### Example 13.7: Flow through Circular Conduit at Partial Depth (Slide 18)
**Problem:** Find the discharge through a circular pipe of diameter $D = 3.0 \text{ m}$ if the depth of water is $y = 1.0 \text{ m}$ and the pipe is laid on a slope of $1 \text{ in } 1000$. Take Chezy's $C = 70$.

:::sol Detailed Step-by-Step Solution
1. **Geometric Angle $\theta$:**
   Radius $r = D/2 = 1.50 \text{ m}$. Depth $y = 1.0 \text{ m}$.
   Distance from center to water level: $d = r - y = 1.50 - 1.0 = 0.50 \text{ m}$.
   $$\cos \theta = \frac{d}{r} = \frac{0.50}{1.50} = \frac{1}{3} = 0.3333 \implies \theta = 70.529^\circ = 1.2309 \text{ rad}$$
   Wetted subtended angle at center:
   $$2\beta = 2(180^\circ - \theta) = 2(180^\circ - 70.529^\circ) = 2(109.471^\circ) = 218.942^\circ = 3.8213 \text{ rad}$$
   Wait, using direct half-angle formula from bed: $\cos \left(\frac{2\theta_{\text{sub}}}{2}\right) = 1 - \frac{2y}{D} = 1 - \frac{2(1)}{3} = \frac{1}{3} \implies \frac{2\theta}{2} = 70.529^\circ \implies 2\theta = 141.058^\circ = 2.4619 \text{ rad}$.
2. **Wetted Area and Perimeter:**
   $$A = \frac{r^2}{2} (2\theta - \sin 2\theta) = \frac{1.5^2}{2} (2.4619 - \sin 141.058^\circ) = 1.125 \times (2.4619 - 0.6285) = 1.125 \times 1.8334 = \mathbf{2.0626 \text{ m}^2}$$
   $$P = r (2\theta) = 1.5 \times 2.4619 = \mathbf{3.6929 \text{ m}}$$
   $$R = \frac{A}{P} = \frac{2.0626}{3.6929} = \mathbf{0.5585 \text{ m}}$$
3. **Discharge:**
   $$Q = A C \sqrt{R S_0} = 2.0626 \times 70 \times \sqrt{0.5585 \times 0.001} = 144.38 \times \sqrt{0.0005585} = 144.38 \times 0.02363 = \mathbf{3.412 \text{ m}^3/\text{s}}$$
:::

### Example 13.8: Discharge in a Partially Full Pipe (Slide 19)
**Problem:** Calculate the quantity of water that will be discharged at a uniform depth of $y = 0.90 \text{ m}$ in a $D = 1.20 \text{ m}$ diameter pipe laid on a slope of $1 \text{ in } 1000$. Assume Chezy's $C = 58$.

:::sol Detailed Step-by-Step Solution
1. **Subtended Angle:**
   Here depth $y = 0.90 \text{ m} > r = 0.60 \text{ m}$ (water surface lies above pipe center!).
   Distance above center: $d = y - r = 0.90 - 0.60 = 0.30 \text{ m}$.
   $$\sin \alpha = \frac{d}{r} = \frac{0.30}{0.60} = 0.50 \implies \alpha = 30^\circ$$
   Wetted central angle:
   $$2\theta = 180^\circ + 2\alpha = 180^\circ + 60^\circ = 240^\circ = \frac{4\pi}{3} = \mathbf{4.1888 \text{ rad}}$$
2. **Hydraulic Parameters:**
   - Area:
     $$A = \text{Total Circle Area} - \text{Dry Crown Segment} = \pi r^2 - \frac{r^2}{2} (120^\circ \text{ in rad} - \sin 120^\circ)$$
     $$A = \pi (0.6)^2 - \frac{0.36}{2} \left(\frac{2\pi}{3} - \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\right) = 1.1310 - 0.18(2.0944 - 0.8660) = 1.1310 - 0.18(1.2284) = 1.1310 - 0.2211 = \mathbf{0.9099 \text{ m}^2}$$
   - Perimeter:
     $$P = r (2\theta) = 0.60 \times 4.1888 = \mathbf{2.5133 \text{ m}}$$
   - Hydraulic Radius:
     $$R = \frac{A}{P} = \frac{0.9099}{2.5133} = \mathbf{0.3620 \text{ m}}$$
3. **Discharge:**
   $$Q = A C \sqrt{R S_0} = 0.9099 \times 58 \times \sqrt{0.3620 \times 10^{-3}} = 52.774 \times \sqrt{0.0003620} = 52.774 \times 0.019026 = \mathbf{1.004 \text{ m}^3/\text{s}}$$
:::

### Real-World Field Nuances & Lining Practice
1. **Practical Soil Slope Limitations vs. Mathematical Optimality:**
   While mathematics dictates that the half-hexagon ($60^\circ$ side slope, $1\text{V} : 0.577\text{H}$) is hydraulically superior, **unlined canals can never be constructed at $60^\circ$** because soil slumps and fails under gravity. Standard field side slopes (IS: 7112):
   - Soft clay / silt: $1\text{V} : 2\text{H}$ to $1\text{V} : 3\text{H}$ ($\theta \approx 18^\circ - 26^\circ$).
   - Firm alluvial loam: $1\text{V} : 1.5\text{H}$ ($\theta \approx 33.7^\circ$).
   - Hard fissured rock: $1\text{V} : 0.25\text{H}$ ($\theta \approx 76^\circ$).
   - Cast-in-place concrete lining (IS: 3873): Can safely hold the optimal $1\text{V} : 1\text{H}$ ($45^\circ$) or $1\text{V} : 0.577\text{H}$ ($60^\circ$).
2. **Circular Sewer Cleansing Velocities (CPHEEO Manual):**
   The Central Public Health and Environmental Engineering Organisation (CPHEEO) mandates that circular gravity sewers must achieve a self-cleansing velocity of at least **$0.60 \text{ m/s}$** at present peak flow, and **$0.75 - 0.80 \text{ m/s}$** at design ultimate flow, while capping maximum velocity at **$3.0 \text{ m/s}$** to prevent pipe invert erosion.
