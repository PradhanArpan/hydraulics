> [!note] Core Concept
> Direct Numerical Simulation (DNS) of all turbulent eddies in a river would require trillions of grid cells, exceeding global supercomputer capacity. Consequently, engineering CFD relies on **Turbulence Models** to close the Reynolds-Averaged Navier-Stokes (RANS) equations, using eddy viscosity approximations like the **$k$-$\epsilon$** and **$k$-$\omega$ SST** models.

## 1. Boussinesq Eddy Viscosity Hypothesis
Analogous to Newton's law of viscosity, Joseph Boussinesq (1877) postulated that turbulent Reynolds stresses are proportional to mean rate of strain:
$$-\rho \overline{u_i' u_j'} = \mu_t \left(\frac{\partial \bar{u}_i}{\partial x_j} + \frac{\partial \bar{u}_j}{\partial x_i}\right) - \frac{2}{3}\rho k \delta_{ij}$$
Where $\mu_t$ is the **turbulent eddy viscosity** (a property of the flow, NOT the fluid), and $k = \frac{1}{2}(\overline{u'^2} + \overline{v'^2} + \overline{w'^2})$ is **turbulent kinetic energy**.

## 2. Standard Two-Equation Models
1. **$k$-$\epsilon$ Model (Launder & Spalding):**
   - Solves transport equations for $k$ (turbulent kinetic energy) and $\epsilon$ (rate of dissipation of $k$).
   - Eddy viscosity: $\mu_t = \rho C_\mu \frac{k^2}{\epsilon}$ (where $C_\mu = 0.09$).
   - Excellent for free-shear flows, jets, and mixing layers; poor near walls with adverse pressure gradients.
2. **$k$-$\omega$ SST Model (Menter):**
   - Uses $k$-$\omega$ formulation in the near-wall boundary layer and blends smoothly into $k$-$\epsilon$ in the outer stream.
   - Industry gold standard for adverse pressure gradients, boundary layer separation, and spillway chute aerators.

## 3. Near-Wall Treatment & Dimensionless Wall Distance ($y^+$)
$$y^+ = \frac{u_* y}{\nu} = \frac{\sqrt{\tau_0 / \rho} \cdot y}{\nu}$$
- **Viscous sublayer ($y^+ < 5$):** Requires ultra-fine mesh to resolve linear profile.
- **Log-law region ($30 < y^+ < 300$):** Used with **Standard Wall Functions** to bridge the near-wall layer without resolving the sublayer, saving millions of cells.

### Example 43.1: First Cell Height Estimation for Wall Functions
**Problem:** A 3D CFD simulation of flow over a spillway crest is being set up in OpenFOAM. Water flows at $U = 8.0 \text{ m/s}$ along a wall with skin friction coefficient estimated as $C_f = 0.0035$. Kinematic viscosity $ν = 1.0 \times 10^{-6} \text{ m}^2/\text{s}$.
To use standard logarithmic wall functions with the $k$-$\epsilon$ turbulence model, the non-dimensional wall distance for the first cell centroid should be target $y^+ = 50$.
Calculate the required physical height $y_1$ of the first grid cell adjacent to the concrete wall.

**Solution:**
1. **Wall Shear Stress $\tau_0$ & Shear Velocity $u_*$:**
   $$\tau_0 = C_f \cdot \frac{1}{2}\rho U^2 = 0.0035 \times 0.5 \times 1000 \times (8.0)^2 = 0.0035 \times 500 \times 64.0 = 112.0 \text{ N/m}^2$$
   $$u_* = \sqrt{\frac{\tau_0}{\rho}} = \sqrt{\frac{112.0}{1000}} = \sqrt{0.112} = 0.3347 \text{ m/s}$$
2. **First Cell Centroid Distance $y$:**
   From the definition $y^+ = \frac{u_* y}{\nu}$:
   $$y = \frac{y^+ \cdot \nu}{u_*} = \frac{50 \times 1.0 \times 10^{-6}}{0.3347} = \frac{5.0 \times 10^{-5}}{0.3347} = 1.494 \times 10^{-4} \text{ m} = 0.149 \text{ mm}$$
3. **First Cell Total Height $\Delta y_1$:**
   Since the cell centroid is at the midpoint of the first cell ($y = \Delta y_1 / 2$):
   $$\Delta y_1 = 2 y = 2 \times 0.149 \text{ mm} = 0.299 \text{ mm} \approx 0.30 \text{ mm}$$
   The first cell thickness at the wall must be **$0.30 \text{ mm}$**.

### Key Takeaways
- Boussinesq hypothesis: $-\rho \overline{u'v'} = \mu_t \frac{\partial u}{\partial y}$.
- $k$-$\epsilon$ is suited for free shear; $k$-$\omega$ SST excels at adverse pressure gradients and separation.
- Wall functions bridge $30 < y^+ < 300$, saving millions of boundary layer cells.
