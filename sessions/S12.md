> [!note] Force Equilibrium in Uniform Flow
> Uniform flow represents the dynamic equilibrium state of open channel flow: the depth of flow, wetted area, velocity, and discharge remain constant at every section along the channel reach ($dy/dx = 0, dV/dx = 0$). The total energy line, water surface, and channel bottom are strictly parallel: **$S_f = S_w = S_0 = S$**. The constant depth is termed the **Normal Depth ($y_n$)**.

## 1. Fundamental Equation of Uniform Flow & Chezy's Formula
Applying Newton's Second Law of Motion to a control volume of fluid of length $L$ in a channel with bed slope $S_0 = \sin \theta \approx \tan \theta$:
- Since velocity is constant along the channel, acceleration is zero ($a_x = 0$).
- Therefore, the sum of all external forces in the direction of flow must be equal to zero: $\sum F_x = 0$.

```
                              WSL (Water Surface)  Sf = Sw = S0
           ────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────►
                      │                                    │
           Hydrostatic│                                    │ Hydrostatic
           Thrust P1  ▼                                    ▼ Thrust P2
                      │          W sin θ (Gravity)         │
           ───────────┼────────────────────────────────────┼───────────►
                      │                                    │
                      ◄────────────────────────────────────┘
                              Boundary Shear τ0 P L
           ═════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════ Bed
```

1. **Hydrostatic Thrust Balance:** The hydrostatic pressure forces at sections 1 and 2 balance out identically ($P_1 = P_2$) because the flow depth and cross-sectional area are constant.
2. **Downstream Gravity Component:**
   $$W_x = W \sin \theta = (\rho g A L) S_0 = \gamma A L S_0$$
3. **Opposing Boundary Shear Resistance:**
   Along the wetted perimeter $P$ over reach length $L$:
   $$F_f = \tau_0 P L$$
4. **Force Equilibrium:**
   $$\gamma A L S_0 = \tau_0 P L \implies \mathbf{\tau_0 = \gamma \left(\frac{A}{P}\right) S_0 = \gamma R S_0}$$
   where $R = A/P$ is the hydraulic radius.

Adopting the fluid boundary shear relationship $\tau_0 = \frac{f}{8}\rho V^2$ (Darcy-Weisbach friction factor $f$):
$$\gamma R S_0 = \frac{f}{8}\rho V^2 \implies \rho g R S_0 = \frac{f}{8}\rho V^2 \implies V = \sqrt{\frac{8g}{f}} \sqrt{R S_0} = \mathbf{C \sqrt{R S_0}}$$
This is **Chezy's Formula** (Antoine de Chézy, 1769), where $C = \sqrt{8g/f}$ is Chezy's resistance coefficient.
*Dimensions of Chezy's $C$:*
$$[C] = \left[\frac{V}{\sqrt{R S_0}}\right] = \frac{L T^{-1}}{L^{1/2}} = \mathbf{[L^{1/2} T^{-1}]} \quad (\text{SI units: } \text{m}^{1/2}/\text{s})$$

## 2. Manning's Equation & Channel Conveyance ($K$)
In 1889, Irish engineer Robert Manning established that $C = \frac{1}{n} R^{1/6}$, yielding **Manning's Formula**:
$$\mathbf{V = \frac{1}{n} R^{2/3} S_0^{1/2}} \quad \text{(SI Units)}$$
$$\mathbf{Q = A V = \frac{1}{n} A R^{2/3} S_0^{1/2} = K \sqrt{S_0}}$$
where $K = \frac{1}{n} A R^{2/3}$ is the **Channel Conveyance** ($\text{m}^3/\text{s}$), representing the discharge capacity of the cross-section per unit bed slope.

## 3. Cowan's Method & 10 Factors Affecting Manning's $n$
In engineering practice, $n$ is not a single immutable constant. Ven Te Chow identified 10 primary interdependent factors that govern flow resistance:
1. **Vegetation:** Retards flow and reduces cross-sectional capacity.
2. **Surface Roughness:** Size and angularity of boundary grains (fine sand vs coarse gravel/boulders).
3. **Channel Irregularity:** Sand bars, ridges, depressions, and scoured holes.
4. **Channel Alignment:** Curvature and degree of meandering.
5. **Silting and Scouring:** Deposition smoothing depressions vs erosion carving jagged gullies.
6. **Obstructions:** Bridge piers, fallen timber, trash racks, and debris jams.
7. **Size and Shape of Channel:** Hydraulic radius changes across sub-sections.
8. **Stage and Discharge:** $n$ generally **decreases with increasing stage and depth** as deep flow submerges bed irregularities.
9. **Seasonal Change:** Heavy weed growth in warm summer months vs die-off in winter.
10. **Suspended Material and Bedload:** Energy consumed in transport increases apparent resistance.

### Cowan's Predictive Formulation (1956)
$$\mathbf{n = (n_0 + n_1 + n_2 + n_3 + n_4) \cdot m_5}$$
where:
- $n_0$: Basic material roughness ($0.012$ concrete, $0.020$ earth, $0.025$ rock cut, $0.028$ coarse gravel).
- $n_1$: Surface irregularity ($0.000$ smooth to $0.020$ severe erosion).
- $n_2$: Cross-section variation ($0.000$ gradual to $0.015$ frequent alternations).
- $n_3$: Obstructions ($0.000$ negligible to $0.060$ severe debris).
- $n_4$: Vegetation ($0.000$ none to $0.080$ dense saplings and brush).
- $m_5$: Meandering ratio ($1.00$ straight to $1.30$ severe meandering).

## 4. Composite Channel Roughness
When a channel has different boundary materials (e.g. concrete bed with $n_1$ and rip-rap or vegetated banks with $n_2$), the equivalent roughness $n_e$ is calculated by:
1. **Horton & Einstein Method:**
   $$\mathbf{n_e = \left[ \frac{\sum P_i n_i^{3/2}}{P} \right]^{2/3}}$$
2. **Pavlovskij Method:**
   $$\mathbf{n_e = \left[ \frac{\sum P_i n_i^2}{P} \right]^{1/2}}$$
3. **Lotter Method:**
   $$\mathbf{n_e = \frac{P R^{5/3}}{\sum \frac{P_i R_i^{5/3}}{n_i}}}$$

### Example 12.1: Discharge in a Concrete Trapezoidal Canal (Slide 54)
**Problem:** A smooth concrete-lined channel has a trapezoidal cross-section with base width $B = 6.0 \text{ m}$ and side slopes $1\text{V} : 2\text{H}$ ($m = 2.0$). If the bed slope is $S_0 = 1 \text{ in } 500$ ($0.002$) and normal depth is $y_n = 2.0 \text{ m}$, calculate the quantity of flow (take Manning $n = 0.015$).

:::sol Detailed Step-by-Step Calculation
1. **Geometric Elements:**
   - Flow Area:
     $$A = (B + my)y = [6.0 + (2.0 \times 2.0)] \times 2.0 = [6.0 + 4.0] \times 2.0 = 10.0 \times 2.0 = \mathbf{20.0 \text{ m}^2}$$
   - Wetted Perimeter:
     $$P = B + 2y\sqrt{1 + m^2} = 6.0 + 2(2.0)\sqrt{1 + 2^2} = 6.0 + 4.0\sqrt{5} = 6.0 + 4.0(2.2361) = \mathbf{14.944 \text{ m}}$$
   - Hydraulic Radius:
     $$R = \frac{A}{P} = \frac{20.0}{14.944} = \mathbf{1.3383 \text{ m}}$$
     $$R^{2/3} = (1.3383)^{2/3} = \mathbf{1.2144 \text{ m}^{2/3}}$$

2. **Bed Slope & Manning Velocity:**
   - $S_0^{1/2} = \sqrt{\frac{1}{500}} = \sqrt{0.002} = 0.04472$
   - Velocity:
     $$V = \frac{1}{n} R^{2/3} S_0^{1/2} = \frac{1}{0.015} \times 1.2144 \times 0.04472 = 66.667 \times 0.05431 = \mathbf{3.621 \text{ m/s}}$$

3. **Discharge:**
   $$Q = A \cdot V = 20.0 \times 3.621 = \mathbf{72.42 \text{ m}^3/\text{s}}$$
:::

### Example 12.2: Normal Depth Determination for Specified Discharge (Slide 55)
**Problem:** For the same trapezoidal channel ($B = 6.0 \text{ m}$, $m = 2.0$, $S_0 = 0.002$, $n = 0.015$), if the discharge is $Q = 40.0 \text{ m}^3/\text{s}$, determine the normal depth $y_n$.

:::sol Newton-Raphson Iterative Solution
1. **Manning Equation Formulation:**
   $$Q = \frac{1}{n} A R^{2/3} S_0^{1/2} \implies A R^{2/3} = \frac{Q \cdot n}{\sqrt{S_0}} = \frac{40.0 \times 0.015}{\sqrt{0.002}} = \frac{0.60}{0.04472} = \mathbf{13.4164}$$
   Since $R = A/P$, this requires:
   $$\frac{A^{5/3}}{P^{2/3}} = 13.4164 \iff \frac{[(6.0 + 2y)y]^{5/3}}{[6.0 + 2y\sqrt{5}]^{2/3}} = 13.4164$$

2. **Iterative Trial and Error:**
   - *Trial 1:* Assume $y = 1.50 \text{ m}$:
     $$A = (6 + 3)(1.5) = 13.5 \text{ m}^2, \quad P = 6 + 3\sqrt{5} = 12.708 \text{ m}$$
     $$R = 13.5 / 12.708 = 1.0623 \text{ m}, \quad A R^{2/3} = 13.5 \times (1.0623)^{2/3} = 13.5 \times 1.0410 = 14.053 > 13.416$$
   - *Trial 2:* Assume $y = 1.45 \text{ m}$:
     $$A = (6 + 2.9)(1.45) = 12.905 \text{ m}^2, \quad P = 6 + 2.9(2.2361) = 12.485 \text{ m}$$
     $$R = 12.905 / 12.485 = 1.0336 \text{ m}, \quad A R^{2/3} = 12.905 \times (1.0336)^{2/3} = 12.905 \times 1.0223 = 13.193 < 13.416$$
   - *Interpolation:*
     $$y_n \approx 1.45 + (1.50 - 1.45) \times \frac{13.416 - 13.193}{14.053 - 13.193} = 1.45 + 0.05 \times \frac{0.223}{0.860} = \mathbf{1.463 \text{ m}}$$

3. **Verification:**
   At $y_n = 1.463 \text{ m}$: $A = 13.06 \text{ m}^2$, $P = 12.54 \text{ m}$, $R = 1.041 \text{ m}$, $Q = \frac{1}{0.015} \times 13.06 \times (1.041)^{2/3} \times \sqrt{0.002} = \mathbf{40.0 \text{ m}^3/\text{s}}$.
:::

### Civil Engineering Standards & Canals (IS: 10430)
1. **Permissible Velocities in Canal Design (IS: 10430):**
   Canals are engineered between two limiting velocities:
   - **Non-Silting Velocity ($V \ge 0.6 \text{ m/s}$):** Flow must stay fast enough to keep suspended silt in motion, preventing sediment deposition from choking the canal.
   - **Non-Scouring Velocity ($V \le V_{\text{max}}$):** Flow must not exceed the scour limit of the boundary material:
     - Unlined alluvial earth: $V_{\text{max}} = 0.9 - 1.1 \text{ m/s}$.
     - Hard gravel / boulder bed: $V_{\text{max}} = 1.5 - 2.0 \text{ m/s}$.
     - Concrete lining (IS 3873): $V_{\text{max}} = 2.5 - 3.0 \text{ m/s}$.
2. **Freeboard Requirements:**
   To prevent flood overtopping due to wave action and wind surges, canals must provide minimum vertical freeboard above the normal water surface:
   - For $Q < 0.75 \text{ m}^3/\text{s}$: Freeboard $= 0.30 \text{ m}$.
   - For $Q = 10 - 30 \text{ m}^3/\text{s}$: Freeboard $= 0.75 \text{ m}$.
   - For $Q > 85 \text{ m}^3/\text{s}$: Freeboard $= 1.00 \text{ m}$.
