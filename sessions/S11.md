> [!note] From Point Velocity to the Three Conservation Laws
> In real open channels, friction along the solid bed and banks creates a three-dimensional velocity field $u(y, z)$ where velocity is zero at the boundaries and reaches a maximum below the free surface. 
> To apply practical one-dimensional engineering formulas, civil engineers define a single **Mean Velocity ($V = Q/A$)**. However, because momentum is proportional to $u^2$ and kinetic energy is proportional to $u^3$, replacing point velocity $u$ with mean velocity $V$ requires two vital weighting factors: the **Coriolis Coefficient ($\alpha$)** for energy and the **Boussinesq Coefficient ($\beta$)** for momentum. These coefficients empower the three universal conservation principles: **Mass (Continuity)**, **Energy (Bernoulli)**, and **Linear Momentum (Newton's 2nd Law)**.

## 1. Point Velocity $u(y, z)$ vs. Cross-Sectional Mean Velocity $V$

### Why Velocity Varies Across the Channel Section
Water flowing in an open channel is subjected to viscous shear stresses:
1. **At the solid boundary (bed and sides):** The fluid adheres to the wall due to the fluid *no-slip condition*, so velocity $u = 0$.
2. **Away from boundaries:** Fluid layers slide past one another with turbulent shear mixing, causing velocity to increase with distance from the bed.
3. **Near the free surface:** Counter-intuitively, the maximum velocity does **not** occur at the water surface, but at a depth of **$0.05$ to $0.25 \, y$ below the surface**. This phenomenon is known as the **Surface Velocity Dip**, caused by secondary transverse helical circulation currents that transport low-momentum surface fluid from the corners towards the channel center and downward!

### Defining the Cross-Sectional Mean Velocity $V$
Dealing with a continuous 3D velocity field $u(y, z)$ for every calculation is impossible in routine design. Civil engineers therefore define the **Mean (Average) Velocity $V$**:
$$\mathbf{V = \frac{Q}{A} = \frac{1}{A} \int_A u \, dA}$$
*Physical Definition:* Mean velocity $V$ is that uniform hypothetical velocity which, if maintained uniformly across the entire cross-sectional area $A$, would carry the exact same total volumetric discharge $Q$.

### How Mean Velocity is Measured in Engineering Practice (IS:1192 Standards)
In field stream gauging using current meters or acoustic Doppler current profilers (ADCP):
1. **One-Point Method (Shallow streams, depth $< 0.6 \text{ m}$):**
   - The current meter is positioned at a depth of **$0.6 \, y$ below the water surface** (i.e. $0.4 \, y$ from the bed).
   - Field observation confirms: $\mathbf{V \approx v_{0.6}}$.
2. **Two-Point Method (Deep streams, depth $\ge 0.6 \text{ m}$):**
   - Velocity readings are taken at **$0.2 \, y$** and **$0.8 \, y$** below the water surface.
   - The mean velocity is the arithmetic average: $\mathbf{V \approx \frac{v_{0.2} + v_{0.8}}{2}}$.
3. **Surface Float Method (Rapid reconnaissance / floods):**
   - Timing a surface float over a known distance yields the surface velocity $v_{\text{surf}}$.
   - Mean velocity is calculated using a reduction factor $k \approx 0.85 \text{ to } 0.90$: $\mathbf{V \approx 0.85 \cdot v_{\text{surf}}}$.

## 2. The Non-Linearity Problem: Why Mean Velocity $V$ Fails for Momentum & Energy

When we substitute the constant average velocity $V$ in place of the variable local velocity $u$:
- **Mass Flux (Linear in $u$):**
  $$\text{Mass Flux} = \rho \int_A u \, dA = \rho A \left(\frac{1}{A} \int_A u \, dA\right) = \mathbf{\rho A V = \rho Q} \quad \text{(100% Exact!)}$$
- **Linear Momentum Flux (Quadratic in $u$):**
  $$\text{True Momentum Flux} = \rho \int_A u^2 dA \ne \mathbf{\rho A V^2}$$
- **Kinetic Energy Flux (Cubic in $u$):**
  $$\text{True Kinetic Energy Flux} = \frac{1}{2} \rho \int_A u^3 dA \ne \mathbf{\frac{1}{2} \rho A V^3}$$

> [!important] The Physical Reason for Underestimation
> Because kinetic energy scales with the **cube** of velocity ($u^3$) and momentum scales with the **square** ($u^2$), high-velocity filaments near the core carry disproportionately enormous amounts of energy and momentum! 
> For example: if two equal stream tubes have velocities $u_1 = 3 \text{ m/s}$ and $u_2 = 1 \text{ m/s}$, the mean velocity is $V = 2 \text{ m/s}$.
> - True kinetic energy flux: $\frac{1}{2}\rho (3^3 + 1^3) = \frac{1}{2}\rho (27 + 1) = \mathbf{14 \, \rho}$
> - Energy computed from mean velocity: $\frac{1}{2}\rho \cdot 2 \cdot (2^3) = \mathbf{8 \, \rho}$
> - Simply using average velocity $V$ would **underestimate the true energy by 43%!**

## 3. Kinetic Energy ($\alpha$) and Momentum ($\beta$) Correction Factors

To correct this mathematical disparity while preserving the convenience of 1D mean velocity $V$, we introduce two non-dimensional correction coefficients:

### 1. Kinetic Energy Correction Factor (Coriolis Coefficient, $\alpha$)
Defined as the ratio of true kinetic energy flux to the kinetic energy flux computed using mean velocity $V$:
$$\mathbf{\alpha = \frac{\int_A u^3 dA}{V^3 A} \ge 1.0}$$
Therefore, the true total energy head at any cross section is:
$$\mathbf{H = z + y + \alpha \frac{V^2}{2g}}$$

### 2. Momentum Correction Factor (Boussinesq Coefficient, $\beta$)
Defined as the ratio of true momentum flux to the momentum flux computed using mean velocity $V$:
$$\mathbf{\beta = \frac{\int_A u^2 dA}{V^2 A} \ge 1.0}$$
The true dynamic momentum force carried by the flowing stream is:
$$\mathbf{F_{\text{dyn}} = \beta \rho Q V}$$

### Mathematical Proof that $\alpha > \beta \ge 1.0$ and $\alpha - 1 \approx 3(\beta - 1)$
Let the local point velocity be $u = V + \Delta u$, where $\Delta u$ is the deviation from the mean velocity $V$. By definition of mean velocity, the integral of deviations across the cross-section is identically zero: $\int_A \Delta u \, dA = 0$.

1. **For Momentum Factor $\beta$:**
   $$\int_A u^2 dA = \int_A (V + \Delta u)^2 dA = \int_A (V^2 + 2V\Delta u + \Delta u^2) dA = V^2 A + 0 + \int_A \Delta u^2 dA$$
   $$\beta = \frac{\int_A u^2 dA}{V^2 A} = 1 + \frac{\int_A \Delta u^2 dA}{V^2 A} = \mathbf{1 + \eta^2 \ge 1.0}$$
   where $\eta = \frac{\sqrt{\frac{1}{A}\int \Delta u^2 dA}}{V}$ is the coefficient of variation of the velocity distribution. Because $\eta^2 \ge 0$, **$\beta$ is strictly $\ge 1.0$**!

2. **For Coriolis Factor $\alpha$:**
   $$\int_A u^3 dA = \int_A (V + \Delta u)^3 dA = \int_A (V^3 + 3V^2 \Delta u + 3V \Delta u^2 + \Delta u^3) dA$$
   $$= V^3 A + 0 + 3V \int_A \Delta u^2 dA + \int_A \Delta u^3 dA$$
   Dividing by $V^3 A$ and neglecting the small third-order skewness term ($\int \Delta u^3 dA \approx 0$):
   $$\alpha \approx 1 + 3 \frac{\int \Delta u^2 dA}{V^2 A} = \mathbf{1 + 3\eta^2}$$

3. **Universal Relationship:**
   $$\mathbf{\alpha - 1 \approx 3(\beta - 1) \implies \alpha > \beta \ge 1.0}$$
   This elegant mathematical proof proves that:
   - For an idealized uniform velocity profile ($\Delta u = 0$), $\alpha = \beta = 1.00$.
   - In any real velocity distribution with boundary shear, **$\alpha$ is always strictly greater than $\beta$, and both are $\ge 1.0$**!

| Channel Setting | Coriolis Factor $\alpha$ | Boussinesq Factor $\beta$ | Practical Engineering Guidance |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Regular Prismatic Canals** | $1.03 - 1.10$ | $1.01 - 1.05$ | Usually set $\alpha \approx 1.0, \beta \approx 1.0$ in routine canal design. |
| **Natural River Reaches** | $1.15 - 1.30$ | $1.05 - 1.12$ | Must be incorporated into river flood routing, bridge afflux, and scour. |
| **Compound Channels (Main + Floodplain)** | $1.20 - 1.60$ | $1.10 - 1.30$ | Severe velocity contrast between main channel ($2.5 \text{ m/s}$) and floodplain ($0.5 \text{ m/s}$). |

## 4. The Three Universal Conservation Laws of Hydraulics

With mean velocity $V$ properly coupled to correction factors $\alpha$ and $\beta$, we apply the three conservation laws of physics to a channel control volume bounded by Section 1 and Section 2:

### Law 1: Conservation of Mass (The Continuity Equation)
For a continuous, steady, incompressible liquid flow, fluid mass can neither be created nor destroyed within the control volume:
- Mass inflow per second at Section 1: $\dot{m}_1 = \rho A_1 V_1$
- Mass outflow per second at Section 2: $\dot{m}_2 = \rho A_2 V_2$
- Since water density $\rho \approx \text{constant}$:
  $$\mathbf{Q = A_1 V_1 = A_2 V_2 = \text{Constant}}$$
*Key Engineering Consequence:* If a channel contracts in width or shallows over a bed hump ($A_2 < A_1$), the flow velocity must accelerate inversely ($V_2 > V_1$).

### Law 2: Conservation of Energy (The Bernoulli / Energy Principle)
The total mechanical energy head at Section 1 equals the total mechanical energy head at Section 2 plus frictional and turbulent eddy losses $h_L$:
$$\mathbf{z_1 + y_1 + \alpha_1 \frac{V_1^2}{2g} = z_2 + y_2 + \alpha_2 \frac{V_2^2}{2g} + h_L}$$
*When to Use:* The Energy Equation is applied whenever boundary friction and form losses $h_L$ can be calculated explicitly (e.g. gradually varied flow profiles, flow over weirs, flumes, and smooth transitions).

### Law 3: Conservation of Linear Momentum (Newton's Second Law)
The sum of all external forces acting on the fluid control volume in the direction of motion equals the net time rate of momentum change:
$$\mathbf{\sum F_x = \dot{M}_{\text{out}} - \dot{M}_{\text{in}} = \rho Q (\beta_2 V_2 - \beta_1 V_1)}$$
The external forces acting along the channel axis include:
1. Hydrostatic thrust forces: $F_1 = \gamma A_1 \bar{y}_1$ and $F_2 = \gamma A_2 \bar{y}_2$.
2. Downslope component of water weight: $W \sin \theta = \gamma \bar{A} L S_0$.
3. Opposing boundary shear resistance: $F_f = \tau_0 P L$.

*When to Use:* The Momentum Equation is **essential whenever internal turbulent energy dissipation is violent or unknown** (for example, across a **Hydraulic Jump**, where massive energy head loss $\Delta E$ occurs in turbulent breaking rollers and cannot be predicted a priori).

### Example 11.1: Power-Law Velocity Distribution Integration
**Problem:** In a wide rectangular channel of depth $y_0 = 2.0 \text{ m}$, the velocity profile is measured as $v(y) = 3.0 \left(\frac{y}{y_0}\right)^{1/6} \text{ m/s}$.
1. Derive the expression for mean velocity $V$.
2. Calculate the momentum correction factor $\beta$.
3. Calculate the kinetic energy correction factor $\alpha$.
4. Compute the percentage error in velocity head if $\alpha$ is neglected ($V = 3.0 \text{ m/s}$).

:::sol Step-by-Step Analytical Derivation
1. **Mean Velocity $V$:**
   $$V = \frac{1}{y_0}\int_0^{y_0} v(y) dy = \frac{3.0}{y_0} \int_0^{y_0} \left(\frac{y}{y_0}\right)^{1/6} dy = \frac{3.0}{y_0} \left[ \frac{y^{7/6}}{\frac{7}{6} y_0^{1/6}} \right]_0^{y_0} = 3.0 \times \frac{6}{7} = \mathbf{2.571 \text{ m/s}}$$

2. **Momentum Correction Factor $\beta$:**
   $$\beta = \frac{1}{y_0 V^2} \int_0^{y_0} v^2 dy = \frac{1}{y_0 (2.571)^2} \int_0^{y_0} (3.0)^2 \left(\frac{y}{y_0}\right)^{2/6} dy = \frac{9.0}{y_0 (6.612)} \int_0^{y_0} \left(\frac{y}{y_0}\right)^{1/3} dy$$
   $$\beta = \frac{9.0}{6.612} \left[ \frac{3}{4} \right] = \frac{27.0}{26.448} = \mathbf{1.0208}$$
   *General Analytical Formula for $v \propto y^{1/m}$:*
   $$\beta = \frac{(m+1)^2}{m(m+2)} = \frac{(6+1)^2}{6(6+2)} = \frac{49}{48} = \mathbf{1.0208}$$

3. **Kinetic Energy Correction Factor $\alpha$:**
   $$\alpha = \frac{1}{y_0 V^3} \int_0^{y_0} v^3 dy = \frac{1}{y_0 (2.571)^3} \int_0^{y_0} (3.0)^3 \left(\frac{y}{y_0}\right)^{3/6} dy = \frac{27.0}{17.004} \int_0^{y_0} \left(\frac{y}{y_0}\right)^{1/2} dy$$
   $$\alpha = \frac{27.0}{17.004} \left[ \frac{2}{3} \right] = \frac{18.0}{17.004} = \mathbf{1.0586}$$
   *General Analytical Formula for $v \propto y^{1/m}$:*
   $$\alpha = \frac{(m+1)^3}{m^2 (m+3)} = \frac{7^3}{6^2 (6+3)} = \frac{343}{36 \times 9} = \frac{343}{324} = \mathbf{1.0586}$$

4. **Error in Velocity Head:**
   - 1D Velocity Head: $h_{v,1D} = \frac{V^2}{2g} = \frac{2.571^2}{2 \times 9.81} = \frac{6.612}{19.62} = 0.337 \text{ m}$
   - True Velocity Head: $h_{v,\text{true}} = \alpha \frac{V^2}{2g} = 1.0586 \times 0.337 = 0.357 \text{ m}$
   - Underestimation without $\alpha$: $\Delta h_v = 0.020 \text{ m}$ ($5.86\%$ error).
:::

### Example 11.2: Compound Flood Channel Analysis
**Problem:** During a severe flood, a river cross-section is divided into a deep main channel and a vegetated overbank floodplain:
- Main Channel: Area $A_1 = 60 \text{ m}^2$, Mean velocity $V_1 = 2.5 \text{ m/s}$.
- Floodplain: Area $A_2 = 140 \text{ m}^2$, Mean velocity $V_2 = 0.5 \text{ m/s}$.
Compute the composite mean velocity $V$, the Coriolis coefficient $\alpha$, and the Boussinesq coefficient $\beta$.

:::sol Complete Compound Channel Solution
1. **Total Flow and Composite Velocity:**
   - Discharge in Main Channel: $Q_1 = A_1 V_1 = 60 \times 2.5 = 150 \text{ m}^3/\text{s}$
   - Discharge in Floodplain: $Q_2 = A_2 V_2 = 140 \times 0.5 = 70 \text{ m}^3/\text{s}$
   - Total Discharge: $Q = Q_1 + Q_2 = 150 + 70 = 220 \text{ m}^3/\text{s}$
   - Total Area: $A = A_1 + A_2 = 60 + 140 = 200 \text{ m}^2$
   - Mean Velocity: $V = \frac{Q}{A} = \frac{220}{200} = \mathbf{1.10 \text{ m/s}}$

2. **Momentum Correction Factor $\beta$:**
   $$\beta = \frac{A_1 V_1^2 + A_2 V_2^2}{A V^2} = \frac{60(2.5)^2 + 140(0.5)^2}{200(1.10)^2} = \frac{60(6.25) + 140(0.25)}{200(1.21)} = \frac{375 + 35}{242} = \frac{410}{242} = \mathbf{1.694}$$

3. **Coriolis Correction Factor $\alpha$:**
   $$\alpha = \frac{A_1 V_1^3 + A_2 V_2^3}{A V^3} = \frac{60(2.5)^3 + 140(0.5)^3}{200(1.10)^3} = \frac{60(15.625) + 140(0.125)}{200(1.331)} = \frac{937.5 + 17.5}{266.2} = \frac{955}{266.2} = \mathbf{3.587}$$

*Crucial Hydraulic Takeaway:* In compound channels during flood submergence, ignoring $\alpha$ underestimates the kinetic energy head by over **$250\%$**!
:::

### Real-World Field Nuances & Hydrometry Standards
1. **Acoustic Doppler Current Profiler (ADCP) Integration:**
   Modern river gauging uses acoustic Doppler current profilers (ADCPs) mounted on survey boats or tethered floats. Rather than relying on two-point averages ($0.2y$ and $0.8y$), the ADCP transmits ultrasonic pulses that divide the water column into dozens of vertical measurement cells (bins), integrating the complete 3D velocity field directly to calculate true discharge and exact values of $\alpha$ and $\beta$.
2. **HEC-RAS Compound Channel Conveyance Treatment:**
   In 1D HEC-RAS simulations, river cross-sections are subdivided into left overbank (LOB), main channel (CHAN), and right overbank (ROB). HEC-RAS computes conveyances separately for each sub-reach:
   $$K = K_{\text{LOB}} + K_{\text{CHAN}} + K_{\text{ROB}}$$
   and internally computes the Coriolis coefficient $\alpha$ for each cross-section to accurately preserve energy heads across bridge contractions and floodway constrictions.
