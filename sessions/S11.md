> [!note] Non-Uniform Velocity & Flux Corrections
> In true open channel flow, fluid velocity is never uniform across the cross section. Frictional shear at the wetted perimeter forces boundary velocity to zero (no-slip condition), while secondary transverse circulation vortices pull the point of maximum velocity below the free surface. To apply one-dimensional energy and momentum equations, engineers introduce two dimensionless integration factors: the **Coriolis coefficient ($\alpha$)** for kinetic energy and the **Boussinesq coefficient ($\beta$)** for momentum flux.

## 1. Velocity Distribution Across Channel Cross-Sections
In a pipe running full, the velocity distribution is axisymmetric, with maximum velocity strictly along the central pipe axis. In an open channel:
1. **Three-Dimensional Nature:** The flow velocity varies both vertically ($y$-direction) and laterally ($z$-direction).
2. **Boundary Shear:** Boundary shear stresses retard fluid particles adjacent to the channel bed and sidewalls.
3. **The Velocity Dip:** Secondary transverse circulation cells drag low-momentum fluid inward along the free surface and downward at the channel centerline. Consequently, the **maximum velocity does not occur at the free surface**, but rather **$0.05 y \text{ to } 0.25 y$ below the surface**.
4. **Field Gauging Depths (IS: 1192):**
   - In shallow streams ($y < 0.6 \text{ m}$): Mean velocity is measured at a depth of **$0.6 y$ below the surface**:
     $$V_{\text{mean}} \approx v_{0.6}$$
   - In deep streams ($y \ge 0.6 \text{ m}$): Mean velocity is taken as the average of readings at **$0.2 y$ and $0.8 y$**:
     $$V_{\text{mean}} \approx \frac{v_{0.2} + v_{0.8}}{2}$$

## 2. Kinetic Energy Correction Factor (Coriolis Coefficient, $\alpha$)
When computing kinetic energy head using the mean cross-sectional velocity $V = Q/A$, the true kinetic energy is underestimated because velocity is cubed:
- True kinetic energy passing an elemental area $dA$ per unit time:
  $$dE_k = \frac{1}{2} (\rho v dA) v^2 = \frac{1}{2} \rho v^3 dA$$
- Total kinetic energy flux across the entire cross-section:
  $$E_{k,\text{true}} = \frac{1}{2} \rho \int_A v^3 dA$$
- One-dimensional approximation based on mean velocity $V$:
  $$E_{k,1D} = \frac{1}{2} (\rho A V) V^2 = \frac{1}{2} \rho A V^3$$
- The ratio of true kinetic energy to 1D kinetic energy is the **Coriolis Coefficient ($\alpha$)**:
  $$\mathbf{\alpha = \frac{\int_A v^3 dA}{V^3 A} \ge 1.0}$$
Therefore, the true total energy head at any section is:
$$\mathbf{H = z + y + \alpha \frac{V^2}{2g}}$$

## 3. Momentum Correction Factor (Boussinesq Coefficient, $\beta$)
Similarly, momentum flux across an open channel section depends on the square of velocity:
- True momentum flux per second across elemental area $dA$:
  $$dM = (\rho v dA) v = \rho v^2 dA$$
- Total true momentum flux:
  $$M_{\text{true}} = \rho \int_A v^2 dA$$
- One-dimensional momentum flux based on mean velocity $V$:
  $$M_{1D} = (\rho A V) V = \rho A V^2$$
- The ratio defines the **Boussinesq Coefficient ($\beta$)**:
  $$\mathbf{\beta = \frac{\int_A v^2 dA}{V^2 A} \ge 1.0}$$
The true dynamic momentum force exerted by the fluid stream is:
$$\mathbf{F_{\text{dyn}} = \beta \rho Q V}$$

## 4. Fundamental Theorem: $\alpha > \beta \ge 1.0$
By the Cauchy-Schwarz mathematical inequality, for any non-uniform positive velocity field:
$$\alpha > \beta \ge 1.0$$
Only in the hypothetical ideal case of perfectly uniform plug flow ($v = V$ everywhere) does $\alpha = \beta = 1.00$.

| Channel Type | Coriolis Factor $\alpha$ | Boussinesq Factor $\beta$ | Practical Engineering Significance |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Regular Prismatic Canals** | $1.03 - 1.10$ | $1.01 - 1.05$ | Often assumed $\alpha \approx 1.0, \beta \approx 1.0$ in routine canal design. |
| **Natural River Reaches** | $1.15 - 1.30$ | $1.05 - 1.12$ | Must be accounted for in backwater and flood routing calculations. |
| **Compound Channels (Main + Floodplain)** | $1.20 - 1.60$ | $1.10 - 1.30$ | Severe velocity mismatch between fast river channel and slow overbank vegetation. |

### Example 11.1: Power-Law Velocity Distribution Integration
**Problem:** In a wide rectangular channel of depth $y_0 = 2.0 \text{ m}$, the velocity profile is measured as $v(y) = 3.0 \left(\frac{y}{y_0}\right)^{1/6} \text{ m/s}$.
1. Derive the expression for mean velocity $V$.
2. Calculate the momentum correction factor $\beta$.
3. Calculate the kinetic energy correction factor $\alpha$.
4. Compute the percentage error in velocity head if $\alpha$ is neglected ($V = 3.0 \text{ m/s}$).

:::sol Step-by-Step Analytical Derivation
1. **Mean Velocity $V$:**
   $$V = \frac{1}{y_0}\int_0^{y_0} v(y) dy = \frac{3.0}{y_0} \int_0^{y_0} \left(\frac{y}{y_0}\right)^{1/6} dy = \frac{3.0}{y_0} \left[ \frac{y^{7/6}}{\frac{7}{6} y_0^{1/6}} \right]_0^{y_0} = 3.0 \times \frac{6}{7} = \mathbf{2.571 \text{ m/s}}$$

2. **Momentum Correction Factor $\beta$:**
   $$\beta = \frac{1}{y_0 V^2} \int_0^{y_0} v^2 dy = \frac{1}{y_0 (2.571)^2} \int_0^{y_0} (3.0)^2 \left(\frac{y}{y_0}\right)^{2/6} dy = \frac{9.0}{y_0 (6.612)} \int_0^{y_0} \left(\frac{y}{y_0}\right)^{1/3} dy$$
   $$\beta = \frac{9.0}{6.612} \left[ \frac{3}{4} \right] = \frac{27.0}{26.448} = \mathbf{1.0208}$$
   *General Analytical Formula for $v \propto y^{1/m}$:*
   $$\beta = \frac{(m+1)^2}{m(m+2)} = \frac{(6+1)^2}{6(6+2)} = \frac{49}{48} = \mathbf{1.0208}$$

3. **Kinetic Energy Correction Factor $\alpha$:**
   $$\alpha = \frac{1}{y_0 V^3} \int_0^{y_0} v^3 dy = \frac{1}{y_0 (2.571)^3} \int_0^{y_0} (3.0)^3 \left(\frac{y}{y_0}\right)^{3/6} dy = \frac{27.0}{17.004} \int_0^{y_0} \left(\frac{y}{y_0}\right)^{1/2} dy$$
   $$\alpha = \frac{27.0}{17.004} \left[ \frac{2}{3} \right] = \frac{18.0}{17.004} = \mathbf{1.0586}$$
   *General Analytical Formula for $v \propto y^{1/m}$:*
   $$\alpha = \frac{(m+1)^3}{m^2 (m+3)} = \frac{7^3}{6^2 (6+3)} = \frac{343}{36 \times 9} = \frac{343}{324} = \mathbf{1.0586}$$

4. **Error in Velocity Head:**
   - 1D Velocity Head: $h_{v,1D} = \frac{V^2}{2g} = \frac{2.571^2}{2 \times 9.81} = \frac{6.612}{19.62} = 0.337 \text{ m}$
   - True Velocity Head: $h_{v,\text{true}} = \alpha \frac{V^2}{2g} = 1.0586 \times 0.337 = 0.357 \text{ m}$
   - Underestimation without $\alpha$: $\Delta h_v = 0.020 \text{ m}$ ($5.86\%$ error).
:::

### Example 11.2: Compound Flood Channel Analysis
**Problem:** During a severe flood, a river cross-section is divided into a deep main channel and a vegetated overbank floodplain:
- Main Channel: Area $A_1 = 60 \text{ m}^2$, Mean velocity $V_1 = 2.5 \text{ m/s}$.
- Floodplain: Area $A_2 = 140 \text{ m}^2$, Mean velocity $V_2 = 0.5 \text{ m/s}$.
Compute the composite mean velocity $V$, the Coriolis coefficient $\alpha$, and the Boussinesq coefficient $\beta$.

:::sol Complete Compound Channel Solution
1. **Total Flow and Composite Velocity:**
   - Discharge in Main Channel: $Q_1 = A_1 V_1 = 60 \times 2.5 = 150 \text{ m}^3/\text{s}$
   - Discharge in Floodplain: $Q_2 = A_2 V_2 = 140 \times 0.5 = 70 \text{ m}^3/\text{s}$
   - Total Discharge: $Q = Q_1 + Q_2 = 150 + 70 = 220 \text{ m}^3/\text{s}$
   - Total Area: $A = A_1 + A_2 = 60 + 140 = 200 \text{ m}^2$
   - Mean Velocity: $V = \frac{Q}{A} = \frac{220}{200} = \mathbf{1.10 \text{ m/s}}$

2. **Momentum Correction Factor $\beta$:**
   $$\beta = \frac{A_1 V_1^2 + A_2 V_2^2}{A V^2} = \frac{60(2.5)^2 + 140(0.5)^2}{200(1.10)^2} = \frac{60(6.25) + 140(0.25)}{200(1.21)} = \frac{375 + 35}{242} = \frac{410}{242} = \mathbf{1.694}$$

3. **Coriolis Correction Factor $\alpha$:**
   $$\alpha = \frac{A_1 V_1^3 + A_2 V_2^3}{A V^3} = \frac{60(2.5)^3 + 140(0.5)^3}{200(1.10)^3} = \frac{60(15.625) + 140(0.125)}{200(1.331)} = \frac{937.5 + 17.5}{266.2} = \frac{955}{266.2} = \mathbf{3.587}$$

*Crucial Hydraulic Takeaway:* In compound channels during flood submergence, ignoring $\alpha$ underestimates the kinetic energy head by over **$250\%$**!
:::

### Real-World Field Nuances & Hydrometry Standards
1. **Acoustic Doppler Current Profiler (ADCP) Integration:**
   Modern river gauging uses acoustic Doppler current profilers (ADCPs) mounted on survey boats or tethered floats. Rather than relying on two-point averages ($0.2y$ and $0.8y$), the ADCP transmits ultrasonic pulses that divide the water column into dozens of vertical measurement cells (bins), integrating the complete 3D velocity field directly to calculate true discharge and exact values of $\alpha$ and $\beta$.
2. **HEC-RAS Compound Channel Conveyance Treatment:**
   In 1D HEC-RAS simulations, river cross-sections are subdivided into left overbank (LOB), main channel (CHAN), and right overbank (ROB). HEC-RAS computes conveyances separately for each sub-reach:
   $$K = K_{\text{LOB}} + K_{\text{CHAN}} + K_{\text{ROB}}$$
   and internally computes the Coriolis coefficient $\alpha$ for each cross-section to accurately preserve energy heads across bridge contractions and floodway constrictions.
