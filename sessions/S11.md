> [!note] Core Concept
> Due to boundary shear along the bed and side walls, together with surface tension and secondary circulations at the free surface, velocity across an open channel cross-section is non-uniform. To apply 1D energy and momentum equations, engineers introduce the **Coriolis energy correction factor ($\alpha$)** and the **Boussinesq momentum correction factor ($\beta$)**.

## 1. Velocity Distribution Pattern
- **Maximum Velocity Point:** Because secondary cross-currents dip down from the corners, the maximum velocity is typically depressed below the water surface at a distance of $0.05 y$ to $0.25 y$.
- **Mean Velocity Measurement:** Field stream-gaging (USGS and CWC standards) measures velocity at $0.6 y$ depth for shallow streams, or the average of measurements at $0.2 y$ and $0.8 y$ depths:
  $$\bar{V} \approx \frac{V_{0.2} + V_{0.8}}{2}$$

## 2. Kinetic Energy Correction Factor ($\alpha$)
True kinetic energy flux across a channel is $\int \frac{1}{2}\rho u^3 dA$. In 1D formulation, we write this as $\alpha \cdot \frac{1}{2}\rho V^3 A$:
$$\alpha = \frac{\int u^3 \, dA}{V^3 A}$$
- For uniform velocity: $\alpha = 1.00$.
- For regular prismatic channels: $\alpha \approx 1.05 - 1.15$.
- For river floodplains with compound channels: $\alpha \approx 1.20 - 2.00$.

## 3. Momentum Correction Factor ($\beta$)
True momentum flux is $\int \rho u^2 dA = \beta \cdot \rho V^2 A$:
$$\beta = \frac{\int u^2 \, dA}{V^2 A}$$
- In regular channels: $\beta \approx 1.02 - 1.05$.
- By Cauchy-Schwarz inequality: $1 \le \beta \le \alpha$.

### Example 11.1: Calculation of α and β for a Parabolic Profile
**Problem:** In an open channel of width $B$ and depth $H$, the velocity profile varies parabolically from bed to surface as:
$$u = U_0 \left(\frac{y}{H}\right)^{1/2}$$
Calculate: (1) Mean velocity $V$, (2) Coriolis energy coefficient $\alpha$, and (3) Boussinesq momentum coefficient $\beta$.

**Solution:**
1. **Mean Velocity $V$:**
   $$V = \frac{1}{H} \int_0^H u \, dy = \frac{U_0}{H} \int_0^H \left(\frac{y}{H}\right)^{1/2} dy = \frac{U_0}{H} \left[\frac{2}{3} \frac{y^{3/2}}{H^{1/2}}\right]_0^H = \frac{2}{3} U_0 \approx 0.667 U_0$$
2. **Kinetic Energy Coefficient $\alpha$:**
   $$\int_0^H u^3 dy = U_0^3 \int_0^H \left(\frac{y}{H}\right)^{3/2} dy = U_0^3 \left[\frac{2}{5} \frac{y^{5/2}}{H^{3/2}}\right]_0^H = \frac{2}{5} H U_0^3$$
   $$\alpha = \frac{\int u^3 dy}{V^3 H} = \frac{\frac{2}{5} H U_0^3}{(2/3 U_0)^3 H} = \frac{2/5}{8/27} = \frac{2}{5} \times \frac{27}{8} = \frac{27}{20} = 1.35$$
3. **Momentum Coefficient $\beta$:**
   $$\int_0^H u^2 dy = U_0^2 \int_0^H \frac{y}{H} dy = \frac{1}{2} H U_0^2$$
   $$\beta = \frac{\int u^2 dy}{V^2 H} = \frac{\frac{1}{2} H U_0^2}{(2/3 U_0)^2 H} = \frac{1/2}{4/9} = \frac{9}{8} = 1.125$$
   Note that $1 < \beta (1.125) < \alpha (1.35)$, satisfying physical theory.

<!--tab:Summary-->
### Key Takeaways
- Energy coefficient: $\alpha = \int u^3 dA / (V^3 A) \ge 1$.
- Momentum coefficient: $\beta = \int u^2 dA / (V^2 A) \ge 1$.
- Always $\alpha > \beta > 1$ for non-uniform flow.
