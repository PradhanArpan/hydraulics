> [!note] Core Concept
> An **open channel** is a conduit in which liquid flows with a free top surface subjected directly to constant atmospheric pressure. While pipe flow is driven primarily by pressure head gradients, open channel flow is driven entirely by the component of gravity acting parallel to the channel bed slope.

## 1. Open Channel vs Pipe Flow Comparison
- **Driving Force:** Pipe flow is driven by piezometric pressure gradient $\Delta(p/\gamma + z)$; Open channel flow is driven by gravity $W \sin\theta$.
- **Flow Boundary:** Pipe has fixed closed solid perimeter; Open channel has a deformable free liquid surface that adjusts dynamically with discharge.
- **Cross-Sectional Area:** Constant in a rigid pipe flowing full; Varies with stage/depth in an open channel.

## 2. Geometric Elements of Channel Sections
Let $y$ be the water depth:
1. **Flow Area ($A$):** Cross-sectional area of water perpendicular to flow direction ($	ext{m}^2$).
2. **Wetted Perimeter ($P$):** Length of the boundary line along the channel bed and sides in direct contact with water ($	ext{m}$).
3. **Hydraulic Radius ($R$):**
   $$R = \frac{A}{P}$$
4. **Top Width ($T$):** Width of the channel cross section at the free water surface ($	ext{m}$).
5. **Hydraulic Depth ($D$):**
   $$D = \frac{A}{T}$$
6. **Section Factor for Critical Flow ($Z$):**
   $$Z = A\sqrt{D} = \frac{A^{1.5}}{\sqrt{T}}$$

## 3. Flow Classifications
- **Uniform vs Non-Uniform:** In uniform flow, $\frac{\partial y}{\partial x} = 0$ (depth, velocity, and area remain constant along the channel).
- **Steady vs Unsteady:** In steady flow, $\frac{\partial y}{\partial t} = 0$.
- **Froude Number Regimes ($Fr = \frac{V}{\sqrt{gD}}$):**
  - **Subcritical ($Fr < 1$):** Tranquil flow, gravity dominates, downstream disturbances travel upstream.
  - **Critical ($Fr = 1$):** Velocity equals gravity wave celerity $c = \sqrt{gD}$.
  - **Supercritical ($Fr > 1$):** Rapid, shooting flow; disturbances swept downstream only.

### Example 10.1: Geometric Elements of a Trapezoidal Canal
**Problem:** A trapezoidal irrigation canal has a bottom bed width $b = 3.50 \text{ m}$, side slopes of $1\text{V} : 1.5\text{H}$ ($z = 1.5$), and carries water at a depth $y = 1.60 \text{ m}$. Calculate: (1) Area $A$, (2) Wetted perimeter $P$, (3) Hydraulic radius $R$, (4) Top width $T$, and (5) Hydraulic depth $D$.

**Solution:**
1. **Flow Area $A$:**
   $$A = (b + z y) y = (3.50 + 1.5 \times 1.60) \times 1.60 = (3.50 + 2.40) \times 1.60 = 5.90 \times 1.60 = 9.44 \text{ m}^2$$
2. **Wetted Perimeter $P$:**
   $$P = b + 2y\sqrt{1 + z^2} = 3.50 + 2(1.60)\sqrt{1 + (1.5)^2} = 3.50 + 3.20 \times \sqrt{3.25} = 3.50 + 3.20 \times 1.8028 = 9.269 \text{ m}$$
3. **Hydraulic Radius $R$:**
   $$R = \frac{A}{P} = \frac{9.44}{9.269} = 1.018 \text{ m}$$
4. **Top Width $T$:**
   $$T = b + 2 z y = 3.50 + 2(1.5)(1.60) = 3.50 + 4.80 = 8.30 \text{ m}$$
5. **Hydraulic Depth $D$:**
   $$D = \frac{A}{T} = \frac{9.44}{8.30} = 1.137 \text{ m}$$

<!--tab:Summary-->
### Key Takeaways
- Open channel flow is gravity-driven with free surface at atmospheric pressure.
- Key geometric parameters: $A = (b+zy)y$, $P = b + 2y\sqrt{1+z^2}$, $R = A/P$, $D = A/T$.
- Flow regime determined by Froude number: $Fr = V / \sqrt{gD}$.
