> [!note] Core Concept
> When a high-velocity fluid jet strikes an obstacle, its velocity vector changes in magnitude and direction. By Newton's second law, the force exerted by the jet on the obstacle equals the rate of change of momentum of the fluid stream in the opposite direction. This **Impulse-Momentum Principle** forms the physical foundation of all impulse turbines and hydraulic propulsion systems.

## 1. Impulse-Momentum Equation for Fluid Jets
Consider a steady jet of fluid with mass density $\rho$, cross-sectional area $a$, flowing with absolute velocity $V$:
1. Mass flow rate: $\dot{m} = \rho a V = \rho Q$
2. Momentum flux entering control volume: $\mathbf{M}_{in} = \dot{m} \mathbf{V}_1$
3. Momentum flux exiting control volume: $\mathbf{M}_{out} = \dot{m} \mathbf{V}_2$
4. By the momentum equation, the hydrodynamic force $\mathbf{F}$ exerted by the jet on the boundary is:
   $$\mathbf{F} = \dot{m} (\mathbf{V}_1 - \mathbf{V}_2) = \rho a V (\mathbf{V}_1 - \mathbf{V}_2)$$

## 2. Nozzle Hydrodynamics & Jet Reaction
A nozzle converts total pressure head $H$ into kinetic energy:
$$V = C_v \sqrt{2 g H}$$
Where $C_v$ is the coefficient of velocity ($0.97 - 0.99$ for machined brass/steel nozzles).
The dynamic reaction force $R$ exerted by a discharging jet on the vessel or pipe nozzle itself is:
$$R = \dot{m} V = \rho a V^2 = 2 C_v^2 \cdot (\rho g a H) \approx 2 \gamma a H$$

### Example 19.1: Dynamic Thrust and Reaction of a Fire Hose Nozzle
**Problem:** A fire hose discharges water through a nozzle of exit diameter $d = 30 \text{ mm}$ under a total pressure head $H = 50.0 \text{ m}$. Assuming $C_v = 0.98$ and water density $\rho = 1000 \text{ kg/m}^3$:
1. Calculate the jet discharge velocity $V$ and flow rate $Q$.
2. Determine the dynamic reaction force $R$ that must be held by the firefighter.

**Solution:**
1. **Jet Velocity $V$ & Area $a$:**
   $$a = \frac{\pi}{4} d^2 = \frac{\pi}{4} (0.030)^2 = 7.069 \times 10^{-4} \text{ m}^2$$
   $$V = C_v \sqrt{2 g H} = 0.98 \times \sqrt{2 \times 9.81 \times 50.0} = 0.98 \times \sqrt{981} = 0.98 \times 31.32 = 30.69 \text{ m/s}$$
2. **Discharge $Q$ & Mass Flow Rate $\dot{m}$:**
   $$Q = a V = 7.069 \times 10^{-4} \times 30.69 = 0.0217 \text{ m}^3/\text{s} = 21.7 \text{ L/s}$$
   $$\dot{m} = \rho Q = 1000 \times 0.0217 = 21.70 \text{ kg/s}$$
3. **Reaction Force $R$:**
   $$R = \dot{m} V = 21.70 \times 30.69 = 666.0 \text{ N}$$
   The firefighter must resist a continuous backward thrust of **666 N** (approx. 68 kgf).

### Key Takeaways
- Impulse-momentum equation: $\mathbf{F} = \dot{m}(\mathbf{V}_{in} - \mathbf{V}_{out})$.
- Jet velocity from nozzle: $V = C_v \sqrt{2gH}$.
- Reaction of jet on nozzle: $R = \rho a V^2 = 2 C_v^2 \gamma a H$.
