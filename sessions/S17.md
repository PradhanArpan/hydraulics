> [!note] The Nature of Hydraulic Jumps
> A **Hydraulic Jump** is a standing hydrodynamic shock wave that occurs whenever an unstable **supercritical shooting flow ($Fr_1 > 1$)** abruptly transitions into a stable **subcritical streaming flow ($Fr_2 < 1$)**.
> It is an example of **Rapidly Varied Flow (RVF)** characterized by intense surface roller vortices, massive air entrainment, and violent turbulent energy dissipation. It is used as a primary energy dissipator downstream of dam spillways and sluice gates to prevent riverbed scouring.

## 1. Assumptions in Hydraulic Jump Analysis
Because flow within the jump roller is violently three-dimensional and turbulent, the following four engineering assumptions are made to apply one-dimensional momentum conservation between sections 1 (upstream supercritical) and 2 (downstream subcritical):
1. **Hydrostatic Pressure Distribution:** Flow is parallel and uniform before (section 1) and after (section 2) the jump, so pressure distribution is strictly hydrostatic: $P = \gamma A \bar{z}$.
2. **Negligible Bed Friction:** The length of the jump $L_j$ is relatively short, so resisting boundary friction on the channel floor is negligible compared to dynamic momentum forces: $F_f \approx 0$.
3. **Horizontal Channel Bed:** The bed slope is small ($\theta \approx 0$), meaning the fluid weight component in the flow direction is negligible: $W \sin \theta \approx 0$.
4. **Uniform Velocity Corrections:** Momentum correction factors are close to unity: $\beta_1 \approx \beta_2 \approx 1.0$.

## 2. Derivation of the Momentum Equation & Specific Force ($F_M$)
Applying Newton's Second Law (momentum balance) to the control volume between sections 1 and 2:
$$\sum F_x = \text{Rate of change of momentum}$$
$$P_1 - P_2 = \rho Q (V_2 - V_1)$$
Substituting hydrostatic thrust $P_1 = \gamma A_1 \bar{z}_1$ and $P_2 = \gamma A_2 \bar{z}_2$:
$$\gamma A_1 \bar{z}_1 - \gamma A_2 \bar{z}_2 = \rho Q (V_2 - V_1)$$
Dividing through by the unit weight of water $\gamma = \rho g$:
$$A_1 \bar{z}_1 - A_2 \bar{z}_2 = \frac{Q}{g} (V_2 - V_1) = \frac{Q^2}{g A_2} - \frac{Q^2}{g A_1}$$
Grouping all section 1 terms on the left and section 2 terms on the right:
$$\mathbf{A_1 \bar{z}_1 + \frac{Q^2}{g A_1} = A_2 \bar{z}_2 + \frac{Q^2}{g A_2}}$$
This fundamental invariant is the **Specific Force Function (Momentum Function, $F_M$):**
$$\mathbf{F_M = A \bar{z} + \frac{Q^2}{g A} = \text{Constant}}$$
- The depths $y_1$ and $y_2$ possessing identical Specific Force ($F_{M1} = F_{M2}$) are termed **Conjugate Depths (or Sequent Depths)**.
- Unlike Alternate Depths (which share equal specific energy $E$), Conjugate Depths share **equal momentum force $F_M$**.

## 3. Derivation of the Bélanger Equation (1828)
For a rectangular channel of width $B$ with unit discharge $q = Q/B$:
- Flow area: $A = B y$
- Centroid depth: $\bar{z} = y/2$
- Specific force per unit width:
  $$\frac{y_1^2}{2} + \frac{q^2}{g y_1} = \frac{y_2^2}{2} + \frac{q^2}{g y_2}$$
Rearranging terms:
$$\frac{y_2^2 - y_1^2}{2} = \frac{q^2}{g} \left( \frac{1}{y_1} - \frac{1}{y_2} \right) = \frac{q^2}{g} \left( \frac{y_2 - y_1}{y_1 y_2} \right)$$
Factoring $(y_2^2 - y_1^2) = (y_2 - y_1)(y_2 + y_1)$:
$$\frac{(y_2 - y_1)(y_2 + y_1)}{2} = \frac{q^2 (y_2 - y_1)}{g y_1 y_2}$$
Since $y_2 \ne y_1$ across a true jump, dividing by $(y_2 - y_1)$:
$$\frac{y_2 + y_1}{2} = \frac{q^2}{g y_1 y_2} \implies y_2 (y_1 + y_2) = \frac{2 q^2}{g y_1}$$
Noting that $q = V_1 y_1$, we have $\frac{q^2}{g y_1} = \frac{V_1^2 y_1^2}{g y_1} = \frac{V_1^2 y_1}{g} = y_1^2 Fr_1^2$:
$$y_2^2 + y_1 y_2 - 2 y_1^2 Fr_1^2 = 0$$
Dividing by $y_1^2$:
$$\left(\frac{y_2}{y_1}\right)^2 + \left(\frac{y_2}{y_1}\right) - 2 Fr_1^2 = 0$$
Solving this quadratic equation using the quadratic formula:
$$\mathbf{\frac{y_2}{y_1} = \frac{-1 + \sqrt{1^2 - 4(1)(-2 Fr_1^2)}}{2} = \frac{1}{2} \left( \sqrt{1 + 8 Fr_1^2} - 1 \right)}$$
This celebrated relationship is the **Bélanger Equation**. Conversely:
$$\mathbf{\frac{y_1}{y_2} = \frac{1}{2} \left( \sqrt{1 + 8 Fr_2^2} - 1 \right)}$$

## 4. Energy Loss Across the Hydraulic Jump ($\Delta E$)
Mechanical energy is **NOT conserved** across a hydraulic jump because turbulent roller vortices and shock waves convert kinetic energy into heat and noise:
$$\Delta E = E_1 - E_2 = \left(y_1 + \frac{V_1^2}{2g}\right) - \left(y_2 + \frac{V_2^2}{2g}\right) = (y_1 - y_2) + \frac{q^2}{2g} \left(\frac{1}{y_1^2} - \frac{1}{y_2^2}\right)$$
Substituting $\frac{q^2}{g} = \frac{y_1 y_2 (y_1 + y_2)}{2}$ from the momentum balance yields the classical formula:
$$\mathbf{\Delta E = \frac{(y_2 - y_1)^3}{4 y_1 y_2}}$$

### Essential Jump Characteristics:
1. **Jump Height ($h_j$):** The vertical rise in water level:
   $$\mathbf{h_j = y_2 - y_1}$$
2. **Jump Length ($L_j$):** Distance from front face to calm downstream tailwater:
   $$\mathbf{L_j \approx 5 \text{ to } 7 h_j \approx 6.1 (y_2 - y_1)}$$
3. **Relative Energy Loss:**
   $$\mathbf{\frac{\Delta E}{E_1} = \frac{(y_2 - y_1)^3}{4 y_1 y_2 E_1}}$$
4. **Dissipated Power ($P$):** Rate of turbulent energy destruction:
   $$\mathbf{P = \gamma Q \Delta E \quad (\text{kW})}$$

### Example 17.1: Sequent Depth in a Rectangular Channel (Slide 12)
**Problem:** The depth of flow of water at a certain section of a rectangular channel $4.0 \text{ m}$ wide is $y_1 = 0.50 \text{ m}$. The discharge through the channel is $Q = 16.0 \text{ m}^3/\text{s}$. If a hydraulic jump takes place on the downstream side, find the depth of flow after the jump.

:::sol Step-by-Step Solution
1. **Initial Flow Parameters:**
   - Channel width: $B = 4.0 \text{ m}$
   - Discharge: $Q = 16.0 \text{ m}^3/\text{s}$
   - Unit discharge:
     $$q = \frac{Q}{B} = \frac{16.0}{4.0} = \mathbf{4.0 \text{ m}^2/\text{s}}$$
   - Initial velocity:
     $$V_1 = \frac{q}{y_1} = \frac{4.0}{0.50} = \mathbf{8.0 \text{ m/s}}$$
   - Initial Froude number:
     $$Fr_1 = \frac{V_1}{\sqrt{g y_1}} = \frac{8.0}{\sqrt{9.81 \times 0.50}} = \frac{8.0}{\sqrt{4.905}} = \frac{8.0}{2.2147} = \mathbf{3.6122}$$
     Since $Fr_1 = 3.612 > 1.0$, the flow is supercritical and a hydraulic jump will form.

2. **Sequent Depth $y_2$ via Bélanger Equation:**
   $$y_2 = \frac{y_1}{2} \left[ \sqrt{1 + 8 Fr_1^2} - 1 \right]$$
   $$8 Fr_1^2 = 8 \times (3.6122)^2 = 8 \times 13.048 = 104.384$$
   $$\sqrt{1 + 8 Fr_1^2} = \sqrt{1 + 104.384} = \sqrt{105.384} = 10.2657$$
   $$y_2 = \frac{0.50}{2} [10.2657 - 1] = 0.25 \times 9.2657 = \mathbf{2.316 \text{ m}}$$
   **Depth after the jump:** $\mathbf{y_2 = 2.316 \text{ m}}$.

3. **Check via Momentum Equation:**
   $$\frac{y_2 (y_1 + y_2)}{2} = \frac{2.316 (0.50 + 2.316)}{2} = \frac{2.316 \times 2.816}{2} = 3.261$$
   $$\frac{q^2}{g y_1} = \frac{4.0^2}{9.81 \times 0.50} = \frac{16.0}{4.905} = 3.262 \quad (\text{Exact Match!})$$
:::

### Example 17.2: Sluice Gate Jump & Energy Head Loss (Slide 13)
**Problem:** A sluice gate discharges water into a horizontal rectangular channel with a velocity of $V_1 = 10.0 \text{ m/s}$ and depth of flow $y_1 = 1.0 \text{ m}$. Determine the depth of flow after the jump and the consequent loss in total head.

:::sol Step-by-Step Solution
1. **Initial Froude Number:**
   $$Fr_1 = \frac{V_1}{\sqrt{g y_1}} = \frac{10.0}{\sqrt{9.81 \times 1.0}} = \frac{10.0}{3.1321} = \mathbf{3.1928}$$

2. **Sequent Depth $y_2$:**
   $$y_2 = \frac{y_1}{2} \left[ \sqrt{1 + 8 Fr_1^2} - 1 \right]$$
   $$8 Fr_1^2 = 8 \times (3.1928)^2 = 8 \times 10.194 = 81.552$$
   $$\sqrt{1 + 81.552} = \sqrt{82.552} = 9.0858$$
   $$y_2 = \frac{1.0}{2} [9.0858 - 1] = 0.50 \times 8.0858 = \mathbf{4.043 \text{ m}}$$

3. **Loss in Total Head ($\Delta E$):**
   $$\Delta E = \frac{(y_2 - y_1)^3}{4 y_1 y_2} = \frac{(4.043 - 1.0)^3}{4(1.0)(4.043)} = \frac{(3.043)^3}{16.172} = \frac{28.178}{16.172} = \mathbf{1.742 \text{ m}}$$

4. **Energy Verification:**
   - Upstream Energy: $E_1 = y_1 + \frac{V_1^2}{2g} = 1.0 + \frac{10.0^2}{19.62} = 1.0 + 5.097 = 6.097 \text{ m}$
   - Downstream Velocity: $V_2 = \frac{V_1 y_1}{y_2} = \frac{10.0 \times 1.0}{4.043} = 2.473 \text{ m/s}$
   - Downstream Energy: $E_2 = y_2 + \frac{V_2^2}{2g} = 4.043 + \frac{2.473^2}{19.62} = 4.043 + 0.3117 = 4.355 \text{ m}$
   - Head loss: $\Delta E = E_1 - E_2 = 6.097 - 4.355 = \mathbf{1.742 \text{ m}}$ (Verified).
   - Percentage Dissipation: $\frac{\Delta E}{E_1} = \frac{1.742}{6.097} \times 100 = \mathbf{28.57\%}$.
:::

### Civil Engineering Stilling Basin Standards (IS: 4997)
1. **Design of Stilling Basins (IS: 4997 / USBR):**
   Dam spillways release catastrophic torrents of water at velocities exceeding $15 - 30 \text{ m/s}$. If allowed to strike the natural riverbed directly, this supercritical jet would scour massive plunge pools that undermine the dam foundation. Engineers construct reinforced concrete **stilling basins** at the spillway toe where a permanent hydraulic jump is anchored.
2. **Tailwater Conjugate Rating Curves:**
   A stable hydraulic jump requires the downstream river tailwater level ($y_{tw}$) to match the sequent depth ($y_2$) across all flood stages:
   - If $y_{tw} < y_2$: The jump is **swept downstream** out of the basin, eroding the unprotected riverbed.
   - If $y_{tw} > y_2$: The jump is **submerged (drowned)** at the spillway toe, creating a high-velocity bottom diving jet that scours downstream.
   - If $y_{tw} = y_2$: The jump is stably anchored inside the basin.
