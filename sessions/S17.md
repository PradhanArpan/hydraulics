> [!note] Core Concept
> A **hydraulic jump** is a rapidly varied open-channel phenomenon in which a high-velocity supercritical stream ($Fr_1 > 1$) abruptly decelerates and transitions into a tranquil subcritical stream ($Fr_2 < 1$). The severe turbulence, air entrainment, and violent eddy rollers dissipate massive kinetic energy, making hydraulic jumps the premier engineering mechanism for **energy dissipation downstream of dam spillways and sluice gates**.

## 1. Momentum Equation & Bélanger's Formula
Because energy loss in the jump is large and initially unknown, the jump is analyzed using the **momentum principle** across sections 1 and 2:
$$P_1 - P_2 = M_2 - M_1 \implies \frac{\gamma b y_1^2}{2} - \frac{\gamma b y_2^2}{2} = \rho Q (V_2 - V_1)$$
Rearranging yields the **Bélanger Equation** for conjugate (sequent) depths in a rectangular channel:
$$\frac{y_2}{y_1} = \frac{1}{2}\left(\sqrt{1 + 8 Fr_1^2} - 1\right)$$
Where $Fr_1 = \frac{V_1}{\sqrt{g y_1}}$.

## 2. Energy Dissipation & Jump Characteristics
- **Head Loss (Energy Dissipated):**
  $$\Delta E = E_1 - E_2 = \frac{(y_2 - y_1)^3}{4 y_1 y_2}$$
- **Jump Efficiency:** $\eta_j = \frac{\Delta E}{E_1}$
- **Jump Height:** $h_j = y_2 - y_1$
- **Jump Length ($L_j$):** USBR empirical relationship: $L_j \approx 5 \text{ to } 7 (y_2 - y_1) \approx 6.9 (y_2 - y_1)$.

## 3. USBR Classification of Hydraulic Jumps
1. $Fr_1 = 1.0 - 1.7$: **Undular Jump** (undulating standing waves, $\Delta E \approx 0$).
2. $Fr_1 = 1.7 - 2.5$: **Weak Jump** (small surface rollers, $\Delta E < 15\%$).
3. $Fr_1 = 2.5 - 4.5$: **Oscillating Jump** (jet oscillates from bed to surface; produces damaging waves downstream).
4. $Fr_1 = 4.5 - 9.0$: **Steady Jump** (best performance; 45%–70% energy dissipation; well-balanced stilling basin design).
5. $Fr_1 > 9.0$: **Strong / Choppy Jump** (rough, violent spray, $>70\%$ dissipation).

### Example 17.1: Stilling Basin Hydraulic Jump Analysis
**Problem:** A spillway discharges water into a rectangular stilling basin $8.0 \text{ m}$ wide. At the toe of the spillway, water depth is $y_1 = 0.50 \text{ m}$ and flow velocity is $V_1 = 14.0 \text{ m/s}$. Calculate:
1. Initial Froude number $Fr_1$ and classify the jump.
2. Sequent depth $y_2$.
3. Head loss $\Delta E$.
4. Power dissipated in the jump (in kilowatts).
5. Approximate length of the stilling basin.

**Solution:**
1. **Initial Froude Number:**
   $$Fr_1 = \frac{V_1}{\sqrt{g y_1}} = \frac{14.0}{\sqrt{9.81 \times 0.50}} = \frac{14.0}{\sqrt{4.905}} = \frac{14.0}{2.2147} = 6.32$$
   Since $4.5 < Fr_1 < 9.0$, this is a **Steady Hydraulic Jump** (optimal for USBR Type II/III stilling basins).
2. **Sequent Depth $y_2$:**
   $$\frac{y_2}{y_1} = \frac{1}{2}\left(\sqrt{1 + 8 Fr_1^2} - 1\right) = \frac{1}{2}\left(\sqrt{1 + 8(6.32)^2} - 1\right) = \frac{1}{2}(\sqrt{1 + 319.5} - 1) = \frac{1}{2}(17.90 - 1) = 8.45$$
   $$y_2 = 8.45 \times 0.50 = 4.225 \text{ m}$$
3. **Head Loss $\Delta E$:**
   $$\Delta E = \frac{(y_2 - y_1)^3}{4 y_1 y_2} = \frac{(4.225 - 0.50)^3}{4 \times 0.50 \times 4.225} = \frac{(3.725)^3}{8.45} = \frac{51.70}{8.45} = 6.118 \text{ m}$$
4. **Power Dissipated:**
   Total discharge: $Q = b y_1 V_1 = 8.0 \times 0.50 \times 14.0 = 56.0 \text{ m}^3/\text{s}$.
   $$P = \gamma Q \Delta E = 9810 \times 56.0 \times 6.118 = 3,360,980 \text{ W} = 3,361 \text{ kW} = 3.36 \text{ MW}$$
5. **Basin Length $L_j$:**
   $$L_j = 6.9 (y_2 - y_1) = 6.9 \times 3.725 = 25.7 \text{ m}$$

### Key Takeaways
- Sequent depth formula: $\frac{y_2}{y_1} = \frac{1}{2}(\sqrt{1 + 8Fr_1^2} - 1)$.
- Energy dissipated: $\Delta E = \frac{(y_2 - y_1)^3}{4 y_1 y_2}$.
- Steady jump ($Fr_1 = 4.5 - 9.0$) provides optimal stilling basin performance.
