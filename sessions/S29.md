> [!note] Core Concept
> Invented by **Lester Pelton in 1878**, the **Pelton wheel** is an impulse turbine where one or more high-velocity free jets strike double-hemispherical ellipsoidal buckets mounted along a rotor periphery. Each bucket splits the jet into two equal symmetrical halves, reversing fluid direction by nearly $170^\circ$ without inducing axial thrust.

## 1. Components of a Pelton Turbine
1. **Spear Nozzle Assembly:** A circular nozzle fitted with a streamlined conical needle (spear). Moving the spear axially via an oil-pressure servomotor throttles the annular jet area, controlling discharge $Q$ to match grid electrical load variations.
2. **Deflector Plate:** In the event of a sudden electrical load rejection (generator trip), moving the heavy spear slowly takes several seconds to avoid penstock water hammer. A quick-acting deflector swings into the jet within 0.1 seconds, diverting water away from the runner directly into the tailrace.
3. **Runner & Buckets:** A heavy forged steel disc carrying double-hemispherical buckets around its rim. Each bucket features a sharp **central splitter ridge** that divides the jet cleanly without shock. A notch is cut into the outer lip of each bucket so oncoming buckets enter the jet smoothly without chopping it prematurely.
4. **Casing:** Houses the runner to prevent splashing and direct water down to the tailrace. It operates under atmospheric pressure and plays no hydraulic pressure role.
5. **Braking Jet:** A small auxiliary nozzle that shoots water against the back of the buckets to bring the massive rotating runner to a rapid halt during shutdown.

### Example 29.1: Spear Valve Regulation and Water Power
**Problem:** A Pelton wheel operates under a net head $H = 400 \text{ m}$. At full load, the nozzle spear is fully retracted, producing a jet diameter $d = 120 \text{ mm}$. At $50\%$ partial load, the spear moves forward to reduce jet diameter to $d_{part} = 85 \text{ mm}$. Assuming $C_v = 0.985$ is constant:
1. Calculate the jet velocity $V_1$.
2. Compute the full-load water power and partial-load water power.

**Solution:**
1. **Jet Velocity $V_1$:**
   $$V_1 = C_v \sqrt{2 g H} = 0.985 \times \sqrt{2 \times 9.81 \times 400} = 0.985 \times \sqrt{7848} = 0.985 \times 88.589 = 87.26 \text{ m/s}$$
   *(Jet velocity remains essentially constant because net head H is unchanged! Discharge is governed by area).*
2. **Full-Load Flow & Power:**
   $$a_{full} = \frac{\pi}{4}(0.120)^2 = 0.01131 \text{ m}^2$$
   $$Q_{full} = a_{full} V_1 = 0.01131 \times 87.26 = 0.9869 \text{ m}^3/\text{s}$$
   $$P_{full} = \rho g Q_{full} H = 1000 \times 9.81 \times 0.9869 \times 400 = 3,872,600 \text{ W} = 3.873 \text{ MW}$$
3. **Partial-Load Flow & Power:**
   $$a_{part} = \frac{\pi}{4}(0.085)^2 = 0.005675 \text{ m}^2$$
   $$Q_{part} = a_{part} V_1 = 0.005675 \times 87.26 = 0.4952 \text{ m}^3/\text{s}$$
   $$P_{part} = \rho g Q_{part} H = 1000 \times 9.81 \times 0.4952 \times 400 = 1,943,200 \text{ W} = 1.943 \text{ MW}$$
   Discharge and power are throttled smoothly by **50%** through spear positioning.

<!--tab:Summary-->
### Key Takeaways
- Main components: spear nozzle, runner with double-ellipsoidal buckets, splitter, deflector, casing.
- Jet deflector protects penstock against water hammer during electrical trips.
- Splitter ridge balances axial thrust forces to zero.
