> [!note] The Normal Depth Challenge
> In open channel uniform flow, the governing Manning equation relates discharge $Q$ to flow depth $y_n$ through:
> $$Q = \frac{1}{n} A(y_n) [R(y_n)]^{2/3} \sqrt{S_0}$$
> Because flow area $A(y)$ and wetted perimeter $P(y)$ are non-linear algebraic functions of depth, $y_n$ **cannot be isolated explicitly** for trapezoidal, circular, or natural cross-sections. Solving for the normal depth requires either the **Section Factor method ($Z_u$)** or numerical iteration (**Newton-Raphson scheme**).

## 1. Section Factor for Uniform Flow ($Z_u$)
Rearranging Manning's equation isolates all geometric parameters on the left-hand side and all hydraulic and bed variables on the right:
$$\mathbf{Z_u = A R^{2/3} = \frac{Q \cdot n}{\sqrt{S_0}}}$$
- The right-hand side is constant for a given design reach.
- The left-hand side $Z_u = A(y) [R(y)]^{2/3}$ is a monotonically increasing function of water depth $y$.
- Once $Z_u$ is computed, $y_n$ can be looked up from dimensionless design charts (e.g. Ven Te Chow's design curves) or solved numerically.

## 2. Newton-Raphson Numerical Solution
To compute normal depth with high engineering precision, we define the residual objective function:
$$f(y) = \frac{1}{n} A R^{2/3} \sqrt{S_0} - Q = 0$$
Using Taylor series linearization, the depth estimate is iteratively updated:
$$\mathbf{y_{k+1} = y_k - \frac{f(y_k)}{f'(y_k)}}$$

### Analytical Derivative of Manning's Equation:
$$f'(y) = \frac{d}{dy}\left[\frac{\sqrt{S_0}}{n} \frac{A^{5/3}}{P^{2/3}}\right] = \frac{\sqrt{S_0}}{n} \frac{P^{2/3} \cdot \frac{5}{3} A^{2/3} \left(\frac{dA}{dy}\right) - A^{5/3} \cdot \frac{2}{3} P^{-1/3} \left(\frac{dP}{dy}\right)}{P^{4/3}}$$
Since $\frac{dA}{dy} = T$ (top width) and $\frac{dP}{dy} = 2\sqrt{1+m^2}$ for trapezoidal channels:
$$\mathbf{f'(y) = V \left[ \frac{5}{3} T - \frac{2}{3} R \frac{dP}{dy} \right]}$$
This formulation guarantees quadratic convergence in typically 3 to 5 iterations!

## 3. Classification of Channel Slopes
By comparing the normal depth $y_n$ to the critical depth $y_c$ (or bed slope $S_0$ to critical slope $S_c$):
1. **Mild Slope ($M$):** $S_0 < S_c \iff \mathbf{y_n > y_c}$. Normal flow is **subcritical (tranquil)**. Upstream disturbances can propagate downstream. This is the normal design regime for irrigation canals.
2. **Critical Slope ($C$):** $S_0 = S_c \iff \mathbf{y_n = y_c}$. Normal flow is **critical** ($Fr = 1.0$). Flow is inherently unstable with undulating surface waves.
3. **Steep Slope ($S$):** $S_0 > S_c \iff \mathbf{y_n < y_c}$. Normal flow is **supercritical (rapid / shooting)**. Control is located upstream.
4. **Horizontal Slope ($H$):** $S_0 = 0$. Since $\sqrt{S_0} = 0$, $y_n \to \infty$; normal depth **cannot exist**.
5. **Adverse Slope ($A$):** $S_0 < 0$ (channel rises in flow direction). Normal flow is physically impossible.

| Slope Class | Symbol | Depth Criterion | Slope Criterion | Flow Regime at Normal Depth |
| :--- | :---: | :---: | :---: | :--- |
| **Mild** | $M$ | $y_n > y_c$ | $S_0 < S_c$ | Subcritical ($Fr < 1$) |
| **Critical** | $C$ | $y_n = y_c$ | $S_0 = S_c$ | Critical ($Fr = 1$) |
| **Steep** | $S$ | $y_n < y_c$ | $S_0 > S_c$ | Supercritical ($Fr > 1$) |
| **Horizontal** | $H$ | $y_n = \infty$ | $S_0 = 0$ | None (Non-uniform only) |
| **Adverse** | $A$ | Does not exist | $S_0 < 0$ | None (Decelerating flow) |

### Example 14.1: Multi-Iteration Newton-Raphson Solution for Normal Depth
**Problem:** A trapezoidal canal carries a discharge of $Q = 30.0 \text{ m}^3/\text{s}$. The canal has a bed width of $B = 5.0 \text{ m}$, side slopes of $1\text{V} : 1.5\text{H}$ ($m = 1.5$), longitudinal bed slope $S_0 = 0.0016$ ($1 \text{ in } 625$), and Manning's roughness coefficient $n = 0.020$.
Compute the normal depth $y_n$ using the Newton-Raphson iterative method to 3 decimal places.

:::sol Step-by-Step Iteration
1. **Target Section Factor:**
   $$Z_u = \frac{Q \cdot n}{\sqrt{S_0}} = \frac{30.0 \times 0.020}{\sqrt{0.0016}} = \frac{0.600}{0.040} = \mathbf{15.000}$$
   We must solve: $f(y) = A(y) [R(y)]^{2/3} - 15.000 = 0$.

2. **Geometric Formulas:**
   - $A = (5 + 1.5y)y$
   - $P = 5 + 2y\sqrt{1 + 1.5^2} = 5 + 2y\sqrt{3.25} = 5 + 3.6056 y$
   - $T = 5 + 3.0 y$
   - $R = A / P$
   - $f'(y) \approx \frac{5}{3} T R^{2/3} - \frac{2}{3} R^{5/3} (3.6056)$

3. **Iteration 1:**
   - Guess $y_1 = 2.00 \text{ m}$:
     $$A_1 = (5 + 3.0)(2) = 16.0 \text{ m}^2, \quad P_1 = 5 + 3.6056(2) = 12.211 \text{ m}$$
     $$R_1 = 16.0 / 12.211 = 1.3103 \text{ m}, \quad R_1^{2/3} = 1.1974$$
     $$A_1 R_1^{2/3} = 16.0 \times 1.1974 = 19.158$$
     $$f(y_1) = 19.158 - 15.000 = +4.158$$
     $$T_1 = 5 + 3(2) = 11.0 \text{ m}$$
     $$f'(y_1) \approx \frac{5}{3}(11)(1.1974) - \frac{2}{3}(1.569)(3.6056) = 21.95 - 3.77 = 18.18$$
     $$y_2 = 2.00 - \frac{4.158}{18.18} = 2.00 - 0.229 = \mathbf{1.771 \text{ m}}$$

4. **Iteration 2:**
   - At $y_2 = 1.771 \text{ m}$:
     $$A_2 = (5 + 1.5 \times 1.771)(1.771) = (7.6565)(1.771) = 13.560 \text{ m}^2$$
     $$P_2 = 5 + 3.6056(1.771) = 11.386 \text{ m}$$
     $$R_2 = 13.560 / 11.386 = 1.1909 \text{ m}, \quad R_2^{2/3} = 1.1234$$
     $$A_2 R_2^{2/3} = 13.560 \times 1.1234 = 15.233$$
     $$f(y_2) = 15.233 - 15.000 = +0.233$$
     $$f'(y_2) \approx 16.8$$
     $$y_3 = 1.771 - \frac{0.233}{16.8} = 1.771 - 0.0139 = \mathbf{1.757 \text{ m}}$$

5. **Iteration 3 (Convergence):**
   - At $y_3 = 1.757 \text{ m}$:
     $$A_3 = 13.416 \text{ m}^2, \quad P_3 = 11.335 \text{ m}, \quad R_3 = 1.1836 \text{ m}$$
     $$A_3 R_3^{2/3} = 13.416 \times 1.1188 = \mathbf{15.009 \approx 15.000}$$
   **Normal Depth:** $\mathbf{y_n = 1.757 \text{ m}}$.
:::

### Example 14.2: Calculating Critical Slope ($S_c$) for a Channel Reach
**Problem:** A rectangular flume $B = 3.0 \text{ m}$ wide carries a discharge of $Q = 9.0 \text{ m}^3/\text{s}$ with Manning's $n = 0.015$.
1. Compute the critical depth $y_c$.
2. Determine the critical slope $S_c$.
3. If the channel is laid on a bed slope of $S_0 = 0.002$, classify the channel slope.

:::sol Step-by-Step Calculation
1. **Critical Depth $y_c$:**
   $$q = \frac{Q}{B} = \frac{9.0}{3.0} = 3.0 \text{ m}^2/\text{s}$$
   $$y_c = \sqrt[3]{\frac{q^2}{g}} = \sqrt[3]{\frac{3.0^2}{9.81}} = \sqrt[3]{\frac{9.0}{9.81}} = \sqrt[3]{0.9174} = \mathbf{0.9717 \text{ m}}$$

2. **Critical Slope $S_c$:**
   At critical flow, normal depth equals critical depth ($y_n = y_c = 0.9717 \text{ m}$):
   - $A_c = B y_c = 3.0 \times 0.9717 = 2.9151 \text{ m}^2$
   - $P_c = B + 2y_c = 3.0 + 2(0.9717) = 4.9434 \text{ m}$
   - $R_c = \frac{A_c}{P_c} = \frac{2.9151}{4.9434} = 0.5897 \text{ m}$
   - Critical slope from Manning's equation:
     $$S_c = \frac{n^2 Q^2}{A_c^2 R_c^{4/3}} = \frac{(0.015)^2 (9.0)^2}{(2.9151)^2 (0.5897)^{4/3}} = \frac{0.000225 \times 81.0}{8.4978 \times 0.4955} = \frac{0.018225}{4.2106} = \mathbf{0.004328} \quad (\mathbf{1 \text{ in } 231})$$

3. **Slope Classification:**
   - Actual bed slope: $S_0 = 0.0020$
   - Critical slope: $S_c = 0.00433$
   - Since $S_0 = 0.0020 < S_c = 0.00433$, the bed is **MILD ($M$)**, meaning $y_n > y_c$ and the normal flow is **subcritical**.
:::

### Real-World Engineering Modeling Nuances
1. **HEC-RAS Downstream Boundary Conditions:**
   When running 1D steady flow simulations in USACE HEC-RAS, engineers must specify the downstream boundary condition. If a physical rating curve or tidal stage is not known, the most common engineering choice is **Normal Depth**. The user inputs the friction slope $S_f$ (typically taken as the average reach bed slope $S_0$), and HEC-RAS solves Manning's equation at the final cross-section using Newton-Raphson to establish the starting water surface elevation.
2. **Newton-Raphson Numerical Blowup Prevention:**
   In automated hydraulics software, if the initial guess $y_0$ is chosen too close to zero, $f'(y)$ can produce a negative depth on the first iteration ($y_1 < 0$). Commercial engines implement a safety line-search or switch to bisection whenever an iteration step yields a negative or unphysical water depth.
