> [!note] Core Concept
> When a flat plate moves with velocity $u$ in the direction of the jet, the velocity of fluid relative to the plate is reduced to $(V - u)$. Distinguishing between a **single moving plate** (where mass striking the plate decreases with speed) and a **series of moving plates on a wheel** (where the entire jet is continuously intercepted) is critical for turbine engineering.

## 1. Single Moving Flat Plate
Let a vertical plate move forward at velocity $u$ away from the nozzle:
- Relative velocity reaching plate: $V_r = V - u$
- Mass of water striking plate per second: $\dot{m} = \rho a (V - u)$
- Dynamic force exerted:
  $$F_x = \dot{m}(V_r - 0) = \rho a (V - u)^2$$
- Work done per second:
  $$W = F_x \cdot u = \rho a (V - u)^2 u$$
- Efficiency (Kinetic energy of jet is $\frac{1}{2}\rho a V^3$):
  $$\eta = \frac{\rho a (V - u)^2 u}{\frac{1}{2}\rho a V^3} = \frac{2 u (V - u)^2}{V^3}$$
Setting $\frac{d\eta}{du} = 0$:
$$(V - u)^2 - 2u(V - u) = 0 \implies (V - u)(V - 3u) = 0 \implies u = \frac{V}{3}$$
Maximum theoretical efficiency for a single moving flat plate:
$$\eta_{max} = \frac{2 (V/3) (2V/3)^2}{V^3} = \frac{8}{27} = 29.63\%$$

## 2. Series of Flat Plates Mounted on a Wheel
When multiple plates are mounted around the periphery of a rotating wheel, as soon as one plate moves away, the next intercepts the jet. Therefore, **the entire discharge $Q = a V$ is utilized**:
- Mass flow rate: $\dot{m} = \rho a V$
- Force exerted: $F_x = \rho a V (V - u)$
- Work done per second: $W = F_x \cdot u = \rho a V u (V - u)$
- Efficiency:
  $$\eta = \frac{\rho a V u (V - u)}{\frac{1}{2}\rho a V^3} = \frac{2 u (V - u)}{V^2}$$
Setting $\frac{d\eta}{du} = 0 \implies V - 2u = 0 \implies u = \frac{V}{2}$:
$$\eta_{max} = \frac{2 (V/2) (V/2)}{V^2} = \frac{1}{2} = 50.0\%$$

### Example 22.1: Series of Plates vs Single Plate Comparison
**Problem:** A nozzle of diameter $d = 40 \text{ mm}$ produces a jet of velocity $V = 30.0 \text{ m/s}$. The vane speed is $u = 12.0 \text{ m/s}$.
1. Calculate the force, work done, and efficiency if a single plate moves away from the jet.
2. Calculate the force, work done, and efficiency if a series of plates are mounted on a wheel rim.

**Solution:**
1. **Jet Area & Mass Rate:**
   $$a = \frac{\pi}{4}(0.040)^2 = 1.2566 \times 10^{-3} \text{ m}^2$$
   $$KE_{jet} = \frac{1}{2}\rho a V^3 = 0.5 \times 1000 \times (1.2566 \times 10^{-3}) \times (30.0)^3 = 16,964 \text{ W} = 16.96 \text{ kW}$$
2. **Single Moving Plate:**
   $$V - u = 30.0 - 12.0 = 18.0 \text{ m/s}$$
   $$F_{single} = \rho a (V - u)^2 = 1000 \times (1.2566 \times 10^{-3}) \times (18.0)^2 = 407.1 \text{ N}$$
   $$W_{single} = F_{single} \times u = 407.1 \times 12.0 = 4,885 \text{ W} = 4.885 \text{ kW}$$
   $$\eta_{single} = \frac{4,885}{16,964} = 28.80\%$$
3. **Series of Plates:**
   $$F_{series} = \rho a V (V - u) = 1000 \times (1.2566 \times 10^{-3}) \times 30.0 \times 18.0 = 678.6 \text{ N}$$
   $$W_{series} = F_{series} \times u = 678.6 \times 12.0 = 8,143 \text{ W} = 8.143 \text{ kW}$$
   $$\eta_{series} = \frac{8,143}{16,964} = 48.00\%$$

### Key Takeaways
- Single plate: $\dot{m} = \rho a (V - u)$, $\eta_{max} = 29.63\%$ at $u = V/3$.
- Series of plates: $\dot{m} = \rho a V$, $\eta_{max} = 50.0\%$ at $u = V/2$.
