> [!note] Core Concept
> **Computational Fluid Dynamics (CFD)** is the branch of fluid mechanics that utilizes numerical analysis, data structures, and computer algorithms to solve and analyze problems involving fluid flows. In hydraulic engineering, CFD bridges the gap between simplified 1D analytical theory and expensive, physical scaled flume experiments.

## 1. The Three Pillars of Hydraulic Engineering
1. **Analytical / Theoretical Hydraulics:** 1D equations (Manning, specific energy, Bélanger) providing rapid closed-form solutions for simplified prismatic geometries.
2. **Experimental (Physical) Hydraulics:** Scaled physical models (flumes, spillway models) offering benchmark empirical measurements, subject to scale effects and measurement instrument limitations.
3. **Computational (Numerical) Hydraulics:** High-fidelity 2D/3D numerical simulations solving governing conservation equations over millions of spatial cells, offering comprehensive flow field visualization of velocity, pressure, turbulence, and scour.

## 2. Standard CFD Simulation Workflow
Every CFD simulation adheres to a rigorous three-stage pipeline:
1. **Pre-Processing:**
   - Defining computational geometry (CAD surfaces, LiDAR bathymetry DEM).
   - Mesh/Grid generation: subdividing the fluid domain into discrete control volumes.
   - Specifying fluid properties (density $\rho$, viscosity $\mu$, surface tension $\sigma$).
   - Imposing boundary conditions (inflow hydrograph, rating curves, wall roughness).
2. **Numerical Solution (Solver):**
   - Discretization of partial differential equations into algebraic matrix systems.
   - Iterative solution using pressure-velocity coupling schemes (SIMPLE, PISO, PIMPLE).
   - Monitoring convergence residuals and mass/momentum balances.
3. **Post-Processing:**
   - Extraction of engineering metrics: discharge rating curves, water surface profiles, pressure distributions.
   - Visualization: 2D vector plots, 3D streamlines, depth contours, and free surface animations.

### Example 37.1: Mesh Resolution and Computational Cell Count
**Problem:** A civil engineer is setting up a 2D hydrodynamic flood simulation for an $8.0 \text{ km}$ stretch of a river floodplain of average width $B = 400 \text{ m}$.
1. If a uniform square grid of $\Delta x = \Delta y = 10.0 \text{ m}$ is chosen, calculate the total number of computational cells.
2. If the grid is refined to $\Delta x = 2.0 \text{ m}$ to resolve urban levee crests, calculate the new cell count and estimate the increase in computational effort (assuming effort scales as $N_{cells} \times N_{timesteps} \propto (1/\Delta x)^3$).

**Solution:**
1. **Total Domain Area:**
   $$A = L \times B = 8,000 \text{ m} \times 400 \text{ m} = 3,200,000 \text{ m}^2 = 3.20 \text{ km}^2$$
   For $\Delta x = 10 \text{ m}$, cell area $A_{cell} = 10 \times 10 = 100 \text{ m}^2$:
   $$N_1 = \frac{A}{A_{cell}} = \frac{3,200,000}{100} = 32,000 \text{ cells}$$
2. **Refined Grid ($\Delta x = 2 \text{ m}$):**
   Cell area: $A_{cell2} = 2 \times 2 = 4 \text{ m}^2$:
   $$N_2 = \frac{3,200,000}{4} = 800,000 \text{ cells}$$
   Cell count increases by a factor of:
   $$\frac{N_2}{N_1} = \frac{800,000}{32,000} = 25 \times$$
3. **Increase in Computational Effort:**
   Because cell size is reduced by a factor of 5 ($10/2 = 5$), the Courant numerical stability condition requires time step $\Delta t$ to be reduced by a factor of 5 as well.
   $$\text{Total computational effort increase} = 25 \times 5 = 125 \text{ times}$$

### Key Takeaways
- Three pillars: Analytical, Experimental, Computational.
- Three CFD stages: Pre-processing (mesh/BCs) $	o$ Solver (discretization/iteration) $	o$ Post-processing.
- Refining grid by $k$ increases total 2D computational workload by $\approx k^3$.
