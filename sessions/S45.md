> [!note] Core Concept
> For complex 3D hydraulic phenomena—such as aerated spillway nappes, vortex ingestion at power intakes, and 3D turbulent rollers in stilling basins—2D depth-averaged models are insufficient. Engineers deploy **3D Multiphase CFD (Volume of Fluid - VOF)** using tools like **OpenFOAM / SimFlow (interFoam solver)** to track the sharp interface between air and water. Validating these simulations against physical laboratory flume data closes the loop of modern hydraulic design.

## 1. Volume of Fluid (VOF) Method
The VOF formulation treats water and air as a single continuous effective mixture whose properties vary with the liquid volume fraction $\alpha \in [0, 1]$:
- $\alpha = 1$: Cell is 100% water.
- $\alpha = 0$: Cell is 100% air.
- $0 < \alpha < 1$: Cell contains the free surface interface.

Effective density and dynamic viscosity:
$$\rho = \alpha \rho_{water} + (1 - \alpha) \rho_{air}$$
$$\mu = \alpha \mu_{water} + (1 - \alpha) \mu_{air}$$

Transport equation for phase fraction $\alpha$ (with artificial interface compression velocity $\mathbf{u}_r$ to keep the free surface razor-sharp):
$$\frac{\partial \alpha}{\partial t} + \nabla \cdot (\alpha \mathbf{u}) + \nabla \cdot (\alpha (1 - \alpha) \mathbf{u}_r) = 0$$

## 2. The Verification & Validation (V&V) Matrix
1. **1D Analytical Theory:** Rapid baseline calculations of normal depth, critical depth, and Bélanger conjugate depths.
2. **Laboratory Flume Benchmarks:** Physical measurements using point gauges, Pitot tubes, and electromagnetic velocity meters (Experiments L01–L11).
3. **2D Hydrodynamic Models (HEC-RAS):** Basin-wide water surface profiles and flood extent mapping.
4. **3D Multiphase CFD (SimFlow / OpenFOAM):** Detailed 3D velocity vectors, dynamic wall pressures, and air-entrainment rollers.

### Example 45.1: Three-Way Model Validation of a Hydraulic Jump
**Problem:** A laboratory flume experiment (L10) has a supercritical inflow of depth $y_1 = 0.040 \text{ m}$ at velocity $V_1 = 3.20 \text{ m/s}$ in a rectangular flume of width $b = 0.30 \text{ m}$.
1. Calculate the theoretical sequent depth $y_{2\_theory}$ using Bélanger's equation.
2. In the physical flume, the point gauge measures downstream depth $y_{2\_lab} = 0.235 \text{ m}$.
3. In a 3D OpenFOAM VOF simulation, the post-processed free surface contour ($lpha = 0.5$) yields $y_{2\_cfd} = 0.241 \text{ m}$.
Compute the percentage error of the laboratory flume and CFD simulation relative to classical analytical theory.

**Solution:**
1. **Analytical Bélanger Solution:**
   $$Fr_1 = \frac{V_1}{\sqrt{g y_1}} = \frac{3.20}{\sqrt{9.81 \times 0.040}} = \frac{3.20}{\sqrt{0.3924}} = \frac{3.20}{0.6264} = 5.108$$
   $$y_{2\_theory} = \frac{y_1}{2}\left(\sqrt{1 + 8 Fr_1^2} - 1\right) = \frac{0.040}{2}\left(\sqrt{1 + 8(5.108)^2} - 1\right)$$
   $$y_{2\_theory} = 0.020 \times (\sqrt{1 + 208.73} - 1) = 0.020 \times (14.482 - 1) = 0.020 \times 13.482 = 0.2696 \text{ m}$$
2. **Laboratory Flume Comparison:**
   $$\text{Error}_{lab} = \frac{0.235 - 0.2696}{0.2696} \times 100\% = \frac{-0.0346}{0.2696} \times 100\% = -12.83\%$$
   *(Physical flume depth is slightly lower due to side wall boundary layer friction not accounted for in 1D theory).*
3. **CFD Simulation Comparison:**
   $$\text{Error}_{cfd} = \frac{0.241 - 0.2696}{0.2696} \times 100\% = \frac{-0.0286}{0.2696} \times 100\% = -10.61\%$$
   Relative difference between 3D CFD and Physical Laboratory data:
   $$\Delta = \frac{|0.241 - 0.235|}{0.235} \times 100\% = \frac{0.006}{0.235} \times 100\% = 2.55\%$$
   The 3D CFD simulation matches the physical laboratory flume measurement to **within 2.6%**, proving excellent model validation!

### Key Takeaways
- VOF tracks the air-water interface using liquid volume fraction $\alpha \in [0, 1]$.
- Properties blend smoothly: $\rho = \alpha \rho_w + (1-\alpha)\rho_a$.
- Integration of theory, flume benchmarks, and numerical CFD represents the pinnacle of modern hydraulic engineering practice.
