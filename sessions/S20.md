> [!note] Core Concept
> When a jet strikes a stationary plate, fluid is deflected along the surface without change in speed (neglecting friction). For a **normal plate**, fluid spreads symmetrically in all directions; for an **inclined plate**, fluid divides into two streams whose discharge ratio depends entirely on the plate inclination angle $\theta$.

## 1. Jet Striking a Normal Stationary Plate
Let a jet of area $a$ and velocity $V$ strike a flat plate held perpendicular to the jet ($90^\circ$):
- Mass flow hitting plate: $\dot{m} = \rho a V$
- Initial velocity in $x$-direction: $V_{1x} = V$
- Final velocity in $x$-direction: $V_{2x} = 0$ (deflected along $y$ and $z$)
$$F_x = \dot{m}(V_{1x} - V_{2x}) = \rho a V(V - 0) = \rho a V^2$$

## 2. Jet Striking an Inclined Stationary Plate
Consider a plate inclined at an angle $\theta$ to the axis of the incoming horizontal jet:
1. Normal Force to the Plate ($F_n$):
   Velocity component normal to plate initially is $V \sin\theta$; after deflection along the plate, velocity normal to plate is zero:
   $$F_n = \dot{m}(V \sin\theta - 0) = \rho a V^2 \sin\theta$$
2. Components in $x$ and $y$ Directions:
   $$F_x = F_n \sin\theta = \rho a V^2 \sin^2\theta$$
   $$F_y = F_n \cos\theta = \rho a V^2 \sin\theta \cos\theta$$
3. Division of Discharge ($Q_1$ and $Q_2$):
   Applying momentum conservation parallel to the plate (where no external force acts):
   $$\rho Q V \cos\theta = \rho Q_1 V - \rho Q_2 V \implies Q_1 - Q_2 = Q \cos\theta$$
   Combined with continuity $Q_1 + Q_2 = Q$:
   $$Q_1 = \frac{Q}{2}(1 + \cos\theta) \quad \text{(forward flow)}$$
   $$Q_2 = \frac{Q}{2}(1 - \cos\theta) \quad \text{(backward flow)}$$

### Example 20.1: Discharge Division on an Inclined Plate
**Problem:** A water jet of diameter $d = 50 \text{ mm}$ moving at $V = 20.0 \text{ m/s}$ strikes a smooth flat plate inclined at $\theta = 30^\circ$ to the jet axis. Calculate:
1. Normal force $F_n$, horizontal force $F_x$, and vertical force $F_y$.
2. Discharges $Q_1$ and $Q_2$ in the two deflected streams.

**Solution:**
1. **Jet Area & Total Discharge:**
   $$a = \frac{\pi}{4}(0.050)^2 = 1.9635 \times 10^{-3} \text{ m}^2$$
   $$Q = a V = (1.9635 \times 10^{-3}) \times 20.0 = 0.03927 \text{ m}^3/\text{s} = 39.27 \text{ L/s}$$
   $$\dot{m} = \rho Q = 1000 \times 0.03927 = 39.27 \text{ kg/s}$$
2. **Force Components:**
   $$F_n = \rho a V^2 \sin\theta = 39.27 \times 20.0 \times \sin(30^\circ) = 785.4 \times 0.50 = 392.7 \text{ N}$$
   $$F_x = F_n \sin\theta = 392.7 \times 0.50 = 196.35 \text{ N}$$
   $$F_y = F_n \cos\theta = 392.7 \times \cos(30^\circ) = 392.7 \times 0.8660 = 340.08 \text{ N}$$
3. **Division of Discharge:**
   $$Q_1 = \frac{Q}{2}(1 + \cos(30^\circ)) = \frac{39.27}{2}(1 + 0.8660) = 19.635 \times 1.8660 = 36.64 \text{ L/s}$$
   $$Q_2 = \frac{Q}{2}(1 - \cos(30^\circ)) = \frac{39.27}{2}(1 - 0.8660) = 19.635 \times 0.1340 = 2.63 \text{ L/s}$$
   *(93.3% of flow proceeds forward; 6.7% deflects backward)*

### Key Takeaways
- Normal plate: $F_x = \rho a V^2$.
- Inclined plate: $F_n = \rho a V^2 \sin\theta$, $F_x = \rho a V^2 \sin^2\theta$.
- Discharge division: $Q_1 = \frac{Q}{2}(1+\cos\theta)$, $Q_2 = \frac{Q}{2}(1-\cos\theta)$.
