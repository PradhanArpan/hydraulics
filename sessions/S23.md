> [!note] Core Concept
> Replacing flat plates with curved vanes on a rotating wheel enables the jet to be deflected through large angles (up to $165^\circ - 170^\circ$). Under optimum speed ratio, this configuration can extract **nearly 100% of the jet's kinetic energy**, which is the fundamental basis of the **Pelton wheel turbine**.

## 1. Jet Striking a Series of Curved Vanes
Consider a series of curved vanes moving at tangential velocity $u$:
- Nozzle discharge: $Q = a V$, so mass striking per second is $\dot{m} = \rho a V$
- Relative velocity entering vane: $V_{r1} = V - u$
- Neglecting friction along vane, relative exit velocity is $V_{r2} = V_{r1} = (V - u)$
- Deflection angle: $\theta$ (or exit vane angle $\beta_2 = 180^\circ - \theta$)

Force in direction of motion ($x$-direction):
$$F_x = \dot{m}[V_{r1} - (-V_{r2} \cos\beta_2)] = \rho a V (V - u)(1 + \cos\beta_2)$$
Work done per second:
$$W = F_x \cdot u = \rho a V u (V - u)(1 + \cos\beta_2)$$
Kinetic energy of jet:
$$KE = \frac{1}{2}\rho a V^3$$
Hydraulic efficiency:
$$\eta = \frac{W}{KE} = \frac{2 u (V - u)(1 + \cos\beta_2)}{V^2}$$

## 2. Condition for Maximum Efficiency
Differentiating with respect to blade velocity $u$:
$$\frac{d\eta}{du} = \frac{2 (1 + \cos\beta_2)}{V^2} (V - 2u) = 0 \implies u = \frac{V}{2}$$
Maximum theoretical efficiency:
$$\eta_{max} = \frac{1 + \cos\beta_2}{2}$$
For a **semicircular bucket** ($\beta_2 = 0^\circ$, deflection $180^\circ$):
$$\eta_{max} = \frac{1 + 1}{2} = 1.00 = 100\%$$

> [!important] Real-World Limit
> In actual Pelton buckets, $\beta_2$ is designed around $10^\circ - 15^\circ$ (deflection $165^\circ - 170^\circ$) so that exiting water clears the oncoming bucket without splashing against its back, achieving real hydraulic efficiencies of $88\% - 93\%$.

### Example 23.1: Power and Efficiency of Curved Vanes on a Wheel
**Problem:** A water jet $50 \text{ mm}$ in diameter moving at $V = 40.0 \text{ m/s}$ strikes a series of curved vanes mounted on a wheel rim. The vane deflection angle is $165^\circ$ (bucket angle $\beta_2 = 15^\circ$), and the wheel peripheral speed is $u = 18.0 \text{ m/s}$. Due to blade friction, $V_{r2} = 0.90 V_{r1}$. Calculate:
1. Dynamic force $F_x$ on the wheel.
2. Power developed by the wheel in kilowatts.
3. Hydraulic efficiency $\eta$.

**Solution:**
1. **Jet Area & Mass Rate:**
   $$a = \frac{\pi}{4}(0.050)^2 = 1.9635 \times 10^{-3} \text{ m}^2$$
   $$\dot{m} = \rho a V = 1000 \times (1.9635 \times 10^{-3}) \times 40.0 = 78.54 \text{ kg/s}$$
   $$KE_{jet} = \frac{1}{2} \dot{m} V^2 = 0.5 \times 78.54 \times (40.0)^2 = 62,832 \text{ W} = 62.83 \text{ kW}$$
2. **Relative Velocities:**
   $$V_{r1} = V - u = 40.0 - 18.0 = 22.0 \text{ m/s}$$
   $$V_{r2} = 0.90 V_{r1} = 0.90 \times 22.0 = 19.80 \text{ m/s}$$
3. **Dynamic Force $F_x$:**
   $$F_x = \dot{m}[V_{r1} - (-V_{r2} \cos(15^\circ))] = 78.54 [22.0 + 19.80 \times 0.9659] = 78.54 [22.0 + 19.125] = 78.54 \times 41.125 = 3230 \text{ N}$$
4. **Power Developed:**
   $$P = F_x \cdot u = 3230 \times 18.0 = 58,140 \text{ W} = 58.14 \text{ kW}$$
5. **Hydraulic Efficiency:**
   $$\eta = \frac{P}{KE_{jet}} = \frac{58.14}{62.83} = 92.54\%$$

### Key Takeaways
- Work done: $W = \rho a V u (V - u)(1 + k \cos\beta_2)$.
- Maximum efficiency condition: $u = V/2$.
- Semicircular frictionless vane theoretical $\eta_{max} = 100\%$.
