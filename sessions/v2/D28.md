## Taming a runaway wheel

Imagine a massive $50$ tonne steel Pelton runner spinning at $500$ rpm, hit by high-speed water jets under $500$ metres of head. Suddenly, a lightning strike trips a major city transmission line!

With the electrical load gone, what prevents the giant wheel from accelerating wildly until centrifugal forces tear the steel disc apart?

## Anatomy & Governing of a Pelton Wheel

A Pelton wheel is a pure **impulse turbine** operating at constant atmospheric pressure.

### 1. The Four Essential Components
1. **Nozzle and Spear (Needle Valve):**
   - A conical needle moves axially inside the nozzle tip.
   - Pushing the needle forward reduces nozzle exit area and throttles discharge without changing the jet exit velocity $V_1 = C_v \sqrt{2gH}$!
2. **Runner with Double-Hemispherical Buckets:**
   - Circular disc forged from high-tensile steel or stainless steel alloy.
   - Each bucket has a sharp central ridge called the **splitter** that splits the jet into two equal halves, turning them backward through $\approx 165^\circ$.
3. **Casing:**
   - Cast-iron or fabricated steel hood that prevents splashing and guides spent water into the tailrace.
   - Does **not** run under pressure (vented to atmosphere).
4. **Breaking Jet:**
   - A small auxiliary nozzle aimed at the backs of the buckets, used to bring the massive rotor to a standstill during shutdowns.

### 2. The Governing Mechanism
Electrical grids require constant alternating current frequency ($50$ Hz in India, requiring constant shaft rpm: $N = \frac{120 f}{P_{\text{poles}}}$).

When the electrical load changes:
- **Centrifugal Governor & Pilot Valve:** Senses speed changes and directs high-pressure oil to a double-acting servomotor piston.
- **Deflector Plate Action (Instantaneous):**
  - If load suddenly drops, the deflector plate pivots into the jet within **$0.1$ seconds**, slicing the jet away from the buckets into the tailrace.
  - This prevents turbine over-speeding instantly.
- **Spear Needle Closure (Gradual):**
  - Over the next **$10$ to $30$ seconds**, an oil dashpot slowly drives the spear needle forward to throttle discharge, protecting the long penstock from water hammer!

## Why water hammer dictates two-stage governing

Click through the physical reason why a deflector plate is mandatory.

```steps
say: A penstock contains a massive column of moving water of length L and speed V_p.
eq: M = \rho\,A_p\,L \quad (\text{often thousands of tonnes of water})
why: Mass equals density times cross-sectional area times penstock pipe length.
---
say: If this water column is stopped in time t_c, Joukowsky's law gives the water hammer pressure surge.
eq: \Delta p = \rho\,C_s\,V_p \quad \text{for rapid closure } (t_c < 2L/C_s)
why: Pressure spike is proportional to water density, acoustic wave speed Cs (~1200 m/s), and flow velocity.
---
say: A 2 m/s velocity stopped rapidly produces over 24 atmospheres (240 m) of extra pressure!
eq: \Delta H_{\text{surge}} = \frac{C_s\,V_p}{g} \approx \frac{1200 \times 2}{9.81} \approx 245\ \text{m of extra burst head}
why: Such massive shock waves would rupture steel penstocks and demolish powerhouses.
---
say: The governor solves this by splitting the action into two independent systems.
eq: \text{Deflector: } t_{\text{deflector}} \approx 0.1\ \text{s (protects runner)} \quad \text{vs} \quad \text{Spear: } t_{\text{spear}} \approx 20\ \text{s (protects penstock)}
why: The deflector saves the generator from runaway speed, while slow spear movement avoids penstock water hammer.
```

## Multi-jet Pelton arrangement

> [!note] Problem
> A Pelton wheel is required to develop $9000$ kW under a net head of $300$ m at a speed of $500$ rpm.
> The overall efficiency is $\eta_o = 86\%$, coefficient of velocity is $C_v = 0.98$, and speed ratio is $\phi = 0.46$.
> 1. Find the total discharge $Q$ and runner pitch diameter $D$.
> 2. Determine the jet diameter $d$ and jet ratio $m$ if a single nozzle is used.
> 3. If standard design requires jet ratio $m \ge 11$, determine the number of nozzles $n_j$ required.

**Step 1. Total discharge required:**
$$P = \eta_o\,\rho\,g\,Q\,H \implies Q = \frac{P}{\eta_o\,\rho\,g\,H}$$
$$Q = \frac{9{,}000{,}000}{0.86 \times 1000 \times 9.81 \times 300} = \frac{9{,}000{,}000}{2{,}530{,}980} = 3.556\ \text{m}^3/\text{s}$$

**Step 2. Jet velocity and runner diameter:**
$$V_1 = C_v \sqrt{2gH} = 0.98 \times \sqrt{2 \times 9.81 \times 300} = 0.98 \times \sqrt{5886} = 0.98 \times 76.72 = 75.19\ \text{m}/\text{s}$$
$$u = \phi \sqrt{2gH} = 0.46 \times 76.72 = 35.29\ \text{m}/\text{s}$$
$$u = \frac{\pi\,D\,N}{60} \implies D = \frac{60\,u}{\pi\,N} = \frac{60 \times 35.29}{\pi \times 500} = \frac{2117.4}{1570.8} = 1.348\ \text{m}$$

**Step 3. Single jet diameter and jet ratio:**
$$\text{Total nozzle area } a_t = \frac{Q}{V_1} = \frac{3.556}{75.19} = 0.04729\ \text{m}^2$$
For a single jet ($n_j = 1$):
$$d_1 = \sqrt{\frac{4 \times a_t}{\pi}} = \sqrt{\frac{4 \times 0.04729}{\pi}} = \sqrt{0.06021} = 0.2454\ \text{m} = 245.4\ \text{mm}$$
$$m_1 = \frac{D}{d_1} = \frac{1.348}{0.2454} = 5.49$$
Since $m = 5.49 < 11$, a single jet is far too fat! Buckets would be excessively huge and crowd the rim.

**Step 4. Determine number of nozzles $n_j$:**
To achieve $m \approx 12$:
$$n_j = \left(\frac{m_{\text{target}}}{m_1}\right)^2 = \left(\frac{12}{5.49}\right)^2 = (2.185)^2 = 4.77 \to \text{Choose } n_j = 4\ \text{nozzles}$$
For 4 nozzles:
$$d = \frac{d_1}{\sqrt{4}} = \frac{245.4}{2} = 122.7\ \text{mm} = 0.1227\ \text{m}$$
$$m = \frac{D}{d} = \frac{1.348}{0.1227} = 10.99 \approx 11.0 \quad \text{(Perfect!)}$$

> [!note] What to take away
> Using a 4-nozzle runner kept the wheel diameter compact ($1.35$ m) while dividing the massive $3.55$ m³/s river flow into four easily handled $123$ mm jets!

```quiz
a: 2
why: Because V_1 = C_v * sqrt(2gH) depends solely on net head H, moving the needle alters the flow area and discharge Q without degrading the jet speed.

why: Due to enormous rotational inertia and low bearing friction, a freewheeling Pelton runner can take hours to coast to a stop unless a breaking jet is applied.
```

## Three things to remember

1. **Spear valve throttles discharge:** Needle movement controls flow rate $Q$ while preserving full jet speed $V_1$.
2. **Two-stage governing:** Deflector plate diverts jet instantly ($0.1$ s) to prevent over-speed, while spear needle closes slowly ($20$ s) to prevent penstock burst.
3. **Multi-jet rule:** When single jet yields jet ratio $m = D/d < 10$, add nozzles ($2, 4,$ or $6$) to maintain ideal runner proportions.
