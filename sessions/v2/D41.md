## Water running against the boundary

Imagine you build a scaled plastic model of a 5 km river reach.
You pour water into the upstream end from a hose.
At the downstream end, you place an adjustable tailgate weir.

What dictates the water surface elevation inside your model flume?
Does the water level depend purely on the flow rate from your hose?
Or does the downstream weir hold back the water and dictate the depth?

## Mathematical Well-Posedness & Boundary Types

A numerical problem is said to be **well-posed** in the sense of Hadamard if:
1. A solution **exists**.
2. The solution is **unique**.
3. The solution depends **continuously** on the boundary and initial data.

If you specify too few boundary conditions, the model is **under-specified** (infinite solutions).
If you specify too many, the model is **over-specified** (no solution exists; severe numerical oscillations occur).

### The Method of Characteristics Rule
The 2D Shallow Water Equations have characteristic wave speeds:
$$\lambda_1 = u + c, \quad \lambda_2 = u - c \quad \text{where } c = \sqrt{gy}$$

| Flow Regime | Froude Number ($Fr$) | Inflow Boundary Requirements | Outflow Boundary Requirements |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Subcritical** | $Fr < 1$ ($u < c$) | **1 Condition** (Discharge $Q(t)$ or velocity) | **1 Condition** (Water surface stage $z(t)$ or rating curve) |
| **Supercritical** | $Fr > 1$ ($u > c$) | **2 Conditions** (Discharge $Q$ **and** depth $y$) | **0 Conditions** (Flow exits freely; zero downstream feedback) |

> [!warning] Common modeling mistake
> Specifying both discharge $Q$ and water depth $y$ at the upstream boundary of a subcritical river creates artificial shockwaves that bounce off the boundary and crash the solver.

## How boundary fluxes enter the finite volume equations

Follow how incoming boundary characteristics determine boundary equations.

```steps
say: Write the 1D shallow water characteristic equations along characteristic paths dx/dt = u ± c.
eq: \frac{d}{dt}(u \pm 2c) = g(S_0 - S_f)
why: Riemann invariants u + 2c and u - 2c propagate along the characteristic wave rays.
---
say: In subcritical flow where u < c, the positive wave ray dx/dt = u + c travels downstream, while the negative ray dx/dt = u - c travels upstream.
eq: \lambda_+ = u + c > 0, \quad \lambda_- = u - c < 0
why: One wave enters from upstream, and one wave enters from downstream!
---
say: At the upstream inflow boundary, only one physical characteristic enters the domain.
eq: Q(t) = \int u\,dA \quad \text{is prescribed from hydrology}
why: The second variable (depth y) must be calculated from inside the domain along the exiting negative characteristic!
---
say: At the downstream outflow boundary, the negative wave enters the domain from downstream.
eq: z(t) = z_{\text{bed}} + y_0 \quad \text{or} \quad Q = K \cdot S_f^{1/2}
why: Setting a stage hydrograph or normal depth friction slope provides the required incoming information.
```

## Sizing downstream friction slope boundary

> [!note] Problem
> A flood wave enters a rectangular concrete river channel ($b = 20\text{ m}$, $n = 0.015$, bed slope $S_0 = 0.0009$).
> The design peak discharge is $Q = 140\text{ m}^3/\text{s}$.
> 1. Determine whether flow is subcritical or supercritical at normal depth.
> 2. Specify the exact boundary conditions required at the upstream and downstream ends of the numerical reach.
> 3. Calculate normal depth $y_0$ to set the downstream friction slope boundary.

**Step 1. Compute normal depth $y_0$:**
Using Manning's equation for a rectangular channel:
$$Q = \frac{1}{n} A R^{2/3} S_0^{1/2}$$
$$140 = \frac{1}{0.015} (20 y_0) \left(\frac{20 y_0}{20 + 2y_0}\right)^{2/3} (0.0009)^{1/2}$$
$$140 = 40 y_0 \left(\frac{20 y_0}{20 + 2y_0}\right)^{2/3} \implies y_0 \left(\frac{20 y_0}{20 + 2y_0}\right)^{2/3} = 3.50$$

Solving iteratively gives:
$$y_0 \approx 2.30\text{ m}$$
Checking conveyance:
$$A = 46.0\text{ m}^2, \quad P = 24.6\text{ m}, \quad R = 1.870\text{ m}$$
$$Q = \frac{1}{0.015} (46.0) (1.870)^{2/3} (0.03) \approx 140.0\text{ m}^3/\text{s}$$

**Step 2. Check Froude number $Fr$:**
$$V = \frac{Q}{A} = \frac{140}{46.0} = 3.043\text{ m/s}$$
$$c = \sqrt{g y_0} = \sqrt{9.81 \times 2.30} = 4.750\text{ m/s}$$
$$Fr = \frac{V}{c} = \frac{3.043}{4.750} = 0.640 < 1.0 \quad \text{(Subcritical flow!)}$$

**Step 3. Determine required boundary conditions:**
- **Upstream:** Specify **1 condition** $\to$ Flow Hydrograph $Q(t)$ ($Q_{\text{peak}} = 140\text{ m}^3/\text{s}$).
- **Downstream:** Specify **1 condition** $\to$ Normal Depth Friction Slope $S_f = S_0 = 0.0009$ (or Stage $z = z_{\text{bed}} + 2.30\text{ m}$).

## Civil engineering connections

- **Tidal Estuary Modeling:** In coastal rivers, the downstream boundary is a dynamic ocean tide stage hydrograph $z(t) = z_{\text{mean}} + A \sin(\omega t)$. High tide sends backwater waves upstream, altering flood evacuation routes in low-lying delta cities.
- **Rating Curves at Gauging Stations:** When downstream water levels are unmeasured, engineers assign empirical stage-discharge rating curves $Q = C_r (z - z_0)^m$ established by Central Water Commission (CWC) streamflow monitoring stations.

<!--beat:recap|Recap-->
## Three things to remember

1. **Subcritical flow requires two ends:** 1 boundary condition upstream (flow $Q$) and 1 condition downstream (stage $z$ or normal depth slope $S_f$).
2. **Supercritical flow requires upstream only:** 2 boundary conditions upstream ($Q$ and $y$) and 0 conditions downstream.
3. **Avoid artificial reflections:** Place downstream boundaries far downstream of your actual project area of interest.
