From your point of view as a runner, the ball approaches at a relative angle!

## Anatomy of Velocity Vector Triangles

At any point on a moving turbine blade, fluid has three interconnected velocities related by vector addition:

$$\vec{V} = \vec{u} + \vec{V}_r$$

- **Absolute velocity $\vec{V}$ (m/s):** Velocity of water relative to the stationary ground (or nozzle).
- **Blade velocity $\vec{u}$ (m/s):** Linear peripheral speed of the rotating wheel blade.
- **Relative velocity $\vec{V}_r$ (m/s):** Velocity of water relative to the moving blade (what an observer standing on the blade measures).

### Resolving Absolute Velocity into Components
In cylindrical coordinates, absolute velocity $\vec{V}$ is resolved into two orthogonal components:
1. **Whirl Velocity $V_w$ (m/s):** The component parallel to blade motion (tangential direction). **Only whirl velocity produces useful torque and power!**
2. **Flow Velocity $V_f$ (m/s):** The component perpendicular to blade motion (radial or axial direction). Flow velocity carries fluid through the machine.

### Angles to Know
- $\alpha$: Angle between absolute velocity $\vec{V}$ and blade motion direction $\vec{u}$.
- $\theta$: Inlet blade angle (angle of relative velocity $\vec{V}_{r1}$ with $\vec{u}$).
- $\beta$: Outlet blade angle (angle of relative velocity $\vec{V}_{r2}$ with direction opposite to $\vec{u}$).

### Work Done & Hydraulic Efficiency (Euler Precursor)
For a series of curved vanes moving in the direction of the jet:

$$F_x = \rho\,Q\,(V_{w1} + V_{w2})$$

$$P = F_x \cdot u = \rho\,Q\,(V_{w1} + V_{w2})\,u$$

$$\eta_h = \frac{\rho\,Q\,(V_{w1} + V_{w2})\,u}{\frac{1}{2}\rho\,Q\,V_1^2} = \frac{2\,(V_{w1} + V_{w2})\,u}{V_1^2}$$

*In words: force equals mass flow rate times total change in whirl velocity.*

*(Note: $(V_{w1} + V_{w2})$ is used when $V_{w2}$ is directed opposite to blade motion $u$, which adds momentum transfer).*

## Derive work done per unit mass flow

Click through the steps to see how velocity triangles yield Euler's work equation.

```steps
say: Write Newton's second law for tangential driving force on the moving runner blades.
eq: F_x = \dot{m}\,(V_{x,\text{in}} - V_{x,\text{out}})
why: Net horizontal force equals rate of momentum destruction in the tangential direction.
---
say: In turbomachinery, the tangential component of absolute velocity is defined as whirl velocity Vw.
eq: V_{x,\text{in}} = V_{w1}, \quad V_{x,\text{out}} = -V_{w2}
why: Whirl velocity is by definition the projection of absolute velocity in the direction of blade motion.
---
say: Substitute into the force equation.
eq: F_x = \dot{m}\,[V_{w1} - (-V_{w2})] = \rho\,Q\,(V_{w1} + V_{w2})
why: Reversing the tangential whirl velocity adds to the forward impulse on the blade.
---
say: Power developed equals force times blade speed u.
eq: P = F_x \cdot u = \rho\,Q\,(V_{w1} + V_{w2})\,u
why: Mechanical work done per second equals driving force times travel velocity.
---
say: Divide power by mass flow rate rho * Q to get work done per kilogram of water.
eq: w = \frac{P}{\rho\,Q} = (V_{w1} + V_{w2})\,u
why: This is the tangential form of Euler's turbine equation: work depends entirely on whirl velocities!
```

## Velocity triangle analysis of a moving curved vane

> [!note] Problem
> A jet of water from a nozzle of diameter $d = 50$ mm with velocity $V_1 = 30$ m/s enters tangentially ($\alpha_1 = 0^\circ$) onto a series of curved vanes moving at $u = 14$ m/s.
> The vane deflects the water so that the relative velocity leaves at an angle $\beta_2 = 25^\circ$ to the direction of motion. Vane friction reduces relative velocity by $10\%$ ($k = 0.90$). Calculate:
> 1. Relative velocity at inlet $V_{r1}$ and outlet $V_{r2}$
> 2. Whirl velocity at outlet $V_{w2}$ and absolute exit speed $V_2$
> 3. Driving force $F_x$ on the runner
> 4. Power developed in kW and hydraulic efficiency $\eta_h$

**Step 1. Inlet velocity triangle:**
Since the jet enters tangentially ($\alpha_1 = 0^\circ$):
$$V_{w1} = V_1 = 30\ \text{m}/\text{s}$$
$$V_{r1} = V_1 - u = 30 - 14 = 16\ \text{m}/\text{s}$$

**Step 2. Outlet velocity triangle:**
Relative exit velocity with friction:
$$V_{r2} = k \cdot V_{r1} = 0.90 \times 16 = 14.4\ \text{m}/\text{s}$$
Resolving $V_{r2}$ along blade direction:
$$\beta_2 = 25^\circ \implies \cos(25^\circ) = 0.9063, \quad \sin(25^\circ) = 0.4226$$
$$V_{r2x} = V_{r2} \cos\beta_2 = 14.4 \times 0.9063 = 13.05\ \text{m}/\text{s} \quad (\text{directed backward})$$
$$V_{f2} = V_{r2} \sin\beta_2 = 14.4 \times 0.4226 = 6.09\ \text{m}/\text{s} \quad (\text{directed upward})$$

Whirl velocity at outlet:
$$V_{w2} = V_{r2} \cos\beta_2 - u = 13.05 - 14.0 = -0.95\ \text{m}/\text{s}$$
*(Since $V_{r2x} < u$, the absolute exit water actually has a small residual forward velocity of $0.95$ m/s, so $V_{w1} + V_{w2} = 30 - 0.95 = 29.05$ m/s).*

Absolute exit speed:
$$V_2 = \sqrt{(V_{w2})^2 + (V_{f2})^2} = \sqrt{(0.95)^2 + (6.09)^2} = \sqrt{0.90 + 37.09} = 6.16\ \text{m}/\text{s}$$

**Step 3. Mass flow rate and driving force:**
$$a = \frac{\pi}{4}(0.050)^2 = 1.9635 \times 10^{-3}\ \text{m}^2$$
$$\dot{m} = \rho\,a\,V_1 = 1000 \times 1.9635 \times 10^{-3} \times 30 = 58.9\ \text{kg}/\text{s}$$
$$F_x = \dot{m}\,(V_{w1} + V_{w2}) = 58.9 \times (30 + (-0.95)) = 58.9 \times 29.05 = 1711\ \text{N} = 1.711\ \text{kN}$$

**Step 4. Power developed & hydraulic efficiency:**
$$P = F_x \cdot u = 1711 \times 14 = 23{,}954\ \text{W} = 23.95\ \text{kW}$$
$$P_{\text{jet}} = \frac{1}{2}\dot{m}\,V_1^2 = \frac{1}{2} \times 58.9 \times (30)^2 = 26{,}505\ \text{W} = 26.51\ \text{kW}$$
$$\eta_h = \frac{23.95}{26.51} = 0.904 = 90.4\%$$

> [!note] What to take away
> Curving the vane and recovering whirl momentum boosts hydraulic efficiency from $50\%$ (flat plate limit) to over $90\%$!

## Civil engineering connections

- **Pelton Runner Bucket Design:** Hydroelectric design engineers use velocity triangles to specify the bucket splitter angle and exit lip curl, ensuring exit water leaves with minimal kinetic energy ($V_2 \to \text{minimum}$) and zero back-splash.
- **Hydro-Turbine Upgrades:** When rehabilitating vintage hydropower plants, CFD-optimized velocity triangles allow retrofitting existing turbine wheels with modern curved runner profiles to gain $5$ to $8\%$ more electrical output from the same river head!

<!--beat:recap|Recap-->
## Three things to remember

1. **Velocity triangle relation:** Absolute velocity is vector sum of blade speed and relative velocity: $\vec{V} = \vec{u} + \vec{V}_r$.
2. **Whirl velocity rules power:** Power developed is $P = \rho Q (V_{w1} \pm V_{w2}) u$. Only the whirl component does work!
3. **Shockless entry:** Relative velocity $\vec{V}_r$ must match the physical blade inlet angle to prevent turbulent entrance losses.
