## Measuring a river without catching every drop

How do municipal water boards and irrigation engineers measure millions of litres flowing in a canal every hour? You cannot hold a bucket under a river.

```predict
q: A broad-crested concrete block is placed across a canal. By simply reading the water height $H$ on an upstream gauge rod, you can calculate the exact flow rate $Q$. Why is this single reading enough?
o: Because water moves at the same speed everywhere on Earth
o: Because water accelerates to critical depth ($Fr = 1$) over the crest, locking in a unique one-to-one head-to-discharge relation
o: Because the gauge rod floats
a: 2
why: Passing through critical depth creates a hydraulic control. At critical depth, discharge depends solely on the upstream energy head, so measuring one water level gives the exact discharge.
```

Today you will see how broad-crested weirs and Parshall flumes turn water depth into flow rate.

## Two primary open-channel flow meters

1. **Broad-Crested Weir:**
   A raised rectangular sill with a flat horizontal crest of length $L \ge 2.5 H$.
   Flow accelerates over the crest and passes through **critical depth $y_c = \frac{2}{3}H$**.

   $$Q = 1.705\,C_d\,b\,H^{3/2}$$

   *In words: discharge is proportional to crest width $b$ times head $H$ to the power $1.5$.*

2. **Parshall Flume:**
   A self-cleaning venturi flume with a contracting inlet, a depressed throat floor, and an expanding outlet.
   It operates with very small head loss, making it ideal for wastewater and sediment-laden irrigation canals.

   $$Q = C\,W\,H_a^n$$

   *In words: standard formula where throat width $W$ and exponent $n \approx 1.52$ to $1.60$ are specified by IS:14371 standards.*

## Derive the ideal broad-crested weir formula

Click through to derive the constant $1.705$.

```steps
say: Assume ideal flow with energy head $H$ measured from the weir crest.
eq: E_1 = H
why: Neglecting approach velocity head, the total energy above the crest is the measured head $H$.
---
say: Critical depth occurs on the flat crest. At critical flow, depth is two-thirds of specific energy.
eq: y_c = \frac{2}{3}E_1 = \frac{2}{3}H
why: From Day 12, $E_c = 1.5 y_c \implies y_c = E_c / 1.5 = \frac{2}{3}E_c$.
---
say: Critical velocity at this depth is $V_c = \sqrt{g\,y_c}$.
eq: V_c = \sqrt{g\left(\frac{2}{3}H\right)}
why: Froude number is exactly 1 at critical depth.
---
say: Discharge is area times velocity: $Q = b \cdot y_c \cdot V_c$.
eq: Q = b\left(\frac{2}{3}H\right)\sqrt{g\left(\frac{2}{3}H\right)} = \sqrt{g}\left(\frac{2}{3}\right)^{3/2} b\,H^{3/2}
why: Multiplying the width, critical depth, and critical speed gives total volume rate.
---
say: Calculate the numerical constant: $\sqrt{9.81} \times (2/3)^{1.5} = 3.132 \times 0.5443 = 1.705$.
eq: Q_{\text{ideal}} = 1.705\,b\,H^{3/2}
why: With a discharge coefficient $C_d \approx 0.95$, $Q = 1.705\,C_d\,b\,H^{3/2}$.
```

## Sizing flow over a broad-crested weir

> [!note] Problem
> A broad-crested weir is built across a rectangular channel with crest width $b = 2.40$ m. The measured upstream head above the crest is $H = 0.45$ m. If $C_d = 0.94$, compute the discharge $Q$ and the water depth on the crest $y_c$.

**Step 1. Depth on the weir crest.**
$$y_c = \frac{2}{3}H = \frac{2}{3} \times 0.45 = 0.30\ \text{m}$$

**Step 2. Compute discharge $Q$.**
$$Q = 1.705 \times C_d \times b \times H^{1.5}$$
$$Q = 1.705 \times 0.94 \times 2.40 \times (0.45)^{1.5}$$
$$(0.45)^{1.5} = 0.3019$$
$$Q = 1.705 \times 0.94 \times 2.40 \times 0.3019 = 1.162\ \text{m}^3/\text{s}$$

**Step 3. Check crest length requirement.**
For the weir to function strictly as broad-crested, the crest length $L$ in the flow direction must satisfy:
$$2.5\,H \le L \le 10\,H \implies 2.5(0.45) \le L \implies L \ge 1.125\ \text{m}$$
