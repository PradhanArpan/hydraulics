## When time moves too fast for water

Suppose you are watching a movie of a river in flood.
If the film projector runs at 24 frames per second, the water flows smoothly across the screen.
Now imagine the projector skips ahead by 10 minutes between every single frame.

What happens? A flood wave that was 2 km upstream in Frame 1 is suddenly past the entire city in Frame 2!
The physics in between was completely skipped.

```predict
q: If a computer simulation uses a time step that is too large, what happens to the mathematical solution?
o: The computer automatically slows down the real river
o: Numbers grow infinitely large, oscillating wildly until the solver crashes with NaN (Not a Number)
o: The simulation becomes 100% accurate
a: 2
why: When time steps exceed the Courant stability limit, numerical errors amplify exponentially every iteration, causing catastrophic instability.
```

In computer simulation, time does not flow continuously; it advances in discrete ticks called time steps ($\Delta t$).
If your time step is too large, a water wave will leap over entire grid cells without the computer calculating its pressure or momentum.
How fast can water move before your computational grid fails?

## The Courant-Friedrichs-Lewy (CFL) Condition

In 1928, Richard Courant, Kurt Friedrichs, and Hans Lewy proved the fundamental stability theorem for numerical solutions of hyperbolic partial differential equations.

### The CFL Stability Theorem
For an **explicit** numerical scheme to be stable, the numerical domain of dependence must contain the physical domain of dependence.

*In plain words: **Information cannot travel further than one grid cell in a single time step!***

$$Cr = \frac{(|u| + c)\,\Delta t}{\Delta x} \le Cr_{\max}$$

where:
- $u$ = Flow velocity ($\text{m/s}$).
- $c = \sqrt{gy}$ = Gravity wave celerity ($\text{m/s}$).
- $(|u| + c)$ = Total speed of the fastest physical disturbance.
- $\Delta t$ = Computational time step ($\text{s}$).
- $\Delta x$ = Grid cell dimension ($\text{m}$).
- $Cr$ = Courant number (dimensionless).

### Explicit vs. Implicit Numerical Solvers
- **Explicit Schemes:** Future values are computed directly from known current values. Strictly limited to $Cr \le 1.0$.
- **Implicit Schemes:** Future values are linked across adjacent cells through matrix equations. Unconditionally stable mathematically, but practically limited to $Cr \le 2.0 \text{ to } 5.0$ in HEC-RAS 2D to preserve wave steepness and arrival timing accuracy.

## Deriving the maximum stable time step

Step through how gravity wave physics dictates the computational clock.

```steps
say: Write the speed of the fastest physical signal traveling across an open-channel grid cell.
eq: V_{\text{wave}} = |u| + c = |u| + \sqrt{gy}
why: Physical gravity waves ride on top of the bulk water velocity.
---
say: In time delta t, the physical wave travels a distance delta x_physical.
eq: \Delta x_{\text{physical}} = (|u| + \sqrt{gy}) \Delta t
why: Distance equals speed multiplied by time.
---
say: For an explicit grid cell of length delta x, information must not bypass the cell.
eq: (|u| + \sqrt{gy}) \Delta t \le \Delta x
why: The numerical grid must catch the wave before it exits the cell!
---
say: Rearranging for the maximum allowable time step:
eq: \Delta t \le \frac{\Delta x}{|u| + \sqrt{gy}}
why: This is the Courant-Friedrichs-Lewy (CFL) explicit time step limit!
```

## Time step selection for a river reach

> [!note] Problem
> A 2D hydrodynamic river model has a minimum grid cell size of $\Delta x = 10\text{ m}$.
> During a peak flood event, maximum flow depth is $y = 4.0\text{ m}$ and maximum velocity is $u = 3.2\text{ m/s}$.
> 1. Calculate the shallow water wave celerity $c$.
> 2. Determine the maximum allowable time step $\Delta t_{\max}$ for an explicit numerical solver ($Cr_{\max} = 1.0$).
> 3. If an engineer sets $\Delta t = 15\text{ s}$, compute the Courant number and predict stability.
> 4. For an implicit HEC-RAS 2D model with target $Cr \le 2.0$, determine the safe time step.

**Step 1. Compute wave celerity $c$:**
$$c = \sqrt{gy} = \sqrt{9.81 \times 4.0} = \sqrt{39.24} = 6.264\text{ m/s}$$

**Step 2. Compute explicit time step limit ($Cr = 1.0$):**
$$V_{\text{wave}} = u + c = 3.2 + 6.264 = 9.464\text{ m/s}$$
$$\Delta t_{\max} = \frac{\Delta x}{V_{\text{wave}}} = \frac{10\text{ m}}{9.464\text{ m/s}} = 1.057\text{ s}$$
$$\therefore \text{Select an operational time step } \Delta t \le 1.0\text{ second (e.g. } 0.8\text{ s)}.$$

**Step 3. Evaluate $\Delta t = 15\text{ seconds}$:**
$$Cr = \frac{(u + c)\Delta t}{\Delta x} = \frac{9.464 \times 15}{10} = 14.20 \gg 1.0$$
The explicit model will blow up on the very first time step with severe floating-point overflow!

**Step 4. Safe time step for implicit solver ($Cr = 2.0$):**
$$\Delta t = \frac{Cr \cdot \Delta x}{u + c} = \frac{2.0 \times 10}{9.464} = 2.11\text{ s}$$
$$\therefore \text{Set } \Delta t = 2.0\text{ seconds in HEC-RAS 2D.}$$

## Civil engineering connections

- **Adaptive Time-Stepping in HEC-RAS:** Modern solvers continuously monitor the Courant number across all 200,000 cells in a basin. During dry periods, the solver steps forward at $\Delta t = 10\text{ s}$; when the flash flood wave hits, it automatically throttles down to $\Delta t = 1.0\text{ s}$ to maintain stability.
- **Urban Flood Modeling:** In street curb gutters, flow depth is very small ($y \approx 0.05\text{ m}$) but velocities are high ($u \approx 3\text{ m/s}$). These shallow curb cells often dictate the global time step of city stormwater models.

<!--beat:recap|Recap-->
## Three things to remember

1. **The CFL rule:** A wave must not travel more than one grid cell in a single time step ($Cr \le 1.0$ for explicit).
2. **Total wave speed is $u + \sqrt{gy}$:** Gravity waves travel faster than the flowing water itself.
3. **Implicit vs explicit:** HEC-RAS 2D implicit solvers operate reliably at $Cr \le 2.0 \text{ to } 5.0$, but excessive $\Delta t$ smears flood peaks.
