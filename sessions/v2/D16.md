## How far back does a dam flood?

When a barrage or weir is built across a river, the water level rises behind the structure. But the water does not form a horizontal swimming pool. It creates a long, curved surface that slopes gently upstream for kilometers.

```predict
q: A weir raises the river level by 3 metres. Does the water depth stay at normal depth right up to the weir, or does it gradually curve upward over several kilometers upstream?
o: Depth stays at normal depth until 1 metre before the weir, where it jumps vertically
o: Water gradually swells upward in a gentle curve that can extend for 10 to 50 kilometers upstream
o: The river dries out upstream
a: 2
why: In subcritical river flow, downstream backwater information travels upstream. The free surface curves smoothly upward in a backwater curve (M1 profile) over many kilometers.
```

## The GVF governing equation

The rate of change of water depth with distance along the canal $x$ is given by the dynamic equation of gradually varied flow:

$$\frac{dy}{dx} = \frac{S_0 - S_f}{1 - Fr^2}$$

*In words: the surface slope depends on the difference between bed slope and friction slope, divided by 1 minus Froude squared.*

### Slope classification (5 categories)

Compare normal depth $y_n$ with critical depth $y_c$:
1. **Mild Slope (M):** $y_n > y_c$ (Subcritical normal flow. Most natural rivers and irrigation canals).
2. **Steep Slope (S):** $y_n < y_c$ (Supercritical normal flow. Chutes and spillways).
3. **Critical Slope (C):** $y_n = y_c$ (Unstable surface).
4. **Horizontal Bed (H):** $S_0 = 0$ ($y_n = \infty$).
5. **Adverse Slope (A):** $S_0 < 0$ (Bed rises in flow direction, no real $y_n$).

### The 3 Zones of Flow

- **Zone 1:** Depth is above both $y_n$ and $y_c$ ($y > y_n$ and $y > y_c$). Always **backwater curve ($dy/dx > 0$)**.
- **Zone 2:** Depth lies between $y_n$ and $y_c$. Always **drawdown curve ($dy/dx < 0$)**.
- **Zone 3:** Depth is below both $y_n$ and $y_c$. Always **rising curve ($dy/dx > 0$)**.

> [!note] Hydraulic Control Direction
> - **Subcritical flow ($Fr < 1$):** Controlled from **downstream**. Compute profiles stepping from downstream to upstream!
> - **Supercritical flow ($Fr > 1$):** Controlled from **upstream**. Compute profiles stepping from upstream to downstream!

## The Direct Step Method formulation

Click through to see how we calculate the distance $\Delta x$ between two depths $y_1$ and $y_2$.

```steps
say: Write the total energy head $H = z + E$, where $z$ is bed elevation and $E$ is specific energy.
eq: H = z + y + \frac{V^2}{2g} = z + E
why: Total head equals bed elevation plus specific energy.
---
say: Differentiate with respect to channel distance $x$.
eq: \frac{dH}{dx} = \frac{dz}{dx} + \frac{dE}{dx} = -S_f
why: The rate of total head loss is the friction slope: $dH/dx = -S_f$, and bed slope is $dz/dx = -S_0$.
---
say: Equate the slopes: $-S_f = -S_0 + dE/dx \implies dE/dx = S_0 - S_f$.
eq: \frac{dE}{dx} = S_0 - S_f
why: The change in specific energy along the canal equals bed slope minus friction slope.
---
say: Convert the differential equation into finite steps $\Delta x$ between section 1 and 2.
eq: \Delta x = \frac{E_2 - E_1}{S_0 - \bar{S}_f}
why: This is the Direct Step Method formula.
---
say: The average friction slope $\bar{S}_f$ across the step is computed using Manning's equation at both ends.
eq: \bar{S}_f = \frac{S_{f1} + S_{f2}}{2} \quad \text{where} \quad S_f = \frac{n^2 V^2}{R^{4/3}}
why: Averaging friction slopes across small depth steps gives high numerical accuracy.
```

## Calculating backwater length behind a weir

> [!note] Problem
> A wide rectangular channel ($n = 0.02$, $S_0 = 0.0009$) carries $q = 3.0\ \text{m}^2/\text{s}$ at normal depth $y_n = 1.45$ m. A weir raises the water depth just upstream to $y_2 = 2.20$ m. Using one direct step, calculate how far upstream ($\Delta x$) the water depth reaches $y_1 = 1.90$ m.

**Step 1. Check slope class and control.**
- Critical depth: $y_c = (3.0^2/9.81)^{1/3} = (0.9174)^{1/3} = 0.972$ m.
- Since $y_n = 1.45 > y_c = 0.972$, the slope is **Mild (M)**.
- Both depths (1.90 m and 2.20 m) are above $y_n$, so this is an **M1 profile** in Zone 1.
- Flow is subcritical, so computation proceeds **upstream** from $y_2 = 2.20$ m to $y_1 = 1.90$ m.

**Step 2. Section 2 ($y_2 = 2.20$ m, weir end).**
- $V_2 = q / y_2 = 3.0 / 2.20 = 1.364$ m/s.
- $E_2 = y_2 + V_2^2/(2g) = 2.20 + 1.364^2 / 19.62 = 2.20 + 0.0948 = 2.295$ m.
- Wide channel ($R_2 \approx y_2 = 2.20$ m):
  $$S_{f2} = \frac{n^2 V_2^2}{R_2^{4/3}} = \frac{0.0004 \times 1.860}{(2.20)^{1.333}} = \frac{0.000744}{2.864} = 0.000260$$

**Step 3. Section 1 ($y_1 = 1.90$ m, upstream end).**
- $V_1 = q / y_1 = 3.0 / 1.90 = 1.579$ m/s.
- $E_1 = y_1 + V_1^2/(2g) = 1.90 + 1.579^2 / 19.62 = 1.90 + 0.127 = 2.027$ m.
- $S_{f1} = \dfrac{0.0004 \times 2.493}{(1.90)^{1.333}} = \dfrac{0.000997}{2.348} = 0.000425$.

**Step 4. Compute reach length $\Delta x$.**
- $\bar{S}_f = (0.000260 + 0.000425) / 2 = 0.0003425$.
- $S_0 - \bar{S}_f = 0.000900 - 0.0003425 = 0.0005575$.
- $E_2 - E_1 = 2.295 - 2.027 = 0.268$ m.
$$\Delta x = \frac{E_2 - E_1}{S_0 - \bar{S}_f} = \frac{0.268}{0.0005575} \approx 481\ \text{m}$$

> [!note] What to take away
> The backwater swell extends nearly half a kilometer upstream just to drop from 2.20 m to 1.90 m.

## Remember

> [!note] Day 16 in five lines
> - GVF dynamic equation: $\dfrac{dy}{dx} = \dfrac{S_0 - S_f}{1 - Fr^2}$.
> - Five slope classes: Mild ($y_n > y_c$), Steep ($y_n < y_c$), Critical ($y_n = y_c$), Horizontal ($S_0 = 0$), Adverse ($S_0 < 0$).
> - Three zones: Zone 1 (backwater, $dy/dx > 0$), Zone 2 (drawdown, $dy/dx < 0$), Zone 3 (rising, $dy/dx > 0$).
> - Subcritical flow is controlled from downstream; supercritical flow from upstream.
> - Direct Step Method computes distance between depths: $\Delta x = \dfrac{E_2 - E_1}{S_0 - \bar{S}_f}$.
