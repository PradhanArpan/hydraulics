## The fire hose punch

Have you ever seen firefighters holding a high-pressure water hose? Two or three strong firefighters must brace their entire bodies to hold the nozzle steady. Where does that immense backward kick come from?

```predict
q: A nozzle shoots a high-speed jet of water into the air. When that jet hits a flat concrete wall and spreads out sideways at 90 degrees, how much force does it exert on the wall?
o: Exactly equal to the rate at which momentum is destroyed as water stops moving forward
o: Zero, because water is a liquid that easily splashes away
o: Twice the weight of the water in the tank
a: 1
why: By Newton's second law (impulse-momentum principle), the dynamic force exerted on the wall equals the mass flow rate times the change in velocity in the forward direction.
```

Today you will see how Newton's second law governs the dynamic impact of water jets hitting stationary flat plates.

## The Impulse-Momentum Principle for Jets

Newton's second law states:
$$\text{Force} = \frac{d(\text{Momentum})}{dt} = \dot{m} \cdot \Delta V$$

When a steady jet of water strikes a solid stationary barrier:

### 1. Jet Geometry & Mass Flow Rate
- **Nozzle area $a$ (m²):**
  $$a = \frac{\pi}{4}d^2$$
- **Discharge $Q$ (m³/s):**
  $$Q = a \cdot V$$
- **Mass flow rate $\dot{m}$ (kg/s):**
  $$\dot{m} = \rho \cdot Q = \rho \cdot a \cdot V$$

### 2. Force on Fixed Vertical Flat Plate
Assuming the plate is smooth and perpendicular to the jet:
- The incoming velocity in the $x$-direction is $+V$.
- After striking the plate, water deflects symmetrically through $90^\circ$ parallel to the plate.
- The final velocity in the $x$-direction is $V_{x2} = 0$.

$$F_x = \dot{m}\,(V_{x1} - V_{x2}) = (\rho\,a\,V)\,(V - 0) = \rho\,a\,V^2$$

*In words: force equals density times jet area times velocity squared.*

## Derive jet force on a stationary flat plate

Click through the steps to see the impulse-momentum balance.

```steps
say: Define a control volume enclosing the jet impact zone right at the flat plate.
eq: \Sigma F_x = \dot{m}_{\text{out}}\,V_{x,\text{out}} - \dot{m}_{\text{in}}\,V_{x,\text{in}}
why: Newton's second law applied to steady fluid flow through a control volume.
---
say: In the x-direction, water enters at speed V and leaves purely in the y-direction along the plate.
eq: V_{x,\text{in}} = V, \quad V_{x,\text{out}} = 0
why: The plate is perpendicular to the jet, deflecting all fluid radially at 90 degrees.
---
say: The only external horizontal force acting on the fluid control volume is the reaction force R_x from the plate.
eq: -R_x = \dot{m}\,(0 - V) = -\rho\,a\,V^2 \implies R_x = \rho\,a\,V^2
why: The plate pushes backward against the water to halt its forward momentum.
---
say: By Newton's third law, the force exerted by the water jet ON the plate is equal and opposite to R_x.
eq: F_{\text{jet on plate}} = R_x = \rho\,a\,V^2
why: Action and reaction are equal and opposite.
```

## Impact force from a hydraulic monitor jet

> [!note] Problem
> A high-pressure mining monitor nozzle of diameter $d = 50$ mm discharges water ($\rho = 1000\ \text{kg}/\text{m}^3$) at a velocity of $V = 30$ m/s normal to a stationary vertical rock face. Calculate:
> 1. Discharge $Q$ in L/s
> 2. Mass flow rate $\dot{m}$ in kg/s
> 3. Dynamic force $F_x$ exerted on the rock face in kN

**Step 1. Jet cross-sectional area $a$:**
$$a = \frac{\pi}{4}(0.050)^2 = \frac{\pi}{4}(0.0025) = 1.9635 \times 10^{-3}\ \text{m}^2$$

**Step 2. Discharge $Q$:**
$$Q = a \cdot V = 1.9635 \times 10^{-3} \times 30 = 0.0589\ \text{m}^3/\text{s} = 58.9\ \text{L}/\text{s}$$

**Step 3. Mass flow rate $\dot{m}$:**
$$\dot{m} = \rho\,Q = 1000 \times 0.0589 = 58.9\ \text{kg}/\text{s}$$

**Step 4. Force exerted on the wall $F_x$:**
$$F_x = \rho\,a\,V^2 = \dot{m} \cdot V = 58.9 \times 30 = 1767\ \text{N} = 1.767\ \text{kN}$$

> [!note] What to take away
> A nozzle smaller than your coffee mug delivers nearly $1.8$ kilonewtons of continuous punch (equal to the weight of two adult passengers) simply by destroying fluid velocity!

## Civil engineering connections

- **Water Jet Cutting & Hydro-Demolition:** Ultra-high-pressure water jets ($V > 300$ m/s) are used in bridge rehabilitation to cut away damaged concrete without damaging steel rebar or inducing micro-cracks.
- **Fire-Fighting Nozzle Thrust:** Firefighters experience substantial nozzle reaction thrust, requiring calculated nozzle anchors and safety grip handles.

<!--beat:recap|Recap-->
## Three things to remember

1. **Impulse-momentum foundation:** Force equals mass flow rate times change in velocity: $F = \dot{m} \cdot \Delta V$.
2. **Fixed vertical plate:** $F_x = \rho a V^2$ because all forward velocity is arrested.
3. **Quadratic velocity scaling:** Doubling jet speed quadruples the impact force.
