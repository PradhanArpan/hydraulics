## The mystery of dust on a spinning fan

Have you ever looked at a ceiling fan that has been spinning for months? Even at top speed, dust sticks firmly to the spinning blades. Why doesn't the rushing air blow the dust away?

```predict

o: Exactly the same as the fast river surface speed
o: Exactly zero — water molecules stick directly to the solid bed
o: Half of the surface speed
a: 2
why: Because of viscosity, fluid molecules in direct contact with a solid surface stick to it completely. This fundamental physical fact is called the no-slip condition.
```

Today you will see how the **no-slip condition** creates a thin, slow-moving blanket of fluid next to any solid surface: the **boundary layer**.

## What is a boundary layer?

In 1904, Ludwig Prandtl showed that fluid flow around a body can be divided into two distinct zones:

1. **The Boundary Layer:** A very thin region hugging the solid wall where friction (viscosity) dominates and velocity ramps up from zero to free-stream speed.
2. **The Outer Flow:** Everything outside that thin layer, where viscosity can be neglected and fluid behaves as frictionless (ideal) fluid.

### Key Terms to Know

- **No-slip condition:** The layer of water touching the solid boundary cannot slide. Its speed relative to the wall is precisely zero ($u = 0$ at $y = 0$).
- **Free-stream velocity $U_\infty$ (m/s):** The undisturbed speed of the fluid far away from the wall.
- **Nominal boundary layer thickness $\delta$ (m):** The perpendicular distance from the wall where local speed reaches $99\%$ of the free-stream speed:

$$u(y = \delta) = 0.99\,U_\infty$$

*In words: the thickness where fluid has recovered 99 percent of its full rushing speed.*

- **Reynolds number along plate $Re_x$:**

$$Re_x = \frac{U_\infty\,x}{\nu}$$

*In words: how many times stronger inertia forces are compared to viscous friction forces at distance $x$.*

| Zone | Reynolds Number $Re_x$ | How fluid moves |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
| **Laminar zone** | $Re_x < 5 \times 10^5$ | Smooth, orderly fluid sheets sliding over each other |
| **Transition zone** | $5 \times 10^5 \le Re_x \le 10^6$ | Tiny waves amplify into turbulent bursts |
| **Turbulent zone** | $Re_x > 10^6$ | Rapid, chaotic swirling eddies mixing fast and slow fluid |

## Why does the layer grow with $\sqrt{x}$?

Click through the steps to see why the boundary layer thickness grows proportionally to the square root of distance along the plate.

```steps
say: Inside the boundary layer, the forward inertia force per unit volume balances the viscous shear resistance.
eq: \rho\,u\,\frac{\partial u}{\partial x} \approx \mu\,\frac{\partial^2 u}{\partial y^2}
why: Prandtl simplified the full Navier-Stokes equations by keeping only the dominant terms near the wall.
---
say: Approximate the scale of velocity as U, length along the plate as x, and distance from the wall as delta.
eq: \rho\,\frac{U_\infty^2}{x} \sim \mu\,\frac{U_\infty}{\delta^2}
why: The velocity changes by U over distance x horizontally, and by U over distance delta vertically.
---
say: Rearrange to solve for the boundary layer thickness delta.
eq: \delta^2 \sim \frac{\mu\,x}{\rho\,U_\infty} = \frac{\nu\,x}{U_\infty} \implies \delta \sim \sqrt{\frac{\nu\,x}{U_\infty}} = \frac{x}{\sqrt{Re_x}}
why: Viscous diffusion takes time to penetrate outward, so thickness grows with the square root of travel distance x.
```

## Checking flow state on a submerged bridge pier model

> [!note] Problem
> A flat wooden model of a bridge pier, length $L = 2.0$ m, is tested in a towing tank filled with water ($\nu = 1.0 \times 10^{-6}\ \text{m}^2/\text{s}$). The towing speed is $U_\infty = 1.2$ m/s.
> 1. Find the Reynolds number at $x = 0.3$ m and at the trailing edge $x = 2.0$ m.
> 2. Determine the distance $x_{cr}$ from the leading edge where transition to turbulence occurs, assuming critical Reynolds number $Re_{cr} = 5 \times 10^5$.

**Step 1. Reynolds number at $x = 0.3$ m:**
$$Re_x = \frac{U_\infty\,x}{\nu} = \frac{1.2 \times 0.3}{1.0 \times 10^{-6}} = 360{,}000 = 3.6 \times 10^5$$
Since $Re_x < 5 \times 10^5$, the flow at $x = 0.3$ m is completely **laminar**.

**Step 2. Reynolds number at the trailing edge $x = 2.0$ m:**
$$Re_L = \frac{U_\infty\,L}{\nu} = \frac{1.2 \times 2.0}{1.0 \times 10^{-6}} = 2{,}400{,}000 = 2.4 \times 10^6$$
Since $Re_L > 5 \times 10^5$, the flow near the back of the plate is **turbulent**.

**Step 3. Find the transition location $x_{cr}$:**
Set $Re_{cr} = 5 \times 10^5$:
$$x_{cr} = \frac{Re_{cr}\,\nu}{U_\infty} = \frac{5 \times 10^5 \times 1.0 \times 10^{-6}}{1.2} = \frac{0.50}{1.2} = 0.417\ \text{m} = 41.7\ \text{cm}$$

> [!note] What to take away
> The boundary layer is laminar for the first $41.7$ cm, then transitions to turbulent for the remaining $158.3$ cm of the pier model.

```quiz

o: Water is pushed back by atmospheric pressure
o: Fluid molecules stick to solid walls due to viscosity (no-slip condition)
o: Gravity only acts on the surface
a: 2
why: Viscous adhesion forces fluid molecules touching the solid surface to remain stationary relative to the surface.
---
q: If the flow velocity over a flat plate is doubled, what happens to the transition distance x_cr?
o: It doubles
o: It stays the same
o: It halves (transitions twice as close to the front)
a: 3
why: Because x_cr = Re_cr * nu / U, higher speed reaches the critical Reynolds number in half the distance.
```

## Civil engineering connections

- **Bridge Pier Scour:** The boundary layer slowing down near the bed creates a downward vertical jet at the pier face, digging deep scour holes that undermine bridge foundations.
- **Canal Bed Sediment Transport:** The shear stress inside the thin boundary layer determines whether sand grains on a river bed remain stationary or wash away.
- **Wind Engineering:** Atmospheric wind blowing over cities forms a boundary layer hundreds of metres thick, causing wind speeds at the top of a skyscraper to be much higher than at street level.

<!--beat:recap|Recap-->
## Three things to remember

1. **No-slip condition:** Fluid touching a solid surface has zero velocity relative to that surface.
2. **Nominal thickness $\delta$:** The height above the wall where fluid reaches $99\%$ of the free-stream velocity $U_\infty$.
3. **Transition threshold:** Flow over a smooth flat plate transitions from laminar to turbulent when $Re_x = \frac{U_\infty\,x}{\nu} \approx 5 \times 10^5$.
