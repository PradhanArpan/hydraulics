## The architect's blueprint for turbine blades

How do engineers know the exact angle at which a metal turbine blade must be curved? If the angle is off by just three degrees, high-speed water slams violently into the blade back, tearing metal away by cavitation and vibration.

```predict
q: If a jet approaches a turbine runner at an angle alpha_1 = 20 degrees to the direction of blade motion, will the water enter the blade smoothly if the blade's leading edge is also angled at 20 degrees?
o: Yes, the blade angle must match the jet angle alpha_1
o: No! The blade angle theta must match the relative velocity vector V_r1, not the absolute jet vector V_1!
o: It does not matter what angle the blade has
a: 2
why: Because the blade is moving at speed u, the fluid approaches the blade at relative velocity V_r1 and relative angle theta. The blade must be angled at theta, not alpha_1.
```

Today is an in-depth clinic on drawing, solving, and troubleshooting **velocity vector triangles**.

## The Complete Velocity Triangle Dictionary

Every velocity triangle connects five parameters on the blade:

$$\begin{aligned}
\vec{V} &= \text{Absolute velocity of fluid (m/s)} \\
\vec{u} &= \text{Peripheral velocity of blade (m/s)} \\
\vec{V}_r &= \text{Relative velocity of fluid to blade (m/s)} \\
V_w &= V \cos\alpha = \text{Whirl velocity (tangential component)} \\
V_f &= V \sin\alpha = \text{Flow velocity (radial / axial component)}
\end{aligned}$$

### Geometric Formulas for Inlet Triangle
1. **Flow velocity:**
   $$V_{f1} = V_1 \sin\alpha_1 = V_{r1} \sin\theta$$
2. **Whirl velocity:**
   $$V_{w1} = V_1 \cos\alpha_1$$
3. **Inlet blade angle $\theta$:**
   $$\tan\theta = \frac{V_{f1}}{V_{w1} - u_1}$$
4. **Inlet relative velocity $V_{r1}$:**
   $$V_{r1} = \sqrt{(V_{w1} - u_1)^2 + V_{f1}^2} = \frac{V_{f1}}{\sin\theta}$$

### Geometric Formulas for Outlet Triangle
1. **Relative exit velocity with friction $k$:**
   $$V_{r2} = k \cdot V_{r1} \quad (k = 1.0\ \text{for smooth blades})$$
2. **Outlet flow velocity:**
   $$V_{f2} = V_{r2} \sin\beta_2 = V_2 \sin\alpha_2$$
3. **Outlet whirl velocity $V_{w2}$:**
   $$V_{w2} = V_{r2} \cos\beta_2 - u_2$$
   *(If $V_{r2}\cos\beta_2 > u_2$, $V_{w2}$ points opposite to blade motion. If $V_{r2}\cos\beta_2 = u_2$, exit is radial with $V_{w2} = 0$!)*
4. **Absolute exit speed $V_2$:**
   $$V_2 = \sqrt{V_{w2}^2 + V_{f2}^2}$$

## Derive the blade angle equation $\tan\theta$

Click through the triangle trigonometry to see where $\tan\theta$ comes from.

```steps
say: Look at the right triangle formed by dropping a vertical line from the tip of vector V_1.
eq: \text{Opposite side} = V_{f1} = V_1 \sin\alpha_1
why: The vertical component of absolute velocity is the flow velocity Vf1.
---
say: Look at the horizontal base of that right triangle. It equals the inlet whirl velocity.
eq: \text{Total base} = V_{w1} = V_1 \cos\alpha_1
why: The horizontal projection of absolute velocity is whirl velocity Vw1.
---
say: The blade speed u occupies part of this base, starting from the left origin.
eq: \text{Base of relative triangle} = V_{w1} - u_1
why: Subtracting the blade speed u from total whirl Vw1 leaves the horizontal base of the relative velocity vector Vr1.
---
say: Take the tangent of the inlet blade angle theta.
eq: \tan\theta = \frac{\text{Opposite}}{\text{Adjacent}} = \frac{V_{f1}}{V_{w1} - u_1}
why: In the relative right triangle, tangent of theta is flow velocity divided by (Vw1 - u1).
```

## Clinic Problem: Full velocity triangle solution

> [!note] Problem
> A jet of water with velocity $V_1 = 40$ m/s strikes a curved vane at an angle $\alpha_1 = 30^\circ$ to the direction of motion of the vane. The vane moves at $u = 20$ m/s.
> The water leaves the vane at an angle $\beta_2 = 30^\circ$ to the direction of motion. Vane friction reduces relative velocity by $15\%$ ($k = 0.85$). Find:
> 1. Vane inlet angle $\theta$ for shockless entry
> 2. Relative velocity at inlet $V_{r1}$ and outlet $V_{r2}$
> 3. Absolute exit velocity $V_2$ and exit angle $\alpha_2$
> 4. Work done per unit weight of water (Euler head $H_e$)

**Step 1. Inlet velocity triangle:**
- Whirl velocity:
  $$V_{w1} = V_1 \cos(30^\circ) = 40 \times 0.8660 = 34.64\ \text{m}/\text{s}$$
- Flow velocity:
  $$V_{f1} = V_1 \sin(30^\circ) = 40 \times 0.5000 = 20.00\ \text{m}/\text{s}$$
- Base of relative velocity triangle:
  $$V_{w1} - u = 34.64 - 20.00 = 14.64\ \text{m}/\text{s}$$
- Inlet blade angle $\theta$:
  $$\tan\theta = \frac{V_{f1}}{V_{w1} - u} = \frac{20.00}{14.64} = 1.366 \implies \theta = 53.79^\circ \approx 53.8^\circ$$
- Relative velocity $V_{r1}$:
  $$V_{r1} = \sqrt{(14.64)^2 + (20.00)^2} = \sqrt{214.33 + 400.0} = \sqrt{614.33} = 24.79\ \text{m}/\text{s}$$

**Step 2. Outlet velocity triangle:**
- Relative velocity with friction:
  $$V_{r2} = k \cdot V_{r1} = 0.85 \times 24.79 = 21.07\ \text{m}/\text{s}$$
- Resolving along blade direction ($\beta_2 = 30^\circ$):
  $$V_{r2x} = V_{r2} \cos(30^\circ) = 21.07 \times 0.8660 = 18.25\ \text{m}/\text{s} \quad (\text{backward})$$
  $$V_{f2} = V_{r2} \sin(30^\circ) = 21.07 \times 0.5000 = 10.54\ \text{m}/\text{s}$$
- Outlet whirl velocity in direction of blade motion:
  $$V_{w2} = u - V_{r2x} = 20.00 - 18.25 = +1.75\ \text{m}/\text{s} \quad (\text{forward direction})$$
- Absolute exit speed $V_2$:
  $$V_2 = \sqrt{(V_{w2})^2 + (V_{f2})^2} = \sqrt{(1.75)^2 + (10.54)^2} = \sqrt{3.06 + 111.09} = 10.68\ \text{m}/\text{s}$$
- Exit angle $\alpha_2$:
  $$\tan\alpha_2 = \frac{V_{f2}}{V_{w2}} = \frac{10.54}{1.75} = 6.02 \implies \alpha_2 = 80.6^\circ$$

**Step 3. Euler Head $H_e$ (work done per unit weight):**
Change in whirl velocity:
$$\Delta V_w = V_{w1} - V_{w2} = 34.64 - 1.75 = 32.89\ \text{m}/\text{s}$$
$$H_e = \frac{\Delta V_w \cdot u}{g} = \frac{32.89 \times 20.00}{9.81} = \frac{657.8}{9.81} = 67.05\ \text{m}$$

> [!note] What to take away
> Notice how the blade inlet angle $\theta = 53.8^\circ$ is completely different from the jet angle $\alpha_1 = 30.0^\circ$. Accounting for blade motion $u$ is the entire secret to shockless turbine design!

```quiz

o: theta = 0 degrees (tangential)
o: theta = 90 degrees
a: 2
why: When alpha_1 = 0, V_f1 = 0 and V_r1 lies purely along the horizontal axis, so inlet blade angle theta = 0 deg.
---
q: What does a negative value of V_w2 signify in the outlet velocity triangle?
o: Water is flowing backward into the pipe
o: The absolute velocity of exiting water has a horizontal component directed opposite to blade motion
o: The turbine is operating as a pump
a: 2
why: A negative V_w2 means water is thrown backward relative to the ground, which imparts extra forward reaction momentum to the turbine runner.
```

## Civil engineering connections

- **Cavitation Prevention on Runner Tips:** If flow enters with an angle of attack (non-zero incidence angle $\Delta\theta$), flow separates at the blade leading edge, causing local pressure to drop below water vapor pressure ($p_v \approx 2.3$ kPa). Cavitation bubbles form and implode violently, chewing holes through solid stainless steel!
- **Run-of-River Turbine Tuning:** In seasonal rivers where discharge varies widely from monsoon to dry season, variable-pitch Kaplan turbine runner blades rotate automatically to keep $\theta$ perfectly aligned with $V_{r1}$ across all flow rates.

<!--beat:recap|Recap-->
## Three things to remember

1. **Shockless entry formula:** $\tan\theta = \frac{V_{f1}}{V_{w1} - u_1}$ aligns blade leading edge with relative velocity vector.
2. **Whirl is power:** Useful work is strictly proportional to change in whirl velocity: $P = \dot{m} \Delta V_w \cdot u$.
3. **Exit loss minimization:** Designing for radial exit ($V_{w2} = 0$) ensures minimal kinetic energy is discarded in the discharge.
