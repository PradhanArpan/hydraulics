## The wall of water when a dam fails

At 3:15 PM on August 11, 1979, the Machchhu-2 earthen dam in Morbi, Gujarat, failed under extraordinary monsoon rains.
A catastrophic wall of water, towering 6 to 9 meters high, roared through the downstream valley at over 25 km/h.
Within 20 minutes, the industrial city of Morbi was submerged, resulting in one of the deadliest dam disasters in human history.

When a dam fails, how fast does the flood wave travel?
Which residential colonies will be underwater in 15 minutes, 1 hour, or 4 hours?
Where should evacuation sirens sound first?

## Dam-Break Hydrodynamics & Breach Mechanics

A dam break is an extreme **unsteady, non-uniform, discontinuous flow** event characterized by:
1. **The Positive Surge:** A steep shock wave (bore) surging down the downstream river valley.
2. **The Negative Wave:** A depression wave traveling upstream into the reservoir pool, drawing down the water surface.

### Froehlich (2008) Breach Regression Equations
Based on 74 documented embankment dam failures worldwide:

#### 1. Average Breach Width ($B_{\text{avg}}$):
$$B_{\text{avg}} = 0.27 \, K_0 \, V_w^{0.32} \, h_b^{0.04} \quad \text{[meters]}$$
where:
- $K_0 = 1.3$ for overtopping failure; $K_0 = 1.0$ for piping/internal erosion.
- $V_w$ = Reservoir volume stored above breach invert ($\text{m}^3$).
- $h_b$ = Height of the breach ($\text{m}$).

#### 2. Breach Formation Time ($t_f$):
$$t_f = 63.2 \, \sqrt{\frac{V_w}{g \, h_b^2}} \quad \text{[seconds]}$$

### Flood Hazard Rating
Life-safety hazard to humans and buildings is governed by the depth-velocity product:
$$\text{Hazard Product} = y \times V \quad (\text{m}^2/\text{s})$$

| Hazard Level | $y \times V$ Product ($\text{m}^2/\text{s}$) | Physical Consequence |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Low Hazard** | $y \times V < 0.5$ | Safe for walking evacuation; vehicles may stall |
| **Medium Hazard** | $0.5 \le y \times V \le 1.5$ | Unsafe for walking; brick masonry withstands flow |
| **Extreme Hazard**| $y \times V > 1.5$ | Structural collapse of buildings; mandatory evacuation |

## The analytical Ritter dam-break solution

Follow the classical solution for an instantaneous dam break in a dry channel.

```steps
say: Consider a reservoir of initial depth y0 behind a vertical gate at x = 0. At t = 0, the gate is removed instantaneously.
eq: y(x, 0) = y_0 \quad (x \le 0), \quad y(x, 0) = 0 \quad (x > 0)
why: The idealized classical dam-break initial condition (Ritter, 1892).
---
say: Integrate the 1D shallow water characteristic equations along incoming and outgoing waves.
eq: u + 2\sqrt{gy} = 2\sqrt{gy_0} = \text{constant}
why: Riemann invariant along the forward characteristic originating from the undisturbed reservoir.
---
say: At the fixed breach location (x = 0), flow depth remains constant for all time.
eq: y_{\text{breach}} = \frac{4}{9} y_0 \approx 0.444\, y_0
why: The negative expansion wave and forward acceleration reach dynamic equilibrium at the crest!
---
say: Velocity at the breach location and the leading wave tip velocity:
eq: V_{\text{breach}} = \frac{2}{3}\sqrt{gy_0}, \quad V_{\text{tip}} = 2\sqrt{gy_0}
why: The flood front advances onto dry bed at twice the shallow water wave celerity!
```

## Sizing dam breach & downstream hazard

> [!note] Problem
> An earthen dam holds a reservoir volume of $V_w = 4.5 \times 10^6\text{ m}^3$ above the breach invert.
> The dam height from invert to crest is $h_b = 18\text{ m}$.
> Due to extreme monsoon rainfall, the dam experiences an **overtopping failure** ($K_0 = 1.3$).
> 1. Compute the average breach width $B_{\text{avg}}$ using Froehlich (2008).
> 2. Compute the breach development time $t_f$ in minutes.
> 3. If peak outflow is $Q_p = 3,600\text{ m}^3/\text{s}$ and downstream flood depth is $y = 5.0\text{ m}$ in a $120\text{ m}$ wide valley, evaluate the hazard rating.

**Step 1. Compute average breach width $B_{\text{avg}}$:**
$$B_{\text{avg}} = 0.27 \, K_0 \, V_w^{0.32} \, h_b^{0.04}$$
- $K_0 = 1.3$
- $V_w^{0.32} = (4,500,000)^{0.32} \approx 134.48$
- $h_b^{0.04} = (18)^{0.04} \approx 1.122$
$$B_{\text{avg}} = 0.27 \times 1.3 \times 134.48 \times 1.122 = 52.96\text{ m} \approx 53.0\text{ m}$$

**Step 2. Compute breach formation time $t_f$:**
$$t_f = 63.2 \, \sqrt{\frac{V_w}{g \, h_b^2}}$$
$$g \, h_b^2 = 9.81 \times (18)^2 = 3,178.44\text{ m}^3/\text{s}^2$$
$$\frac{V_w}{g \, h_b^2} = \frac{4,500,000}{3,178.44} = 1,415.79\text{ s}^2 \implies \sqrt{1,415.79} = 37.627\text{ s}$$
$$t_f = 63.2 \times 37.627 = 2,378\text{ seconds} \approx 39.6\text{ minutes}$$

**Step 3. Downstream hazard product evaluation:**
$$A = 120\text{ m} \times 5.0\text{ m} = 600\text{ m}^2$$
$$V = \frac{Q_p}{A} = \frac{3,600\text{ m}^3/\text{s}}{600\text{ m}^2} = 6.0\text{ m/s}$$
$$\text{Hazard Product} = y \times V = 5.0\text{ m} \times 6.0\text{ m/s} = 30.0\text{ m}^2/\text{s}$$
- Because $30.0 \gg 1.5\text{ m}^2/\text{s}$, this is classified as **Extreme Catastrophic Hazard**. Ordinary structures will collapse; emergency sirens must trigger immediate evacuation.

o: 1D models require no computer

## Civil engineering connections

- **Emergency Action Plans (EAPs):** Central Water Commission (CWC) mandates that all large dams in India have approved EAPs with 2D inundation maps showing flood arrival times in 15-minute increments for downstream districts.
- **Evacuation Route Hydro-Enforcement:** High-resolution LiDAR DEMs are coupled with road networks in GIS to identify which evacuation bridges will submerge first, ensuring evacuation traffic is routed across high ground.

## Three things to remember

1. **Dam breaks are unsteady shock bores:** Rapid flood wave propagation with steep fronts and high velocities.
2. **Breaches take time:** Froehlich (2008) provides realistic empirical estimates of width ($B_{\text{avg}} \approx 50\text{ m}$) and formation time ($t_f \approx 40\text{ min}$).
3. **Hazard product $y \times V > 1.5\text{ m}^2/\text{s}$:** Critical threshold where ordinary civil buildings collapse.
