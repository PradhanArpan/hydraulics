## Testing a 500 MW giant in a laboratory bucket

Before a power company spends \$500 million building a massive dam turbine with a runner diameter of $8$ metres, how do they know it will work efficiently?

They don't build the giant first. They build a precision scale model with a runner diameter of just $0.4$ metres, test it in a laboratory flume under a modest head, and predict the full-scale machine's output to within $0.5\%$ accuracy!

## Unit Quantities & Specific Speed

### 1. Unit Quantities (Performance under $1$ metre head)
To compare a turbine tested under variable river heads, engineers define its unit parameters:
- **Unit Speed $N_u$:** Speed of the turbine under $1$ metre net head:
  $$N_u = \frac{N}{\sqrt{H}}$$
- **Unit Discharge $Q_u$:** Discharge of the turbine under $1$ metre net head:
  $$Q_u = \frac{Q}{\sqrt{H}}$$
- **Unit Power $P_u$:** Shaft power produced under $1$ metre net head:
  $$P_u = \frac{P}{H^{3/2}}$$

*If head on an existing plant changes from $H_1$ to $H_2$, unit quantities stay constant: $N_1 / \sqrt{H_1} = N_2 / \sqrt{H_2}$.*

### 2. Specific Speed of a Turbine $N_s$
The speed of a geometrically similar runner that would produce **$1$ kW of power under a net head of $1$ metre**:

$$N_s = \frac{N\sqrt{P}}{H^{5/4}}$$

*In words: rotational speed $N$ (rpm) times square root of shaft power $P$ (kW) divided by head $H$ to the power $1.25$.*

### 3. Master Turbine Selection Matrix

| Turbine Type | Head Range $H$ (m) | Specific Speed $N_s$ (SI units) | Flow Direction |
| :-- | :-- | :-- | :-- |
| **Pelton (Single Jet)** | $> 300$ m | $10 \text{ to } 35$ | Tangential impulse |
| **Pelton (Multi-Jet)** | $200 \text{ to } 400$ m | $30 \text{ to } 60$ | Tangential impulse |
| **Francis (Slow/Medium)** | $100 \text{ to } 300$ m | $60 \text{ to } 180$ | Inward radial to axial |
| **Francis (Fast)** | $50 \text{ to } 150$ m | $180 \text{ to } 300$ | Inward radial to axial |
| **Kaplan & Propeller** | $< 50$ m | $300 \text{ to } 1000$ | Purely axial |

*Low $N_s$ means high head and small flow (Pelton). High $N_s$ means low head and giant flow (Kaplan).*

## Derive the specific speed formula $N_s$

Click through the scaling laws to see how $H^{5/4}$ arises.

```steps
say: From velocity triangles, peripheral blade speed u is proportional to jet Torricelli velocity sqrt(H).
eq: u \propto \sqrt{H} \implies \frac{\pi\,D\,N}{60} \propto \sqrt{H} \implies D \propto \frac{\sqrt{H}}{N}
why: Linear velocity u scales directly with the square root of net head H.
---
say: Flow area scales with runner diameter squared, and flow velocity scales with sqrt(H).
eq: Q \propto (\text{Area}) \cdot V_f \propto D^2 \sqrt{H}
why: Discharge equals cross-sectional flow area times velocity.
---
say: Substitute diameter D proportional to sqrt(H)/N into the discharge formula.
eq: Q \propto \left(\frac{\sqrt{H}}{N}\right)^2 \sqrt{H} = \frac{H \cdot \sqrt{H}}{N^2} = \frac{H^{3/2}}{N^2}
why: Eliminating physical runner diameter D in favor of head and speed.
---
say: Shaft power P is proportional to discharge Q times head H.
eq: P \propto Q \cdot H \propto \frac{H^{3/2}}{N^2} \cdot H = \frac{H^{5/2}}{N^2}
why: Total fluid power equals density times gravity times Q times H.
---
say: Take the square root and solve for N.
eq: \sqrt{P} \propto \frac{H^{5/4}}{N} \implies N \frac{\sqrt{P}}{H^{5/4}} = \text{constant} = N_s
why: By setting P = 1 kW and H = 1 m, the constant of proportionality is precisely the specific speed Ns!
```

## Model testing and prototype prediction

> [!note] Problem
> A $1:5$ scale model of a proposed turbine is tested in a laboratory flume under a head of $H_m = 6.0$ m at $N_m = 450$ rpm, producing $P_m = 18$ kW.
> The full-scale prototype will operate under a dam head of $H_p = 36$ m. Calculate:
> 1. Specific speed $N_s$ of the model
> 2. Type of turbine indicated by $N_s$
> 3. Rotational speed $N_p$ of the prototype
> 4. Power output $P_p$ of the prototype in megawatts (MW)

**Step 1. Specific speed of the model:**
$$H_m^{1.25} = (6.0)^{1.25} = 9.389$$
$$N_s = \frac{N_m \sqrt{P_m}}{H_m^{1.25}} = \frac{450 \times \sqrt{18}}{9.389} = \frac{450 \times 4.2426}{9.389} = \frac{1909.2}{9.389} = 203.3$$

**Step 2. Turbine type:**
Since $N_s = 203.3$ lies in the range $180$ to $300$, this is a **Francis reaction turbine**.

**Step 3. Prototype rotational speed $N_p$:**
From peripheral speed scaling: $\frac{D_m N_m}{\sqrt{H_m}} = \frac{D_p N_p}{\sqrt{H_p}}$.
Given scale ratio $\frac{D_p}{D_m} = 5$:
$$N_p = N_m \times \left(\frac{D_m}{D_p}\right) \times \sqrt{\frac{H_p}{H_m}} = 450 \times \left(\frac{1}{5}\right) \times \sqrt{\frac{36}{6}} = 90 \times \sqrt{6} = 90 \times 2.4495 = 220.5\ \text{rpm}$$

**Step 4. Prototype power output $P_p$:**
Since $N_s$ is identical for model and prototype:
$$N_s = \frac{N_p \sqrt{P_p}}{H_p^{1.25}} \implies \sqrt{P_p} = \frac{N_s \cdot H_p^{1.25}}{N_p}$$
$$H_p^{1.25} = (36)^{1.25} = 88.18$$
$$\sqrt{P_p} = \frac{203.3 \times 88.18}{220.5} = \frac{17927}{220.5} = 81.30 \implies P_p = (81.30)^2 = 6610\ \text{kW} = 6.61\ \text{MW}$$

> [!note] What to take away
> Testing an $18$ kW scale model under $6$ m head predicted the exact speed ($220.5$ rpm) and multi-megawatt output ($6.61$ MW) of the full-scale dam turbine!

## Civil engineering connections

- **Moody's Step-Up Formula:** Large prototypes actually have slightly higher efficiency than small laboratory models because boundary layer friction forms a smaller fraction of physical flow passages. Civil engineers use Moody's formula: $1 - \eta_p = (1 - \eta_m)(D_m / D_p)^{1/5}$.
- **Grid Generator Synchronization:** Specific speed calculations dictate synchronous generator selection ($f = N \cdot P_{\text{poles}} / 120$), locking in the civil turbine layout to the electrical grid.

<!--beat:recap|Recap-->
## Three things to remember

1. **Unit quantities:** $N_u = N/\sqrt{H}$, $Q_u = Q/\sqrt{H}$, $P_u = P/H^{3/2}$ allow instant scaling under varying reservoir levels.
2. **Specific speed index:** $N_s = \frac{N\sqrt{P}}{H^{5/4}}$ characterizes runner geometry independent of physical size.
3. **Selection boundaries:** Pelton ($N_s < 35$), Francis ($60 < N_s < 300$), Kaplan ($N_s > 300$).
