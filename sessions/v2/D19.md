## The 200% force multiplier

If you shoot a high-speed jet of water at a flat wall, you get a certain force. If you simply bend that flat wall into a curved cup, the force instantly **doubles**! Why does curving the surface deliver twice the punch?

```predict
q: A jet of water hits a hemispherical cup and is smoothly turned 180 degrees backwards. How does the impact force compare to hitting a flat plate?
o: Exactly the same force
o: Half the force
o: Exactly double the force (2x)
a: 3
why: On a flat plate, water stops moving forward (velocity change = V - 0 = V). In a 180-degree cup, water exits backward at -V (velocity change = V - (-V) = 2V). Doubling the velocity change doubles the momentum change and doubles the force!
```

Today you will see why hydraulic turbines and Pelton wheels use curved buckets instead of flat paddles.

## Jet Force on Stationary Curved Vanes

### Case 1: Jet Striking the Center of a Symmetrical Curved Vane

Let a horizontal jet strike the center of a curved vane smoothly and divide symmetrically, deflecting through angle $\phi$ (so deflection angle is $\theta = 180^\circ - \phi$):

- Initial velocity in $x$-direction: $V_{x1} = V$.
- Exit velocity in $x$-direction: $V_{x2} = -V \cos\phi$ (or $V \cos\theta$).

$$F_x = \rho\,a\,V\,[V - (-V\cos\phi)] = \rho\,a\,V^2\,(1 + \cos\phi)$$

*In words: force equals $\rho a V^2$ multiplied by $(1 + \cos\phi)$, where $\phi$ is the angle of the exit lip with the incoming jet axis.*

### The Hemispherical Cup ($\phi = 0^\circ$ or Deflection $\theta = 180^\circ$)

When the vane is a semi-circle, the water is turned completely around backward:
$$\cos(0^\circ) = 1 \implies F_x = \rho\,a\,V^2\,(1 + 1) = 2\,\rho\,a\,V^2$$

The force is exactly **$200\%$** of the force on a flat vertical plate!

### Case 2: Jet Striking a Curved Vane Tangentially at One Tip

When the jet enters smoothly along the tangent at angle $\theta$ to the horizontal and exits at angle $\phi$:

$$F_x = \rho\,a\,V^2\,(\cos\theta + \cos\phi)$$
$$F_y = \rho\,a\,V^2\,(\sin\theta - \sin\phi)$$

If the vane is symmetrical ($\theta = \phi$):
- $F_x = 2\,\rho\,a\,V^2 \cos\theta$
- $F_y = 0$ (vertical reactions cancel completely!)

## Derive the 2x force on a hemispherical cup

Click through the steps to see the momentum doubling.

```steps
say: Write Newton's second law for horizontal momentum on the fluid control volume.
eq: F_x = \dot{m}\,(V_{x,\text{in}} - V_{x,\text{out}})
why: Net horizontal force exerted by the vane on the water.
---
say: The water enters the center traveling to the right at speed V.
eq: V_{x,\text{in}} = +V
why: Initial jet direction defines the positive x-axis.
---
say: The smooth hemispherical cup turns the water completely around by 180 degrees.
eq: V_{x,\text{out}} = -V
why: Neglecting friction, Bernoulli's equation shows exit speed equals inlet speed V, directed opposite to incoming flow.
---
say: Substitute into the momentum equation.
eq: F_x = (\rho\,a\,V)\,[V - (-V)] = \rho\,a\,V\,(2V) = 2\,\rho\,a\,V^2
why: Subtracting a negative velocity doubles the total momentum transfer!
```

## Force comparison: flat plate vs hemispherical cup

> [!note] Problem
> A nozzle of diameter $d = 50$ mm discharges a jet of water at $V = 24$ m/s ($\rho = 1000\ \text{kg}/\text{m}^3$). Calculate the force exerted on:
> 1. A stationary flat vertical plate
> 2. A stationary curved vane that deflects the jet through $120^\circ$
> 3. A stationary hemispherical cup (deflection $180^\circ$)

**Step 1. Jet cross-sectional area and base momentum flux:**
$$a = \frac{\pi}{4}(0.050)^2 = 1.9635 \times 10^{-3}\ \text{m}^2$$
$$F_{\text{flat}} = \rho\,a\,V^2 = 1000 \times 1.9635 \times 10^{-3} \times (24)^2 = 1131\ \text{N} = 1.131\ \text{kN}$$

**Step 2. Curved vane deflecting through $120^\circ$ ($\phi = 180^\circ - 120^\circ = 60^\circ$):**
$$F_{120} = \rho\,a\,V^2\,(1 + \cos 60^\circ) = 1131 \times (1 + 0.50) = 1131 \times 1.50 = 1696.5\ \text{N} = 1.697\ \text{kN}$$

**Step 3. Hemispherical cup ($180^\circ$ deflection, $\phi = 0^\circ$):**
$$F_{180} = 2\,\rho\,a\,V^2 = 2 \times 1131 = 2262\ \text{N} = 2.262\ \text{kN}$$

> [!note] What to take away
> Simply shaping the plate into a cup increases the force from $1.13$ kN to $2.26$ kN — an extra $1131$ N of thrust created for free just by rebounding the fluid!

## Civil engineering connections

- **Pelton Turbine Buckets:** High-head hydroelectric plants (like the 800-meter head plants in the Himalayas and Western Ghats) use double-hemispherical Pelton buckets to harness this exact $2\times$ momentum reversal, extracting over $90\%$ of water's kinetic energy into clean electricity.
- **Thrust Blocks on Bends:** In high-pressure water mains, every pipe elbow acts like a curved vane trying to blow off its joints. Civil engineers build heavy concrete thrust blocks around every bend to resist this dynamic momentum thrust.

<!--beat:recap|Recap-->
## Three things to remember

1. **Curved vane force formula:** $F_x = \rho a V^2 (1 + \cos\phi)$, where $\phi$ is the exit lip angle.
2. **Hemispherical multiplier:** A $180^\circ$ reversal produces exactly $F_x = 2 \rho a V^2$ (twice a flat plate).
3. **Foundation of impulse turbines:** Pelton wheels rely directly on this $2\times$ momentum reversal to extract rotational work.
