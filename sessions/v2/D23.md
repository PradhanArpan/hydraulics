## The twisting power of water

Why does a lawn sprinkler start spinning as soon as you turn on the water tap? Because the exiting water pushes with a lever arm, exerting a **torque** (rotational twist) that spins the rotor!

```predict

o: Conservation of angular momentum (moment of momentum)
o: Hooke's law of elasticity
a: 2
why: In rotational systems, torque equals the rate of change of angular momentum: Torque = m_dot * (V_w1 * r_1 - V_w2 * r_2).
```

## Angular Momentum & Shaft Torque

For a rotating wheel or turbine runner spinning at constant angular velocity $\omega$ (rad/s):

### 1. Angular Momentum of Fluid (Moment of Momentum)
The angular momentum of a fluid particle of mass $m$ at radius $r$ is:
$$L = m \cdot (\vec{r} \times \vec{V}) = m \cdot r \cdot V_w$$

*Only the tangential whirl velocity $V_w$ contributes to angular momentum! Flow velocity $V_f$ points through the center and has zero moment arm.*

### 2. Torque Exerted by Fluid on the Wheel $T$
By the principle of angular momentum, net torque equals the rate of change of angular momentum:

$$T = \dot{m}\,(V_{w1}\,r_1 \pm V_{w2}\,r_2)$$

*In words: mass flow rate times (inlet whirl angular momentum minus outlet whirl angular momentum).*

### 3. Power Developed by the Shaft $P$
Multiplying torque by angular speed $\omega$:

$$P = T \cdot \omega = \dot{m}\,(V_{w1}\,r_1\,\omega \pm V_{w2}\,r_2\,\omega)$$

Since linear blade velocity is $u = \omega \cdot r$:
$$u_1 = \omega\,r_1 \quad \text{and} \quad u_2 = \omega\,r_2$$

$$P = \dot{m}\,(V_{w1}\,u_1 \pm V_{w2}\,u_2)$$

*In words: power equals mass flow rate times the change in the product of whirl velocity and blade velocity.*

## Derive torque from moment of momentum

Click through the steps to see how angular momentum yields torque.

```steps
say: State the moment of momentum theorem for a steady flow control volume rotating about an axis.
eq: \Sigma T_{\text{shaft}} = \dot{m}\,(r_{\text{in}} \times V_{\text{in}} - r_{\text{out}} \times V_{\text{out}})
why: Net torque acting on the rotor equals rate of outflow of angular momentum minus rate of inflow.
---
say: Resolve velocity vectors into tangential (whirl) and radial (flow) components.
eq: \vec{V} = V_w\,\hat{e}_\theta + V_f\,\hat{e}_r
why: The radial component has a line of action passing directly through the shaft axis, producing zero torque.
---
say: Cross product with position vector r gives the scalar angular momentum.
eq: |\vec{r} \times \vec{V}| = r \cdot V_w
why: Only the tangential whirl component is perpendicular to radius r.
---
say: Net torque exerted BY the fluid ON the wheel is equal and opposite to the shaft reaction.
eq: T = \dot{m}\,(V_{w1}\,r_1 \pm V_{w2}\,r_2)
why: By Newton's third law, fluid torque driving the runner is positive in the direction of rotation.
```

## Shaft torque on an inward radial flow wheel

> [!note] Problem
> A series of radial curved vanes rotates on a wheel. Water enters the wheel at an outer radius $r_1 = 0.60$ m at an absolute velocity $V_1 = 25$ m/s inclined at $\alpha_1 = 20^\circ$ to the tangential direction.
> Water discharges at an inner radius $r_2 = 0.30$ m with absolute velocity $V_2 = 6.0$ m/s directed purely radially ($\alpha_2 = 90^\circ$, so $V_{w2} = 0$).
> The wheel rotates at $N = 300$ rpm and discharges $Q = 0.12\ \text{m}^3/\text{s}$ ($\rho = 1000\ \text{kg}/\text{m}^3$). Calculate:
> 1. Angular velocity $\omega$ and peripheral blade speed $u_1$
> 2. Inlet whirl velocity $V_{w1}$
> 3. Shaft torque $T$ in N·m
> 4. Power developed $P$ in kW

**Step 1. Angular velocity and peripheral speed:**
$$\omega = \frac{2\pi\,N}{60} = \frac{2\pi \times 300}{60} = 31.416\ \text{rad}/\text{s}$$
$$u_1 = \omega \cdot r_1 = 31.416 \times 0.60 = 18.85\ \text{m}/\text{s}$$

**Step 2. Inlet whirl velocity:**
$$V_{w1} = V_1 \cos\alpha_1 = 25 \times \cos(20^\circ) = 25 \times 0.9397 = 23.49\ \text{m}/\text{s}$$

**Step 3. Mass flow rate:**
$$\dot{m} = \rho\,Q = 1000 \times 0.12 = 120\ \text{kg}/\text{s}$$

**Step 4. Shaft torque $T$:**
Since water exits radially, $V_{w2} = 0$:
$$T = \dot{m}\,(V_{w1}\,r_1 - 0) = 120 \times (23.49 \times 0.60) = 120 \times 14.095 = 1691.4\ \text{N}\cdot\text{m}$$

**Step 5. Power developed $P$:**
$$P = T \cdot \omega = 1691.4 \times 31.416 = 53{,}137\ \text{W} = 53.14\ \text{kW}$$
*(Or using $P = \dot{m}\,V_{w1}\,u_1 = 120 \times 23.49 \times 18.85 = 53{,}137\ \text{W}$)*

> [!note] What to take away
> Because exit whirl is zero ($V_{w2} = 0$), all angular momentum carried into the wheel was successfully converted into useful shaft torque!

## Civil engineering connections

- **Hydroelectric Generators:** Shaft torque calculated here directly dictates the shaft diameter, keyway shear capacity, and bearing sizing connecting the turbine to the electrical generator.
- **Hydraulic Pump Impellers:** A centrifugal pump operates on the exact reverse principle: a motor applies shaft torque $T$ to inject angular momentum into stationary water, lifting it hundreds of meters into elevated municipal reservoirs.

## Three things to remember

1. **Angular momentum principle:** Torque equals rate of change of angular momentum: $T = \dot{m}(V_{w1} r_1 \pm V_{w2} r_2)$.
2. **Only whirl counts:** Flow velocity $V_f$ has zero lever arm and produces zero torque.
3. **Power relation:** $P = T \cdot \omega = \dot{m}(V_{w1} u_1 \pm V_{w2} u_2)$.
