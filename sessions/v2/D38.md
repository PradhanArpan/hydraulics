## The equations no human can solve by hand

In 1822, Claude-Louis Navier and George Gabriel Stokes wrote the differential equations that govern every droplet of water moving in the universe.

Yet for two hundred years, mathematicians and engineers were completely helpless: these non-linear equations could not be solved with pen and paper for any real river, spillway, or bridge pier. How do modern civil engineers solve the unsolvable?

## The Foundations of Computational Fluid Dynamics (CFD)

CFD is the art and science of replacing continuous differential equations with discrete algebraic equations that computers can solve.

### 1. The Governing Navier-Stokes Equations
For incompressible 3D fluid flow ($\rho = \text{constant}$):

1. **Conservation of Mass (Continuity):**
   $$\nabla \cdot \vec{u} = \frac{\partial u}{\partial x} + \frac{\partial v}{\partial y} + \frac{\partial w}{\partial z} = 0$$

2. **Conservation of Momentum (Newton's 2nd Law):**
   $$\rho \left( \frac{\partial \vec{u}}{\partial t} + (\vec{u} \cdot \nabla)\vec{u} \right) = -\nabla p + \mu \nabla^2 \vec{u} + \rho\,\vec{g}$$

*In words: fluid acceleration (local + convective) equals pressure gradient force plus viscous friction shear plus gravity body force.*

### 2. The Three Steps of Every CFD Model
1. **Pre-Processing:**
   - Define the physical geometry (dam, spillway, river channel).
   - Divide the volume into a mesh of cells (discretization: structured vs unstructured).
   - Specify boundary conditions (inlet velocity, outlet pressure, wall roughness).
2. **Solver Execution:**
   - The numerical engine (OpenFOAM, SimFlow, HEC-RAS, ANSYS Fluent) solves algebraic matrix equations iteratively until residuals drop below a convergence tolerance ($< 10^{-4}$).
3. **Post-Processing:**
   - Visualize depth contours, velocity vectors, flood inundation maps, and streamlines.

### 3. Verification vs Validation (The V&V Habit)
- **Verification:** *Did we solve the equations right?* (Checking that the mesh is fine enough, time steps are stable, and numerical roundoff error is small).
- **Validation:** *Did we solve the right equations?* (Comparing computer depth and velocity predictions against physical flume gauge measurements).

## Why non-linear convective terms make hand-calculation impossible

Click through the convective acceleration breakdown.

```steps
say: Acceleration of a fluid particle consists of two parts: local unsteady change and spatial convective change.
eq: \vec{a} = \frac{D\vec{u}}{Dt} = \frac{\partial\vec{u}}{\partial t} + (\vec{u} \cdot \nabla)\vec{u}
why: By the chain rule of partial derivatives following a moving particle.
---
say: In the x-direction, the convective acceleration term expands into three terms.
eq: (\vec{u}\cdot\nabla)u = u\frac{\partial u}{\partial x} + v\frac{\partial u}{\partial y} + w\frac{\partial u}{\partial z}
why: Velocity u is carried across space by velocities u, v, and w.
---
say: Notice that velocity multiplies its own derivative: u * (du/dx).
eq: u \cdot \frac{\partial u}{\partial x} \quad (\text{Non-linear product})
why: Multiplying the unknown variable by its own rate of change makes the equation non-linear.
---
say: Non-linear equations cannot be solved by simple superposition or textbook calculus.
eq: \text{Solution requires discrete numerical approximations: Finite Difference / Finite Volume}
why: Computers solve these non-linear products by evaluating values at cell centers and iterating until equilibrium!
```

## Convergence residual check on a spillway simulation

> [!note] Problem
> A civil engineer runs a 3D CFD simulation of water flowing over an ogee dam spillway using SimFlow / OpenFOAM.
> The initial mass continuity residual on iteration 1 is $R_0 = 1.0$.
> The solver executes 500 iterations. The target convergence criterion is that all scaled residuals must drop below $1.0 \times 10^{-4}$.
> The recorded residuals are:
> - Iteration 100: $R = 2.4 \times 10^{-2}$
> - Iteration 300: $R = 8.5 \times 10^{-4}$
> - Iteration 500: $R = 6.2 \times 10^{-5}$
> Determine if the solution is converged, and explain why stopping at iteration 200 would have produced an incorrect water level.

**Step 1. Check against convergence tolerance:**
$$\text{Tolerance } \epsilon = 1.0 \times 10^{-4} = 0.0001$$
At iteration 500:
$$R_{500} = 6.2 \times 10^{-5} = 0.000062 < 0.0001$$
Since $R_{500} < \epsilon$, the simulation is **strictly converged**.

**Step 2. Analysis of premature stopping at iteration 200:**
At iteration 100, the residual was $0.024$ ($2.4\%$ mass imbalance).
If the engineer stopped the solver at iteration 200 where $R \approx 3 \times 10^{-3}$, mass conservation is violated: more water would be exiting the spillway than entering from the reservoir, producing false water surface profiles and incorrect pressures!

> [!note] What to take away
> Never trust pretty colored velocity pictures from CFD software until you have verified that numerical residuals have dropped below $10^{-4}$!

## Civil engineering connections

- **Spillway Aeration Slot Design:** Before pouring thousands of cubic metres of concrete on high dam spillways, civil engineers use 3D two-phase CFD (Volume of Fluid method, VOF) to model where air will be entrained by aeration ramps, eliminating cavitation risk without costly full-scale physical trial-and-error.
- **Bridge Scour CFD:** 3D turbulence models ($k-\epsilon$ and Reynolds Stress Models) simulate horseshoe vortices at bridge pier noses, predicting maximum scour depths during 100-year flood events.

<!--beat:recap|Recap-->
## Three things to remember

1. **CFD discretizes physics:** Continuous Navier-Stokes differential equations are converted into discrete algebraic finite volume balances.
2. **Residual convergence:** Always ensure numerical residuals drop below $10^{-4}$ before accepting simulated water depths.
3. **Verify then validate:** First verify numerical mesh and stability, then validate against laboratory flume or river gauge data.
