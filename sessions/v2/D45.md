## Calibration, Validation & The Nash-Sutcliffe Efficiency

- **Calibration:** Adjusting uncertain parameters (primarily Manning's $n$ and eddy viscosity) within physical bounds until model outputs match observed gauge data for a specific historical flood event.
- **Validation (Verification):** Running the calibrated model on an entirely independent storm event **without modifying any parameters** to prove predictive reliability.

### The Nash-Sutcliffe Efficiency (NSE)
The gold standard statistical metric in river engineering ($-\infty < \text{NSE} \le 1.0$):
$$\text{NSE} = 1 - \frac{\sum_{t=1}^N (O_t - S_t)^2}{\sum_{t=1}^N (O_t - \bar{O})^2}$$

where:
- $O_t$ = Observed gauge value at time $t$.
- $S_t$ = Simulated model value at time $t$.
- $\bar{O}$ = Mean of all observed values.

| NSE Value | Model Performance Rating |
| :--- | :--- |
| $\text{NSE} > 0.75$ | **Very Good** |
| $0.65 < \text{NSE} \le 0.75$ | **Good** |
| $0.50 < \text{NSE} \le 0.65$ | **Satisfactory** |
| $\text{NSE} \le 0.50$ | **Unsatisfactory** (The mean of observed data is more accurate than the model!) |

### Root Mean Square Error (RMSE)
Measures error in physical units (meters or $\text{m}^3/\text{s}$):
$$\text{RMSE} = \sqrt{\frac{1}{N} \sum_{t=1}^N (O_t - S_t)^2}$$

## Why the Nash-Sutcliffe metric evaluates predictive skill

Step through the mathematical meaning of the NSE ratio.

```steps
say: Consider the numerator of the NSE fraction: the sum of squared model residuals.
eq: \text{SS}_{\text{res}} = \sum_{t=1}^N (O_t - S_t)^2
why: Measures the total squared error between simulated model predictions and observed field data.
---
say: Consider the denominator: the sum of squared deviations from the observed mean.
eq: \text{SS}_{\text{tot}} = \sum_{t=1}^N (O_t - \bar{O})^2
why: Represents the variance of the observed data if you simply guessed the average value every hour.
---
say: Compute the ratio of model error variance to natural data variance.
eq: \text{NSE} = 1 - \frac{\text{SS}_{\text{res}}}{\text{SS}_{\text{tot}}}
why: Compares model skill against the simplest possible benchmark (the mean value).
---
say: Interpret the limiting cases:
eq: \text{NSE} = 1.0 \implies \text{Perfect match}; \quad \text{NSE} = 0 \implies \text{Model is no better than guessing } \bar{O}
why: An NSE less than 0 means the model is worse than a simple horizontal line at the average depth!
```

## Computing goodness-of-fit for a river model

> [!note] Problem
> A 2D HEC-RAS river model simulated flood stages during a 6-hour monsoon storm.
> The table shows observed gauge stages $O_t$ and simulated stages $S_t$.
>
> | Hour ($t$) | Observed $O_t$ (m) | Simulated $S_t$ (m) |
> | :---: | :---: | :---: |
> | 1 | 2.00 | 2.10 |
> | 2 | 2.60 | 2.80 |
> | 3 | 4.10 | 4.20 |
> | 4 | 3.70 | 3.60 |
> | 5 | 3.00 | 2.90 |
> | 6 | 2.30 | 2.20 |
>
> 1. Calculate the mean observed stage $\bar{O}$.
> 2. Compute the residual sum of squares and total sum of squares.
> 3. Calculate $\text{NSE}$ and $\text{RMSE}$, and classify model performance.

**Step 1. Mean observed stage $\bar{O}$:**
$$\sum O_t = 2.00 + 2.60 + 4.10 + 3.70 + 3.00 + 2.30 = 17.70\text{ m}$$
$$\bar{O} = \frac{17.70}{6} = 2.95\text{ m}$$

**Step 2. Compute squared differences:**
- $(O_1 - S_1)^2 = (-0.10)^2 = 0.0100$; $(O_1 - \bar{O})^2 = (-0.95)^2 = 0.9025$
- $(O_2 - S_2)^2 = (-0.20)^2 = 0.0400$; $(O_2 - \bar{O})^2 = (-0.35)^2 = 0.1225$
- $(O_3 - S_3)^2 = (-0.10)^2 = 0.0100$; $(O_3 - \bar{O})^2 = (+1.15)^2 = 1.3225$
- $(O_4 - S_4)^2 = (+0.10)^2 = 0.0100$; $(O_4 - \bar{O})^2 = (+0.75)^2 = 0.5625$
- $(O_5 - S_5)^2 = (+0.10)^2 = 0.0100$; $(O_5 - \bar{O})^2 = (+0.05)^2 = 0.0025$
- $(O_6 - S_6)^2 = (+0.10)^2 = 0.0100$; $(O_6 - \bar{O})^2 = (-0.65)^2 = 0.4225$

$$\sum (O_t - S_t)^2 = 0.0900\text{ m}^2, \quad \sum (O_t - \bar{O})^2 = 3.3350\text{ m}^2$$

**Step 3. Compute metrics:**
$$\text{NSE} = 1 - \frac{0.0900}{3.3350} = 1 - 0.02698 = 0.973$$
$$\text{RMSE} = \sqrt{\frac{0.0900}{6}} = \sqrt{0.0150} = 0.122\text{ m}$$

**Step 4. Model performance evaluation:**
- $\text{NSE} = 0.973 > 0.75$ $\to$ **Very Good / Outstanding performance**.
- The average prediction error across the flood event is only $12.2\text{ cm}$.

## Three things to remember

1. **Always calibrate and validate:** An uncalibrated model cannot be used for engineering design.
2. **Target $\text{NSE} > 0.75$:** Quantifies model goodness-of-fit against real river staff gauges.
3. **The hydraulic engineer's mission:** Combine fundamental physics, rigorous mathematics, and modern computational tools to manage water for humanity safely and sustainably.
