## One equation to rule them all

In 1754, the Swiss mathematical genius Leonhard Euler wrote a single formula that explains every rotating fluid machine on Earth — from historic water wheels to massive dam turbines, aircraft jet engines, and rocket fuel pumps.

```predict

o: By dynamic impulse (velocity drop) and reaction pressure drop (expansion through blade passages)
o: Purely through gravity
a: 2
why: Fluid transfers energy both by slowing down its absolute speed (impulse) and by dropping pressure as it expands through converging blade passages (reaction). Euler's equation captures both simultaneously.
```

## The Euler Turbine Equation

The fundamental equation of turbomachinery gives the work done per unit weight of fluid (called the **Euler Head $H_e$**):

$$H_e = \frac{V_{w1}\,u_1 \pm V_{w2}\,u_2}{g}$$

*In words: the theoretical head extracted by the turbine runner in metres of fluid column.*

### The Three Energy Components of Euler Head

By trigonometry on the inlet and outlet velocity triangles ($V_r^2 = V^2 + u^2 - 2\,u\,V\cos\alpha = V^2 + u^2 - 2\,u\,V_w$), Euler head can be identically rewritten into three distinct physical energy terms:

$$H_e = \underbrace{\frac{V_1^2 - V_2^2}{2g}}_{\text{Term 1: Dynamic Impulse}} + \underbrace{\frac{u_1^2 - u_2^2}{2g}}_{\text{Term 2: Centrifugal Head}} + \underbrace{\frac{V_{r2}^2 - V_{r1}^2}{2g}}_{\text{Term 3: Reaction Pressure Head}}$$

### Physical Meaning of the Three Terms:
1. **Term 1 $\left(\frac{V_1^2 - V_2^2}{2g}\right)$:** Change in absolute kinetic energy of water. This is the **impulse effect** (dominant in Pelton turbines).
2. **Term 2 $\left(\frac{u_1^2 - u_2^2}{2g}\right)$:** Change in centrifugal pressure head as fluid moves from outer radius $r_1$ to inner radius $r_2$.
3. **Term 3 $\left(\frac{V_{r2}^2 - V_{r1}^2}{2g}\right)$:** Change in relative kinetic energy. If blade passages contract, relative velocity increases ($V_{r2} > V_{r1}$), causing a static pressure drop across the runner. This is the **reaction effect** (dominant in Francis and Kaplan turbines).

### Impulse vs Reaction Turbines
- **Pure Impulse Turbine (Pelton Wheel):** Water flows at constant atmospheric pressure across buckets ($r_1 = r_2 \implies u_1 = u_2$, and $V_{r2} \approx V_{r1}$). Only **Term 1** produces work!
- **Reaction Turbine (Francis/Kaplan):** Water completely fills the runner passages under pressure. Work is produced by a combination of all three terms.

## Derive the three-term expansion of Euler's equation

Click through the trigonometric identity to separate the three energy forms.

```steps
say: From the cosine rule applied to the inlet velocity triangle with angle alpha_1.
eq: V_{r1}^2 = V_1^2 + u_1^2 - 2\,u_1\,V_1 \cos\alpha_1 = V_1^2 + u_1^2 - 2\,u_1\,V_{w1}
why: By definition, V_w1 = V_1 * cos(alpha_1) is the tangential projection of absolute velocity.
---
say: Rearrange to solve for the inlet whirl product u_1 * V_w1.
eq: u_1\,V_{w1} = \frac{1}{2}\left( V_1^2 + u_1^2 - V_{r1}^2 \right)
why: Simple algebraic rearrangement of the cosine law.
---
say: Apply the exact same cosine rule to the outlet velocity triangle.
eq: u_2\,V_{w2} = \frac{1}{2}\left( V_2^2 + u_2^2 - V_{r2}^2 \right)
why: The identical vector geometry holds at the runner exit.
---
say: Subtract outlet from inlet: u_1 * V_w1 - u_2 * V_w2.
eq: u_1\,V_{w1} - u_2\,V_{w2} = \frac{V_1^2 - V_2^2}{2} + \frac{u_1^2 - u_2^2}{2} + \frac{V_{r2}^2 - V_{r1}^2}{2}
why: Grouping terms of the same velocity type together.
---
say: Divide by acceleration due to gravity g.
eq: H_e = \frac{u_1\,V_{w1} - u_2\,V_{w2}}{g} = \frac{V_1^2 - V_2^2}{2g} + \frac{u_1^2 - u_2^2}{2g} + \frac{V_{r2}^2 - V_{r1}^2}{2g}
why: The Euler head is rigorously partitioned into dynamic impulse, centrifugal head, and reaction pressure head!
```

## Calculating Euler head on a radial runner

> [!note] Problem
> In an inward flow radial turbine, water enters the runner at $r_1 = 0.50$ m with velocity $V_1 = 20$ m/s at $\alpha_1 = 15^\circ$ to the tangent. The blade speed at inlet is $u_1 = 12$ m/s.
> Water discharges at $r_2 = 0.25$ m with zero whirl velocity ($V_{w2} = 0$, radial exit) and absolute exit velocity $V_2 = 4.5$ m/s.
> 1. Calculate the inlet whirl velocity $V_{w1}$.
> 2. Calculate the theoretical Euler head $H_e$ extracted by the runner.
> 3. If the actual available net head is $H = 25.0$ m, find the hydraulic efficiency $\eta_h$.

**Step 1. Inlet whirl velocity $V_{w1}$:**
$$V_{w1} = V_1 \cos(15^\circ) = 20 \times 0.9659 = 19.32\ \text{m}/\text{s}$$

**Step 2. Euler head $H_e$:**
Since $V_{w2} = 0$:
$$H_e = \frac{V_{w1}\,u_1 - 0}{g} = \frac{19.32 \times 12}{9.81} = \frac{231.84}{9.81} = 23.63\ \text{m}$$

**Step 3. Hydraulic efficiency $\eta_h$:**
$$\eta_h = \frac{H_e}{H} = \frac{23.63}{25.0} = 0.945 = 94.5\%$$

> [!note] What to take away
> Out of $25.0$ m of water head available from the reservoir, the runner converts $23.63$ m into mechanical shaft work ($94.5\%$ efficiency), leaving only $1.37$ m lost to friction and exit kinetic energy!

## Civil engineering connections

- **Hydro Plant Selection:** The three terms in Euler's equation directly dictate whether civil engineers choose an impulse Pelton turbine (high heads $> 300$ m, pure impulse Term 1) or a reaction Francis/Kaplan turbine (medium to low heads $< 300$ m, reaction Terms 2 and 3).
- **Draft Tube Design:** Below a Francis runner, water still carries exit velocity $V_2$. Civil engineers construct a gradually expanding concrete draft tube to recover up to $80\%$ of $\frac{V_2^2}{2g}$ as suction head!

<!--beat:recap|Recap-->
## Three things to remember

1. **Euler Turbine Equation:** $H_e = \frac{V_{w1} u_1 \pm V_{w2} u_2}{g}$ gives the head extracted by any runner.
2. **Three energy forms:** Impulse head $\frac{V_1^2 - V_2^2}{2g}$, centrifugal head $\frac{u_1^2 - u_2^2}{2g}$, and reaction head $\frac{V_{r2}^2 - V_{r1}^2}{2g}$.
3. **Radial discharge rule:** Setting exit whirl $V_{w2} = 0$ maximizes energy extraction.
