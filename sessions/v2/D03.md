## Bypassing the unsolvable equations of fluid flow

In 1921, the brilliant Hungarian engineer Theodore von Kármán looked at the Navier-Stokes differential equations. They were far too difficult to solve by hand for practical engineering shapes.

Instead of solving every microscopic fluid particle point by point, von Kármán drew a simple box around the whole boundary layer and balanced the gross momentum going in against the gross momentum coming out.

```predict
q: If wall friction pushes backward against the flowing fluid, what must happen to the momentum of the fluid as it travels downstream?
o: Fluid momentum decreases downstream; this loss directly equals the wall friction force
o: Fluid momentum increases because friction speeds water up
o: Fluid momentum stays perfectly constant
a: 1
why: By Newton's second law, net backward wall friction force equals the rate of decrease of forward momentum flux.
```

Today you will see how von Kármán's momentum integral equation turns any assumed velocity profile into an exact formula for boundary layer growth and skin friction drag.

## The Von Kármán Momentum Integral Equation

For steady, incompressible flow over a flat plate with zero pressure gradient ($dp/dx = 0$):

$$\frac{\tau_0}{\rho\,U_\infty^2} = \frac{d\theta}{dx}$$

*In words: the wall shear stress divided by dynamic pressure equals the rate at which momentum thickness grows along the plate.*

### Boundary Conditions for Laminar Profiles

To find the velocity profile $\frac{u}{U_\infty} = f\left(\frac{y}{\delta}\right)$, any realistic model must satisfy:

1. **At the solid wall ($y = 0$):** $u = 0$ (no-slip condition).
2. **At the outer edge ($y = \delta$):** $u = U_\infty$ (smooth merge with free-stream).
3. **Smooth slope at outer edge ($y = \delta$):** $\frac{\partial u}{\partial y} = 0$ (no shear discontinuity).
4. **Curvature at wall ($y = 0$):** $\frac{\partial^2 u}{\partial y^2} = 0$ (from momentum equation with $dp/dx = 0$).

### Blasius Exact vs Cubic Polynomial Approximation

| Parameter | Blasius Exact Solution | Cubic Profile Approximation |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
| **Boundary thickness $\delta$** | $\delta = \frac{5.0\,x}{\sqrt{Re_x}}$ | $\delta = \frac{4.64\,x}{\sqrt{Re_x}}$ |
| **Displacement thickness $\delta^*$** | $\delta^* = \frac{1.72\,x}{\sqrt{Re_x}}$ | $\delta^* = \frac{1.74\,x}{\sqrt{Re_x}}$ |
| **Momentum thickness $\theta$** | $\theta = \frac{0.664\,x}{\sqrt{Re_x}}$ | $\theta = \frac{0.647\,x}{\sqrt{Re_x}}$ |
| **Local friction coeff $C_f$** | $C_f = \frac{0.664}{\sqrt{Re_x}}$ | $C_f = \frac{0.647}{\sqrt{Re_x}}$ |
| **Total drag coeff $C_D$** | $C_D = \frac{1.328}{\sqrt{Re_L}}$ | $C_D = \frac{1.294}{\sqrt{Re_L}}$ |

The simple cubic polynomial comes within $3\%$ of the exact numerical Blasius solution!

## Derive Von Kármán's momentum integral

Click through the control volume balance to see how the momentum equation emerges.

```steps
say: Consider a control volume of length dx, width b, and height extending beyond delta into the free stream.
eq: \Sigma F_x = \dot{M}_{\text{out}} - \dot{M}_{\text{in}}
why: Newton's second law: net horizontal force equals rate of momentum outflow minus momentum inflow.
---
say: In flat plate flow, pressure is constant everywhere, so the only horizontal force is the backward wall shear stress.
eq: \Sigma F_x = -\tau_0\,b\,dx
why: Pressure forces on the left and right faces are identical and cancel out completely.
---
say: Mass entering from the leading face plus mass entering through the top boundary must equal mass leaving the trailing face.
eq: \dot{m}_{\text{top}} = \dot{m}_{\text{out}} - \dot{m}_{\text{in}} = \frac{d}{dx}\left[ \int_0^\delta \rho\,b\,u\,dy \right] dx
why: Fluid pushed out of the boundary layer must cross the top boundary into the free stream.
---
say: The fluid entering the top boundary carries the full free-stream momentum per unit mass, U.
eq: \dot{M}_{\text{top}} = U_\infty\,\dot{m}_{\text{top}} = U_\infty \frac{d}{dx}\left[ \int_0^\delta \rho\,b\,u\,dy \right] dx
why: Fluid at the outer edge is traveling at the undisturbed speed U.
---
say: Combine net momentum flux and equate to the wall shear force.
eq: -\tau_0\,b\,dx = \frac{d}{dx}\left[ \int_0^\delta \rho\,b\,u^2\,dy \right]dx - U_\infty \frac{d}{dx}\left[ \int_0^\delta \rho\,b\,u\,dy \right]dx
why: Rearranging terms directly yields tau_0 = rho * U^2 * d(theta)/dx.
```

## Laminar boundary layer on a glass flume bed

> [!note] Problem
> Water ($\rho = 1000\ \text{kg}/\text{m}^3$, $\nu = 1.0 \times 10^{-6}\ \text{m}^2/\text{s}$) flows smoothly at $U_\infty = 0.40$ m/s over a smooth glass flume plate.
> At distance $x = 0.80$ m from the leading edge, calculate using the Blasius solution:
> 1. Reynolds number $Re_x$
> 2. Nominal boundary layer thickness $\delta$
> 3. Momentum thickness $\theta$
> 4. Wall shear stress $\tau_0$

**Step 1. Reynolds number $Re_x$:**
$$Re_x = \frac{U_\infty\,x}{\nu} = \frac{0.40 \times 0.80}{1.0 \times 10^{-6}} = 320{,}000 = 3.2 \times 10^5$$
Since $Re_x < 5 \times 10^5$, the flow is strictly laminar.

**Step 2. Boundary layer thickness $\delta$:**
$$\delta = \frac{5.0\,x}{\sqrt{Re_x}} = \frac{5.0 \times 0.80}{\sqrt{320{,}000}} = \frac{4.0}{565.69} = 0.00707\ \text{m} = 7.07\ \text{mm}$$

**Step 3. Momentum thickness $\theta$:**
$$\theta = \frac{0.664\,x}{\sqrt{Re_x}} = \frac{0.664 \times 0.80}{565.69} = 0.000939\ \text{m} = 0.939\ \text{mm}$$

**Step 4. Wall shear stress $\tau_0$:**
$$C_f = \frac{0.664}{\sqrt{Re_x}} = \frac{0.664}{565.69} = 1.174 \times 10^{-3}$$
$$\tau_0 = \frac{1}{2}\rho\,U_\infty^2\,C_f = \frac{1}{2} \times 1000 \times (0.40)^2 \times 1.174 \times 10^{-3} = 0.0939\ \text{N}/\text{m}^2 = 0.094\ \text{Pa}$$

> [!note] What to take away
> The boundary layer is only $7$ mm thick after $80$ cm of travel, and the shear stress is under $0.1$ Pa. Viscous forces are subtle but steady!

## Quick check

```quiz
q: How does the local wall shear stress tau_0 change as you move downstream along a flat plate in laminar flow?
o: It increases downstream
o: It remains constant
o: It decreases downstream as 1 / sqrt(x)
a: 3
why: As the boundary layer thickens, the velocity gradient at the wall (du/dy) flattens, causing wall shear to drop as 1/sqrt(x).
---
q: What is the ratio between total drag coefficient C_D and local trailing edge friction coefficient C_f in laminar flow?
o: C_D = C_f
o: C_D = 2 * C_f
o: C_D = 0.5 * C_f
a: 2
why: Integrating tau_0 proportional to x^(-1/2) over length L yields an average shear stress that is exactly twice the local value at x = L.
```

## Civil engineering connections

- **Sediment Settling Basins:** In water treatment plants, water flows slowly over flat settling plates (lamella clarifiers). Keeping the flow laminar ensures suspended silt particles settle peacefully without turbulent eddies kicking them back up.
- **Hydraulic Model Flumes:** In laboratory testing of dams and weirs, engineers must check whether boundary layers on flume walls are laminar or turbulent to correctly scale up results to real rivers.

## Three things to remember

1. **Von Kármán equation:** $\frac{\tau_0}{\rho U_\infty^2} = \frac{d\theta}{dx}$ relates wall shear friction directly to momentum thickness growth.
2. **Laminar thickness growth:** $\delta = \frac{5.0 x}{\sqrt{Re_x}}$, meaning thickness grows with $\sqrt{x}$.
3. **Friction drops downstream:** Wall shear stress $\tau_0 \propto \frac{1}{\sqrt{x}}$, so the leading edge experiences the highest friction.
