## Water dividing at an angle

Hold a kitchen spatula at a tilt under the tap. Water hits the slanted blade, and instead of splashing randomly, it cleanly splits into two streams: a large sheet flowing forward and a smaller sheet rolling backward!

```predict
q: A jet strikes an inclined plate tilted at angle theta to the jet axis. Because the plate is smooth and frictionless, can the plate exert any shear force parallel to its own surface?
o: Yes, large frictional shear force
o: No, a smooth surface can only push perpendicular (normal) to itself
o: Only if the plate is tilted at 90 degrees
a: 2
why: An ideal smooth surface cannot exert shear resistance parallel to its plane. Therefore, all hydrodynamic force must act perpendicular (normal) to the plate.
```

Today you will see how normal momentum balance predicts both the force on an inclined plate and the exact mathematical division of discharge ($Q_1$ vs $Q_2$).

## Impact on an Inclined Stationary Plate

Let a horizontal jet of velocity $V$ and area $a$ strike a stationary plate inclined at angle $\theta$ to the horizontal jet axis.

### 1. Normal Force Exerted on Plate $F_n$

Since the plate is smooth, force can only act normal to the plate:
- Initial velocity normal to plate: $V_{n,\text{in}} = V \sin\theta$.
- Velocity normal to plate after impact: $V_{n,\text{out}} = 0$.

$$F_n = \rho\,a\,V\,(V\sin\theta - 0) = \rho\,a\,V^2 \sin\theta$$

*In words: normal force is the perpendicular component of total jet momentum flux.*

### 2. Components in Horizontal & Vertical Directions
- **Horizontal force (along jet direction):**
  $$F_x = F_n \sin\theta = \rho\,a\,V^2 \sin^2\theta$$
- **Vertical force (perpendicular to jet direction):**
  $$F_y = F_n \cos\theta = \rho\,a\,V^2 \sin\theta \cos\theta = \frac{1}{2}\rho\,a\,V^2 \sin(2\theta)$$

### 3. Discharge Division Along the Plate ($Q_1$ and $Q_2$)

Parallel to the plate, there is no external force. By conservation of momentum along the plate:

$$Q_1 = \frac{Q}{2}(1 + \cos\theta)$$
$$Q_2 = \frac{Q}{2}(1 - \cos\theta)$$

*In words: the forward flow $Q_1$ and backward flow $Q_2$ are partitioned by $(1 \pm \cos\theta)$.*

Notice that when $\theta = 90^\circ$, $\cos(90^\circ) = 0 \implies Q_1 = Q_2 = Q/2$.

## Derive the discharge split $Q_1$ and $Q_2$

Click through the steps to see how zero parallel friction divides the flow.

```steps
say: Apply momentum balance parallel to the plate surface. Since the plate is frictionless, net force parallel to the plate is zero.
eq: \Sigma F_{\text{parallel}} = 0
why: An ideal smooth surface exerts zero shear resistance along its face.
---
say: Initial momentum of the incoming jet parallel to the plate is mass flow rate times V * cos(theta).
eq: M_{\text{in, parallel}} = \rho\,Q\,V \cos\theta
why: Resolving the incoming horizontal velocity vector V along the plate inclined at angle theta gives V * cos(theta).
---
say: After striking, discharge Q1 moves forward with speed V, and discharge Q2 moves backward with speed V.
eq: M_{\text{out, parallel}} = \rho\,Q_1\,V - \rho\,Q_2\,V = \rho\,V\,(Q_1 - Q_2)
why: By Bernoulli's equation with negligible elevation and atmospheric pressure, exit speed equals incoming speed V.
---
say: Equate momentum in to momentum out parallel to the plate.
eq: \rho\,V\,(Q_1 - Q_2) = \rho\,Q\,V \cos\theta \implies Q_1 - Q_2 = Q \cos\theta
why: Cancelling rho * V on both sides leaves the discharge difference.
---
say: Combine with total mass conservation Q1 + Q2 = Q.
eq: Q_1 = \frac{Q}{2}(1 + \cos\theta), \quad Q_2 = \frac{Q}{2}(1 - \cos\theta)
why: Adding and subtracting the two linear equations directly solves for Q1 and Q2.
```

## Force and flow split on a slanted deflector plate

> [!note] Problem
> A water jet of diameter $d = 40$ mm and speed $V = 25$ m/s strikes a smooth stationary flat plate inclined at $\theta = 60^\circ$ to the axis of the jet. Calculate:
> 1. Total discharge $Q$ of the jet
> 2. Normal force $F_n$ on the plate
> 3. Horizontal and vertical forces $F_x$ and $F_y$
> 4. Discharges $Q_1$ and $Q_2$ into which the jet divides

**Step 1. Jet area and total discharge $Q$:**
$$a = \frac{\pi}{4}(0.040)^2 = 1.2566 \times 10^{-3}\ \text{m}^2$$
$$Q = a \cdot V = 1.2566 \times 10^{-3} \times 25 = 0.03142\ \text{m}^3/\text{s} = 31.42\ \text{L}/\text{s}$$

**Step 2. Normal force $F_n$:**
$$\sin(60^\circ) = 0.8660, \quad \cos(60^\circ) = 0.5000$$
$$F_n = \rho\,a\,V^2 \sin\theta = 1000 \times 1.2566 \times 10^{-3} \times (25)^2 \times 0.8660$$
$$F_n = 785.4 \times 0.8660 = 680.2\ \text{N}$$

**Step 3. Horizontal and vertical force components:**
$$F_x = F_n \sin(60^\circ) = 680.2 \times 0.8660 = 589.0\ \text{N}$$
$$F_y = F_n \cos(60^\circ) = 680.2 \times 0.5000 = 340.1\ \text{N}$$

**Step 4. Discharges $Q_1$ and $Q_2$:**
$$Q_1 = \frac{31.42}{2}(1 + 0.500) = 15.71 \times 1.50 = 23.57\ \text{L}/\text{s} \quad (75\%)$$
$$Q_2 = \frac{31.42}{2}(1 - 0.500) = 15.71 \times 0.50 = 7.85\ \text{L}/\text{s} \quad (25\%)$$

> [!note] What to take away
> Notice how $Q_1 + Q_2 = 23.57 + 7.85 = 31.42$ L/s, exactly conserving mass, while three times more water flows forward than backward!

## Quick check

```quiz
q: If a flat plate is tilted at theta = 30 degrees to the jet, what fraction of total discharge flows forward as Q_1?
o: 50%
o: 75%
o: 93.3%
a: 3
why: Q_1 / Q = 0.5 * (1 + cos 30) = 0.5 * (1 + 0.866) = 0.933 (93.3%).
---
q: At what inclination angle theta is the vertical force F_y maximized?
o: theta = 0 degrees
o: theta = 45 degrees
o: theta = 90 degrees
a: 2
why: F_y = 0.5 * rho * a * V^2 * sin(2*theta), which reaches its peak when sin(2*theta) = 1, so 2*theta = 90 deg -> theta = 45 deg.
```

## Civil engineering connections

- **Spillway Flip Buckets:** High-velocity water shooting off a dam spillway hits an inclined ski-jump lip, throwing the jet high into the air to dissipate kinetic energy safely in the river bed downstream.
- **Sediment Vane Deflectors:** Submerged inclined guide vanes installed in river intakes steer heavy bed-load gravel away from irrigation canal headworks.

<!--beat:recap|Recap-->
## Three things to remember

1. **Normal force formula:** $F_n = \rho a V^2 \sin\theta$ because frictionless surfaces only push perpendicularly.
2. **Discharge split:** $Q_1 = \frac{Q}{2}(1 + \cos\theta)$ forward, $Q_2 = \frac{Q}{2}(1 - \cos\theta)$ backward.
3. **Maximum vertical thrust:** $F_y$ peaks at a $45^\circ$ inclination angle.
