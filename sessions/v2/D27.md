## Mountain rain into clean electricity

High up in the mountains, a reservoir holds millions of tonnes of rainwater. Hundreds of metres below in the valley floor, a powerhouse hums, sending electricity across the national grid. How does the potential energy of trapped mountain water turn into watts?


## Layout & Energy Terms of Hydroelectric Plants

### 1. Key Components of the Scheme
- **Catchment & Dam:** Stores river water creating the reservoir storage.
- **Headworks & Trash Rack:** Screens out debris and logs.
- **Headrace Canal or Tunnel:** Conveys water from the intake toward the penstock.
- **Surge Tank:** A vertical open chamber that absorbs sudden water hammer pressure waves when the turbine valves close rapidly.
- **Penstock:** Heavy high-pressure steel pipes conducting water down the steep hillside to the turbines.
- **Powerhouse & Tailrace:** Houses the hydraulic turbines and electrical generators; water exits into the tailrace stream.

### 2. Heads to Know
- **Gross Head $H_g$ (m):** Total elevation difference between headrace reservoir surface and tailrace water level.
- **Frictional Head Loss $h_f$ (m):** Loss in the penstock pipe (Darcy-Weisbach: $h_f = \frac{4 f L V^2}{2 g D}$).
- **Net Available Head $H$ (m):** Effective head available at the turbine entrance:

$$H = H_g - h_f$$

*In words: net head equals gross elevation drop minus penstock pipe friction losses.*

### 3. The Four Efficiencies of a Turbine
1. **Hydraulic Efficiency $\eta_h$:**
   $$\eta_h = \frac{\text{Runner Power (work done by water on blades)}}{\text{Total Water Power entering turbine}} = \frac{P_{\text{runner}}}{\rho\,g\,Q\,H}$$
2. **Mechanical Efficiency $\eta_m$:**
   $$\eta_m = \frac{\text{Shaft Power (power delivered to generator)}}{\text{Runner Power}} = \frac{P_{\text{shaft}}}{P_{\text{runner}}}$$
3. **Volumetric Efficiency $\eta_v$:**
   $$\eta_v = \frac{\text{Water discharge striking blades}}{\text{Total discharge supplied by penstock}} = \frac{Q - \Delta Q}{Q}$$
4. **Overall Efficiency $\eta_o$:**
   $$\eta_o = \frac{\text{Shaft Power}}{\text{Total Water Power}} = \eta_h \cdot \eta_m \cdot \eta_v$$

$$\text{Shaft Power } P = \eta_o\,\rho\,g\,Q\,H$$

*In words: useful shaft power equals overall efficiency times water density times gravity times flow rate times net head.*

## Derive overall efficiency $\eta_o$

Click through the chain of energy conversions.

```steps
say: Total potential energy delivered by the penstock to the turbine per second is water power.
eq: P_{\text{water}} = \rho\,g\,Q\,H
why: Mass flow rate rho * Q multiplied by specific gravity head g * H.
---
say: Fluid friction across the runner blades and exit swirl losses reduce energy transfer to runner power.
eq: P_{\text{runner}} = \eta_h \cdot P_{\text{water}}
why: By definition of hydraulic efficiency eta_h.
---
say: Bearing friction, windage, and mechanical shaft losses reduce runner power to shaft output.
eq: P_{\text{shaft}} = \eta_m \cdot P_{\text{runner}}
why: By definition of mechanical efficiency eta_m.
---
say: Substitute runner power into shaft power.
eq: P_{\text{shaft}} = \eta_m \cdot (\eta_h \cdot P_{\text{water}}) = (\eta_h \cdot \eta_m) \cdot (\rho\,g\,Q\,H)
why: Overall efficiency eta_o is the direct product of hydraulic, mechanical, and volumetric efficiencies.
```

## Powerhouse output calculation

> [!note] Problem
> A hydroelectric power plant has a reservoir level $320$ m above the tailrace (gross head $H_g = 320$ m).
> The penstock is $1200$ m long, $1.5$ m in diameter, with friction factor $f = 0.0075$ ($4f = 0.030$).
> The penstock discharges $Q = 4.0\ \text{m}^3/\text{s}$ ($\rho = 1000\ \text{kg}/\text{m}^3$).
> The turbine has hydraulic efficiency $\eta_h = 92\%$ and mechanical efficiency $\eta_m = 95\%$. Calculate:
> 1. Frictional head loss in penstock $h_f$
> 2. Net head $H$ available to the turbine
> 3. Shaft power output in megawatts (MW)
> 4. Overall plant efficiency $\eta_o$

**Step 1. Flow velocity in penstock:**
$$A_p = \frac{\pi}{4}(1.5)^2 = 1.767\ \text{m}^2$$
$$V_p = \frac{Q}{A_p} = \frac{4.0}{1.767} = 2.264\ \text{m}/\text{s}$$

**Step 2. Frictional head loss $h_f$:**
$$h_f = \frac{4\,f\,L\,V_p^2}{2\,g\,D} = \frac{0.030 \times 1200 \times (2.264)^2}{2 \times 9.81 \times 1.5} = \frac{184.52}{29.43} = 6.27\ \text{m}$$

**Step 3. Net head $H$:**
$$H = H_g - h_f = 320.0 - 6.27 = 313.73\ \text{m}$$

**Step 4. Overall efficiency $\eta_o$:**
$$\eta_o = \eta_h \times \eta_m = 0.92 \times 0.95 = 0.874 = 87.4\%$$

**Step 5. Shaft power output $P$:**
$$P = \eta_o\,\rho\,g\,Q\,H = 0.874 \times 1000 \times 9.81 \times 4.0 \times 313.73 = 10{,}759{,}345\ \text{W} = 10.76\ \text{MW}$$

> [!note] What to take away
> Sizing penstock pipes properly kept friction loss under $2\%$ ($6.27$ m out of $320$ m), ensuring over $10.7$ MW of green power feeds the grid!

## Civil engineering connections

- **Penstock Anchor Blocks:** Penstocks experience immense downward thrust on steep hillsides. Structural civil engineers cast massive reinforced concrete anchor blocks keyed directly into bedrock at every vertical bend.
- **Idukki Arch Dam (Kerala):** Water drops through underground pressure shafts under an impressive head of $660$ m, driving six Pelton turbines generating $780$ MW of power!

<!--beat:recap|Recap-->
## Three things to remember

1. **Net head formula:** $H = H_{\text{gross}} - h_{\text{friction}}$ is the true head driving the turbine.
2. **Four efficiencies:** Hydraulic, mechanical, volumetric, and overall: $\eta_o = \eta_h \cdot \eta_m \cdot \eta_v$.
3. **Power formula:** $P = \eta_o\,\rho\,g\,Q\,H$ dictates the electrical capacity of every hydroelectric powerhouse.
