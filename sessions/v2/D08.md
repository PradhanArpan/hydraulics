## Where is the river fastest?

If you drop a leaf in a stream, it floats along the middle. But if you push a stick down to the bed, it barely moves. Water does not travel at one speed.

```predict
q: In a narrow canal, where is the water moving at its absolute maximum speed?
o: Right at the solid bed
o: Exactly on the top free water surface
o: Dipped 5% to 25% below the top surface
a: 3
why: Water sticks to the bottom and side walls (zero velocity). In narrow channels, side-wall friction creates secondary circulation currents that push the maximum velocity below the free surface.
```

Today you will see how engineers measure the true average speed of a river, and why using one single average speed needs correction factors for energy and momentum.

## From point velocity $u$ to mean velocity $V$

The speed of water at any single coordinate $(y, z)$ is the **point velocity $u$**.
Because $u$ varies from zero at the walls to a peak near the top, we define the **mean velocity $V$**:

$$V = \frac{Q}{A} = \frac{1}{A}\iint u\,dA$$

*In words: the total volume of water passing per second, divided by the total water area.*

### How hydrographers measure $V$ in the field (IS:1192)

You cannot measure every point in a rushing river. Indian Standard **IS:1192** provides three practical rules using a current-meter:

1. **Single-point method (shallow streams):** Measure speed at **$0.6$ of the depth from the surface** ($v_{0.6}$). Then:
   $$V \approx v_{0.6}$$
2. **Two-point method (deep rivers):** Measure at **$0.2$ depth** and **$0.8$ depth** from the surface, then average them:
   $$V \approx \frac{v_{0.2} + v_{0.8}}{2}$$
3. **Surface float method:** Time a floating cork on the water surface ($v_{\text{surf}}$) and multiply by a reduction coefficient $k \approx 0.85$:
   $$V \approx 0.85\,v_{\text{surf}}$$

## Why $V$ underestimates Energy and Momentum

Click through each step to see why $V$ is accurate for mass, but needs $\alpha$ and $\beta$ for energy and momentum.

```steps
say: Continuity (mass flow) depends directly on the first power of velocity.
eq: Q = \int u\,dA = V \cdot A
why: Because $u$ is to the power 1, the mean velocity $V$ reproduces the exact discharge by definition.
---
say: Kinetic energy depends on velocity cubed ($u^3$).
eq: \text{KE flux} = \int \tfrac{1}{2}\,\rho\,u^3\,dA \quad \text{vs} \quad \tfrac{1}{2}\,\rho\,V^3\,A
why: Cubing numbers exaggerates the fast parts. Fast water carries vastly more energy than slow water near the bed.
---
say: Because the average of cubes is always greater than the cube of the average, we multiply by the Coriolis factor $\alpha$.
eq: \alpha = \frac{\int u^3\,dA}{V^3\,A} \ge 1.0
why: In straight uniform channels $\alpha \approx 1.05$ to $1.15$. In natural rivers with floodplains $\alpha$ can reach $1.3$ to $1.6$.
---
say: Momentum depends on velocity squared ($u^2$). We multiply by the Boussinesq factor $\beta$.
eq: \beta = \frac{\int u^2\,dA}{V^2\,A} \ge 1.0
why: For any non-uniform velocity profile, $\alpha - 1 \approx 3(\beta - 1)$, so $\alpha > \beta \ge 1.0$ always.
```

## The Three Conservation Laws in open channels

> [!note] Governing equations
> With $\alpha$ and $\beta$ introduced, open channel flow is governed by three fundamental principles:

1. **Conservation of Mass (Continuity):**
   $$Q = A_1 V_1 = A_2 V_2 = \text{constant}$$
2. **Conservation of Energy (Bernoulli with $\alpha$):**
   $$z_1 + y_1 + \alpha_1\frac{V_1^2}{2g} = z_2 + y_2 + \alpha_2\frac{V_2^2}{2g} + h_L$$
3. **Conservation of Linear Momentum (with $\beta$):**
   $$\sum F_x = \rho\,Q\,(\beta_2 V_2 - \beta_1 V_1)$$

> [!note] When do you use which?
> - Use **Energy** when head losses are smooth and small (gradual transitions, humps, venturi flumes).
> - Use **Momentum** when head losses are violent and unknown (hydraulic jump, hydraulic bore, sluice gates).

## Remember

> [!note] Day 8 in five lines
> - Point velocity $u$ is zero at boundaries and highest near the top surface.
> - Mean velocity $V = Q/A$ represents the average speed through the section.
> - Current meter standards: $V \approx v_{0.6}$ (single point) or $\frac{v_{0.2} + v_{0.8}}{2}$ (two-point).
> - Coriolis factor $\alpha \ge 1$ corrects kinetic energy head $\alpha \frac{V^2}{2g}$.
> - Boussinesq factor $\beta \ge 1$ corrects linear momentum $\beta \rho Q V$.
>
