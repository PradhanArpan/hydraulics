## The sudden explosion of chaotic eddies

Light an incense stick in a calm room. The smoke rises in a straight, elegant ribbon for a few inches — calm and laminar. Then suddenly, it bursts into wild, swirling, chaotic eddies — it turns turbulent!

```predict
q: When flow over a long plate switches from laminar to turbulent, does the boundary layer thickness suddenly swell much faster or shrink?
o: It suddenly swells much thicker because chaotic eddies toss fluid high into the outer stream
o: It shrinks into a razor-thin film
o: It stays completely unchanged
a: 1
why: Turbulent eddies violently mix slow fluid near the wall with fast fluid above, causing the boundary layer thickness to expand rapidly (growing as x^(4/5) instead of x^(1/2)).
```

Today you will see how turbulent mixing reshapes the velocity profile into the famous **1/7th power law** and how to calculate turbulent drag.

## The Turbulent Boundary Layer

Above $Re_{cr} \approx 5 \times 10^5$, laminar flow becomes unstable. Velocity fluctuations ($u', v'$) appear, creating Reynolds shear stresses ($-\rho \overline{u'v'}$) that dwarf molecular viscosity.

### The 1/7th Power Law Velocity Profile

Prandtl proposed that for moderate Reynolds numbers ($5 \times 10^5 < Re_x < 10^7$), the turbulent velocity profile can be approximated as:

$$\frac{u}{U_\infty} = \left(\frac{y}{\delta}\right)^{1/7}$$

*In words: velocity rises very rapidly near the wall and stays nearly uniform across the rest of the layer.*

From this profile:
- **Displacement thickness:** $\delta^* = \frac{1}{8}\delta = 0.125\,\delta$
- **Momentum thickness:** $\theta = \frac{7}{72}\delta \approx 0.0972\,\delta$
- **Shape factor:** $H = \frac{\delta^*}{\theta} = \frac{9}{7} \approx 1.286$

### Wall Shear Stress & Growth Laws

Using Blasius's empirical pipe formula adapted for flat plates:

$$\tau_0 = 0.0225\,\rho\,U_\infty^2 \left(\frac{\nu}{U_\infty\,\delta}\right)^{1/4}$$

Substituting into von Kármán's integral equation yields:

$$\delta(x) = \frac{0.371\,x}{Re_x^{1/5}}$$

*In words: turbulent boundary layer thickness grows as $x^{4/5} = x^{0.8}$, much faster than laminar's $x^{0.5}$.*

- **Local skin friction coefficient:**
$$C_f = \frac{0.0592}{Re_x^{1/5}}$$

- **Total drag coefficient (assuming turbulent from leading edge):**
$$C_D = \frac{0.074}{Re_L^{1/5}}$$

## Derive turbulent thickness growth $\delta(x)$

Click through the steps to see how the 1/7th power law combines with von Kármán's equation.

```steps
say: From the 1/7th power law, momentum thickness is a constant fraction of nominal thickness.
eq: \theta = \frac{7}{72}\delta \implies \frac{d\theta}{dx} = \frac{7}{72}\frac{d\delta}{dx}
why: Integrating (y/delta)^(1/7) * [1 - (y/delta)^(1/7)] from 0 to delta gives exactly 7/72 * delta.
---
say: Von Kármán's momentum integral equates wall shear stress to the growth of momentum thickness.
eq: \tau_0 = \rho\,U_\infty^2 \frac{d\theta}{dx} = \frac{7}{72}\rho\,U_\infty^2 \frac{d\delta}{dx}
why: Horizontal momentum balance across the boundary layer.
---
say: Equate this to the Blasius empirical wall shear stress formula.
eq: 0.0225\,\rho\,U_\infty^2 \left(\frac{\nu}{U_\infty\,\delta}\right)^{1/4} = \frac{7}{72}\rho\,U_\infty^2 \frac{d\delta}{dx}
why: Both expressions describe the exact same wall shear stress tau_0.
---
say: Cancel rho * U^2 and separate variables delta and x.
eq: \delta^{1/4}\,d\delta = 0.2314 \left(\frac{\nu}{U_\infty}\right)^{1/4} dx
why: Group all delta terms on the left and all x terms on the right.
---
say: Integrate both sides assuming delta = 0 at x = 0.
eq: \frac{4}{5}\delta^{5/4} = 0.2314 \left(\frac{\nu}{U_\infty}\right)^{1/4} x \implies \delta = \frac{0.371\,x}{Re_x^{1/5}}
why: Raising to the power 4/5 yields the famous 1/5th power growth law for turbulent boundary layers.
```

## Turbulent boundary layer on a concrete canal lining test plate

> [!note] Problem
> A smooth steel test plate, $L = 5.0$ m long and $b = 1.5$ m wide, is submerged in a water flume ($U_\infty = 2.5$ m/s, $\nu = 1.0 \times 10^{-6}\ \text{m}^2/\text{s}$, $\rho = 1000\ \text{kg}/\text{m}^3$).
> Assuming the boundary layer becomes turbulent right from the leading edge, calculate:
> 1. Reynolds number at the trailing edge $Re_L$
> 2. Boundary layer thickness $\delta$ at $x = 5.0$ m
> 3. Total skin friction drag force $F_D$ on one side of the plate

**Step 1. Reynolds number at trailing edge:**
$$Re_L = \frac{U_\infty\,L}{\nu} = \frac{2.5 \times 5.0}{1.0 \times 10^{-6}} = 12{,}500{,}000 = 1.25 \times 10^7$$
This is well into the turbulent regime ($Re_L > 5 \times 10^5$).

**Step 2. Boundary layer thickness $\delta$ at trailing edge:**
$$Re_L^{1/5} = (1.25 \times 10^7)^{0.2} = 26.265$$
$$\delta = \frac{0.371\,L}{Re_L^{1/5}} = \frac{0.371 \times 5.0}{26.265} = 0.0706\ \text{m} = 70.6\ \text{mm}$$

**Step 3. Total drag coefficient $C_D$:**
$$C_D = \frac{0.074}{Re_L^{1/5}} = \frac{0.074}{26.265} = 2.817 \times 10^{-3}$$

**Step 4. Total drag force $F_D$:**
$$A_{\text{plate}} = L \times b = 5.0 \times 1.5 = 7.50\ \text{m}^2$$
$$F_D = \frac{1}{2}\rho\,U_\infty^2\,A\,C_D = \frac{1}{2} \times 1000 \times (2.5)^2 \times 7.50 \times 2.817 \times 10^{-3} = 66.0\ \text{N}$$

> [!note] What to take away
> Notice how thick the turbulent layer grew: $70.6$ mm! If it had stayed laminar, it would only have been $\frac{5 \times 5.0}{\sqrt{1.25 \times 10^7}} \approx 7.1$ mm — ten times thinner!

o: Turbulent flow increases water density
a: 2

## Civil engineering connections

- **Spillway Chute Aeration:** As water accelerates down a steep concrete dam spillway, the turbulent boundary layer grows outward from the floor until it reaches the free surface. Once it hits the surface, intense turbulence entraps air, turning the water white and protecting the concrete against cavitation pitting.
- **Canal Lining Erosion:** Turbulent wall shear stress determines whether unlined earth canals scour during monsoon floods.

<!--beat:recap|Recap-->
## Three things to remember

1. **1/7th Power Law:** $\frac{u}{U_\infty} = (y/\delta)^{1/7}$ captures the steep near-wall velocity rise of turbulent boundary layers.
2. **Rapid thickness growth:** $\delta(x) \propto x^{0.8}$, growing much faster and thicker than laminar flow.
3. **Higher friction drag:** Wall shear stress is significantly higher in turbulent flow because eddy mixing transports high momentum directly to the wall.
