## Why is solving for normal depth so tricky?

If you know the canal dimensions and water depth $y$, computing discharge $Q$ with Manning's equation is simple arithmetic. But what if you are given the required flood discharge $Q$, and you need to find the depth $y$?

```predict
q: Manning's equation for a trapezoidal channel is $Q = \frac{1}{n} A R^{2/3} S_0^{1/2}$. Can you rearrange this equation by simple algebra to isolate depth $y$ on the left-hand side as $y = \dots$?
o: Yes, easily with basic high-school algebra
o: No, because depth $y$ is trapped non-linearly inside both area $A$ and wetted perimeter $P$ to the 2/3 power
o: Yes, but only if the channel is made of glass
a: 2
why: Manning's equation is mathematically implicit in $y$. Because $y$ appears in both the numerator (area $A$) and denominator (wetted perimeter $P$), it cannot be solved analytically in closed form. Numerical root-finding or trial and error is required.
```

Today you will see how engineers find the normal depth $y_n$ using Bisection, Newton-Raphson, and spreadsheets like Excel Goal Seek.

## Two numerical strategies for normal depth

We want to find $y_n$ such that:

$$f(y) = \frac{1}{n}\,A(y)\,R(y)^{2/3}\,S_0^{1/2} - Q = 0$$

1. **Bisection Method (Bracketing):**
   - Pick a lower bound $y_{\text{low}}$ (where $Q_{\text{calc}} < Q$) and an upper bound $y_{\text{high}}$ (where $Q_{\text{calc}} > Q$).
   - Test the midpoint $y_{\text{mid}} = (y_{\text{low}} + y_{\text{high}}) / 2$.
   - Keep the half where the sign changes. Guaranteed to converge, but takes 10 to 15 steps.
2. **Newton-Raphson Method (Gradient):**
   - Start from an initial guess $y_0$.
   - Update using the tangent slope:
     $$y_{k+1} = y_k - \frac{f(y_k)}{f'(y_k)}$$
   - Converges quadratically in 3 to 4 iterations.

> [!note] In Practice: Excel Goal Seek & Python
> In engineering practice, nobody computes 15 bisection steps by hand. You set up the Manning formula in Excel, click **Data ➔ What-If Analysis ➔ Goal Seek**, set the target cell to $Q$ by changing cell $y$, and Excel solves it instantly.

## Newton-Raphson step for a rectangular channel

Click through each step of the Newton-Raphson formulation.

```steps
say: Define the error function $f(y)$ as computed flow minus required flow.
eq: f(y) = \frac{\sqrt{S_0}}{n}\frac{(b\,y)^{5/3}}{(b + 2y)^{2/3}} - Q
why: When $f(y) = 0$, the depth $y$ is the true normal depth $y_n$.
---
say: Take the derivative $f'(y) = dQ/dy$.
eq: f'(y) = \frac{\sqrt{S_0}}{n} A^{2/3} R^{2/3}\left[\frac{5}{3} T - \frac{2}{3} R \frac{dP}{dy}\right]
why: For a rectangular channel, top width is $T = b$ and perimeter derivative is $dP/dy = 2$.
---
say: Apply the Newton correction formula.
eq: y_{\text{new}} = y_{\text{old}} - \frac{f(y_{\text{old}})}{f'(y_{\text{old}})}
why: The tangent line points directly toward the root.
```

## Find normal depth by trial and error

> [!note] Problem
> A rectangular canal has bed width $b = 3.0$ m, bed slope $S_0 = 0.001$, and Manning $n = 0.015$. Find the normal depth $y_n$ required to carry $Q = 8.0\ \text{m}^3/\text{s}$.

**Step 1. Group the constant term (Section Factor $A R^{2/3}$).**
$$A\,R^{2/3} = \frac{Q\,n}{\sqrt{S_0}} = \frac{8.0 \times 0.015}{\sqrt{0.001}} = \frac{0.120}{0.03162} = 3.795$$

**Step 2. Trial 1: Guess $y = 1.0$ m.**
- $A = 3.0 \times 1.0 = 3.0\ \text{m}^2$.
- $P = 3.0 + 2(1.0) = 5.0$ m.
- $R = 3.0 / 5.0 = 0.60$ m.
- $A R^{2/3} = 3.0 \times (0.60)^{2/3} = 3.0 \times 0.711 = 2.134 < 3.795$ (Too small, depth must be larger!).

**Step 3. Trial 2: Guess $y = 1.5$ m.**
- $A = 3.0 \times 1.5 = 4.5\ \text{m}^2$.
- $P = 3.0 + 2(1.5) = 6.0$ m.
- $R = 4.5 / 6.0 = 0.75$ m.
- $A R^{2/3} = 4.5 \times (0.75)^{2/3} = 4.5 \times 0.825 = 3.715 \approx 3.795$ (Very close!).

**Step 4. Trial 3: Refine to $y = 1.52$ m.**
- $A = 3.0 \times 1.52 = 4.56\ \text{m}^2$.
- $P = 3.0 + 2(1.52) = 6.04$ m.
- $R = 4.56 / 6.04 = 0.755$ m.
- $A R^{2/3} = 4.56 \times (0.755)^{2/3} = 3.780 \approx 3.795$.
- Normal depth is **$y_n = 1.52$ m**.

> [!note] What to take away
> Three structured trial steps give normal depth to within 1 centimetre accuracy.
