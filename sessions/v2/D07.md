## Water with a lid vs water with a sky

Think of water flowing through a closed underground pipe, and water flowing in an open irrigation canal. Both carry water, but they behave very differently.

```predict
q: If you drill a small hole in the top of a running pipe under pressure, water squirts out high into the air. What happens if you touch the free water surface of an open canal?
o: Water also squirts out under pressure
o: Nothing squirts; the surface is open to the atmosphere at zero gauge pressure
o: The water level immediately drops to zero
a: 2
why: An open channel has a free surface exposed directly to the air. The gauge pressure at the water surface is always zero (atmospheric).
```

Today you will see why the free surface makes open-channel flow driven purely by gravity, and how to measure the channel shape.

## Five geometric elements to know

Every canal cross-section is defined by five key numbers:

1. **Depth of flow $y$ (m):** Vertical distance from the lowest bed point to the free water surface.
2. **Wetted area $A$ (m²):** Cross-sectional area occupied solely by the flowing liquid.
3. **Wetted perimeter $P$ (m):** Length of the channel boundary in contact with water (the air surface does not count!).
4. **Hydraulic radius $R$ (m):** The ratio of water area to wetted perimeter.

$$R = \frac{A}{P}$$

*In words: how much flow area you get for each metre of rubbing boundary friction.*

5. **Hydraulic depth $D$ (m):** The average water depth across the section.

$$D = \frac{A}{T}$$

*In words: area divided by top width $T$. For a rectangular channel, $D = y$.*

| Flow Type | What stays constant? | Everyday Example |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
| **Steady** | Flow depth and speed do not change with time | Irrigation canal running at steady rate |
| **Unsteady** | Flow changes from minute to minute | Flood wave surging down a river after rain |
| **Uniform** | Depth and velocity stay identical along the length | Long canal with constant slope and cross-section |
| **Varied (Non-uniform)** | Depth changes along the length | Water backing up behind a dam or drop |

## Why hydraulic radius $R$ matters for friction

Click through each step to see why engineers care about $R = A/P$.

```steps
say: Water experiences friction only where it touches the solid walls, along the wetted perimeter $P$.
eq: F_{\text{friction}} \propto P \cdot L
why: The free water surface touches air, which offers almost zero resistance compared to concrete or soil.
---
say: Gravity pulls the entire body of water forward proportional to its volume, which is area $A$ times length $L$.
eq: F_{\text{gravity}} \propto A \cdot L
why: Every kilogram of water in area $A$ feels the downward pull of gravity.
---
say: Divide the gravity drive by the wall friction drag.
eq: \frac{F_{\text{gravity}}}{F_{\text{friction}}} \propto \frac{A \cdot L}{P \cdot L} = \frac{A}{P} = R
why: A larger hydraulic radius $R$ means more driving power and less rubbing resistance per unit volume.
```

## Geometry elements of a trapezoidal canal

> [!note] Problem
> A trapezoidal irrigation canal has a bottom width $b = 3.5$ m and side slopes of $1:1.5$ (1 vertical to 1.5 horizontal, so $m = 1.5$). The water depth is $y = 1.2$ m. Find the wetted area $A$, wetted perimeter $P$, hydraulic radius $R$, top width $T$, and hydraulic depth $D$.

**Step 1. Top width $T$.**
$T = b + 2\,m\,y = 3.5 + 2(1.5)(1.2) = 3.5 + 3.6 = 7.10$ m.

**Step 2. Wetted area $A$.**
$A = (b + m\,y)\,y = (3.5 + 1.5 \times 1.2) \times 1.2 = (3.5 + 1.8) \times 1.2 = 6.36\ \text{m}^2$.

**Step 3. Wetted perimeter $P$.**
$P = b + 2\,y\sqrt{1 + m^2} = 3.5 + 2(1.2)\sqrt{1 + 1.5^2} = 3.5 + 2.4(1.803) = 7.83$ m.

**Step 4. Hydraulic radius $R$.**
$R = \dfrac{A}{P} = \dfrac{6.36}{7.83} = 0.812$ m.

**Step 5. Hydraulic depth $D$.**
$D = \dfrac{A}{T} = \dfrac{6.36}{7.10} = 0.896$ m.

> [!note] What to take away
> Notice that $R = 0.812$ m is smaller than the actual depth $y = 1.20$ m. Wall friction from both sides drags the average effective depth down.

## Remember

> [!note] Day 7 in five lines
> - Open channel flow has a free water surface at atmospheric gauge pressure ($p = 0$).
> - Gravity drives the flow down the channel slope.
> - Hydraulic radius $R = A / P$ measures the flow efficiency against wall friction.
> - Hydraulic depth $D = A / T$ represents the average depth.
> - Uniform flow means depth is constant along the channel ($dy/dx = 0$).
