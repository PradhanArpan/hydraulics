## The flat cell that hides a canyon

In traditional computer models, if you create a computation cell $50$ metres wide, the software assumes the ground inside that cell is completely flat.

What happens if a narrow concrete drainage ditch or levee wall only two metres wide runs right through that $50$-metre cell? In traditional models, the ditch is erased!

How does modern HEC-RAS 2D capture microscopic ditches inside giant cells?

```predict
q: How does HEC-RAS 2D allow large computation cells (e.g. 30 m to 50 m) to accurately capture narrow terrain ditches and ridges without requiring millions of tiny cells?
o: It ignores terrain features
o: It pre-computes high-resolution elevation-volume tables for every cell and elevation-area tables for every cell face (subgrid bathymetry)
o: It asks the user to draw ditches by hand
a: 2
why: HEC-RAS 2D subgrid technology extracts underlying 1-metre LiDAR terrain data into hydraulic property tables for each cell. Even if a cell is 50 m wide, the solver knows the exact water volume it holds at every millimeter of water elevation!
```

Today you will see the **Finite Volume Method (FVM)** and the revolutionary **subgrid bathymetry** concept that made HEC-RAS 2D the world's most widely used river modeling software.

## The Finite Volume Method (FVM) & Mesh Architecture

Modern hydraulic software (HEC-RAS 2D, OpenFOAM, SimFlow, Telemac) uses the **Finite Volume Method (FVM)**:
- The river domain is divided into a mesh of contiguous polygonal control volumes (cells).
- Governing conservation equations are integrated over each cell volume.
- By **Gauss's Divergence Theorem**, volume integrals of flux divergences become simple surface integrals across cell faces:

$$\int_{\Omega} \nabla \cdot \vec{F}\,d\Omega = \oint_{\partial \Omega} \vec{F} \cdot \vec{n}\,dS \approx \sum_{\text{faces } k} F_k \cdot A_k$$

*In words: net change inside the cell equals the sum of mass and momentum crossing its boundary faces! This guarantees 100% exact numerical mass conservation.*

### 1. Mesh Types
- **Structured Grids:** Regular rectangular or curvilinear grids (indices $i, j$). Simple, but rigid around curved river banks.
- **Unstructured Grids:** Flexible arbitrary polygons (triangles, quadrilaterals, pentagons, hexagons up to 8-sided cells in HEC-RAS). Effortlessly aligns with winding river channels and winding bridge roads!

### 2. The Breakthrough: Subgrid Bathymetry
In conventional CFD grids:
- A cell has a single flat elevation $z$. Water enters only when depth exceeds that flat floor.
In HEC-RAS 2D subgrid bathymetry:
- The solver reads high-resolution underlying terrain (e.g. 1 m LiDAR DEM).
- For each computational cell, it computes an **Elevation-Volume Curve $V(z)$**.
- For each cell face, it computes an **Elevation-Area Curve $A(z)$** and **Wetted Perimeter $P(z)$**.
- A cell can be partially wet and partially dry! Water can flow through a small channel incision inside a cell even when the rest of the cell is completely dry.

### 3. Breaklines in Mesh Generation
When generating an unstructured 2D mesh, civil engineers must draw **Breaklines** along:
- Levees and flood walls
- Road embankments and railway berms
- Channel bank tops

*A breakline forces the automated mesher to align cell faces precisely along the crest of the embankment, ensuring floodwaters cannot artificially seep through a levee until water over-tops the crest!*

## Why Gauss's divergence theorem guarantees mass conservation

Click through the finite volume transformation.

```steps
say: Write the general conservation law for any conserved fluid property phi inside cell volume Omega.
eq: \frac{\partial}{\partial t}\int_{\Omega} \phi\,d\Omega + \int_{\Omega} \nabla \cdot \vec{F}\,d\Omega = \int_{\Omega} S\,d\Omega
why: Fundamental Reynolds transport theorem for an arbitrary control volume.
---
say: Apply Gauss's divergence theorem to the spatial flux divergence term.
eq: \int_{\Omega} \nabla \cdot \vec{F}\,d\Omega = \oint_{\partial\Omega} \vec{F} \cdot \vec{n}\,dS
why: Mathematical theorem converting a volume integral of divergence into a surface integral of flux.
---
say: For a polygonal cell with N discrete flat faces of area A_k and outward normal unit vector n_k.
eq: \oint_{\partial\Omega} \vec{F} \cdot \vec{n}\,dS = \sum_{k=1}^N (\vec{F}_k \cdot \vec{n}_k) A_k
why: Discretizing the continuous perimeter into a sum over all N polygon faces.
---
say: The flux leaving cell i through face k enters neighboring cell j with equal and opposite sign.
eq: (\vec{F} \cdot \vec{n})_{i \to j} = -(\vec{F} \cdot \vec{n})_{j \to i}
why: Internal face fluxes cancel out pairwise across the entire mesh.
---
say: Summing across all cells in the river model:
eq: \sum_{\text{all cells}} \Delta \text{Mass} = \text{Boundary Inflow} - \text{Boundary Outflow}
why: Mass can neither be magically created nor destroyed inside the interior mesh!
```

## Cell storage volume using subgrid table

> [!note] Problem
> A $30\ \text{m} \times 30\ \text{m}$ square cell in HEC-RAS 2D covers an irregular ground surface with a small ditch.
> High-resolution LiDAR pre-processing computes the cell's stage-storage curve:
> - At elevation $z = 100.0$ m (ditch bed): Water volume $V = 0\ \text{m}^3$
> - At elevation $z = 101.0$ m (ditch bankfull): Water volume $V = 120\ \text{m}^3$ (water covers only $120\ \text{m}^2$ of the $900\ \text{m}^2$ cell)
> - At elevation $z = 102.5$ m (fully flooded cell): Water volume $V = 1470\ \text{m}^3$
> A flood inflow adds $\Delta V = 350\ \text{m}^3$ of water into the cell during a 10-second time step.
> Determine the new water surface elevation $z_{\text{new}}$ using linear interpolation between hydraulic table rows.

**Step 1. Initial cell state:**
Assume the cell was initially at bankfull ditch stage:
$$z_1 = 101.0\ \text{m}, \quad V_1 = 120\ \text{m}^3$$

**Step 2. New total water volume:**
$$V_{\text{new}} = V_1 + \Delta V = 120 + 350 = 470\ \text{m}^3$$

**Step 3. Interpolate in the subgrid property table:**
The target volume $V = 470\ \text{m}^3$ lies between table rows:
- Row A: $z_A = 101.0$ m, $V_A = 120\ \text{m}^3$
- Row B: $z_B = 102.5$ m, $V_B = 1470\ \text{m}^3$

$$\Delta V_{\text{bracket}} = 1470 - 120 = 1350\ \text{m}^3$$
$$\Delta z_{\text{bracket}} = 102.5 - 101.0 = 1.50\ \text{m}$$

Interpolation fraction:
$$f = \frac{V_{\text{new}} - V_A}{\Delta V_{\text{bracket}}} = \frac{470 - 120}{1350} = \frac{350}{1350} = 0.2593$$

**Step 4. New water surface elevation $z_{\text{new}}$:**
$$z_{\text{new}} = z_A + f \cdot \Delta z_{\text{bracket}} = 101.0 + 0.2593 \times 1.50 = 101.0 + 0.389 = 101.389\ \text{m}$$

> [!note] What to take away
> Without subgrid tables, a conventional solver would have smeared $350$ m³ across the entire $900$ m² cell, estimating a depth of only $350/900 = 0.39$ m and failing to predict that the ditch overtopped its banks!

## Civil engineering connections

- **Bridge Pier Hydrodynamic Meshing:** When modeling flow around highway bridge piers in HEC-RAS 2D or OpenFOAM, engineers use mesh refinement zones around piers with cell sizes down to $0.5$ m, transitioning smoothly out to $20$ m cells on the wider floodplain.
- **LiDAR DTM Processing:** Before generating a 2D mesh, civil engineers process raw aerial LiDAR point clouds into a bare-earth Digital Terrain Model (DTM), using GIS tools to hydro-enforce culverts and bridge openings through roadway embankments.

## Three things to remember

1. **Finite Volume Method:** Balances fluxes across polygon cell faces, guaranteeing 100% numerical mass conservation.
2. **Subgrid bathymetry power:** Pre-computed stage-storage tables capture micro-ditches and channels inside large cells without mesh explosion.
3. **Enforce breaklines:** Always draw breaklines along levees and road crests to prevent false flood leakage.
