## Lester Pelton's gold rush breakthrough

In the 1870s during the California Gold Rush, a carpenter named Lester Pelton was watching a traditional water wheel power a mine hoist. The water jet was hitting the flat wooden paddles right in the center.

Suddenly, the key holding the wheel shaft slipped, causing the wheel to shift slightly. The jet hit the *outer curved edge* of the cup instead of the middle. To everyone's astonishment, the wheel immediately accelerated to nearly double its speed!

```predict
o: To reduce the weight of the runner
a: 2
why: The central splitter edge divides the incoming round jet into two equal halves. Each half deflects through ~165 degrees sideways, producing twice the momentum change while neutralizing any net axial side force on the turbine shaft!
```

Today you will see how the physical principles of jet impact govern the complete engineering design of the **Pelton wheel runner**.

## Key Components of a Pelton Turbine

1. **Nozzle & Spear (Needle Valve):**
   - Regulates the flow rate without changing the jet velocity.
   - Jet velocity from reservoir head $H$:
     $$V_1 = C_v \sqrt{2gH} \quad (C_v \approx 0.98\text{ to }0.99)$$
2. **Runner & Double-Hemispherical Buckets:**
   - Circular disc carrying curved buckets along its outer rim.
   - Each bucket has a sharp central ridge called the **splitter** that divides the jet into two equal streams.
3. **Deflector Plate:**
   - Pivoted plate that swings into the jet within $0.1$ seconds when electrical load trips, diverting water into the tailrace without closing the penstock rapidly (preventing destructive water hammer).
4. **Breaking Jet:**
   - A tiny auxiliary nozzle shooting water backward against the bucket backs to bring the massive rotating wheel to a halt during maintenance.

### Standard Design Proportions of Pelton Buckets
Let $d$ be the jet diameter and $D$ be the pitch circle diameter of the runner wheel:

| Parameter | Standard Design Rule | Physical Purpose |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
| **Jet Ratio $m$** | $m = \frac{D}{d} \approx 10\text{ to }16$ (optimum $12$) | Prevents buckets from overcrowding the rim |
| **Speed Ratio $\phi$** | $\phi = \frac{u}{\sqrt{2gH}} \approx 0.45\text{ to }0.48$ | Delivers peak hydraulic efficiency |
| **Number of Buckets $Z$** | $Z = 15 + \frac{D}{2d} = 15 + 0.5\,m$ (Taygun's formula) | Ensures continuous jet interception without splashing |
| **Bucket Width $B$** | $B = 3.5\,d\text{ to }5.0\,d$ | Accommodates the expanded water sheet |
| **Bucket Length $L$** | $L = 2.5\,d\text{ to }3.0\,d$ | Ensures full $165^\circ$ deflection |
| **Bucket Depth $T$** | $T = 0.9\,d\text{ to }1.2\,d$ | Prevents water spilling over front lip |

## Derive maximum efficiency of a Pelton wheel

Click through the derivation of hydraulic efficiency for a Pelton runner.

```steps
say: The water enters tangentially without shock (alpha_1 = 0), so relative speed at inlet is V_1 minus u.
eq: V_{r1} = V_1 - u
why: The bucket moves in the exact same direction as the nozzle jet.
---
say: Relative speed at exit is reduced by blade friction factor k (k = 0.85 to 0.95).
eq: V_{r2} = k\,V_{r1} = k\,(V_1 - u)
why: Fluid experiences surface friction sliding across the curved bucket bowl.
---
say: Resolve relative velocity at exit angle beta_2 (typically 10 to 15 degrees).
eq: V_{w2} = V_{r2} \cos\beta_2 - u = k\,(V_1 - u)\cos\beta_2 - u
why: Whirl velocity is the horizontal component in the direction opposite to blade motion.
---
say: Driving force on the runner is mass flow rate times total whirl change.
eq: F_x = \rho\,a\,V_1\,(V_{w1} + V_{w2}) = \rho\,a\,V_1\,(V_1 - u)\,(1 + k\cos\beta_2)
why: All nozzle discharge is intercepted by successive buckets on the wheel.
---
say: Work done per second (Power) equals force times runner speed u.
eq: P = \rho\,a\,V_1\,(V_1 - u)\,u\,(1 + k\cos\beta_2)
why: Multiplying tangential thrust by pitch circle speed u.
---
say: Divide by the kinetic energy flux of the jet (0.5 * rho * a * V_1^3).
eq: \eta_h = \frac{2\,u\,(V_1 - u)\,(1 + k\cos\beta_2)}{V_1^2}
why: Hydraulic efficiency equation for a real Pelton runner.
---
say: Differentiate with respect to u and set to zero.
eq: \frac{d\eta_h}{du} = 0 \implies V_1 - 2u = 0 \implies u = \frac{V_1}{2}
why: Peak efficiency theoretically occurs at u = 0.5 V_1 (in practice, friction shifts optimum to u = 0.46 V_1).
```

## Complete Pelton runner design calculation

> [!note] Problem
> A Pelton wheel develops $4000$ kW under an effective net head $H = 250$ m while rotating at $N = 450$ rpm.
> The overall efficiency is $\eta_o = 85\%$, coefficient of velocity is $C_v = 0.98$, speed ratio is $\phi = 0.46$, and bucket deflection angle is $165^\circ$ ($\beta_2 = 15^\circ$, $\cos 15^\circ = 0.966$). Bucket friction factor is $k = 0.90$. Calculate:
> 1. Jet velocity $V_1$ and runner blade speed $u$
> 2. Total discharge $Q$ and jet diameter $d$
> 3. Pitch diameter of the runner wheel $D$
> 4. Number of buckets $Z$ using Taygun's formula
> 5. Theoretical hydraulic efficiency $\eta_h$

**Step 1. Jet velocity and blade speed:**
$$V_1 = C_v \sqrt{2gH} = 0.98 \times \sqrt{2 \times 9.81 \times 250} = 0.98 \times \sqrt{4905} = 0.98 \times 70.04 = 68.64\ \text{m}/\text{s}$$
$$u = \phi \sqrt{2gH} = 0.46 \times 70.04 = 32.22\ \text{m}/\text{s}$$

**Step 2. Total discharge required:**
$$\text{Shaft Power } P = 4000\ \text{kW} = 4{,}000{,}000\ \text{W}$$
$$\text{Total Water Power } P_w = \frac{P}{\eta_o} = \frac{4000}{0.85} = 4705.9\ \text{kW} = \rho\,g\,Q\,H$$
$$Q = \frac{4{,}705{,}882}{1000 \times 9.81 \times 250} = \frac{4{,}705{,}882}{2{,}452{,}500} = 1.919\ \text{m}^3/\text{s}$$

**Step 3. Jet diameter $d$ (assuming a single jet):**
$$Q = a \cdot V_1 = \frac{\pi}{4}d^2 \cdot V_1 \implies d = \sqrt{\frac{4\,Q}{\pi\,V_1}} = \sqrt{\frac{4 \times 1.919}{\pi \times 68.64}} = \sqrt{\frac{7.676}{215.64}} = \sqrt{0.03559} = 0.1887\ \text{m} = 188.7\ \text{mm}$$

**Step 4. Pitch diameter of runner wheel $D$:**
$$u = \frac{\pi\,D\,N}{60} \implies D = \frac{60\,u}{\pi\,N} = \frac{60 \times 32.22}{\pi \times 450} = \frac{1933.2}{1413.7} = 1.367\ \text{m}$$
Check jet ratio: $m = D/d = 1.367 / 0.1887 = 7.24$ *(For a single jet this is low; in practice, a multi-jet runner with 2 or 4 nozzles would be used to keep $m \approx 12$)*.

**Step 5. Number of buckets $Z$:**
$$Z = 15 + \frac{D}{2d} = 15 + \frac{1.367}{2 \times 0.1887} = 15 + 3.62 = 18.62 \to 19\ \text{buckets}$$

**Step 6. Theoretical hydraulic efficiency $\eta_h$:**
$$\eta_h = \frac{2\,u\,(V_1 - u)\,(1 + k\cos\beta_2)}{V_1^2}$$
$$1 + k\cos(15^\circ) = 1 + 0.90 \times 0.9659 = 1 + 0.8693 = 1.8693$$
$$\eta_h = \frac{2 \times 32.22 \times (68.64 - 32.22) \times 1.8693}{(68.64)^2} = \frac{64.44 \times 36.42 \times 1.8693}{4711.4} = \frac{4387.2}{4711.4} = 0.931 = 93.1\%$$

## Three things to remember

1. **Splitter bucket innovation:** Lester Pelton's double cup splits the jet and turns water $165^\circ$, doubling force and balancing shaft thrust.
2. **Speed ratio rule:** Operating at $u \approx 0.46 \sqrt{2gH}$ extracts maximum kinetic energy ($> 90\%$ efficiency).
