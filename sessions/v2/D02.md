## How much does a boundary layer push the world away?

Imagine water flowing through a wide rectangular flume. As water touches the side walls and bed, it slows down. Because the water near the walls slows down, the faster water in the middle must be squeezed and displaced outward to let all the volume pass through.

```predict
q: Because the boundary layer slows down fluid near the wall, does the boundary layer act like a physical solid cushion that pushes the outer streamlines outward?
o: Yes, exactly: external streamlines are displaced away from the solid wall by a definite distance delta*
o: No, fluid just disappears inside the layer
o: No, outer streamlines are pulled closer to the wall
a: 1
why: The deficit in mass flow rate caused by the slow boundary layer forces the outer streamlines outward by an equivalent thickness called displacement thickness delta*.
```

Today you will see the three mathematical thickness definitions engineers use to measure lost mass, lost momentum, and lost kinetic energy.

## Three thickness definitions to know

Because velocity inside the boundary layer increases gradually, we cannot just measure a single sharp edge. Engineers define three precise equivalent thicknesses:

### 1. Displacement Thickness $\delta^*$ (m)

The distance by which the solid boundary would have to be shifted outward in an ideal, frictionless flow to give the same mass flow rate:

$$\delta^* = \int_0^\delta \left(1 - \frac{u}{U_\infty}\right) dy$$

*In words: the thickness of zero-speed fluid that equals the total mass deficit caused by the boundary layer.*

### 2. Momentum Thickness $\theta$ (m)

The distance by which the boundary would have to be shifted outward to equal the total loss of momentum flux:

$$\theta = \int_0^\delta \frac{u}{U_\infty}\left(1 - \frac{u}{U_\infty}\right) dy$$

*In words: the thickness that represents the total momentum lost to wall friction drag.*

### 3. Energy Thickness $\delta_E$ (m)

The distance by which the boundary would have to be shifted outward to equal the total loss of kinetic energy flux:

$$\delta_E = \int_0^\delta \frac{u}{U_\infty}\left(1 - \frac{u^2}{U_\infty^2}\right) dy$$

*In words: the thickness representing the mechanical energy turned into heat by viscous friction.*

### 4. The Shape Factor $H$

The ratio of displacement thickness to momentum thickness:

$$H = \frac{\delta^*}{\theta}$$

*In words: how "fat" or full the velocity profile is. A lower shape factor means the profile is fuller and resists separation.*

| Profile Type | Velocity Law $\frac{u}{U_\infty}$ | $\frac{\delta^*}{\delta}$ | $\frac{\theta}{\delta}$ | Shape Factor $H$ |
| :-- | :-- | :-- | :-- | :-- |
| **Linear** | $\frac{y}{\delta}$ | $\frac{1}{2} = 0.500$ | $\frac{1}{6} \approx 0.167$ | $3.00$ |
| **Parabolic** | $2\frac{y}{\delta} - \left(\frac{y}{\delta}\right)^2$ | $\frac{1}{3} \approx 0.333$ | $\frac{2}{15} \approx 0.133$ | $2.50$ |
| **Cubic** | $\frac{3}{2}\frac{y}{\delta} - \frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{y}{\delta}\right)^3$ | $\frac{3}{8} = 0.375$ | $\frac{39}{280} \approx 0.139$ | $2.69$ |
| **Turbulent (1/7th)** | $\left(\frac{y}{\delta}\right)^{1/7}$ | $\frac{1}{8} = 0.125$ | $\frac{7}{72} \approx 0.097$ | $1.29$ |

## Derive displacement thickness $\delta^*$

Click through the steps to see how mass conservation directly gives the formula for $\delta^*$.

```steps
say: Consider a slice of fluid of width b and thickness dy inside the boundary layer.
eq: d\dot{m}_{\text{actual}} = \rho\,(b\,dy)\,u
why: Mass flow rate is density times area times local velocity u.
---
say: If there were no friction, the fluid in that same slice would travel at full free-stream speed U.
eq: d\dot{m}_{\text{ideal}} = \rho\,(b\,dy)\,U_\infty
why: In an ideal frictionless fluid, velocity is uniform everywhere right up to the wall.
---
say: The deficit or loss of mass flow rate in this thin strip is the difference between ideal and actual.
eq: d(\Delta\dot{m}) = \rho\,b\,(U_\infty - u)\,dy
why: Subtracting the two gives the mass that failed to pass through because of viscous slowing.
---
say: Integrate across the whole boundary layer from y = 0 to y = delta.
eq: \Delta\dot{m} = \rho\,b \int_0^\delta (U_\infty - u)\,dy = \rho\,b\,U_\infty \int_0^\delta \left(1 - \frac{u}{U_\infty}\right) dy
why: Summing all strips from the wall to the outer edge gives the total mass deficit.
---
say: Equate this total mass deficit to an equivalent layer of zero-velocity fluid of thickness delta*.
eq: \rho\,b\,U_\infty\,\delta^* = \rho\,b\,U_\infty \int_0^\delta \left(1 - \frac{u}{U_\infty}\right) dy \implies \delta^* = \int_0^\delta \left(1 - \frac{u}{U_\infty}\right) dy
why: Cancelling rho * b * U from both sides yields the exact definition of displacement thickness.
```

## Calculating thicknesses for a linear velocity profile

> [!note] Problem
> The velocity profile in a laminar boundary layer is approximated as linear:
> $$\frac{u}{U_\infty} = \frac{y}{\delta} \quad \text{for } 0 \le y \le \delta$$
> If the measured boundary layer thickness is $\delta = 12$ mm, calculate:
> 1. Displacement thickness $\delta^*$
> 2. Momentum thickness $\theta$
> 3. Energy thickness $\delta_E$
> 4. Shape factor $H$

**Step 1. Displacement thickness $\delta^*$:**
$$\delta^* = \int_0^\delta \left(1 - \frac{y}{\delta}\right) dy = \left[ y - \frac{y^2}{2\delta} \right]_0^\delta = \delta - \frac{\delta}{2} = \frac{\delta}{2}$$
$$\delta^* = \frac{12}{2} = 6.00\ \text{mm}$$

**Step 2. Momentum thickness $\theta$:**
$$\theta = \int_0^\delta \frac{y}{\delta}\left(1 - \frac{y}{\delta}\right) dy = \int_0^\delta \left(\frac{y}{\delta} - \frac{y^2}{\delta^2}\right) dy = \left[ \frac{y^2}{2\delta} - \frac{y^3}{3\delta^2} \right]_0^\delta = \frac{\delta}{2} - \frac{\delta}{3} = \frac{\delta}{6}$$
$$\theta = \frac{12}{6} = 2.00\ \text{mm}$$

**Step 3. Energy thickness $\delta_E$:**
$$\delta_E = \int_0^\delta \frac{y}{\delta}\left(1 - \frac{y^2}{\delta^2}\right) dy = \left[ \frac{y^2}{2\delta} - \frac{y^4}{4\delta^3} \right]_0^\delta = \frac{\delta}{2} - \frac{\delta}{4} = \frac{\delta}{4}$$
$$\delta_E = \frac{12}{4} = 3.00\ \text{mm}$$

**Step 4. Shape factor $H$:**
$$H = \frac{\delta^*}{\theta} = \frac{\delta/2}{\delta/6} = \frac{6}{2} = 3.00$$

> [!note] What to take away
> Notice the strict order: $\theta < \delta_E < \delta^* < \delta$. For this linear profile: $2\ \text{mm} < 3\ \text{mm} < 6\ \text{mm} < 12\ \text{mm}$.

## Civil engineering connections

- **Wind Tunnel Blockage:** When testing scale models of tall buildings or bridges in wind tunnels, the boundary layers growing along the tunnel walls narrow the effective test cross-section. Engineers expand the tunnel walls by $\delta^*$ to keep the core velocity constant.
- **Flume Calibration:** In laboratory hydraulic flumes, the effective wetted area is slightly smaller than the physical cross-section by the displacement thickness on the bed and walls.

<!--beat:recap|Recap-->
## Three things to remember

1. **Displacement thickness $\delta^*$:** The outward shift of outer streamlines caused by mass deficit ($= \int (1 - u/U) dy$).
2. **Momentum thickness $\theta$:** The loss of momentum flux caused by wall friction ($= \int \frac{u}{U}(1 - u/U) dy$), which directly equals total drag force.
3. **Shape factor $H = \delta^* / \theta$:** High for laminar flow ($H \approx 2.6$), low for turbulent flow ($H \approx 1.3$).
