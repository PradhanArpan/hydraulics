## Running away from the water

If someone throws a ball at you while you stand still, it hits hard. If you run backward away from the thrower, the ball barely taps you.

What happens when a plate moves away from a shooting water jet?

```predict
q: A water jet with speed V hits a plate moving in the same direction with speed u. Does the mass of water striking the plate per second equal rho * a * V, or rho * a * (V - u)?
o: Exactly rho * a * V
o: Exactly rho * a * (V - u) — because the plate is running away from the jet
o: Zero
a: 2
why: Because the plate moves forward at speed u, the jet catches up to the plate at relative speed (V - u). Only the mass contained in relative distance (V - u) per second can reach the plate.
```

Today you will see why a **single moving plate** can never convert more than **$29.6\%$** of jet energy, while a **series of plates on a wheel** reaches **$50\%$**.

## Moving Flat Plates: Single vs Series

### 1. Single Moving Flat Plate
When a single plate moves forward at speed $u$:
- **Relative speed entering plate:** $V_r = V - u$
- **Mass of water striking plate per second:**
  $$\dot{m}_{\text{single}} = \rho\,a\,(V - u)$$
  *(Notice it is $(V - u)$, not $V$!)*
- **Impact force $F_x$:**
  $$F_x = \dot{m}\,(V_r - 0) = \rho\,a\,(V - u)^2$$
- **Work done per second (Power):**
  $$P = F_x \cdot u = \rho\,a\,(V - u)^2 \cdot u$$
- **Efficiency $\eta$:**
  $$\eta = \frac{\text{Work done}}{\text{Kinetic energy of jet}} = \frac{\rho\,a\,(V - u)^2\,u}{\frac{1}{2}\rho\,a\,V^3} = \frac{2\,u\,(V - u)^2}{V^3}$$

Differentiating with respect to $u$ gives the maximum efficiency:
$$u_{\text{optimal}} = \frac{V}{3} \implies \eta_{\text{max}} = \frac{8}{27} \approx 29.63\%$$

### 2. Series of Flat Plates Mounted on a Wheel
In a rotating wheel, as soon as one plate moves away, the next plate steps into the path of the jet. The wheel intercepts the **entire continuous stream** of water:
- **Mass of water supplied per second:**
  $$\dot{m}_{\text{series}} = \rho\,a\,V$$
  *(All water discharged by nozzle hits a plate!)*
- **Impact force $F_x$:**
  $$F_x = (\rho\,a\,V)\,(V - u) = \rho\,a\,V\,(V - u)$$
- **Work done per second (Power):**
  $$P = F_x \cdot u = \rho\,a\,V\,(V - u)\,u$$
- **Efficiency $\eta$:**
  $$\eta = \frac{\rho\,a\,V\,(V - u)\,u}{\frac{1}{2}\rho\,a\,V^3} = \frac{2\,u\,(V - u)}{V^2}$$

Differentiating with respect to $u$:
$$u_{\text{optimal}} = \frac{V}{2} \implies \eta_{\text{max}} = \frac{2(V/2)(V/2)}{V^2} = 50.0\%$$

| Configuration | Mass striking per sec | Impact Force $F_x$ | Optimal Speed $u$ | Maximum Efficiency $\eta_{\text{max}}$ |
| :-- | :-- | :-- | :-- | :-- |
| **Single Moving Plate** | $\rho a (V - u)$ | $\rho a (V - u)^2$ | $u = \frac{1}{3}V$ | $\frac{8}{27} \approx 29.6\%$ |
| **Series of Plates on Wheel** | $\rho a V$ | $\rho a V (V - u)$ | $u = \frac{1}{2}V$ | $\frac{1}{2} = 50.0\%$ |

## Derive maximum efficiency of series of plates

Click through the calculus optimization to find the $50\%$ limit.

```steps
say: Write the power equation for a series of plates on a rotating wheel.
eq: P = \rho\,a\,V\,(V - u)\,u = \rho\,a\,V\,(V\,u - u^2)
why: All incoming water mass rho * a * V is intercepted by successive plates, changing speed by (V - u).
---
say: Differentiate power P with respect to plate velocity u and set to zero.
eq: \frac{dP}{du} = \rho\,a\,V\,(V - 2u) = 0 \implies V - 2u = 0
why: The peak power occurs where the derivative of work with respect to blade speed vanishes.
---
say: Solve for the optimal blade speed u.
eq: u_{\text{optimal}} = \frac{V}{2}
why: Maximum power transfer occurs when blade moves at exactly half the jet velocity!
---
say: Substitute u = V/2 into the efficiency equation.
eq: \eta_{\text{max}} = \frac{P}{\frac{1}{2}\rho\,a\,V^3} = \frac{\rho\,a\,V\,(V/2)\,(V/2)}{\frac{1}{2}\rho\,a\,V^3} = \frac{1/4}{1/2} = \frac{1}{2} = 50\%
why: Flat plates on a wheel can never exceed 50% hydraulic efficiency.
```

## Power from a series of flat vanes on a water wheel

> [!note] Problem
> A nozzle of diameter $d = 60$ mm delivers water at $V = 30$ m/s ($\rho = 1000\ \text{kg}/\text{m}^3$) against a series of flat vanes mounted on the circumference of a wheel. The wheel moves at $u = 12$ m/s. Calculate:
> 1. Mass flow rate of water striking the wheel
> 2. Force exerted by the jet on the wheel
> 3. Power developed in kilowatts
> 4. Hydraulic efficiency of the wheel

**Step 1. Jet area and mass flow rate:**
$$a = \frac{\pi}{4}(0.060)^2 = 2.8274 \times 10^{-3}\ \text{m}^2$$
$$\dot{m} = \rho\,a\,V = 1000 \times 2.8274 \times 10^{-3} \times 30 = 84.82\ \text{kg}/\text{s}$$

**Step 2. Force exerted on the series of vanes:**
$$F_x = \dot{m}\,(V - u) = 84.82 \times (30 - 12) = 84.82 \times 18 = 1526.8\ \text{N} = 1.527\ \text{kN}$$

**Step 3. Power developed:**
$$P = F_x \cdot u = 1526.8 \times 12 = 18{,}322\ \text{W} = 18.32\ \text{kW}$$

**Step 4. Total kinetic energy flux of the jet:**
$$P_{\text{jet}} = \frac{1}{2}\dot{m}\,V^2 = \frac{1}{2} \times 84.82 \times (30)^2 = 38{,}169\ \text{W} = 38.17\ \text{kW}$$

**Step 5. Hydraulic efficiency $\eta$:**
$$\eta = \frac{P}{P_{\text{jet}}} = \frac{18.32}{38.17} = 0.480 = 48.0\%$$

*(Check using formula: $\frac{2 u (V - u)}{V^2} = \frac{2 \times 12 \times 18}{900} = \frac{432}{900} = 0.480$)*

> [!note] What to take away
> Because $u = 12$ m/s is close to optimal ($V/2 = 15$ m/s), the wheel reaches $48\%$, very close to its theoretical ceiling of $50\%$.

## Civil engineering connections

- **Ancient Undershot Water Wheels:** Traditional wooden water wheels with flat paddles were widely used for milling grain. They were limited by this exact $50\%$ theoretical efficiency, which led 19th-century engineers to invent curved vanes.
- **Impulse Turbine Evolution:** Recognizing that flat vanes discard at least $50\%$ of kinetic energy prompted Lester Pelton to curve the vanes, unlocking over $90\%$ efficiency.

## Three things to remember

1. **Relative speed matters:** Impact force on a moving plate depends on relative speed $(V - u)$.
2. **Single plate limit:** Maximum efficiency is $\frac{8}{27} \approx 29.6\%$ at $u = V/3$.
3. **Series of plates limit:** Maximum efficiency is $50.0\%$ at $u = V/2$, capturing full discharge $\rho a V$.
