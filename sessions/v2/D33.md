## The topographical map of turbine efficiency

If you hike up a mountain, you consult a topographic contour map to find the easiest ridge to climb.

Hydraulic power plant operators have their own topographic map: the **Muschel diagram** (or **Hill diagram**). Instead of elevation contours, its concentric oval rings show lines of equal efficiency! Where on that hill does a turbine operate safely?

## Characteristic Curves & Cavitation Factor

### 1. Three Types of Characteristic Curves
1. **Main Characteristics (Constant Head Curves):**
   - Head $H$ is kept constant while rotational speed $N$ is varied.
   - Plots of Unit Speed $N_u$ against Unit Discharge $Q_u$, Unit Power $P_u$, and Efficiency $\eta$ for various gate openings.
2. **Operating Characteristics (Constant Speed Curves):**
   - Speed $N$ is kept constant (synchronous grid speed) under constant head $H$.
   - Plots of Discharge $Q$ against Power $P$ and Efficiency $\eta$.
   - Shows part-load performance: Pelton has a flat curve; Francis peaks near $90\%$ load then drops; Kaplan maintains an exceptionally flat curve across all flows due to double regulation!
3. **Muschel Curves (Hill Diagrams):**
   - Topographic contour plots with Unit Speed $N_u$ on the x-axis and Unit Discharge $Q_u$ on the y-axis.
   - Concentric closed loop contours represent lines of constant efficiency ($\eta = 80\%, 85\%, 90\%, 92\%$).
   - The highest peak represents the **Best Efficiency Point (BEP)**.

### 2. Thoma's Cavitation Factor $\sigma$
To predict whether cavitation will occur, Dieter Thoma formulated the cavitation factor:

$$\sigma = \frac{H_b - H_s - H_v}{H}$$

- $H_b$: Atmospheric barometric head ($\approx 10.3$ m at sea level).
- $H_s$: Suction height (elevation of runner above tailrace water level).
- $H_v$: Vapor pressure head of water ($\approx 0.25$ to $0.4$ m at room temperature).
- $H$: Total net head acting on the turbine.

### 3. Cavitation Criterion
Each turbine design has a **critical cavitation factor $\sigma_c$** measured experimentally:
- If $\sigma > \sigma_c$: **Safe!** No cavitation occurs.
- If $\sigma \le \sigma_c$: **Danger!** Cavitation bubbles form and erode blades.

To prevent cavitation, the runner setting height $H_s$ must not exceed:
$$H_{s,\text{max}} = H_b - H_v - \sigma_c \cdot H$$

*If $H_{s,\text{max}}$ is negative, the runner must be placed submerged deep below the tailrace surface!*

## Derive maximum allowable setting height $H_{s,\text{max}}$

Click through the derivation from the cavitation threshold.

```steps
say: Cavitation initiates when the lowest absolute pressure inside the runner drops to water vapor pressure.
eq: p_{\text{min}} = p_v
why: Physical inception threshold where liquid phase boils into vapor cavities.
---
say: In a reaction turbine with draft tube, the lowest pressure occurs near the runner blade exit section.
eq: \frac{p_3}{\gamma} = H_b - H_s - h_d
why: From Bernoulli's equation across the draft tube diffuser.
---
say: By definition of critical cavitation factor sigma_c, the dynamic draft tube drop equals sigma_c * H.
eq: h_d = \sigma_c \cdot H - H_v
why: Thoma's empirical definition relating pressure drop to total machine head H.
---
say: Substitute into the runner exit pressure equation.
eq: \frac{p_3}{\gamma} = H_b - H_s - \sigma_c \cdot H
why: Combining static suction height and dynamic draft drop terms.
---
say: Enforce the non-cavitation safety requirement: p_3 / gamma > H_v.
eq: H_b - H_s - \sigma_c \cdot H > H_v \implies H_s \le H_b - H_v - \sigma_c \cdot H
why: This fundamental civil engineering inequality sets the maximum elevation for powerhouse runner floors!
```

## Sizing turbine setting height to prevent cavitation

> [!note] Problem
> A Francis turbine is to be installed under a net head of $H = 65$ m. Laboratory model testing established the critical cavitation factor as $\sigma_c = 0.12$.
> Atmospheric pressure head at the mountain powerhouse elevation is $H_b = 9.80$ m of water.
> The water temperature is $20^\circ\text{C}$ with vapor pressure head $H_v = 0.25$ m of water.
> 1. Calculate the maximum permissible height of the runner above the tailrace $H_{s,\text{max}}$.
> 2. If the tailrace level fluctuates by $1.5$ m during monsoon floods, recommend a safe runner centerline elevation.

**Step 1. Maximum allowable suction height $H_{s,\text{max}}$:**
$$H_{s,\text{max}} = H_b - H_v - \sigma_c \cdot H$$
$$\sigma_c \cdot H = 0.12 \times 65 = 7.80\ \text{m}$$
$$H_{s,\text{max}} = 9.80 - 0.25 - 7.80 = 9.80 - 8.05 = 1.75\ \text{m}$$

The turbine runner must not be set more than **$1.75$ metres** above the tailrace surface.

**Step 2. Safety margin and tailrace fluctuation:**
To account for monsoon tailrace drawdown or sudden load changes, civil design codes recommend an extra safety margin of at least $0.5$ to $1.0$ m:
$$H_{s,\text{design}} = H_{s,\text{max}} - \Delta H_{\text{margin}} = 1.75 - 0.75 = 1.00\ \text{m}$$

Setting the runner at **$1.0$ metre** above the lowest recorded tailrace water level guarantees cavitation-free operation across all seasons!

> [!note] What to take away
> Sizing the turbine runner setting height correctly prevents cavitation bubbles from tearing metal off the blades, avoiding hundreds of thousands of dollars in annual overhaul repairs!

## Civil engineering connections

- **Cavitation Pitting Damage:** When cavitation implosions occur on turbine blades, micro-jets strike the metal at over $1000$ m/s, causing work hardening, micro-cracking, and a distinctive spongy, chewed appearance on runner suction edges.
- **Deep Powerhouse Excavations:** In low-head Kaplan barrage schemes, the requirement for negative suction height ($H_s = -2$ to $-5$ m) requires civil engineers to excavate massive deep pits below riverbed level, protected by heavy cofferdams and dewatering wells during construction.

<!--beat:recap|Recap-->
## Three things to remember

1. **Hill diagrams map sweet spots:** Concentric iso-efficiency contours guide operators to the Best Efficiency Point.
2. **Thoma's factor $\sigma$:** Cavitation occurs whenever $\sigma = \frac{H_b - H_s - H_v}{H} \le \sigma_c$.
3. **Setting height rule:** Runner elevation above tailrace is strictly capped at $H_{s,\text{max}} = H_b - H_v - \sigma_c H$.
