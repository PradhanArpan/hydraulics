## The white water under every dam

Below a spillway or a sluice gate you often see a sudden wall of rough, foamy water. The fast, shallow stream abruptly becomes slow and deep. This is a **hydraulic jump**.

## What to remember about a jump

**Before the jump** the water is shallow and fast (supercritical, $Fr_1 > 1$). **After it** the water is deep and slow (subcritical, $Fr_2 < 1$). The depth after the jump is called the **sequent depth** $y_2$.

$$\frac{y_2}{y_1} = \frac{1}{2}\left(\sqrt{1 + 8\,Fr_1^2} - 1\right)$$

*In words: the faster the incoming flow, the bigger the jump.*

$$\Delta E = \frac{(y_2 - y_1)^3}{4\,y_1\,y_2}$$

*In words: the energy lost grows quickly as the depths move apart.*

| Fr₁ | Type | What you see |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
| 1 to 1.7 | Undular | Gentle ripples, almost no loss |
| 1.7 to 2.5 | Weak | Small rollers |
| 2.5 to 4.5 | Oscillating | Waves that travel downstream |
| 4.5 to 9 | **Steady** | Stable and strong. Best for dissipating energy |
| above 9 | Strong | Rough and violent |

> [!note] Length of the jump
> The jump is roughly $6\,y_2$ long. This is a rule of thumb. For design use the chart in your textbook.

## Why use momentum, not energy?

Energy is lost in the jump, so we cannot use the energy equation across it. But the push of the flow (force plus momentum) is the same on both sides. Click through.

```steps
say: Use the specific force $M$ (the push of the flow per unit width). It is the same just before and just after the jump.
eq: \frac{q^2}{g\,y_1} + \frac{y_1^2}{2} = \frac{q^2}{g\,y_2} + \frac{y_2^2}{2}
why: Momentum is conserved across the jump. Friction on the floor over such a short distance is small, so we ignore it. This is the "equal push" picture in the diagram.
---
say: Divide everything by $y_1^2$ and write $r = y_2/y_1$. Use $Fr_1^2 = q^2/(g\,y_1^3)$.
eq: Fr_1^2 + \frac{1}{2} = \frac{Fr_1^2}{r} + \frac{r^2}{2}
why: This turns the problem into one equation with only $r$ and $Fr_1$.
---
say: Multiply by $2r$ and collect terms.
eq: r^3 - (1 + 2\,Fr_1^2)\,r + 2\,Fr_1^2 = 0
why: A cubic in $r$.
---
say: This cubic factors. The root $r = 1$ means "no jump". The other factor gives the real answer.
eq: (r - 1)\,(r^2 + r - 2\,Fr_1^2) = 0
why: $r = 1$ would mean $y_2 = y_1$, so nothing happens.
---
say: Solve the quadratic for the positive root.
eq: \frac{y_2}{y_1} = \frac{1}{2}\left(\sqrt{1 + 8\,Fr_1^2} - 1\right)
why: The negative root has no physical meaning (negative depth).
```

## A jump below a sluice gate

> [!note] Problem
> Water leaves under a sluice gate at $y_1 = 0.30$ m with speed $V_1 = 6.0$ m/s in a wide channel. A jump forms. Find the sequent depth, the energy lost, and the power burnt off per metre width.

**Step 1. Flow per metre width.** $q = V_1\,y_1 = 6.0 \times 0.30 = 1.80\ \text{m}^2/\text{s}$.

**Step 2. Froude number before.** $Fr_1 = 6.0/\sqrt{9.81 \times 0.30} = 3.50$. This is supercritical, so a jump can form. It is an **oscillating** jump.

**Step 3. Sequent depth.** $y_2/y_1 = \tfrac12\left(\sqrt{1 + 8 \times 3.50^2} - 1\right) = 4.47$, so $y_2 = 4.47 \times 0.30 = 1.34$ m.

**Step 4. Check the other side.** $V_2 = q/y_2 = 1.80/1.34 = 1.34$ m/s and $Fr_2 = 0.37$. It is subcritical, as expected after a jump.

**Step 5. Energy lost.** $\Delta E = \dfrac{(1.341 - 0.30)^3}{4 \times 0.30 \times 1.341} = 0.70$ m. The energy before was $E_1 = 0.30 + 6.0^2/19.62 = 2.13$ m, so about **33 %** is lost.

**Step 6. Power burnt off.** $P = \rho g\,q\,\Delta E = 9.81 \times 1.80 \times 0.70 = 12.4$ kW per metre width of channel.

**Step 7. Length.** $L \approx 6\,y_2 = 8.0$ m. The stilling basin floor must be at least this long.

> [!note] What to take away
> A third of the energy is lost in 8 m, which protects the river bed downstream. Check by using the widget with $y_1 = 0.30$ m and $Fr_1 = 3.5$.

## Remember

> [!note] Day 14 in five lines
> - A jump turns fast and shallow ($Fr_1 > 1$) into slow and deep ($Fr_2 < 1$).
> - $y_2/y_1 = \tfrac12\left(\sqrt{1 + 8Fr_1^2} - 1\right)$ comes from equal specific force, not equal energy.
> - $\Delta E = \dfrac{(y_2 - y_1)^3}{4\,y_1\,y_2}$. Length is about $6\,y_2$.
> - Steady jumps ($Fr_1$ 4.5 to 9) are best for energy dissipation.
> - Tailwater too low: the jump is swept downstream. Too high: it is drowned.
