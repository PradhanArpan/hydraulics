## An airplane propeller inside a river barrage

What do you do when a massive river like the Ganges or the Mississippi carries thousands of tonnes of water every second, but drops by only $10$ to $20$ metres over a low river barrage?

A Pelton wheel would be the size of a football stadium, and a Francis turbine would choke on the immense volume. Instead, Viktor Kaplan looked at ship propellers and turned the idea inside out!

```predict
q: In a Kaplan turbine, water flows through the runner completely parallel to the central spinning shaft (axial flow). Why are the runner blades designed to pivot and tilt automatically during operation?
o: To chop floating logs
o: To adjust blade pitch angle to match the changing water flow rate, maintaining high 90%+ efficiency across a wide range of river discharges!
o: To reverse the river flow
a: 2
why: In a propeller turbine with fixed blades, efficiency collapses if river flow drops. In a Kaplan turbine, both the guide vanes and runner blades pivot in harmony (double regulation), preserving a flat 90%+ efficiency curve from flood season to dry season.
```

Today you will see how **axial-flow Kaplan turbines** dominate low-head river barrages and how to manage their primary enemy: **cavitation**.

## Propeller vs Kaplan Turbines

Both are axial-flow reaction machines:
- **Propeller Turbine:** The runner blades are fixed permanently to the hub. Highly efficient at full rated flow ($100\%$ load), but efficiency drops sharply when river flow varies.
- **Kaplan Turbine:** An advanced propeller turbine where runner blades are pivoted inside the hub by an internal oil-hydraulic servomotor, synchronizing automatically with the guide vanes (**Double Regulation**).

### 1. Key Component Anatomy
1. **Vertical Shaft & Hub (Boss):**
   - Streamlined torpedo-shaped central hub housing the blade-pitch mechanism.
   - Hub ratio: $d_b / D \approx 0.35 \text{ to } 0.60$ (where $d_b$ is hub diameter, $D$ is outer runner tip diameter).
2. **Adjustable Runner Blades:**
   - Usually only $4$ to $8$ blades (compared to 16 to 24 in a Francis runner).
   - Very large open passages allow debris, silt, and fish to pass through safely.
3. **Elbow Draft Tube:**
   - Because net head is low ($H < 40$ m), the exit velocity head $\frac{V_3^2}{2g}$ represents a huge fraction ($30\%$ to $60\%$) of total available head. A high-efficiency elbow draft tube is indispensable!

### 2. Discharge & Annular Area Formula
Water passes through the annular space between runner outer diameter $D$ and hub diameter $d_b$:

$$\text{Annular Area } A = \frac{\pi}{4}(D^2 - d_b^2)$$

$$\text{Discharge } Q = A \cdot V_f = \frac{\pi}{4}(D^2 - d_b^2) \cdot V_f$$

*In words: discharge equals annular flow area times axial flow velocity $V_f$.*

### 3. Cavitation in Kaplan Runners
Because water moves at high speed under low pressure across thin aerofoil blades, Kaplan turbines are extremely prone to cavitation on the suction (underside) of the blade tips.

To suppress cavitation, civil engineers frequently set the Kaplan runner **below** the tailrace water level (negative suction height $H_s < 0$), keeping local pressure well above water vapor pressure!

## Why double regulation produces a flat efficiency curve

Click through the kinematic reason for Kaplan's superior part-load performance.

```steps
say: In an axial flow runner, the inlet blade angle theta depends on flow velocity Vf and peripheral speed u.
eq: \tan\theta = \frac{V_f}{V_{w1} - u}
why: Classical velocity triangle geometry at the blade tip.
---
say: In dry seasons, river flow rate drops, causing axial flow velocity Vf to decrease significantly.
eq: V_{f,\text{low}} < V_{f,\text{rated}}
why: Smaller river discharge through the same physical runner area reduces axial speed.
---
say: In a fixed-blade propeller turbine, blade angle theta cannot move, causing severe flow separation and shock stall.
eq: \Delta\theta_{\text{mismatch}} = \theta_{\text{metal}} - \theta_{\text{flow}} \gg 0 \implies \text{Efficiency plummets below 60%}
why: Water hits the back of the blade at an angle of attack, creating massive turbulent eddies.
---
say: In a Kaplan turbine, the governor tilts the runner blade, matching the physical blade angle theta to the new velocity vector.
eq: \theta_{\text{blade}}(t) = \theta_{\text{flow}}(t) \quad \text{for all flow rates}
why: Shockless, splash-free laminar glide is preserved across the entire operating range, keeping efficiency above 90%!
```

## Sizing a Kaplan runner for a river barrage project

> [!note] Problem
> A Kaplan turbine runner produces $12{,}000$ kW shaft power under a net head of $H = 18$ m.
> The overall efficiency is $\eta_o = 88\%$, speed ratio based on outer diameter is $\phi = 2.0$, and flow ratio is $\psi = V_f / \sqrt{2gH} = 0.65$.
> The hub diameter is $d_b = 0.35\,D$. Calculate:
> 1. Total discharge $Q$ in m³/s
> 2. Runner outer diameter $D$ and hub diameter $d_b$
> 3. Rotational speed $N$ of the turbine in rpm

**Step 1. Total discharge required $Q$:**
$$Q = \frac{P}{\eta_o\,\rho\,g\,H} = \frac{12{,}000{,}000}{0.88 \times 1000 \times 9.81 \times 18} = \frac{12{,}000{,}000}{155{,}390} = 77.22\ \text{m}^3/\text{s}$$

**Step 2. Flow velocity $V_f$:**
$$V_f = \psi \sqrt{2gH} = 0.65 \times \sqrt{2 \times 9.81 \times 18} = 0.65 \times \sqrt{353.16} = 0.65 \times 18.79 = 12.21\ \text{m}/\text{s}$$

**Step 3. Runner outer diameter $D$:**
$$Q = \frac{\pi}{4}(D^2 - d_b^2)\,V_f = \frac{\pi}{4}\left[D^2 - (0.35 D)^2\right]\,V_f = \frac{\pi}{4} D^2 (1 - 0.1225)\,V_f$$
$$Q = \frac{\pi}{4}(0.8775)\,D^2 \times 12.21 = 8.415\,D^2$$
$$D^2 = \frac{77.22}{8.415} = 9.176 \implies D = \sqrt{9.176} = 3.029\ \text{m} \approx 3.03\ \text{m}$$
$$\text{Hub diameter } d_b = 0.35 \times 3.029 = 1.060\ \text{m} = 1.06\ \text{m}$$

**Step 4. Peripheral velocity and rotational speed $N$:**
$$u = \phi \sqrt{2gH} = 2.0 \times 18.79 = 37.58\ \text{m}/\text{s}$$
$$u = \frac{\pi\,D\,N}{60} \implies N = \frac{60\,u}{\pi\,D} = \frac{60 \times 37.58}{\pi \times 3.029} = \frac{2254.8}{9.516} = 236.9\ \text{rpm}$$
*(In practice, generator pole synchronization at $50$ Hz selects synchronous speed: e.g. with 24 poles, $N = \frac{120 \times 50}{24} = 250$ rpm or with 26 poles $N = 230.8$ rpm)*.

> [!note] What to take away
> A 3-metre diameter Kaplan propeller passes an enormous river discharge ($77.2$ m³/s) under just 18 metres of fall, generating $12$ MW of clean renewable baseload power!

<!--beat:recap|Recap-->
## Three things to remember

1. **Axial reaction principle:** Water flows parallel to shaft through an annular ring $A = \frac{\pi}{4}(D^2 - d_b^2)$.
2. **Double regulation supremacy:** Coordinated guide vane and runner blade adjustment preserves $> 90\%$ efficiency across flood and drought flows.
3. **Draft tube sensitivity:** Because net head is low, recovering exit velocity head in the draft tube is critical to project viability.
