## The 8-Step Pelton Sizing Wizard

Every commercial Pelton turbine design follows these eight sequential steps:

### Step 1. Jet Velocity $V_1$
$$V_1 = C_v \sqrt{2gH} \quad (C_v \approx 0.98\text{ to }0.99)$$

*In words: theoretical Torricelli velocity multiplied by nozzle velocity coefficient.*

### Step 2. Runner Pitch Peripheral Velocity $u$
$$u = \phi \sqrt{2gH} \quad (\phi \approx 0.45\text{ to }0.48)$$

*In words: optimal speed ratio $\phi$ ensures bucket travels at nearly half the jet speed.*

### Step 3. Total Discharge Required $Q$
$$Q = \frac{P}{\eta_o\,\rho\,g\,H}$$

### Step 4. Pitch Circle Diameter of Runner $D$
$$u = \frac{\pi\,D\,N}{60} \implies D = \frac{60\,u}{\pi\,N}$$

### Step 5. Jet Diameter $d$ and Number of Jets $n_j$
$$\text{Total nozzle area } a_t = \frac{Q}{V_1}$$
For $n_j$ identical nozzles:
$$a = \frac{a_t}{n_j} = \frac{\pi}{4}d^2 \implies d = \sqrt{\frac{4\,a_t}{\pi\,n_j}}$$

### Step 6. Jet Ratio $m$ Check
$$m = \frac{D}{d}$$
- **Recommended Range:** $10 \le m \le 16$ (Optimal $m \approx 12$).
- If $m < 10$: Increase the number of nozzles $n_j$ to reduce jet diameter $d$.

### Step 7. Number of Buckets $Z$ (Taygun's Empirical Formula)
$$Z = 15 + \frac{D}{2d} = 15 + 0.5\,m$$

### Step 8. Bucket Mechanical Dimensions
- **Bucket Width $B$:** $B = 3.5\,d \text{ to } 5.0\,d$ (typically $4.0\,d$)
- **Bucket Length $L$:** $L = 2.5\,d \text{ to } 3.0\,d$ (typically $2.8\,d$)
- **Bucket Depth $T$:** $T = 0.9\,d \text{ to } 1.2\,d$ (typically $1.0\,d$)
- **Bucket Splitter Angle:** $10^\circ \text{ to } 20^\circ$ (sharp central wedge)
- **Bucket Outlet Angle $\beta_2$:** $10^\circ \text{ to } 15^\circ$ (deflection $165^\circ \text{ to } 170^\circ$)

## Derive why speed ratio $\phi$ is slightly less than 0.50

Click through the physical reason why $\phi = 0.46$ rather than $0.50$.

```steps
say: From ideal momentum theory with zero friction, maximum hydraulic efficiency occurs at exactly u = 0.50 V1.
eq: \frac{d\eta}{du} = 0 \implies u = \frac{V_1}{2}
why: Classical frictionless calculus maximum.
---
say: In a real turbine, water sliding across the bucket bowl experiences boundary layer skin friction.
eq: V_{r2} = k\,V_{r1} \quad (k \approx 0.85\text{ to }0.92)
why: Boundary layer shear along the stainless steel bucket surface dissipates kinetic energy into heat.
---
say: Mechanical windage of the spinning wheel against surrounding air also creates resisting drag torque.
eq: T_{\text{windage}} \propto \rho_{\text{air}}\,\omega^2\,D^5
why: High-speed rotation in ambient air creates aerodynamic drag on the buckets.
---
say: Accounting for surface friction k and windage shifts the true peak efficiency slightly to the left.
eq: \phi_{\text{real}} = \frac{u}{\sqrt{2gH}} \approx 0.45\text{ to }0.47 \quad (\text{industry standard } 0.46)
why: Operating at phi = 0.46 maximizes measured mechanical power at the generator shaft.
```

## Complete Pelton design for an alpine hydro project

> [!note] Problem
> Design a Pelton runner for a hydroelectric plant developing $6000$ kW shaft power under an effective net head $H = 300$ m at a speed of $N = 600$ rpm.
> Take overall efficiency $\eta_o = 85\%$, $C_v = 0.98$, $\phi = 0.46$, and bucket exit angle $\beta_2 = 15^\circ$.
> Determine:
> 1. Total discharge $Q$
> 2. Pitch diameter $D$ of the runner wheel
> 3. Jet diameter $d$ and number of nozzles $n_j$ to ensure $m \approx 12$
> 4. Number of buckets $Z$
> 5. Workshop dimensions of the bucket $(B, L, T)$

**Step 1. Discharge required $Q$:**
$$Q = \frac{P}{\eta_o\,\rho\,g\,H} = \frac{6{,}000{,}000}{0.85 \times 1000 \times 9.81 \times 300} = \frac{6{,}000{,}000}{2{,}501{,}550} = 2.398\ \text{m}^3/\text{s}$$

**Step 2. Jet speed and peripheral speed:**
$$V_1 = C_v \sqrt{2gH} = 0.98 \times \sqrt{2 \times 9.81 \times 300} = 0.98 \times 76.72 = 75.19\ \text{m}/\text{s}$$
$$u = \phi \sqrt{2gH} = 0.46 \times 76.72 = 35.29\ \text{m}/\text{s}$$

**Step 3. Pitch diameter of runner wheel $D$:**
$$D = \frac{60\,u}{\pi\,N} = \frac{60 \times 35.29}{\pi \times 600} = \frac{2117.4}{1884.96} = 1.123\ \text{m}$$

**Step 4. Nozzle sizing & number of jets:**
$$\text{Total nozzle area } a_t = \frac{Q}{V_1} = \frac{2.398}{75.19} = 0.03189\ \text{m}^2$$
If single nozzle ($n_j = 1$):
$$d_1 = \sqrt{\frac{4 \times 0.03189}{\pi}} = 0.2015\ \text{m} \implies m_1 = \frac{1.123}{0.2015} = 5.57 \quad (\text{too small!})$$
To achieve $m \approx 12$:
$$n_j = \left(\frac{12}{5.57}\right)^2 = (2.15)^2 = 4.64 \to \text{Choose } n_j = 4\ \text{jets}$$
With 4 jets:
$$d = \sqrt{\frac{4 \times (0.03189 / 4)}{\pi}} = \frac{0.2015}{2} = 0.1008\ \text{m} = 100.8\ \text{mm}$$
$$m = \frac{D}{d} = \frac{1.123}{0.1008} = 11.14 \quad (\text{Ideal! Inside } 10-16)$$

**Step 5. Number of buckets $Z$:**
$$Z = 15 + 0.5\,m = 15 + 0.5(11.14) = 15 + 5.57 = 20.57 \to \text{Select } 21\ \text{buckets}$$

**Step 6. Workshop bucket dimensions:**
- Width $B = 4.0\,d = 4.0 \times 100.8 = 403.2\ \text{mm} \approx 405\ \text{mm}$
- Length $L = 2.8\,d = 2.8 \times 100.8 = 282.2\ \text{mm} \approx 285\ \text{mm}$
- Depth $T = 1.0\,d = 100.8\ \text{mm} \approx 100\ \text{mm}$

> [!note] What to take away
> From raw river head and flow rate, you have produced the complete engineering specification for casting the runner wheel and machining the nozzles!

## Quick check

```quiz
q: Why is the jet ratio m = D/d recommended to stay between 10 and 16?
o: If m < 10, buckets are too large and interfere with each other; if m > 16, buckets are too small and many, driving up cost
o: To keep the wheel spinning at the speed of light
o: It only depends on water color
a: 1
why: Jet ratios below 10 cause severe physical crowding of bulky buckets on the wheel rim. Jet ratios above 16 lead to an oversized, heavy, and costly wheel.
---
q: Using Taygun's formula, how many buckets are required on a Pelton wheel with jet ratio m = 14?
o: 15 buckets
o: 22 buckets
o: 35 buckets

why: Z = 15 + 0.5 * m = 15 + 0.5 * (14) = 15 + 7 = 22 buckets.
```

## Civil engineering connections

- **Manufacturing Tolerances:** Pelton runner buckets are cast as monolithic single-piece stainless steel discs (often 13Cr-4Ni martensitic stainless steel) using 5-axis CNC milling to eliminate weld fatigue under millions of cyclical impulse blows.
- **Micro-Hydro Rural Installations:** In mountain communities of Nepal, Colombia, and Northern India, local mini-hydro turbines use standardized Pelton runners sized with this exact step wizard to deliver 10 to 100 kW off-grid power.

<!--beat:recap|Recap-->
## Three things to remember

1. **Step-wizard flow:** $V_1 \to u \to Q \to D \to d \to m \to Z \to (B, L, T)$.
2. **Taygun's formula:** Bucket count $Z = 15 + 0.5m$ guarantees complete jet interception without gaps.
3. **Jet ratio sweet spot:** Always check $10 \le m \le 16$; if $m$ is too small, divide discharge across multiple nozzles.
