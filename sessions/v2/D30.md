## Sucking energy from water underwater

A Pelton wheel works like an open bicycle wheel in the air — water strikes it and splashes down. But walk into the powerhouse of a massive river dam like Hoover Dam or Tehri Dam, and you see no water splashing anywhere.

The turbine is sealed inside a giant steel snail shell, completely flooded under pressure. Below the spinning wheel hangs a long expanding cone leading into the river. Why does hanging a submerged cone below the turbine boost its power?

```predict
q: A sealed reaction turbine runner discharges water at 8 m/s into an expanding draft tube. What happens to the water pressure right at the runner exit as the tube expands toward the river?
o: Water pressure increases above atmospheric pressure
o: Water pressure drops to a sub-atmospheric vacuum, sucking more energy out of the runner!
o: Water pressure drops to absolute zero
a: 2
why: As the draft tube widens, water decelerates (kinetic head drops). By Bernoulli's equation, dropping velocity head converts into a pressure rise toward atmospheric at the river exit, creating a suction vacuum right beneath the runner exit.
```

Today you will see how James Francis invented the inward-flow reaction turbine and why the **draft tube** is the secret weapon of dam powerhouses.

## The Francis Reaction Turbine & Draft Tube

The Francis turbine is an **inward-flow reaction turbine**:
- Water enters the runner radially from the outer rim under high pressure.
- As it flows inward across the curved blades, both pressure and velocity drop simultaneously.
- Water discharges axially downward through the center into the draft tube.

### 1. Key Structural Components
1. **Spiral Volute Casing:** Cross-sectional area gradually decreases around the periphery to maintain uniform flow velocity as water feeds continuously into the guide ring.
2. **Stay Ring:** Stationary structural steel struts that carry the enormous hydrostatic weight of the dam and powerhouse floor.
3. **Guide Vanes (Wicket Gates):** Streamlined pivoting aerofoils controlled by governor regulating rings. They control flow rate $Q$ and impart the required swirl angle $\alpha_1$ into the runner.
4. **Francis Runner:** Complex 3D double-curved blades made of cavitation-resistant stainless steel.
5. **Draft Tube:** A gradually diverging pipe connecting the runner exit to the tailrace stream.

### 2. Two Crucial Functions of the Draft Tube
1. **Regains Net Fall Height ($H_s$):**
   - Allows placing the heavy turbine runner several metres safely above high flood levels in the tailrace river without losing any of that vertical head $H_s$.
2. **Kinetic Energy Recovery ($h_d$):**
   - Water leaves the runner with significant leftover kinetic energy $\frac{V_3^2}{2g}$.
   - The expanding tube decelerates water from $V_3$ to $V_4$ at the tailrace, converting wasted kinetic energy into useful vacuum suction head:

$$h_d = \eta_{\text{dt}} \left( \frac{V_3^2 - V_4^2}{2g} \right)$$

*In words: head regained equals draft tube diffuser efficiency times the reduction in kinetic energy.*

### 3. Pressure at Runner Exit ($p_3 / \gamma$)
Applying Bernoulli's equation between runner exit (Point 3) and tailrace surface (Point 4):

$$\frac{p_3}{\gamma} = \frac{p_{\text{atm}}}{\gamma} - H_s - \left( \frac{V_3^2 - V_4^2}{2g} - h_f \right) = \frac{p_{\text{atm}}}{\gamma} - H_s - h_d$$

*In words: absolute pressure at runner exit is sub-atmospheric (a vacuum). If this vacuum becomes too severe, water boils at room temperature, causing catastrophic cavitation pitting!*

## Derive draft tube pressure recovery

Click through Bernoulli's equation between runner exit and tailrace.

```steps
say: Apply Bernoulli's equation between runner exit (section 3-3) and tailrace discharge (section 4-4).
eq: \frac{p_3}{\gamma} + \frac{V_3^2}{2g} + z_3 = \frac{p_4}{\gamma} + \frac{V_4^2}{2g} + z_4 + h_f
why: Conservation of mechanical energy for steady incompressible flow through the draft tube diffuser.
---
say: Take the tailrace free surface as datum (z_4 = 0). The runner exit is at height z_3 = H_s.
eq: \frac{p_3}{\gamma} + \frac{V_3^2}{2g} + H_s = \frac{p_{\text{atm}}}{\gamma} + \frac{V_4^2}{2g} + 0 + h_f
why: The tailrace discharges directly into the open river at atmospheric pressure p_atm.
---
say: Rearrange to solve for the pressure head at the runner exit p_3 / gamma.
eq: \frac{p_3}{\gamma} = \frac{p_{\text{atm}}}{\gamma} - H_s - \left( \frac{V_3^2 - V_4^2}{2g} - h_f \right)
why: Grouping static suction height H_s and dynamic diffuser recovery together on the right.
---
say: Define draft tube recovery head h_d = (V_3^2 - V_4^2)/(2g) - h_f.
eq: \frac{p_3}{\gamma} = \frac{p_{\text{atm}}}{\gamma} - (H_s + h_d)
why: The runner exit experiences a sub-atmospheric vacuum equal to the physical suction height PLUS the dynamic diffuser head recovery!
```

## Draft tube pressure and cavitation check

> [!note] Problem
> A Francis turbine operates under a net head of $80$ m. Water leaves the runner at section 3-3 with velocity $V_3 = 9.0$ m/s, located $H_s = 3.2$ m above the tailrace.
> The draft tube expands so that the discharge velocity at the tailrace is $V_4 = 2.2$ m/s. Frictional loss in the tube is $h_f = 0.40$ m.
> Atmospheric pressure head is $10.3$ m of water, and water vapor pressure head is $0.35$ m of water.
> 1. Calculate the head recovered by the draft tube $h_d$ and draft tube efficiency $\eta_{\text{dt}}$.
> 2. Determine the absolute pressure head at the runner exit $p_3/\gamma$.
> 3. Check whether the turbine is safe from cavitation inception.

**Step 1. Theoretical and actual head recovery:**
$$\text{Theoretical kinetic head drop } = \frac{V_3^2 - V_4^2}{2g} = \frac{(9.0)^2 - (2.2)^2}{2 \times 9.81} = \frac{81.0 - 4.84}{19.62} = \frac{76.16}{19.62} = 3.882\ \text{m}$$
$$\text{Actual head recovered } h_d = 3.882 - h_f = 3.882 - 0.40 = 3.482\ \text{m}$$
$$\text{Draft tube efficiency } \eta_{\text{dt}} = \frac{h_d}{\text{Theoretical}} = \frac{3.482}{3.882} = 0.897 = 89.7\%$$

**Step 2. Absolute pressure head at runner exit ($p_3/\gamma$):**
$$\frac{p_3}{\gamma} = \frac{p_{\text{atm}}}{\gamma} - H_s - h_d = 10.30 - 3.20 - 3.482 = 3.618\ \text{m of water (absolute)}$$

**Step 3. Check for cavitation:**
$$\text{Vapor pressure head } \frac{p_v}{\gamma} = 0.35\ \text{m}$$
$$\text{Available margin above boiling } = \frac{p_3}{\gamma} - \frac{p_v}{\gamma} = 3.618 - 0.35 = 3.268\ \text{m}$$

Since the local pressure ($3.62$ m) is well above the vapor pressure ($0.35$ m), the turbine operates **safely without cavitation**.

> [!note] What to take away
> The draft tube successfully salvaged $3.48$ m of head that would have otherwise splashed away uselessly into the river, boosting turbine output while maintaining a safe $3.27$ m buffer against cavitation!

```quiz
q: What would happen if a reaction turbine had no draft tube and discharged directly into open air?

o: It would lose both the suction height H_s and all the exit kinetic energy (V_3^2 / 2g), significantly reducing power and efficiency
o: It would consume no water
a: 2
why: Without a sealed draft tube, runner exit pressure is atmospheric. The vertical elevation drop H_s to the river and the kinetic energy V_3^2/(2g) are completely wasted.
---
q: Why must the draft tube angle of flare (expansion angle) be kept below 8 to 10 degrees?
o: To make it easy to paint
o: To prevent boundary layer flow separation and eddy stall inside the diffuser
o: To increase water temperature
a: 2
why: If the diffuser walls diverge too rapidly (greater than 8 to 10 degrees), the adverse pressure gradient causes the boundary layer to detach, destroying head recovery and causing violent vibrations.
```

## Civil engineering connections

- **Tehri Dam Underground Powerhouse (Uttarakhand):** Housing four $250$ MW Francis turbines, the powerhouse excavation sits deep inside rock caverns where massive concrete elbow draft tubes transition from circular runner cones into rectangular tailrace tunnels.
- **Tailrace Aeration during Part-Load:** When a Francis turbine runs at low gate openings ($< 50\%$), swirling flow in the draft tube forms a corkscrew vortex rope causing loud booming vibrations. Civil engineers inject compressed air into the draft tube center to cushion the vortex core!

<!--beat:recap|Recap-->
## Three things to remember

1. **Inward reaction principle:** High-pressure water accelerates inward across guide vanes, dropping both pressure and velocity across the runner.
2. **Draft tube dual purpose:** Regains vertical height $H_s$ and recovers kinetic energy $\frac{V_3^2 - V_4^2}{2g}$ as useful suction vacuum.
3. **Cavitation limit:** Runner setting height $H_s$ must be calculated carefully so exit pressure head $p_3/\gamma$ never drops to water vapor pressure.
