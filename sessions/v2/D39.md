## Squeezing three dimensions into two

A river might be two kilometres wide and fifty kilometres long, but only three metres deep.

If you try to model every centimetre of that river with 3D Navier-Stokes equations, a supercomputer would take weeks to simulate a single afternoon storm. How do river models like HEC-RAS 2D simulate entire river valleys in minutes?

```predict
q: Because rivers are vast in horizontal length and width but very shallow in vertical depth, what simplification do hydraulic engineers make to speed up computations by 1000 times?
o: Assume water has zero mass
o: Assume vertical acceleration is negligible and integrate the equations over the water depth, reducing 3D flow to 2D depth-averaged Shallow Water Equations
o: Assume the river is completely dry
a: 2
why: Because water depth is tiny compared to river width, vertical accelerations are negligible. Pressure is purely hydrostatic (p = rho * g * y). Integrating vertically yields the 2D Shallow Water Equations (Saint-Venant equations).
```

Today you will see the **Shallow Water Equations (SWE)** that power all river flood forecasting models.

## The Shallow Water Equations (Saint-Venant Equations)

The 2D Shallow Water Equations (SWE) are derived by integrating the 3D Navier-Stokes equations vertically from the riverbed $z_b$ to the free water surface $\eta = z_b + h$:

### 1. 2D Continuity Equation (Mass Conservation)
$$\frac{\partial h}{\partial t} + \frac{\partial (h\,u)}{\partial x} + \frac{\partial (h\,v)}{\partial y} = q_{\text{source}}$$

- $h$: Water depth (m).
- $u, v$: Depth-averaged velocities in $x$ and $y$ directions (m/s).
- $q_{\text{source}}$: Lateral inflow (rainfall or tributary inflows, m/s).

*In words: rate of water depth change equals the net convergence of horizontal flow volume.*

### 2. 2D Momentum Equations (Newton's 2nd Law)
In the $x$-direction:
$$\frac{\partial (h\,u)}{\partial t} + \frac{\partial (h\,u^2)}{\partial x} + \frac{\partial (h\,u\,v)}{\partial y} = -g\,h\frac{\partial \eta}{\partial x} - \frac{\tau_{bx}}{\rho} + \nu_t \nabla^2(h\,u)$$

In the $y$-direction:
$$\frac{\partial (h\,v)}{\partial t} + \frac{\partial (h\,u\,v)}{\partial x} + \frac{\partial (h\,v^2)}{\partial y} = -g\,h\frac{\partial \eta}{\partial y} - \frac{\tau_{by}}{\rho} + \nu_t \nabla^2(h\,v)$$

### 3. Physical Meaning of Each Term:
1. **$\frac{\partial (h u)}{\partial t}$:** Local acceleration (unsteady wave surge).
2. **$\frac{\partial (h u^2)}{\partial x} + \frac{\partial (h u v)}{\partial y}$:** Convective acceleration (momentum carried across cell faces).
3. **$-g\,h\frac{\partial \eta}{\partial x}$:** Water surface slope drive (hydrostatic pressure gradient).
4. **$-\frac{\tau_{bx}}{\rho}$:** Bed shear friction resistance (Manning's formula: $\tau_b = \rho\,g\,n^2\frac{u\sqrt{u^2 + v^2}}{h^{1/3}}$).
5. **$\nu_t \nabla^2(hu)$:** Turbulent eddy viscosity diffusion.

### 4. Full Momentum vs Diffusion Wave Approximation
In many flat river valleys and floodplains, acceleration terms (terms 1 and 2) are small compared to friction and gravity. Dropping acceleration terms yields the **Diffusion Wave Equation**, which solves up to 5 times faster while capturing backwater ponding accurately!

## Derive 1D continuity equation for open channels

Click through the control volume mass balance across length $\Delta x$.

```steps
say: Consider a slice of channel of length dx with cross-sectional water area A(x, t).
eq: \text{Mass stored in slice} = \rho\,A\,dx
why: Total water mass equals density times area times slice length.
---
say: Inflow mass per second across the upstream face at x is rho * Q.
eq: \dot{m}_{\text{in}} = \rho\,Q
why: Mass flow rate is density times volumetric discharge Q.
---
say: Outflow mass across the downstream face at x + dx is expanded using Taylor series.
eq: \dot{m}_{\text{out}} = \rho\left( Q + \frac{\partial Q}{\partial x} dx \right)
why: Spatial gradient of discharge across distance dx.
---
say: Rate of mass accumulation in the slice equals inflow minus outflow plus lateral rain inflow q_L.
eq: \frac{\partial (\rho\,A\,dx)}{\partial t} = \rho\,Q - \rho\left( Q + \frac{\partial Q}{\partial x} dx \right) + \rho\,q_L\,dx
why: Conservation of mass for an incompressible fluid.
---
say: Divide through by rho * dx.
eq: \frac{\partial A}{\partial t} + \frac{\partial Q}{\partial x} = q_L
why: This is the legendary 1D Saint-Venant continuity equation governing all river routing!
```

## Wave celerity and flood arrival time

> [!note] Problem
> A sudden cloudburst upstream discharges a flood wave into a rectangular river reach of width $b = 40$ m.
> The initial normal water depth is $y_0 = 3.0$ m and the base river velocity is $u = 1.2$ m/s.
> A town is located $L = 18$ km downstream. Calculate:
> 1. Small gravity wave celerity $c = \sqrt{g y_0}$
> 2. Total dynamic wave propagation speed $S_{\text{dynamic}} = u + c$
> 3. Kinematic flood crest speed $S_{\text{kinematic}} = \frac{5}{3} u$
> 4. Expected arrival time of the initial flood surge at the downstream town in hours

**Step 1. Gravity wave celerity $c$:**
$$c = \sqrt{g\,y_0} = \sqrt{9.81 \times 3.0} = \sqrt{29.43} = 5.425\ \text{m}/\text{s}$$

**Step 2. Dynamic wave front speed $S_{\text{dynamic}}$:**
The initial pressure wave disturbance travels at flow speed plus celerity:
$$S_{\text{dynamic}} = u + c = 1.20 + 5.425 = 6.625\ \text{m}/\text{s} = 23.85\ \text{km}/\text{h}$$

**Step 3. Kinematic flood crest speed $S_{\text{kinematic}}$:**
From Manning's equation ($Q \propto A \cdot R^{2/3} \propto y^{5/3}$), the main bulk flood peak travels at:
$$S_{\text{kinematic}} = \frac{dQ}{dA} = \frac{5}{3} u = \frac{5}{3}(1.20) = 2.00\ \text{m}/\text{s} = 7.20\ \text{km}/\text{h}$$

**Step 4. Arrival time at town ($L = 18$ km):**
- **First arrival of water level rise (dynamic front):**
  $$t_{\text{front}} = \frac{18{,}000\ \text{m}}{6.625\ \text{m}/\text{s}} = 2717\ \text{s} = 45.3\ \text{minutes}$$
- **Arrival of the peak flood crest (kinematic peak):**
  $$t_{\text{peak}} = \frac{18{,}000\ \text{m}}{2.00\ \text{m}/\text{s}} = 9000\ \text{s} = 2.50\ \text{hours}$$

> [!note] What to take away
> The initial river surge arrives in under $46$ minutes, giving authorities crucial early warning before the massive peak crest arrives $2.5$ hours later!

## Civil engineering connections

- **National Flood Inundation Mapping:** Agencies like the Central Water Commission (CWC India) and USACE use 2D SWE solvers in HEC-RAS to map flood zones for 25-year, 50-year, and 100-year return periods, dictating town planning zoning regulations.
- **Tsunami & Dam-Break Early Warning:** When a dam breach occurs, the 2D Shallow Water Equations simulate the catastrophic dam-break wave front as it sweeps through downstream valleys.

<!--beat:recap|Recap-->
## Three things to remember

1. **Hydrostatic assumption:** Depth integration eliminates the vertical dimension, turning 3D Navier-Stokes into fast 2D SWE.
2. **Two wave speeds:** Dynamic front moves at $u + \sqrt{gy}$; main kinematic flood crest moves at $\approx \frac{5}{3}u$.
3. **Diffusion wave speedup:** Neglecting minor acceleration terms on mild slopes accelerates runtimes by 5× without sacrificing accuracy.
