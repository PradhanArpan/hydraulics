## Maximum water for minimum cost

Lining a canal with concrete is expensive. To save public money, an engineer wants to build a channel that carries the largest possible flow for the smallest possible perimeter of concrete lining.

```predict
q: You have enough concrete to line a perimeter of 6 metres. Which rectangular shape carries more discharge: very wide and shallow (width 5 m, depth 0.5 m), or a proportioned channel where width is twice the depth (width 3 m, depth 1.5 m)?
o: The wide and shallow channel carries more
o: The proportioned channel (width = 2 × depth) carries far more
o: Both carry exactly the same discharge because perimeter is 6 m
a: 2
why: Area of the wide shallow channel is only 5 × 0.5 = 2.5 m², whereas the proportioned channel gives 3 × 1.5 = 4.5 m² (80% more water for the exact same amount of concrete!).
```

Today you will find the golden proportions for rectangles, trapezoids, triangles, and circular pipes.

## The four most economical shapes

From Manning's equation, $Q = \frac{1}{n} A R^{2/3} S_0^{1/2} = \frac{S_0^{1/2}}{n} \frac{A^{5/3}}{P^{2/3}}$.
For a given cross-sectional area $A$, **discharge $Q$ is maximum when wetted perimeter $P$ is minimum**.

| Shape | Golden Rule for Minimum Perimeter | Hydraulic Radius |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
| **Rectangle** | Bottom width is twice the depth: $b = 2y$ | $R = \frac{y}{2}$ |
| **Trapezoid** | Half-hexagon ($\theta = 60^\circ, m = 1/\sqrt{3}$). Top width $T = \frac{4}{\sqrt{3}}y$ | $R = \frac{y}{2}$ |
| **Triangle** | Vertex angle is $90^\circ$ (side slope 1:1, so $m = 1$) | $R = \frac{y}{2\sqrt{2}}$ |
| **Circle** | Maximum discharge occurs before full: $y = 0.938\,D$<br>Maximum velocity occurs at $y = 0.810\,D$ | $R > \frac{D}{4}$ |

> [!note] The Crown Friction Effect in Pipes
> When a circular pipe fills past 93.8% depth, the top roof adds a lot of wetted perimeter friction while adding almost negligible flow area. As a result, the pipe chokes slightly and carries less water when full than when nearly full!

## Derive the economical rectangle

Click through the calculus steps to prove $b = 2y$ and $R = y/2$.

```steps
say: Write the wetted perimeter $P$ in terms of area $A$ and depth $y$.
eq: P = b + 2y = \frac{A}{y} + 2y
why: Area is fixed ($A = b \cdot y \implies b = A/y$), so $P$ depends only on $y$.
---
say: To find the minimum perimeter, differentiate $P$ with respect to $y$ and set to zero.
eq: \frac{dP}{dy} = -\frac{A}{y^2} + 2 = 0
why: At a minimum, the slope of the curve is zero.
---
say: Rearrange to find $A$ in terms of depth $y$.
eq: A = 2y^2
why: Multiplying by $y^2$ gives $A = 2y^2$.
---
say: Substitute $A = b \cdot y$ back into the result.
eq: b \cdot y = 2y^2 \implies b = 2y
why: The bottom width must be exactly twice the water depth.
---
say: Calculate the hydraulic radius $R$ for this condition.
eq: R = \frac{A}{P} = \frac{2y^2}{2y + 2y} = \frac{2y^2}{4y} = \frac{y}{2}
why: For any economical rectangle or trapezoid, the hydraulic radius is always half the water depth.
```

## Design an economical trapezoidal canal

> [!note] Problem
> Design the most economical trapezoidal concrete canal ($n = 0.014$) to convey a discharge $Q = 15.0\ \text{m}^3/\text{s}$ on a bed slope of $1$ in $2500$ ($S_0 = 0.0004$). Side slope is $1:1$ ($m = 1$).

**Step 1. Condition for most economical trapezoid.**
For a trapezoidal channel with given side slope $m$, the condition for minimum perimeter is:
$$\frac{b + 2\,m\,y}{2} = y\sqrt{1 + m^2} \implies \frac{T}{2} = \text{sloping side length}$$
This also guarantees $R = y/2$.

**Step 2. Express geometry in terms of depth $y$.**
With $m = 1$:
- $b = 2\,y(\sqrt{1 + m^2} - m) = 2\,y(\sqrt{2} - 1) = 2(0.4142)\,y = 0.8284\,y$.
- Area $A = (b + m\,y)\,y = (0.8284\,y + y)\,y = 1.8284\,y^2$.
- Hydraulic radius $R = y / 2 = 0.5\,y$.

**Step 3. Apply Manning's equation.**
$$Q = \frac{1}{n}\,A\,R^{2/3}\,S_0^{1/2}$$
$$15.0 = \frac{1}{0.014} \times (1.8284\,y^2) \times (0.5\,y)^{2/3} \times (0.0004)^{1/2}$$
$$15.0 = \frac{0.02}{0.014} \times 1.8284 \times 0.62996 \times y^{8/3} = 1.6456 \times y^{8/3}$$

**Step 4. Solve for depth $y$ and width $b$.**
$$y^{8/3} = \frac{15.0}{1.6456} = 9.115 \implies y = (9.115)^{3/8} = 2.28\ \text{m}$$
$$b = 0.8284 \times 2.28 = 1.89\ \text{m}$$

> [!note] What to take away
> Sizing the canal with $b = 1.89$ m and $y = 2.28$ m ensures the minimum amount of concrete lining per kilometre, reducing construction cost.

## Remember

> [!note] Day 10 in five lines
> - Economical section: minimum wetted perimeter $P$ for a given area $A$ maximizes discharge $Q$.
> - Rectangle: $b = 2y$ and $R = y/2$.
> - Trapezoid: half-hexagon with 60° side slope ($m = 1/\sqrt{3}$), circumscribing a semicircle with $R = y/2$.
> - Triangle: 90° vertex ($m = 1$).
> - Circular pipe: $Q_{\text{max}}$ occurs at $y = 0.938\,D$; $V_{\text{max}}$ occurs at $y = 0.810\,D$.
