## Why doesn't a canal speed up forever?

Water flows downhill under gravity. A ball rolling down a frictionless hill goes faster and faster. But in a long canal with constant slope, water settles at one constant speed.

```predict
q: A canal has a steady downslope of 1 in 1000. Why doesn't the water continuously accelerate like a car rolling down a hill?
o: Gravity stops pulling after a certain distance
o: Resistance from the channel bed and banks grows with speed until friction exactly balances the gravity pull
o: Water pressure at the bottom pushes the flow back uphill
a: 2
why: As water speeds up, turbulent friction on the wetted perimeter increases. The water reaches terminal velocity when bed shear friction exactly balances the forward component of gravity. This is called uniform flow.
```

Today you will see the exact balance of forces that controls uniform flow, and how Robert Manning's formula computes canal discharge.

## The force equilibrium and Chezy's equation

Consider a control volume of water of length $L$, cross-sectional area $A$, and wetted perimeter $P$ on a bed slope $S_0 = \sin\theta$:

1. **Hydrostatic pressure force at section 1 (upstream):**
   $$F_1 = \gamma\,A\,\bar{y}$$
   The pressure triangle has its **apex at the surface ($p = 0$)** and its **base at the bed ($p = \gamma y_n$)**.
2. **Hydrostatic pressure force at section 2 (downstream):**
   $$F_2 = \gamma\,A\,\bar{y}$$
   Because depth is uniform ($y_1 = y_2 = y_n$), **$F_1 - F_2 = 0$**. The water pressure forces completely cancel out!
3. **Gravity pull down the slope:**
   $$W\sin\theta = (\gamma\,A\,L)\,S_0$$
4. **Boundary shear resistance along the wetted walls:**
   $$F_{\text{shear}} = \tau_0\,P\,L$$

Equating forward gravity pull to resisting wall friction:

$$(\gamma\,A\,L)\,S_0 = \tau_0\,P\,L \implies \tau_0 = \gamma\left(\frac{A}{P}\right)S_0 = \gamma\,R\,S_0$$

*In words: the average bed shear stress equals the unit weight times hydraulic radius times slope.*

## From bed shear to Chezy and Manning

Click through to derive Chezy's formula and Manning's equation.

```steps
say: Fluid dynamics shows wall shear stress is proportional to the square of velocity.
eq: \tau_0 = k\,\rho\,V^2
why: Turbulent boundary drag scales with kinetic energy ($\rho V^2/2$).
---
say: Equate the fluid shear to the gravity shear $\tau_0 = \gamma R S_0$.
eq: k\,\rho\,V^2 = \rho\,g\,R\,S_0
why: The forward gravity drive balances the backward wall drag in uniform flow.
---
say: Solve for velocity $V$. We group the constants into Chezy's coefficient $C = \sqrt{g/k}$.
eq: V = C\,\sqrt{R\,S_0}
why: This is Chezy's formula, formulated by Antoine de Chézy in 1775.
---
say: In 1889, Robert Manning found that $C$ is not constant, but scales with $R^{1/6}$. Setting $C = R^{1/6}/n$:
eq: V = \frac{1}{n}\,R^{2/3}\,S_0^{1/2}
why: This is Manning's equation. It is the universal standard in civil and water resources engineering.
---
say: Multiply velocity by area $A$ to get the volume rate of flow (discharge $Q$).
eq: Q = A\,V = \frac{1}{n}\,A\,R^{2/3}\,S_0^{1/2}
why: Flow rate equals cross-sectional area times mean velocity.
```

## Sizing a concrete lined canal

> [!note] Problem
> A rectangular concrete canal ($n = 0.015$) has a bed width $b = 4.0$ m and bed slope $S_0 = 0.0008$ (1 in 1250). If water flows at a uniform normal depth $y_n = 1.50$ m, find the discharge $Q$, mean velocity $V$, and average bed shear stress $\tau_0$.

**Step 1. Section geometry.**
- Area: $A = b \times y = 4.0 \times 1.5 = 6.0\ \text{m}^2$.
- Wetted perimeter: $P = b + 2y = 4.0 + 2(1.5) = 7.0$ m.
- Hydraulic radius: $R = A / P = 6.0 / 7.0 = 0.857$ m.

**Step 2. Mean velocity by Manning's equation.**
$$V = \frac{1}{0.015}\,(0.857)^{2/3}\,(0.0008)^{1/2} = \frac{1}{0.015} \times 0.9023 \times 0.02828 = 1.701\ \text{m/s}$$

**Step 3. Total discharge $Q$.**
$$Q = A \times V = 6.0 \times 1.701 = 10.21\ \text{m}^3/\text{s}$$

**Step 4. Bed shear stress $\tau_0$.**
$$\tau_0 = \gamma\,R\,S_0 = 9810 \times 0.857 \times 0.0008 = 6.73\ \text{N/m}^2\ (\text{Pa})$$

> [!note] What to take away
> Because depths are identical along the canal, the water surface slope, energy slope, and bed slope are all identical ($S_w = S_f = S_0$).

## Remember

> [!note] Day 9 in five lines
> - In uniform flow, downslope gravity balances wetted boundary shear: $W\sin\theta = \tau_0 P L$.
> - Opposing hydrostatic load triangles cancel ($F_1 - F_2 = 0$) because water depth is constant.
> - Mean boundary shear: $\tau_0 = \gamma R S_0$.
> - Chezy formula: $V = C\sqrt{R S_0}$.
> - Manning formula: $V = \frac{1}{n} R^{2/3} S_0^{1/2}$ and $Q = \frac{1}{n} A R^{2/3} S_0^{1/2}$.
>
