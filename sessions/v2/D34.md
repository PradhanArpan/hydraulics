## Running a pump that refuses to pump

You install a brand-new centrifugal water pump in the basement of an apartment building to lift drinking water to the rooftop storage tank. You switch on the electric motor — it hums and spins smoothly at $1450$ rpm.

Five minutes pass. Not a single drop of water emerges from the delivery pipe! Why does a spinning pump refuse to pump?

```predict
q: A centrifugal pump is started while the casing and suction pipe are full of air instead of water. Why can't it lift water from the underground sump?
o: Air is too heavy for the motor to spin
o: The pressure rise created by centrifugal action is proportional to fluid density! Air density is 800 times lighter than water, creating almost zero suction pressure.
o: The pipes are too clean
a: 2
why: Centrifugal head depends on fluid density (rho). Because air is ~800 times lighter than water, spinning air creates a vacuum of only fractions of a millimeter of water — completely unable to pull water up from the sump. The pump must be primed (filled with water) before starting!
```

Today you will see how **centrifugal pumps** work and why **priming** is the golden rule of pump engineering.

## Centrifugal Pump Anatomy & Working Principle

A centrifugal pump is a reversed reaction turbine:
- An external electric motor applies shaft power to spin an **impeller**.
- Centrifugal force flings water radially outward at high velocity.
- The casing decelerates the high-speed water, converting kinetic energy into pressure head that lifts water through the delivery pipe.

### 1. The Four Essential Components
1. **Impeller:** A rotating wheel with backward-curved vanes keyed to the driveshaft.
2. **Casing:** An airtight chamber surrounding the impeller:
   - **Volute Casing:** A spiral casing whose area gradually increases toward the delivery pipe to convert velocity head into static pressure.
   - **Vortex Casing:** Introduces a circular chamber between impeller and volute to reduce eddy losses.
   - **Diffuser (Turbine Pump):** Fitted with stationary guide vanes surrounding the impeller (highest efficiency).
3. **Suction Pipe with Foot Valve & Strainer:**
   - **Strainer:** Wire mesh preventing debris from entering and clogging the impeller.
   - **Foot Valve:** A spring-loaded or hinged **one-way check valve** at the bottom of the suction pipe that allows water to enter but prevents water from draining out when the pump stops!
4. **Delivery Pipe & Delivery Valve:** Conveys pressurized water to the overhead reservoir.

### 2. The Priming Process
**Priming** is the operation of filling the suction pipe, pump casing, and delivery pipe up to the delivery valve with water, completely expelling all trapped air before starting the motor.

- **Why Priming is Mandatory:**
  $$\Delta p = \rho\,(u_2^2 - u_1^2)$$
  Since air density ($\rho_{\text{air}} \approx 1.2\ \text{kg}/\text{m}^3$) is roughly $\frac{1}{800}$ of water density ($\rho_{\text{water}} = 1000\ \text{kg}/\text{m}^3$), an unprimed impeller generates a pressure drop 800 times too weak to lift water from the sump!
- **Self-Priming Pumps:** Incorporate an internal water reservoir and air separation chamber that automatically circulates water and evacuates air during startup.

## Why centrifugal pressure depends directly on density

Click through the radial equilibrium derivation.

```steps
say: Consider a thin annular ring of fluid of radius r, thickness dr, and density rho rotating at angular speed omega.
eq: dF_{\text{centrifugal}} = dm \cdot \omega^2\,r = (\rho \cdot A\,dr) \cdot \omega^2\,r
why: Mass equals density times cross-sectional area times radial thickness dr.
---
say: In radial equilibrium, this outward centrifugal force creates a radial pressure gradient dp.
eq: A \cdot dp = \rho\,A\,\omega^2\,r\,dr \implies \frac{dp}{dr} = \rho\,\omega^2\,r
why: Net pressure force across area A balances the centrifugal inertia force.
---
say: Integrate from the impeller inlet eye (radius r1) to the outer tip (radius r2).
eq: \Delta p = \int_{r1}^{r2} \rho\,\omega^2\,r\,dr = \frac{1}{2}\rho\,\omega^2(r_2^2 - r_1^2) = \frac{1}{2}\rho\,(u_2^2 - u_1^2)
why: Pressure rise created by rotation is directly proportional to fluid density rho!
---
say: If the casing contains air instead of water, density is 800 times smaller.
eq: \Delta p_{\text{air}} = \frac{\Delta p_{\text{water}}}{830} \approx 0.0012 \times \Delta p_{\text{water}}
why: The pressure rise created by spinning air cannot lift water even 2 centimetres!
```

## Sizing pump speed to initiate delivery

> [!note] Problem
> A centrifugal pump has an impeller of internal diameter $D_1 = 150$ mm and external diameter $D_2 = 300$ mm.
> The pump lifts water against a total manometric head $H_m = 25$ m.
> Find the minimum rotational speed $N_{\text{min}}$ (rpm) at which the pump will start delivering water, assuming no flow occurs until the centrifugal pressure head equals the manometric head.

**Step 1. Centrifugal head created by the impeller:**
Centrifugal head generated across the impeller is:
$$h_c = \frac{u_2^2 - u_1^2}{2g}$$
Where peripheral speeds are:
$$u_1 = \frac{\pi\,D_1\,N}{60}, \quad u_2 = \frac{\pi\,D_2\,N}{60}$$
$$u_2^2 - u_1^2 = \left(\frac{\pi\,N}{60}\right)^2 \left(D_2^2 - D_1^2\right)$$

**Step 2. Minimum starting condition:**
Flow starts only when the centrifugal head equals or exceeds total manometric head:
$$\frac{u_2^2 - u_1^2}{2g} \ge H_m$$
$$\left(\frac{\pi\,N}{60}\right)^2 \frac{D_2^2 - D_1^2}{2g} = H_m$$

**Step 3. Solve for $N_{\text{min}}$:**
$$D_2^2 - D_1^2 = (0.30)^2 - (0.15)^2 = 0.090 - 0.0225 = 0.0675\ \text{m}^2$$
$$\left(\frac{\pi\,N}{60}\right)^2 = \frac{2\,g\,H_m}{D_2^2 - D_1^2} = \frac{2 \times 9.81 \times 25}{0.0675} = \frac{490.5}{0.0675} = 7266.67$$
$$\frac{\pi\,N}{60} = \sqrt{7266.67} = 85.244$$
$$N_{\text{min}} = \frac{85.244 \times 60}{\pi} = \frac{5114.67}{3.14159} = 1628\ \text{rpm}$$

> [!note] What to take away
> If an operator runs this pump with a standard 4-pole motor ($1450$ rpm), it will spin in water forever without discharging a single drop! A 2-pole motor ($2900$ rpm) or variable frequency drive (VFD) is required.

a: 2
why: The foot valve lets water enter freely while pumping, but seats shut immediately when the pump stops, keeping the suction line full of water for the next start.
---
q: How does a volute casing convert kinetic energy into pressure head?
o: By spinning faster
o: By gradually expanding its cross-sectional area toward the delivery outlet, causing fluid to decelerate smoothly

why: By Bernoulli's principle, gradually increasing cross-sectional area slows flow velocity down, recovering kinetic energy as static pressure.

## Civil engineering connections

- **Sewage Lift Stations:** Municipal sewage pumps use non-clogging open or vortex impellers with only two or three wide blades, allowing solid rags and plastic debris to pass through without jamming.
- **Air Relief Valves in Water Mains:** In long municipal transmission pipelines, civil engineers install automatic kinetic air release valves at all pipeline summits to vent air pockets that would otherwise create artificial friction chokes and trigger pump vapor lock.

<!--beat:recap|Recap-->
## Three things to remember

1. **Centrifugal action:** Impeller rotation creates radial pressure rise proportional to fluid density: $\Delta p = \frac{1}{2}\rho(u_2^2 - u_1^2)$.
2. **Priming is non-negotiable:** Trapped air must be evacuated because air density cannot create sufficient suction head to lift water.
3. **Minimum speed threshold:** The pump will not deliver until peripheral speed satisfies $\frac{u_2^2 - u_1^2}{2g} \ge H_m$.
