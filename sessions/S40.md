> [!note] Core Concept
> Computers cannot directly solve continuous differential equations. **Discretization** converts differential equations into solvable systems of algebraic equations across discrete grid cells. In computational hydraulics, the **Finite Volume Method (FVM)** is universally preferred because it guarantees exact conservation of mass and momentum even on coarse, irregular river meshes.

## 1. Discretization Schemes
1. **Finite Difference Method (FDM):** Replaces continuous derivatives with Taylor series difference quotients (Forward, Backward, Central Differencing). Simple for Cartesian grids, but struggles with complex river boundaries.
2. **Finite Volume Method (FVM):** Integrates governing conservation equations over arbitrary polygonal control volumes (cells). Using Gauss's Divergence Theorem:
   $$\int_V \nabla \cdot \mathbf{F} \, dV = \oint_S \mathbf{F} \cdot \mathbf{n} \, dS$$
   The rate of change of fluid inside a cell equals the sum of convective and diffusive fluxes crossing its perimeter faces. **Mass is conserved identically.**

## 2. Courant-Friedrichs-Lewy (CFL) Stability Condition
For explicit numerical time-stepping schemes, numerical information must propagate faster than physical fluid and gravity waves across the grid. The non-dimensional **Courant Number ($CFL$)** must satisfy:
$$CFL = \frac{(|u| + \sqrt{gh}) \Delta t}{\Delta x} \le 1.0$$

If $CFL > 1.0$, numerical calculations become mathematically unstable, producing wild numerical oscillations and division-by-zero crashes (numerical blowup).

To maintain stability:
$$\Delta t \le \frac{\Delta x}{|u| + \sqrt{gh}}$$

### Example 40.1: Setting a Stable Time Step in a River Model
**Problem:** In a 2D HEC-RAS flume model, computational grid cells are sized at $\Delta x = 0.50 \text{ m}$. In a high-speed spillway chute, maximum water depth is $h = 1.20 \text{ m}$ and maximum flow velocity is $u = 6.50 \text{ m/s}$.
1. Calculate the wave celerity $c$ and maximum characteristic speed.
2. Determine the maximum allowable time step $\Delta t_{max}$ for explicit numerical stability ($CFL \le 1.0$).
3. If an engineer sets $\Delta t = 0.10 \text{ s}$, calculate the resulting Courant number and predict model behavior.

**Solution:**
1. **Characteristic Wave Speed:**
   $$c = \sqrt{g h} = \sqrt{9.81 \times 1.20} = \sqrt{11.772} = 3.431 \text{ m/s}$$
   $$V_{char} = u + c = 6.50 + 3.431 = 9.931 \text{ m/s}$$
2. **Maximum Stable Time Step:**
   $$\Delta t_{max} = \frac{\Delta x}{u + c} = \frac{0.50}{9.931} = 0.0503 \text{ s}$$
   To ensure stability with a safety factor, the engineer should choose $\Delta t = 0.040 \text{ s}$.
3. **Evaluation of $\Delta t = 0.10 \text{ s}$:**
   $$CFL = \frac{(u + c) \Delta t}{\Delta x} = \frac{9.931 \times 0.10}{0.50} = \frac{0.9931}{0.50} = 1.986$$
   Since $CFL = 1.986 > 1.0$, the Courant criterion is violated. The explicit solver will become **numerically unstable**, generating artificial water surface spikes and causing the run to crash.

<!--tab:Summary-->
### Key Takeaways
- FVM balances fluxes over control volume faces, ensuring conservation.
- Courant number: $CFL = \frac{(u + \sqrt{gh})\Delta t}{\Delta x} \le 1.0$.
- Finer spatial grids ($\Delta x$) demand proportionally smaller time steps ($\Delta t$).
