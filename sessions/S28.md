> [!note] Core Concept
> **Hydraulic Turbines** are rotodynamic prime movers that extract hydraulic potential and kinetic energy from falling water and convert it into mechanical rotation of a shaft, which directly drives an electrical generator. Turbines are classified fundamentally by their mechanism of energy conversion into **Impulse Turbines** and **Reaction Turbines**.

## 1. Hydroelectric Power Plant Layout
1. **Dam & Reservoir:** Creates gross head and impounds seasonal water runoff.
2. **Intake & Trash Rack:** Prevents river debris, logs, and sediment boulders from entering penstocks.
3. **Forebay & Surge Tank:** Acts as a pressure relief reservoir to absorb dangerous **water hammer pressure surges** caused by sudden governor valve closures.
4. **Penstock:** High-pressure steel or concrete conduit conveying water from the dam to the powerhouse.
5. **Power House:** Accommodates hydraulic turbine runners, governors, generators, transformers, and control consoles.
6. **Tailrace:** Canal discharging spent water back into the river course.

## 2. Classification of Turbines
1. **By Action of Water:**
   - **Impulse Turbine (Pelton):** Total available head is converted entirely into kinetic energy in one or more high-speed atmospheric nozzles before striking buckets. Pressure throughout the casing remains atmospheric ($p = p_{atm}$).
   - **Reaction Turbine (Francis, Kaplan):** Water enters the enclosed runner under high pressure; part of the head is kinetic, but the majority is pressure head. Pressure drops continuously as water expands across moving blades.
2. **By Available Head & Specific Speed ($N_s$):**
   - High Head ($H > 250 \text{ m}$): Pelton Wheel ($N_s = 10 - 60$).
   - Medium Head ($60 \text{ m} \le H \le 250 \text{ m}$): Francis Turbine ($N_s = 60 - 300$).
   - Low Head ($H < 60 \text{ m}$): Kaplan & Propeller Turbines ($N_s = 300 - 1000$).
3. **By Direction of Flow Across Runner:**
   - Tangential Flow: Pelton wheel.
   - Inward Radial / Mixed Flow: Modern Francis runner.
   - Axial Flow: Kaplan and Propeller turbines.

### Example 28.1: Head Loss and Net Power of a Hydroelectric Station
**Problem:** A hydroelectric scheme has an available reservoir gross head $H_g = 220 \text{ m}$. Water is conveyed through a penstock $1.50 \text{ m}$ in diameter and $600 \text{ m}$ long with Darcy friction factor $f = 0.020$. Flow discharge is $Q = 4.0 \text{ m}^3/\text{s}$. Minor head losses in intake and bends equal $1.50 \text{ m}$. Turbine overall efficiency is $\eta_o = 88\%$. Calculate:
1. Head lost to friction in the penstock.
2. Net available head $H$ at the turbine inlet.
3. Electrical power output generated (MW).

**Solution:**
1. **Flow Velocity in Penstock:**
   $$A_p = \frac{\pi}{4}(1.50)^2 = 1.767 \text{ m}^2$$
   $$V_p = \frac{Q}{A_p} = \frac{4.0}{1.767} = 2.264 \text{ m/s}$$
2. **Friction Loss $h_f$:**
   $$h_f = \frac{f L V_p^2}{2 g D_p} = \frac{0.020 \times 600 \times (2.264)^2}{2 \times 9.81 \times 1.50} = \frac{12.0 \times 5.126}{29.43} = \frac{61.51}{29.43} = 2.09 \text{ m}$$
   Total head loss: $h_{loss} = 2.09 + 1.50 = 3.59 \text{ m}$.
3. **Net Available Head $H$:**
   $$H = H_g - h_{loss} = 220.0 - 3.59 = 216.41 \text{ m}$$
4. **Electrical Power Output:**
   $$P_{shaft} = \eta_o \rho g Q H = 0.88 \times 1000 \times 9.81 \times 4.0 \times 216.41 = 7,477,800 \text{ W} = 7.478 \text{ MW}$$

### Key Takeaways
- Impulse turbines operate at atmospheric casing pressure with pure kinetic energy.
- Reaction turbines operate in enclosed pressurized casings with gradual pressure drop.
- Net head: $H = H_g - h_f$.
- Selection matrix: Pelton ($>250$ m), Francis ($60-250$ m), Kaplan ($<60$ m).
