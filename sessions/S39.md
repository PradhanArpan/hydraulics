> [!note] Core Concept
> Second-order partial differential equations (PDEs) governing fluid mechanics fall into three distinct mathematical classifications: **Elliptic**, **Parabolic**, and **Hyperbolic**. In open channel river hydraulics, integrating the Navier-Stokes equations over water depth yields the **2D Shallow Water Equations (SWE)**—a hyperbolic system where gravity wave disturbances propagate at wave celerity $c = \sqrt{gh}$.

## 1. Mathematical Classification of PDEs
For a general 2D second-order PDE $A \frac{\partial^2 \phi}{\partial x^2} + B \frac{\partial^2 \phi}{\partial x \partial y} + C \frac{\partial^2 \phi}{\partial y^2} = F$:
- Discriminant: $D = B^2 - 4 A C$
1. **Elliptic ($D < 0$):** Steady potential flow, Laplace equation $\nabla^2 \phi = 0$. Disturbances propagate instantaneously in all directions; requires closed boundary conditions on all sides.
2. **Parabolic ($D = 0$):** Transient heat diffusion, boundary layer equations $\frac{\partial u}{\partial t} = \nu \frac{\partial^2 u}{\partial y^2}$. Information marches forward in time/space like a wave front with diffusion.
3. **Hyperbolic ($D > 0$):** Unsteady shallow water waves, Saint-Venant equations. Information travels along distinct physical **characteristic lines** at finite speeds ($u \pm c$).

## 2. 2D Shallow Water Equations (Saint-Venant System)
Assuming water depth $h$ is small compared to wavelength, vertical acceleration is negligible, yielding hydrostatic pressure distribution:
1. **Depth-Averaged Continuity:**
   $$\frac{\partial h}{\partial t} + \frac{\partial (h u)}{\partial x} + \frac{\partial (h v)}{\partial y} = 0$$
2. **$x$-Momentum Equation:**
   $$\frac{\partial (h u)}{\partial t} + \frac{\partial (h u^2 + \frac{1}{2} g h^2)}{\partial x} + \frac{\partial (h u v)}{\partial y} = -g h \frac{\partial z_b}{\partial x} - g \frac{n^2 u \sqrt{u^2 + v^2}}{h^{1/3}}$$
3. **$y$-Momentum Equation:**
   $$\frac{\partial (h v)}{\partial t} + \frac{\partial (h u v)}{\partial x} + \frac{\partial (h v^2 + \frac{1}{2} g h^2)}{\partial y} = -g h \frac{\partial z_b}{\partial y} - g \frac{n^2 v \sqrt{u^2 + v^2}}{h^{1/3}}$$

Where $z_b$ is riverbed elevation and the last term is Manning's bed friction slope $g h S_f$.

### Example 39.1: Wave Celerity and Characteristic Speeds
**Problem:** In an open channel reach of water depth $h = 2.50 \text{ m}$, flow velocity is $u = 3.00 \text{ m/s}$ in the downstream $x$-direction ($v = 0$).
1. Calculate the shallow water gravity wave celerity $c$.
2. Determine the two characteristic wave propagation velocities in the channel.
3. Check whether flood wave information can travel upstream.

**Solution:**
1. **Gravity Wave Celerity $c$:**
   $$c = \sqrt{g h} = \sqrt{9.81 \times 2.50} = \sqrt{24.525} = 4.952 \text{ m/s}$$
2. **Characteristic Speeds:**
   In shallow water theory, information travels along the positive and negative characteristic paths:
   $$\lambda_1 = u + c = 3.00 + 4.952 = +7.952 \text{ m/s} \quad \text{(downstream characteristic)}$$
   $$\lambda_2 = u - c = 3.00 - 4.952 = -1.952 \text{ m/s} \quad \text{(upstream characteristic)}$$
3. **Upstream Information Travel:**
   Since $\lambda_2 = -1.952 \text{ m/s} < 0$, the negative wave propagates upstream at $1.952 \text{ m/s}$ relative to the bank.
   This confirms that the flow is **subcritical** ($Fr = u/c = 3.00 / 4.952 = 0.606 < 1$), so downstream flood control structures will influence upstream water levels.

### Key Takeaways
- PDE types: Elliptic (steady/global), Parabolic (diffusion/marching), Hyperbolic (waves/characteristics).
- 2D Shallow Water Equations govern depth-averaged river and floodplain hydrodynamics.
- Characteristic speeds: $\lambda = u \pm \sqrt{gh}$.
