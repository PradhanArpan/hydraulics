> [!note] Core Concept
> All fluid mechanics problems are governed by three fundamental physical principles: **Conservation of Mass (Continuity)**, **Conservation of Momentum (Newton's 2nd Law / Navier-Stokes)**, and **Conservation of Energy (1st Law of Thermodynamics)**. In hydraulic engineering where water is treated as an incompressible Newtonian fluid under isothermal conditions, continuity and the Navier-Stokes equations form a closed mathematical system.

## 1. Continuity Equation (Conservation of Mass)
In differential conservation form:
$$\frac{\partial \rho}{\partial t} + \nabla \cdot (\rho \mathbf{u}) = 0$$
For incompressible liquid flow ($\rho = \text{constant}$):
$$\nabla \cdot \mathbf{u} = \frac{\partial u}{\partial x} + \frac{\partial v}{\partial y} + \frac{\partial w}{\partial z} = 0$$

## 2. Incompressible Navier-Stokes Equations (Momentum)
Formulated by **Claude-Louis Navier (1822)** and **George Gabriel Stokes (1845)**:
$$\rho \left(\frac{\partial \mathbf{u}}{\partial t} + \mathbf{u} \cdot \nabla \mathbf{u}\right) = -\nabla p + \mu \nabla^2 \mathbf{u} + \rho \mathbf{g}$$

In Cartesian tensor conservation form ($i = 1, 2, 3$):
$$\frac{\partial (\rho u_i)}{\partial t} + \frac{\partial (\rho u_i u_j)}{\partial x_j} = -\frac{\partial p}{\partial x_i} + \frac{\partial}{\partial x_j}\left[\mu \left(\frac{\partial u_i}{\partial x_j} + \frac{\partial u_j}{\partial x_i}\right)\right] + \rho g_i$$

Where:
- $\frac{\partial (\rho u_i)}{\partial t}$: Local transient acceleration.
- $\frac{\partial (\rho u_i u_j)}{\partial x_j}$: Convective (advective) momentum flux tensor.
- $-\frac{\partial p}{\partial x_i}$: Driving static pressure gradient.
- $\frac{\partial \tau_{ij}}{\partial x_j}$: Molecular viscous diffusion tensor.
- $\rho g_i$: Body force (gravity).

## 3. Reynolds-Averaged Navier-Stokes (RANS)
Decomposing instantaneous velocity into mean and turbulent fluctuations ($u = \bar{u} + u'$):
$$\frac{\partial \bar{u}_i}{\partial t} + \bar{u}_j \frac{\partial \bar{u}_i}{\partial x_j} = -\frac{1}{\rho}\frac{\partial \bar{p}}{\partial x_i} + \nu \nabla^2 \bar{u}_i - \frac{\partial (\overline{u_i' u_j'})}{\partial x_j} + g_i$$
Where $-\rho \overline{u_i' u_j'}$ represents the **Reynolds Stress Tensor** requiring turbulence modeling closure.

### Example 38.1: Continuity and Velocity Field Verification
**Problem:** The velocity field of an incompressible steady 2D flow in an open channel junction is given by:
$$u = 2x^2 - 3y, \quad v = -4xy + 5$$
1. Determine whether this velocity field satisfies the physical law of conservation of mass (continuity).
2. Calculate the acceleration components $a_x$ and $a_y$ at the point $(x = 1.0 \text{ m}, y = 2.0 \text{ m})$.

**Solution:**
1. **Continuity Verification:**
   For 2D incompressible flow, continuity requires $\frac{\partial u}{\partial x} + \frac{\partial v}{\partial y} = 0$:
   $$\frac{\partial u}{\partial x} = \frac{\partial}{\partial x}(2x^2 - 3y) = 4x$$
   $$\frac{\partial v}{\partial y} = \frac{\partial}{\partial y}(-4xy + 5) = -4x$$
   $$\frac{\partial u}{\partial x} + \frac{\partial v}{\partial y} = 4x + (-4x) = 0$$
   Continuity is **identically satisfied everywhere** in the flow domain!
2. **Acceleration at $(1.0, 2.0)$:**
   At point $(1.0, 2.0)$:
   $u = 2(1)^2 - 3(2) = 2 - 6 = -4.0 \text{ m/s}$
   $v = -4(1)(2) + 5 = -8 + 5 = -3.0 \text{ m/s}$

   Convective acceleration in $x$:
   $$a_x = u \frac{\partial u}{\partial x} + v \frac{\partial u}{\partial y} = (-4.0)(4x) + (-3.0)(-3) = (-4.0)(4) + 9.0 = -16.0 + 9.0 = -7.0 \text{ m/s}^2$$
   Convective acceleration in $y$:
   $$a_y = u \frac{\partial v}{\partial x} + v \frac{\partial v}{\partial y} = (-4.0)(-4y) + (-3.0)(-4x) = (-4.0)(-8.0) + (-3.0)(-4.0) = 32.0 + 12.0 = 44.0 \text{ m/s}^2$$

### Key Takeaways
- Incompressible continuity: $\nabla \cdot \mathbf{u} = 0$.
- Navier-Stokes equations balance acceleration against pressure gradient, viscous diffusion, and gravity.
- RANS time-averaging introduces Reynolds stress tensor $-\rho \overline{u_i' u_j'}$.
