> [!note] Core Concept
> **Specific Speed ($N_s$)** is a fundamental dimensionless/characteristic parameter defined as the speed of a geometrically similar model turbine that produces 1 kW of power under a net head of 1 meter. It serves as the primary criterion for turbine selection regardless of prototype size.

## 1. Specific Speed Formula Derivation
From hydraulic similitude:
- $V \propto \sqrt{H} \implies u = \frac{\pi D N}{60} \propto \sqrt{H} \implies D \propto \frac{\sqrt{H}}{N}$
- Discharge: $Q \propto D^2 V \propto \left(\frac{\sqrt{H}}{N}\right)^2 \sqrt{H} = \frac{H^{3/2}}{N^2}$
- Power: $P = \rho g Q H \propto \frac{H^{3/2}}{N^2} \cdot H = \frac{H^{5/2}}{N^2}$
Rearranging for rotational speed:
$$N^2 \propto \frac{H^{5/2}}{P} \implies N \propto \frac{H^{5/4}}{\sqrt{P}}$$
Setting $P = 1 \text{ kW}$ and $H = 1 \text{ m}$ yields the definition of **Specific Speed ($N_s$)**:
$$N_s = \frac{N \sqrt{P}}{H^{5/4}}$$
*(Where $N$ is in rpm, $P$ in kW, and $H$ in meters).*

Turbine Selection by Specific Speed:
- Pelton single-jet: $N_s = 10 - 35$
- Pelton multi-jet: $N_s = 35 - 60$
- Francis Turbine: $N_s = 60 - 300$
- Kaplan Turbine: $N_s = 300 - 1000$

## 2. Unit Quantities (Performance under $H = 1 \text{ m}$)
- **Unit Speed ($N_u$):** $N_u = \frac{N}{\sqrt{H}}$
- **Unit Discharge ($Q_u$):** $Q_u = \frac{Q}{\sqrt{H}}$
- **Unit Power ($P_u$):** $P_u = \frac{P}{H^{3/2}}$

## 3. Scale Effect & Moody's Efficiency Formula
Because full-scale prototype turbines have larger dimensions and relatively smoother surface boundary layers, prototype efficiency $\eta_p$ is slightly higher than model efficiency $\eta_m$:
$$\frac{1 - \eta_p}{1 - \eta_m} = \left(\frac{D_m}{D_p}\right)^{1/5} \left(\frac{H_m}{H_p}\right)^{1/10}$$

### Example 33.1: Model Testing and Similitude of a Francis Turbine
**Problem:** A prototype Francis turbine develops $15,000 \text{ kW}$ under a net head $H_p = 50.0 \text{ m}$ at speed $N_p = 250 \text{ rpm}$. A $1:5$ scale model ($D_m / D_p = 0.20$) is tested in a hydraulic laboratory under a test head $H_m = 8.0 \text{ m}$.
1. Determine the specific speed $N_s$ and confirm turbine type.
2. Find the operating speed $N_m$ of the laboratory model.
3. Determine the power developed by the model turbine if model efficiency is $\eta_m = 87\%$.
4. Predict the prototype efficiency using Moody's formula.

**Solution:**
1. **Specific Speed $N_s$:**
   $$N_s = \frac{N_p \sqrt{P_p}}{H_p^{5/4}} = \frac{250 \times \sqrt{15,000}}{(50.0)^{1.25}} = \frac{250 \times 122.474}{133.352} = \frac{30,618.6}{133.352} = 229.6 \text{ rpm}$$
   Since $60 < N_s < 300$, this confirms a **Francis reaction turbine**.
2. **Model Speed $N_m$:**
   From unit speed similitude:
   $$\frac{D_m N_m}{\sqrt{H_m}} = \frac{D_p N_p}{\sqrt{H_p}} \implies N_m = N_p \left(\frac{D_p}{D_m}\right) \sqrt{\frac{H_m}{H_p}} = 250 \times (5) \times \sqrt{\frac{8.0}{50.0}} = 1250 \times 0.40 = 500 \text{ rpm}$$
3. **Model Power $P_m$:**
   $$\frac{P_m}{D_m^2 H_m^{3/2}} = \frac{P_p}{D_p^2 H_p^{3/2}} \implies P_m = P_p \left(\frac{D_m}{D_p}\right)^2 \left(\frac{H_m}{H_p}\right)^{1.5} = 15,000 \times (0.20)^2 \times (0.16)^{1.5}$$
   $$P_m = 15,000 \times 0.040 \times 0.064 = 38.4 \text{ kW}$$
4. **Prototype Efficiency $\eta_p$ (Moody's Formula):**
   $$\frac{1 - \eta_p}{1 - 0.87} = (0.20)^{0.2} \times \left(\frac{8.0}{50.0}\right)^{0.1} = 0.7248 \times 0.8326 = 0.6034$$
   $$1 - \eta_p = 0.13 \times 0.6034 = 0.0784 \implies \eta_p = 1 - 0.0784 = 0.9216 = 92.16\%$$

### Key Takeaways
- Specific speed formula: $N_s = \frac{N \sqrt{P}}{H^{5/4}}$.
- Unit quantities: $N_u = N/\sqrt{H}$, $Q_u = Q/\sqrt{H}$, $P_u = P/H^{1.5}$.
- Moody scale formula predicts efficiency step-up from model to prototype.
