> [!note] Core Concept
> Invented by **Viktor Kaplan in 1913**, the **Kaplan turbine** is an axial-flow reaction turbine featuring adjustable propeller blades coordinated automatically with adjustable guide vanes (**double regulation**). It operates under low heads ($2 - 60 \text{ m}$) with massive discharges, maintaining flat, peak efficiencies across $30\% - 100\%$ load range.

## 1. Double Regulation & Axial Kinematics
- In a fixed-blade **Propeller turbine**, efficiency drops sharply at partial loads because off-design flow angles cause shock and separation.
- In a **Kaplan turbine**, the runner blades rotate on internal trunnions driven by an oil-pressure servomotor inside the central hub. When wicket gates throttle discharge, runner blades pitch simultaneously, preserving shockless entry tangential flow.

## 2. Draft Tube Theory & Pressure Recovery
The draft tube connects the runner exit to the tailrace. It performs two vital engineering functions:
1. **Head Elevation Recovery:** Allows placing the turbine runner above the tailrace flood level without losing suction head ($z_2$).
2. **Kinetic Energy Pressure Recovery:** Converts large wasted exit velocity head $\frac{V_2^2}{2g}$ into useful pressure head across a diverging conduit.

Applying Bernoulli's equation between runner exit (2) and draft tube discharge (3):
$$\frac{p_2}{\gamma} + z_2 + \frac{V_2^2}{2g} = \frac{p_3}{\gamma} + z_3 + \frac{V_3^2}{2g} + h_f$$
Since section 3 is at the tailrace ($p_3 = p_{atm}, z_3 = 0$):
$$\frac{p_2}{\gamma} = \frac{p_{atm}}{\gamma} - z_2 - \left(\frac{V_2^2 - V_3^2}{2g} - h_f\right)$$
Pressure at the runner exit is **sub-atmospheric (suction)**!
Draft tube efficiency:
$$\eta_{dt} = \frac{\frac{V_2^2 - V_3^2}{2g} - h_f}{\frac{V_2^2}{2g}}$$

### Example 32.1: Draft Tube Pressure Head and Efficiency
**Problem:** A straight conical draft tube of a Kaplan turbine has inlet diameter $D_2 = 2.0 \text{ m}$ set $z_2 = 2.50 \text{ m}$ above the tailrace. Exit diameter is $D_3 = 3.20 \text{ m}$. Discharge is $Q = 35.0 \text{ m}^3/\text{s}$. Atmospheric pressure is $10.30 \text{ m}$ of water. Head loss in the draft tube is $h_f = 0.15 \frac{V_2^2}{2g}$. Calculate:
1. Inlet velocity $V_2$ and outlet velocity $V_3$.
2. Vacuum pressure head at the runner exit.
3. Draft tube efficiency $\eta_{dt}$.

**Solution:**
1. **Velocities at Inlet and Outlet:**
   $$A_2 = \frac{\pi}{4}(2.0)^2 = 3.1416 \text{ m}^2 \implies V_2 = \frac{35.0}{3.1416} = 11.14 \text{ m/s}$$
   $$A_3 = \frac{\pi}{4}(3.20)^2 = 8.0425 \text{ m}^2 \implies V_3 = \frac{35.0}{8.0425} = 4.35 \text{ m/s}$$
   $$\frac{V_2^2}{2g} = \frac{(11.14)^2}{2 \times 9.81} = 6.325 \text{ m}, \quad \frac{V_3^2}{2g} = \frac{(4.35)^2}{19.62} = 0.964 \text{ m}$$
2. **Head Loss $h_f$ & Pressure Recovery:**
   $$h_f = 0.15 \times 6.325 = 0.949 \text{ m}$$
   $$\text{Pressure Head Regained} = \frac{V_2^2 - V_3^2}{2g} - h_f = (6.325 - 0.964) - 0.949 = 5.361 - 0.949 = 4.412 \text{ m}$$
3. **Vacuum Pressure Head at Runner Exit ($p_2/\gamma$):**
   $$\frac{p_2}{\gamma} = 10.30 - 2.50 - 4.412 = 3.388 \text{ m of water (absolute)}$$
   $$\text{Vacuum Head} = 10.30 - 3.388 = 6.912 \text{ m of water vacuum}$$
4. **Draft Tube Efficiency:**
   $$\eta_{dt} = \frac{4.412}{6.325} = 0.6976 = 69.76\%$$

### Key Takeaways
- Double regulation: adjustable wicket gates + pitchable runner blades.
- Draft tube recovers kinetic energy $\frac{V_2^2 - V_3^2}{2g}$ as pressure head.
- Setting height $z_2$ strictly limited by cavitation threshold.
