> [!note] Core Concept
> Designing a Pelton turbine requires optimizing the runner pitch diameter $D$, jet diameter $d$, number of buckets $Z$, and rotational speed $N$. The optimum speed ratio $\phi \approx 0.46$ balances centrifugal bucket forces against hydrodynamic momentum transfer.

## 1. Design Parameters and Empirical Rules
1. **Jet Velocity ($V_1$):**
   $$V_1 = C_v \sqrt{2 g H} \quad (C_v \approx 0.98 - 0.99)$$
2. **Peripheral Blade Speed ($u$):**
   $$u = \phi \sqrt{2 g H} = \frac{\pi D N}{60}$$
   Where $\phi$ is the **speed ratio** ($0.43 \le \phi \le 0.48$, optimal at $\phi \approx 0.46$).
3. **Jet Diameter ($d$):**
   For a single-jet turbine with discharge $Q$:
   $$d = \sqrt{\frac{4 Q}{\pi V_1}}$$
4. **Jet Ratio ($m$):**
   $$m = \frac{D}{d}$$
   *(Commercial design standards require $m = 10 \text{ to } 16$ to prevent bucket crowding).*
5. **Number of Buckets ($Z$ - Tygun's Formula):**
   $$Z = 15 + \frac{D}{2 d} = 15 + 0.5 m$$
6. **Bucket Dimensions:**
   - Width: $B = 3d \text{ to } 5d$
   - Depth: $T = 0.9d \text{ to } 1.2d$
   - Radial Length: $L_b = 2.5d \text{ to } 3d$

### Example 30.1: Complete Hydraulic Design of a Pelton Runner
**Problem:** Design a Pelton wheel to develop $4,500 \text{ kW}$ of shaft power under a net head $H = 360 \text{ m}$ at speed $N = 500 \text{ rpm}$. Take overall efficiency $\eta_o = 86\%$, $C_v = 0.98$, speed ratio $\phi = 0.46$, and jet ratio $m = D/d = 12$. Determine:
1. Discharge $Q$ and number of nozzles required if nozzle diameter is limited to $d \le 100 \text{ mm}$.
2. Pitch circle diameter $D$ of the runner.
3. Actual jet diameter $d$.
4. Number of buckets $Z$.

**Solution:**
1. **Total Flow Rate $Q$:**
   $$P_{shaft} = \eta_o \rho g Q H \implies 4,500,000 = 0.86 \times 1000 \times 9.81 \times Q \times 360$$
   $$4,500,000 = 3,037,176 \times Q \implies Q = 1.4817 \text{ m}^3/\text{s}$$
2. **Jet Velocity $V_1$ & Peripheral Speed $u$:**
   $$V_1 = C_v \sqrt{2 g H} = 0.98 \times \sqrt{2 \times 9.81 \times 360} = 0.98 \times 84.043 = 82.36 \text{ m/s}$$
   $$u = \phi \sqrt{2 g H} = 0.46 \times 84.043 = 38.66 \text{ m/s}$$
3. **Runner Pitch Circle Diameter $D$:**
   $$u = \frac{\pi D N}{60} \implies D = \frac{60 u}{\pi N} = \frac{60 \times 38.66}{\pi \times 500} = \frac{2319.6}{1570.8} = 1.477 \text{ m}$$
4. **Jet Diameter & Nozzle Configuration:**
   Using jet ratio $m = D/d = 12$:
   $$d = \frac{D}{12} = \frac{1.477}{12} = 0.123 \text{ m} = 123 \text{ mm}$$
   Since $d = 123 \text{ mm} > 100 \text{ mm}$ (exceeds single-jet limit), we use **two nozzles** ($n_{jets} = 2$):
   Discharge per jet: $Q_{jet} = 1.4817 / 2 = 0.7408 \text{ m}^3/\text{s}$.
   $$d_{actual} = \sqrt{\frac{4 Q_{jet}}{\pi V_1}} = \sqrt{\frac{4 \times 0.7408}{\pi \times 82.36}} = \sqrt{\frac{2.9632}{258.74}} = \sqrt{0.01145} = 0.107 \text{ m} = 107 \text{ mm}$$
   New jet ratio: $m = 1.477 / 0.107 = 13.8$.
5. **Number of Buckets $Z$:**
   $$Z = 15 + 0.5 m = 15 + 0.5(13.8) = 15 + 6.9 = 21.9 \approx 22 \text{ buckets}$$

### Key Takeaways
- Pitch diameter: $D = 60u / (\pi N)$.
- Jet ratio: $m = D/d \approx 10 - 16$.
- Number of buckets: $Z = 15 + 0.5 m$ (Tygun formula).
- Multi-jet configurations allow higher capacity without oversize buckets.
