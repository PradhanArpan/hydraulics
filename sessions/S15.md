> [!note] Specific Energy Core Principle
> The **Specific Energy ($E$)** of flowing water at any channel section is defined as the total energy head measured with reference to the **channel bed as datum**:
> $$\mathbf{E = y + \frac{\alpha V^2}{2g} = y + \frac{Q^2}{2g A^2}}$$
> Unlike the total energy head $H = z + E$ which monotonically drops in the direction of flow by friction loss $h_L$, specific energy $E$ can increase, decrease, or remain constant when the channel bed slope, elevation, or width varies.

## 1. The Specific Energy Curve ($E-y$ Diagram)
For a constant discharge $Q$ in a channel of given geometry, plotting flow depth $y$ against specific energy $E$ yields a parabolic curve with two distinct asymptotic limbs:
1. **Potential Energy Asymptote ($E = y$):** At high depths, velocity $V = Q/A$ becomes tiny, so velocity head $V^2/2g \to 0$. The curve approaches the $45^\circ$ line $E = y$.
2. **Kinetic Energy Asymptote ($E \to \infty$ as $y \to 0$):** As depth approaches zero, velocity approaches infinity, causing $V^2/2g \to \infty$. The curve approaches the horizontal axis asymptotically.
3. **The Vertex (Critical Point):** The curve possesses an absolute minimum specific energy **$E_{\text{min}}$** at a unique depth termed the **Critical Depth ($y_c$)**.

```
         Flow Depth y
              ▲
              │                 Subcritical Limb (Fr < 1, Tranquil)
              │               . '  /
           y₂ ┼─────────────●'    /
              │           . '    / 45° Line (E = y)
              │         . '     /
           yc ┼────────● Vertex (Emin, yc)  Fr = 1.0
              │         ` .   /
           y₁ ┼───────────●' /
              │             `────── Supercritical Limb (Fr > 1, Rapid)
              └───────────────┼───────────────► Specific Energy E
                             Emin
```

## 2. General Mathematical Derivation of Critical Flow
Differentiating specific energy $E = y + \frac{Q^2}{2g A^2}$ with respect to depth $y$ (taking $\alpha = 1.0$):
$$\frac{dE}{dy} = 1 - \frac{2 Q^2}{2g A^3} \left(\frac{dA}{dy}\right) = 1 - \frac{Q^2}{g A^3} \left(\frac{dA}{dy}\right)$$
Since an incremental change in area is $dA = T \cdot dy$, where $T$ is the top width at the water surface:
$$\frac{dA}{dy} = T$$
Setting $\frac{dE}{dy} = 0$ to find the condition of minimum energy:
$$1 - \frac{Q^2 T}{g A^3} = 0 \iff \mathbf{\frac{Q^2 T}{g A^3} = 1.0}$$
Noting that hydraulic depth is $D = A/T$ and velocity is $V = Q/A$:
$$\frac{Q^2}{A^2} \frac{T}{g A} = \frac{V^2}{g (A/T)} = \frac{V^2}{g D} = 1.0 \iff \mathbf{Fr = \frac{V}{\sqrt{g D}} = 1.0}$$
**Fundamental Theorem:** Critical flow occurs when the Froude number equals unity ($Fr = 1.0$). At critical flow:
- Flow velocity equals the shallow gravity wave celerity: $V_c = \sqrt{g D_c}$.
- Surface disturbances cannot propagate upstream.

## 3. Relationships for Rectangular Channels
For a rectangular channel of width $B$ with discharge intensity $q = Q/B$:
1. **Critical Depth ($y_c$):**
   $$\frac{Q^2 B}{g (B y_c)^3} = 1 \implies \frac{q^2}{g y_c^3} = 1 \implies \mathbf{y_c = \sqrt[3]{\frac{q^2}{g}}}$$
2. **Minimum Specific Energy ($E_{\text{min}}$):**
   $$E_{\text{min}} = y_c + \frac{V_c^2}{2g} = y_c + \frac{g y_c}{2g} = y_c + 0.5 y_c = \mathbf{1.5 y_c}$$
   Conversely: $\mathbf{y_c = \frac{2}{3} E_{\text{min}}}$.
3. **Maximum Discharge for a Given Specific Energy ($q_{\text{max}}$):**
   Rearranging: $E = y + \frac{q^2}{2g y^2} \implies q = \sqrt{2g (E - y) y^2}$.
   Maximizing $q$ with respect to $y$ yields $y = \frac{2}{3} E = y_c$.
   $$\mathbf{q_{\text{max}} = \sqrt{g y_c^3} = \sqrt{g \left(\frac{2}{3} E\right)^3} = 1.705 E^{3/2}}$$

## 4. Alternate Depths
For any specific energy $E > E_{\text{min}}$, a horizontal line drawn across the $E-y$ curve intersects it at **two distinct depths**:
- **Supercritical Alternate Depth ($y_1 < y_c$):** Shallow depth, high velocity ($Fr > 1$, lower limb).
- **Subcritical Alternate Depth ($y_2 > y_c$):** Deep depth, low velocity ($Fr < 1$, upper limb).
Both depths carry the **identical discharge $Q$ with the identical specific energy $E$**.

### Example 15.1: Critical Depth and Alternate Depths in a Rectangular Canal
**Problem:** A rectangular canal $4.0 \text{ m}$ wide carries a discharge of $12.0 \text{ m}^3/\text{s}$.
1. Compute the critical depth $y_c$ and minimum specific energy $E_{\text{min}}$.
2. If the actual flow depth is $y_1 = 2.0 \text{ m}$, calculate the specific energy $E_1$, Froude number $Fr_1$, and the alternate depth $y_2$.

:::sol Detailed Step-by-Step Solution
1. **Critical State Calculations:**
   - Unit discharge: $q = \frac{Q}{B} = \frac{12.0}{4.0} = 3.0 \text{ m}^2/\text{s}$
   - Critical depth:
     $$y_c = \sqrt[3]{\frac{q^2}{g}} = \sqrt[3]{\frac{3.0^2}{9.81}} = \sqrt[3]{\frac{9.0}{9.81}} = \sqrt[3]{0.91743} = \mathbf{0.9717 \text{ m}}$$
   - Minimum specific energy:
     $$E_{\text{min}} = 1.5 y_c = 1.5 \times 0.9717 = \mathbf{1.4576 \text{ m}}$$

2. **Flow at Depth $y_1 = 2.0 \text{ m}$:**
   - Velocity: $V_1 = \frac{q}{y_1} = \frac{3.0}{2.0} = 1.50 \text{ m/s}$
   - Velocity head: $\frac{V_1^2}{2g} = \frac{1.50^2}{2 \times 9.81} = \frac{2.25}{19.62} = 0.1147 \text{ m}$
   - Specific energy:
     $$E_1 = y_1 + \frac{V_1^2}{2g} = 2.0 + 0.1147 = \mathbf{2.1147 \text{ m}}$$
   - Froude number:
     $$Fr_1 = \frac{V_1}{\sqrt{g y_1}} = \frac{1.50}{\sqrt{9.81 \times 2.0}} = \frac{1.50}{4.4294} = \mathbf{0.339} \quad (\text{Subcritical Flow})$$

3. **Determination of Alternate Depth $y_2$:**
   At alternate depth $y_2$, specific energy is identical ($E_2 = E_1 = 2.1147 \text{ m}$):
   $$E = y_2 + \frac{q^2}{2g y_2^2} \implies 2.1147 = y_2 + \frac{3.0^2}{2(9.81) y_2^2} = y_2 + \frac{0.4587}{y_2^2}$$
   Multiplying through by $y_2^2$:
   $$y_2^3 - 2.1147 y_2^2 + 0.4587 = 0$$
   We know $y = 2.0 \text{ m}$ is one root. Factoring out $(y - 2.0)$:
   $$(y - 2.0)(y^2 - 0.1147 y - 0.2294) = 0$$
   Solving the quadratic equation:
   $$y_2 = \frac{0.1147 + \sqrt{0.1147^2 - 4(1)(-0.2294)}}{2} = \frac{0.1147 + \sqrt{0.01316 + 0.9176}}{2} = \frac{0.1147 + 0.9648}{2} = \mathbf{0.540 \text{ m}}$$
   *Verification:* At $y_2 = 0.540 \text{ m}$, $V_2 = \frac{3.0}{0.540} = 5.556 \text{ m/s}$, $V_2^2/2g = 1.573 \text{ m}$, $E = 0.540 + 1.573 = 2.113 \text{ m} \approx 2.115 \text{ m}$.
   - Froude number at $y_2$: $Fr_2 = \frac{5.556}{\sqrt{9.81 \times 0.540}} = \frac{5.556}{2.302} = \mathbf{2.413} > 1.0$ (Supercritical).
:::

### Example 15.2: Maximum Discharge Delivered by a Spillway Crest
**Problem:** Water flows over a broad-crested weir where the total energy head upstream of the crest is $H_0 = 3.0 \text{ m}$ above the crest floor. Assuming frictionless approach flow, calculate:
1. The critical depth $y_c$ over the crest.
2. The critical velocity $V_c$.
3. The maximum unit discharge $q_{\text{max}}$ flowing over the weir.

:::sol Detailed Step-by-Step Solution
1. **Critical Depth Over Crest:**
   Over a broad-crested weir, flow naturally passes through critical depth ($E_{\text{min}} = H_0 = 3.0 \text{ m}$):
   $$y_c = \frac{2}{3} E_{\text{min}} = \frac{2}{3} (3.0) = \mathbf{2.00 \text{ m}}$$
2. **Critical Velocity:**
   $$V_c = \sqrt{g y_c} = \sqrt{9.81 \times 2.00} = \sqrt{19.62} = \mathbf{4.429 \text{ m/s}}$$
3. **Maximum Unit Discharge:**
   $$q_{\text{max}} = y_c V_c = 2.00 \times 4.429 = \mathbf{8.859 \text{ m}^2/\text{s}}$$
   *Direct Verification via General Formula:*
   $$q_{\text{max}} = \sqrt{g} \left(\frac{2}{3} H_0\right)^{3/2} = \sqrt{9.81} \times (2.0)^{1.5} = 3.132 \times 2.8284 = \mathbf{8.859 \text{ m}^2/\text{s}}$$
:::

### Real-World Field Applications & Critical Flow Meters
1. **Critical Depth as a Primary Discharge Gauge:**
   Because the relationship between critical depth and discharge is governed solely by gravity and geometry ($Q = \sqrt{g A_c^3 / T_c}$) with **zero dependence on channel roughness $n$ or downstream backwater conditions**, hydraulic engineers deliberately force flow through critical depth to measure streamflow accurately!
2. **Broad-Crested Weirs and Parshall Flumes (IS: 6088 / ISO: 3846):**
   Structures like broad-crested weirs and Parshall flumes create a contracted throat where flow passes through critical depth ($Fr = 1.0$). Measuring a single upstream water level allows instantaneous, highly accurate discharge determination without needing downstream depth or bed slope information.
