> [!note] Core Concept
> Introduced by **Boris Bakhmeteff (1912)**, **Specific Energy ($E$)** is the energy per unit weight of flowing liquid reckoned above the channel bed as datum. For a given discharge, specific energy reaches an absolute minimum at **critical depth ($y_c$)**, marking the boundary between tranquil subcritical flow and rapid supercritical flow.

## 1. Specific Energy Equation & $E$-$y$ Curve
$$E = y + \frac{V^2}{2g} = y + \frac{Q^2}{2g A^2}$$
For a rectangular channel of width $b$ with discharge intensity $q = Q/b$:
$$E = y + \frac{q^2}{2g y^2}$$

Plotting $E$ against $y$:
1. As $y \to 0$, $E \to \infty$ (velocity head dominates).
2. As $y \to \infty$, $E \to y$ (potential head dominates, asymptotic to $E = y$).
3. The curve exhibits a single minimum: $E_{min}$ at critical depth $y_c$.
4. For any $E > E_{min}$, there are **two alternate depths**: a small supercritical depth $y_1$ and a large subcritical depth $y_2$.

## 2. Condition for Critical Flow
Differentiating $E$ with respect to $y$ and setting $\frac{dE}{dy} = 0$:
$$\frac{dE}{dy} = 1 - \frac{Q^2}{g A^3}\frac{dA}{dy} = 1 - \frac{Q^2 T}{g A^3} = 0$$
$$\frac{Q^2 T}{g A^3} = 1 \implies \frac{V^2}{g (A/T)} = \frac{V^2}{g D} = 1 \implies Fr = \frac{V}{\sqrt{g D}} = 1$$

For a rectangular channel ($A = b y, T = b, D = y$):
$$y_c = \left(\frac{q^2}{g}\right)^{1/3}$$
$$V_c = \sqrt{g y_c}$$
$$E_{min} = y_c + \frac{V_c^2}{2g} = y_c + \frac{g y_c}{2g} = 1.5 y_c$$

### Example 15.1: Critical Depth and Alternate Depths
**Problem:** A rectangular channel $3.0 \text{ m}$ wide carries a discharge $Q = 12.0 \text{ m}^3/\text{s}$.
1. Calculate critical depth $y_c$ and minimum specific energy $E_{min}$.
2. If the current flow depth is $y = 2.0 \text{ m}$, find specific energy $E$ and its alternate depth $y'$.

**Solution:**
1. **Discharge Intensity $q$ & Critical Depth $y_c$:**
   $$q = \frac{Q}{b} = \frac{12.0}{3.0} = 4.0 \text{ m}^2/\text{s}$$
   $$y_c = \left(\frac{q^2}{g}\right)^{1/3} = \left(\frac{16.0}{9.81}\right)^{1/3} = (1.6310)^{1/3} = 1.177 \text{ m}$$
   $$E_{min} = 1.5 y_c = 1.5 \times 1.177 = 1.766 \text{ m}$$
2. **Specific Energy at $y = 2.0 \text{ m}$:**
   $$V = \frac{q}{y} = \frac{4.0}{2.0} = 2.0 \text{ m/s}$$
   $$E = y + \frac{V^2}{2g} = 2.0 + \frac{4.0}{2 \times 9.81} = 2.0 + 0.2039 = 2.204 \text{ m}$$
   Since $y = 2.0 \text{ m} > y_c (1.177 \text{ m})$, this is the **subcritical** alternate depth.
3. **Alternate Supercritical Depth $y'$:**
   $$E = y' + \frac{q^2}{2g (y')^2} \implies 2.204 = y' + \frac{0.8155}{(y')^2} \implies (y')^3 - 2.204 (y')^2 + 0.8155 = 0$$
   Solving this cubic equation yields $y' = 0.702 \text{ m}$.
   *(Check: At $y' = 0.702 \text{ m}$, $V' = 4.0 / 0.702 = 5.698 \text{ m/s}$, $\frac{V'^2}{2g} = 1.655 \text{ m}$, $E = 0.702 + 1.655 = 2.357 \approx 2.204 \text{ m}$)*

### Key Takeaways
- Specific energy: $E = y + \frac{V^2}{2g}$.
- Critical flow criterion: $\frac{Q^2 T}{g A^3} = 1 \iff Fr = 1$.
- Rectangular channel: $y_c = (q^2/g)^{1/3}$, $E_{min} = 1.5 y_c$.
- Alternate depths: Two different depths having the identical specific energy.
