> [!note] Core Concept
> Within any turbulent boundary layer, the immediate presence of a solid wall dampens turbulent eddy fluctuations to zero. Directly at the wall lies an ultra-thin **laminar (viscous) sublayer** of thickness $\delta'$. Whether a surface behaves as hydrodynamically smooth or rough depends entirely on the ratio of surface roughness projection height $k_s$ to $\delta'$.

## 1. Viscous Sublayer Thickness
The thickness of the laminar sublayer $\delta'$ is determined by the shear velocity $u_* = \sqrt{\tau_0 / \rho}$:
$$\delta' = \frac{11.6 \nu}{u_*}$$

## 2. Hydrodynamic Roughness Classification (Nikuradse)
Let $k_s$ denote the equivalent sand grain roughness height:
1. **Hydrodynamically Smooth Boundary ($\frac{k_s}{\delta'} < 0.25$ or $\frac{u_* k_s}{\nu} < 5$):**
   Roughness peaks are completely submerged within the viscous sublayer. The boundary behaves as glass-smooth; drag depends solely on Reynolds number and is independent of roughness.
2. **Transition Boundary ($0.25 \le \frac{k_s}{\delta'} \le 6.0$ or $5 \le \frac{u_* k_s}{\nu} \le 70$):**
   Roughness peaks protrude partly through the sublayer, creating minor form drag alongside viscous drag.
3. **Completely Rough Boundary ($\frac{k_s}{\delta'} > 6.0$ or $\frac{u_* k_s}{\nu} > 70$):**
   Roughness projections project far beyond the sublayer, completely disrupting it. Drag is dominated by form drag on roughness peaks and becomes independent of Reynolds number.

### Example 6.1: Boundary Roughness Regime of a Concrete Flume
**Problem:** Water flows in a concrete channel with wall shear stress $\tau_0 = 4.90 \text{ N/m}^2$. Kinematic viscosity $ν = 1.0 \times 10^{-6} \text{ m}^2/\text{s}$, density $\rho = 1000 \text{ kg/m}^3$. If the concrete surface has equivalent roughness $k_s = 0.35 \text{ mm}$:
1. Compute the shear velocity $u_*$.
2. Calculate the thickness of the laminar sublayer $\delta'$.
3. Classify the boundary regime.

**Solution:**
1. **Shear Velocity $u_*$:**
   $$u_* = \sqrt{\frac{\tau_0}{\rho}} = \sqrt{\frac{4.90}{1000}} = \sqrt{0.0049} = 0.070 \text{ m/s}$$
2. **Laminar Sublayer Thickness $\delta'$:**
   $$\delta' = \frac{11.6 \nu}{u_*} = \frac{11.6 \times 1.0 \times 10^{-6}}{0.070} = 1.657 \times 10^{-4} \text{ m} = 0.166 \text{ mm}$$
3. **Roughness Ratio & Classification:**
   $$\frac{k_s}{\delta'} = \frac{0.35 \text{ mm}}{0.166 \text{ mm}} = 2.11$$
   Since $0.25 \le 2.11 \le 6.0$, the boundary is in the **Transition Regime** (neither completely smooth nor fully rough).

<!--tab:Summary-->
### Key Takeaways
- Viscous sublayer thickness: $\delta' = 11.6 \nu / u_*$.
- Smooth boundary: $k_s / \delta' < 0.25$ (submerged roughness).
- Transition boundary: $0.25 \le k_s / \delta' \le 6.0$.
- Completely rough boundary: $k_s / \delta' > 6.0$ (drag independent of Re).
