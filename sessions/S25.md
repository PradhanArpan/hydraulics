> [!note] Core Concept
> **Velocity triangles** are vector diagrams that resolve absolute fluid velocity ($\mathbf{V}$) into tangential blade speed ($\mathbf{u}$) and relative fluid velocity ($\mathbf{V}_r$). Mastering inlet and outlet velocity triangles is the master skill for analyzing any rotodynamic machine.

## 1. Vector Relationship: $\mathbf{V} = \mathbf{u} + \mathbf{V}_r$
At any blade section:
- $\mathbf{V}$: Absolute velocity of water.
- $\mathbf{u}$: Peripheral speed of runner blade ($u = \omega r = \frac{\pi D N}{60}$).
- $\mathbf{V}_r$: Relative velocity of water with respect to blade.

Components of Absolute Velocity $\mathbf{V}$:
- **Whirl Velocity ($V_w = V \cos\alpha$):** Tangential component producing torque.
- **Flow Velocity ($V_f = V \sin\alpha$):** Radial or axial component carrying discharge ($Q = \pi D b V_f$).

Angles:
- **$\alpha$:** Guide vane angle (angle between absolute velocity and direction of motion).
- **$\theta$ (or $\beta_1$):** Runner blade angle at inlet.
- **$\beta$:** Exit absolute flow angle.
- **$\phi$ (or $\beta_2$):** Runner blade angle at outlet.

## 2. Condition for Shockless Entry
To prevent flow separation, violent eddy turbulence, and energy loss at entry, the fluid must enter tangentially to the blade tip:
$$\tan\theta = \frac{V_{f1}}{V_{w1} - u_1}$$

### Example 25.1: Construction of Inlet & Outlet Velocity Triangles
**Problem:** An inward flow reaction turbine runner has inlet diameter $D_1 = 1.0 \text{ m}$, exit diameter $D_2 = 0.50 \text{ m}$, speed $N = 450 \text{ rpm}$. Water enters with absolute velocity $V_1 = 28.0 \text{ m/s}$ at guide angle $\alpha = 18^\circ$. Flow velocity is constant ($V_{f1} = V_{f2}$). Discharge at exit is radial ($V_{w2} = 0$). Calculate:
1. Inlet whirl velocity $V_{w1}$ and flow velocity $V_{f1}$.
2. Runner blade angle $\theta$ at inlet.
3. Runner blade angle $\phi$ at exit.

**Solution:**
1. **Peripheral Speeds:**
   $$u_1 = \frac{\pi D_1 N}{60} = \frac{\pi \times 1.0 \times 450}{60} = 23.56 \text{ m/s}$$
   $$u_2 = \frac{\pi D_2 N}{60} = \frac{\pi \times 0.50 \times 450}{60} = 11.78 \text{ m/s}$$
2. **Inlet Velocity Components:**
   $$V_{w1} = V_1 \cos\alpha = 28.0 \times \cos(18^\circ) = 28.0 \times 0.9511 = 26.63 \text{ m/s}$$
   $$V_{f1} = V_1 \sin\alpha = 28.0 \times \sin(18^\circ) = 28.0 \times 0.3090 = 8.65 \text{ m/s}$$
3. **Inlet Blade Angle $\theta$:**
   $$\tan\theta = \frac{V_{f1}}{V_{w1} - u_1} = \frac{8.65}{26.63 - 23.56} = \frac{8.65}{3.07} = 2.818 \implies \theta = 70.4^\circ$$
4. **Outlet Blade Angle $\phi$:**
   At exit, $V_{w2} = 0$, so the outlet triangle is a right-angled triangle with base $u_2$ and height $V_{f2} = V_{f1} = 8.65 \text{ m/s}$:
   $$\tan\phi = \frac{V_{f2}}{u_2} = \frac{8.65}{11.78} = 0.7343 \implies \phi = 36.3^\circ$$

### Key Takeaways
- Vector relationship: $\mathbf{V} = \mathbf{u} + \mathbf{V}_r$.
- Tangential whirl $V_w$ produces torque; Flow velocity $V_f$ conveys discharge $Q$.
- Shockless entry condition: $\tan\theta = \frac{V_{f1}}{V_{w1} - u_1}$.
