## 1. Introduction & Physical Mechanics

In civil and water resources engineering, **Numerical Workshop: Velocity Triangles** governs the behavior of water in natural rivers, engineered canals, closed conduits, spillways, and hydraulic machinery.

Understanding this topic allows the engineer to:
1. Accurately predict stage, velocity distributions, and boundary shear stress.
2. Size hydraulic infrastructure safely to prevent scouring, cavitation, or unpredicted submergence.
3. Bridge traditional empirical formulas with modern numerical simulations (HEC-RAS 2D / CFD).

### Fundamental Governing Formulations
Depending on the flow regime and geometry, the governing equations are expressed as:

$$\sum \vec{F} = \frac{d}{dt} \left(m \vec{V}\right) = \rho Q (\vec{V}_{\text{out}} - \vec{V}_{\text{in}})$$

$$E = y + \frac{V^2}{2g} = y + \frac{Q^2}{2g A^2}$$

$$Q = \frac{1}{n} A R^{2/3} S_0^{1/2}$$

### Key Engineering Takeaways
- Boundary shear stress $\tau_0 = \rho g R S_0$ balances gravity forces in uniform open channel flow.
- Froude number $Fr = V / \sqrt{g D}$ distinguishes tranquil subcritical flow ($Fr < 1$) from torrential supercritical flow ($Fr > 1$).
- Specific speed $N_s = N \sqrt{P} / H^{5/4}$ dictates whether an impulse (Pelton), mixed (Francis), or axial (Kaplan) turbine is chosen.

## Worked Example 1: Standard Design Calculation

### Problem Statement
A rectangular open channel $b = 4.0 \text{ m}$ wide carries a discharge $Q = 12.0 \text{ m}^3/\text{s}$ on a longitudinal bed slope $S_0 = 0.0008$. The channel boundary is lined with smooth concrete ($n = 0.015$). Compute the normal depth of flow $y_n$ and verify the flow regime.

:::sol Solution
1. **Express Area and Wetted Perimeter:**
   $$A = b y_n = 4.0 y_n$$
   $$P = b + 2 y_n = 4.0 + 2 y_n$$
   $$R = \frac{A}{P} = \frac{4.0 y_n}{4.0 + 2 y_n}$$

2. **Substitute into Manning's Equation:**
   $$Q = \frac{1}{n} A R^{2/3} S_0^{1/2}$$
   $$12.0 = \frac{1}{0.015} (4.0 y_n) \left(\frac{4.0 y_n}{4.0 + 2 y_n}\right)^{2/3} \sqrt{0.0008}$$

3. **Iterative Solution (Newton-Raphson or Trial):**
   - Trial $y_n = 1.40 \text{ m} \rightarrow Q = 11.85 \text{ m}^3/\text{s}$
   - Trial $y_n = 1.42 \text{ m} \rightarrow Q = 12.08 \text{ m}^3/\text{s}$
   - Interpolated normal depth: **$y_n = 1.413 \text{ m}$**

4. **Regime Verification:**
   $$V = \frac{Q}{A} = \frac{12.0}{4.0 \times 1.413} = 2.123 \text{ m/s}$$
   $$Fr = \frac{V}{\sqrt{g y_n}} = \frac{2.123}{\sqrt{9.81 \times 1.413}} = 0.570 < 1.0 \quad \text{(Subcritical Flow)}$$
:::

## Worked Example 2: Non-Uniform Transition Calculation

### Problem Statement
Water flows at $V_1 = 6.0 \text{ m/s}$ and depth $y_1 = 0.50 \text{ m}$ in a rectangular canal. A hydraulic jump occurs downstream. Calculate the sequent depth $y_2$, the head loss $\Delta E$, and power dissipated.

:::sol Solution
1. **Initial Froude Number:**
   $$Fr_1 = \frac{V_1}{\sqrt{g y_1}} = \frac{6.0}{\sqrt{9.81 \times 0.50}} = 2.709$$

2. **Belanger Sequent Depth:**
   $$y_2 = \frac{y_1}{2} \left(\sqrt{1 + 8 Fr_1^2} - 1\right) = \frac{0.50}{2} \left(\sqrt{1 + 8(2.709)^2} - 1\right) = 1.704 \text{ m}$$

3. **Energy Head Loss across Jump:**
   $$\Delta E = \frac{(y_2 - y_1)^3}{4 y_1 y_2} = \frac{(1.704 - 0.50)^3}{4 \times 0.50 \times 1.704} = 0.512 \text{ m}$$

4. **Power Dissipated:**
   $$q = V_1 y_1 = 6.0 \times 0.50 = 3.0 \text{ m}^3/\text{s per meter width}$$
   $$P = \rho g q \Delta E = 1000 \times 9.81 \times 3.0 \times 0.512 = 15.07 \text{ kW/m width}$$
:::

## University Examination Practice Questions

### 2-Mark Questions
1. **Define displacement thickness and write its mathematical formula.**
   *Model Answer:* Displacement thickness is the distance by which the solid boundary would have to be displaced outward in a frictionless fluid flow to produce the same mass deficit as occurs in the real fluid boundary layer: delta* = integral(1 - u/U_inf) dy.
2. **State the condition for the most economical trapezoidal open channel section.**
   *Model Answer:* A trapezoidal section is most economical when: (i) Half top width equals the length of one sloping side, and (ii) Hydraulic radius equals half flow depth (R = y/2).

### 5-Mark Questions
1. **Derive the dynamic equation for Gradually Varied Flow (GVF) in a prismatic channel.**
   *Outline:* Start with total head H = z + y + V^2/(2g). Differentiate with respect to channel distance x: dH/dx = dz/dx + dy/dx + d/dx(V^2/2g). Note dH/dx = -Sf, dz/dx = -S0. Show d/dx(V^2/2g) = -Fr^2 dy/dx. Rearrange to obtain: dy/dx = (S0 - Sf) / (1 - Fr^2).

## Lecture Recap & Formulas

| Quantity | Formula | Remarks |
|:---|:---:|:---|
| **Displacement Thickness** | delta* = int(1 - u/U) dy | Mass deficit equivalent |
| **Momentum Thickness** | theta = int u/U (1 - u/U) dy | Momentum loss equivalent |
| **Manning's Equation** | Q = (1/n) A R^(2/3) S0^(1/2) | Uniform open channel flow |
| **Critical Depth (Rectangular)** | yc = (q^2/g)^(1/3) | Minimum energy condition (Fr = 1) |
| **Belanger Sequent Depth** | y2/y1 = 0.5 * (sqrt(1 + 8 Fr1^2) - 1) | Hydraulic jump in rectangular flume |
| **Euler Turbine Work** | W = rho Q (Vw1 u1 +- Vw2 u2) | Fundamental machine torque balance |
