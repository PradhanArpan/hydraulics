> [!note] Channel Transitions & The Paradox of Open Surface Flow
> In closed pipe flow, a constriction always increases flow velocity and drops pressure. In open channel flow, however, the response of the free water surface to a **raised bed hump ($\Delta z$)** or a **width contraction ($\Delta B$)** depends completely on the initial flow regime (**Subcritical vs. Supercritical**):
> - In **Subcritical Flow ($Fr < 1$)**, the water surface **dips downward** over a hump!
> - In **Supercritical Flow ($Fr > 1$)**, the water surface **rises upward (heaves)** over a hump!
> If the transition constriction is too severe, the flow becomes **choked**, forcing an upstream **backwater rise (afflux)**.

## 1. Flow Over a Raised Bed Hump ($\Delta z$)
Consider a horizontal, frictionless channel reach where the bed is raised locally by a smooth hump of height $\Delta z$:
- Upstream section (1): Bed elevation $= 0$, Depth $= y_1$, Velocity $= V_1$, Specific Energy $= E_1 = y_1 + \frac{V_1^2}{2g}$.
- Section over the hump (2): Bed elevation $= \Delta z$, Depth $= y_2$, Velocity $= V_2$, Specific Energy $= E_2 = y_2 + \frac{V_2^2}{2g}$.
Applying energy conservation between sections 1 and 2:
$$H_1 = H_2 \implies 0 + E_1 = \Delta z + E_2 \iff \mathbf{E_2 = E_1 - \Delta z}$$
Because $\Delta z > 0$, the specific energy over the hump is **always less than upstream specific energy ($E_2 < E_1$)**.

```
       WSL (Subcritical) ────.                 .────
                              `───────._______.'   Surface Dip Δy < 0
       ──────────────────────────────────────────────
                                   ┌─────┐
       Bed                     Δz  │Hump │
       ════════════════════════════╧═════╧═══════════
```

### Analysis via the Specific Energy Curve ($E-y$):
1. **Subcritical Inflow ($Fr_1 < 1$, Upper Limb):**
   As $E$ decreases from $E_1$ to $E_2$, tracing downward along the upper limb requires the depth to **decrease** ($y_2 < y_1$).
   $$\mathbf{\Delta y = y_2 - y_1 < 0 \quad (\text{Water surface dips down!})}$$
2. **Supercritical Inflow ($Fr_1 > 1$, Lower Limb):**
   As $E$ decreases from $E_1$ to $E_2$, tracing upward along the lower limb requires the depth to **increase** ($y_2 > y_1$).
   $$\mathbf{\Delta y = y_2 - y_1 > 0 \quad (\text{Water surface heaves up!})}$$

## 2. The Choking Phenomenon & Maximum Hump ($\Delta z_{\text{max}}$)
The specific energy over the hump $E_2$ cannot decrease indefinitely because the minimum possible energy to convey discharge $Q$ is the critical energy $E_{\text{min}}$:
$$E_2 \ge E_{\text{min}} = 1.5 y_c$$
The maximum permissible hump height without altering upstream flow conditions is:
$$\mathbf{\Delta z_{\text{max}} = E_1 - E_{\text{min}} = E_1 - 1.5 y_c}$$

### What Happens When $\Delta z > \Delta z_{\text{max}}$? (Choked Flow!)
If the constructed hump exceeds $\Delta z_{\text{max}}$:
1. The channel is physically incapable of passing discharge $Q$ at the original energy $E_1$.
2. The excess bed obstruction acts as a dam, causing water to **head up upstream** to a new, higher subcritical depth $y_1' > y_1$.
3. The upstream specific energy rises to:
   $$\mathbf{E_1' = E_{\text{min}} + \Delta z}$$
4. The flow directly over the crest of the hump reaches the **critical depth ($y_2 = y_c$)**, passing into supercritical flow downstream.

## 3. Channel Width Contraction ($\Delta B$)
When a rectangular channel transitions smoothly from width $B_1$ to a narrower throat width $B_2 < B_1$:
- The unit discharge increases: $q_2 = \frac{Q}{B_2} > q_1 = \frac{Q}{B_1}$.
- Because $q_2 > q_1$, the critical depth increases: $y_{c2} = \sqrt[3]{q_2^2/g} > y_{c1}$.
- The minimum specific energy increases: $E_{\text{min},2} = 1.5 y_{c2} > E_{\text{min},1}$.
Assuming a flat bed ($\Delta z = 0$), total energy is conserved: $E_1 = E_2$.

### Critical Contraction Width ($B_{2,\text{min}}$):
To avoid choking, the throat width cannot be smaller than:
$$\mathbf{B_{2,\text{min}} = \frac{Q}{q_{\text{max}}} = \frac{Q}{\sqrt{g \left(\frac{2}{3} E_1\right)^3}}}$$
If $B_2 < B_{2,\text{min}}$, **width choking** occurs: upstream water heads up to depth $y_1'$, and critical flow establishes itself at the throat ($y_2 = y_{c2}$).

### Example 16.1: Subcritical Flow Over a Bed Hump
**Problem:** A rectangular channel $3.0 \text{ m}$ wide carries a discharge of $6.0 \text{ m}^3/\text{s}$ at an upstream depth of $y_1 = 2.0 \text{ m}$. A smooth bed hump of height $\Delta z = 0.20 \text{ m}$ is constructed on the channel floor.
1. Determine the depth of water over the hump $y_2$.
2. Compute the drop in the free water surface over the hump.
3. Calculate the maximum permissible hump height $\Delta z_{\text{max}}$ without causing choking.

:::sol Step-by-Step Solution
1. **Upstream Hydraulic Conditions:**
   - Unit discharge: $q = \frac{Q}{B} = \frac{6.0}{3.0} = 2.0 \text{ m}^2/\text{s}$
   - Velocity: $V_1 = \frac{q}{y_1} = \frac{2.0}{2.0} = 1.0 \text{ m/s}$
   - Specific energy:
     $$E_1 = y_1 + \frac{V_1^2}{2g} = 2.0 + \frac{1.0^2}{2 \times 9.81} = 2.0 + 0.0510 = \mathbf{2.051 \text{ m}}$$
   - Froude number:
     $$Fr_1 = \frac{V_1}{\sqrt{g y_1}} = \frac{1.0}{\sqrt{9.81 \times 2.0}} = \frac{1.0}{4.429} = 0.226 < 1.0 \quad (\text{Subcritical})$$

2. **Flow Over Hump ($\Delta z = 0.20 \text{ m}$):**
   $$E_2 = E_1 - \Delta z = 2.051 - 0.20 = \mathbf{1.851 \text{ m}}$$
   Since $E_2 = y_2 + \frac{q^2}{2g y_2^2}$:
   $$1.851 = y_2 + \frac{2.0^2}{2(9.81) y_2^2} = y_2 + \frac{0.20387}{y_2^2}$$
   $$y_2^3 - 1.851 y_2^2 + 0.20387 = 0$$
   Solving for the subcritical root (since approach is subcritical):
   - For $y = 1.80 \text{ m}$: $f(1.80) = 1.80^3 - 1.851(1.80^2) + 0.2039 = 5.832 - 5.997 + 0.2039 = +0.0389$
   - For $y = 1.78 \text{ m}$: $f(1.78) = 5.6398 - 5.8647 + 0.2039 = -0.0210$
   - By linear interpolation:
     $$\mathbf{y_2 = 1.787 \text{ m}}$$

3. **Change in Free Water Surface Elevation ($\Delta \text{WSL}$):**
   - Upstream WSL $= 0 + y_1 = 2.000 \text{ m}$
   - WSL over hump $= \Delta z + y_2 = 0.20 + 1.787 = 1.987 \text{ m}$
   - Surface dip:
     $$\Delta \text{WSL} = 1.987 - 2.000 = \mathbf{-0.013 \text{ m}} \quad (\mathbf{1.3 \text{ cm drop}})$$

4. **Maximum Hump Height $\Delta z_{\text{max}}$:**
   - Critical depth:
     $$y_c = \sqrt[3]{\frac{q^2}{g}} = \sqrt[3]{\frac{2.0^2}{9.81}} = \sqrt[3]{0.4077} = \mathbf{0.7415 \text{ m}}$$
   - Minimum specific energy:
     $$E_{\text{min}} = 1.5 y_c = 1.5 \times 0.7415 = \mathbf{1.1123 \text{ m}}$$
   - Maximum hump:
     $$\mathbf{\Delta z_{\text{max}} = E_1 - E_{\text{min}} = 2.051 - 1.112 = 0.939 \text{ m}}$$
:::

### Example 16.2: Width Contraction in a Venturi Flume
**Problem:** A rectangular canal transitions from width $B_1 = 4.0 \text{ m}$ down to a throat width $B_2 = 2.5 \text{ m}$ with a level bed ($\Delta z = 0$). Discharge is $Q = 8.0 \text{ m}^3/\text{s}$ at an upstream depth of $y_1 = 1.6 \text{ m}$.
1. Determine the depth of water in the throat $y_2$.
2. Calculate the minimum throat width $B_{2,\text{min}}$ to prevent choking.

:::sol Step-by-Step Solution
1. **Upstream Energy $E_1$:**
   $$V_1 = \frac{Q}{B_1 y_1} = \frac{8.0}{4.0 \times 1.6} = \frac{8.0}{6.4} = 1.25 \text{ m/s}$$
   $$E_1 = y_1 + \frac{V_1^2}{2g} = 1.6 + \frac{1.25^2}{2 \times 9.81} = 1.6 + 0.0796 = \mathbf{1.6796 \text{ m}}$$

2. **Throat Conditions ($B_2 = 2.5 \text{ m}$):**
   $$q_2 = \frac{Q}{B_2} = \frac{8.0}{2.5} = 3.20 \text{ m}^2/\text{s}$$
   Since bed is level: $E_2 = E_1 = 1.6796 \text{ m}$.
   $$E_2 = y_2 + \frac{q_2^2}{2g y_2^2} \implies 1.6796 = y_2 + \frac{3.20^2}{2(9.81) y_2^2} = y_2 + \frac{0.5219}{y_2^2}$$
   $$y_2^3 - 1.6796 y_2^2 + 0.5219 = 0$$
   Solving for subcritical depth:
   $$\mathbf{y_2 = 1.458 \text{ m}}$$
   *Water surface dip:* $\Delta y = y_2 - y_1 = 1.458 - 1.600 = \mathbf{-0.142 \text{ m}}$ (14.2 cm drop in throat).

3. **Minimum Throat Width $B_{2,\text{min}}$:**
   To avoid choking, flow at throat must not exceed critical condition at available energy $E_1 = 1.6796 \text{ m}$:
   $$y_{c2} = \frac{2}{3} E_1 = \frac{2}{3}(1.6796) = 1.1197 \text{ m}$$
   $$q_{2,\text{max}} = \sqrt{g y_{c2}^3} = \sqrt{9.81 \times (1.1197)^3} = \sqrt{9.81 \times 1.4038} = \sqrt{13.771} = 3.711 \text{ m}^2/\text{s}$$
   $$B_{2,\text{min}} = \frac{Q}{q_{2,\text{max}}} = \frac{8.0}{3.711} = \mathbf{2.156 \text{ m}}$$
   Since actual throat width $B_2 = 2.5 \text{ m} > B_{2,\text{min}} = 2.156 \text{ m}$, the flow is **un-choked**.
:::

### Real-World Civil Engineering Applications: Afflux & Bridges
1. **Bridge Pier Contraction & Afflux (IRC: 5 / IS: 7784):**
   When multiple bridge piers are erected in a riverway, they obstruct a portion of the cross-sectional flow area ($\Delta B = \sum b_{\text{piers}}$). This constriction creates an upstream water surface rise termed **Afflux**. Indian Roads Congress (IRC: 5) mandates strict afflux calculation to ensure that bridge approach embankments and nearby towns are not submerged during design flood stages.
2. **Weir and Barrage Crest Design (IS: 6966):**
   River barrages utilize raised concrete crests with radial gates. When gates are lifted during monsoons, the crest acts as a broad hump. The crest height $\Delta z$ is designed strictly within $\Delta z_{\text{max}}$ to avoid inducing excessive backwater flooding upstream of the barrage pondage area.
