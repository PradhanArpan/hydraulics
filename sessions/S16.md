> [!note] Core Concept
> Channel transitions—such as bed steps (humps) or width constrictions (fluming)—alter the specific energy or discharge intensity. Analyzing these transitions determines whether the upstream flow can pass without obstruction or whether **choking** occurs, forcing an upstream heading-up of water (**afflux**).

## 1. Flow Over a Raised Bed Step (Hump $\Delta z$)
Neglecting frictional head loss over a short transition:
$$E_1 = E_2 + \Delta z$$
Where $E_1 = y_1 + \frac{V_1^2}{2g}$ and $E_2 = y_2 + \frac{V_2^2}{2g}$.

1. **Subcritical Upstream Flow ($Fr_1 < 1$):**
   As bed rises ($\Delta z > 0$), specific energy decreases ($E_2 < E_1$). On the subcritical branch of the $E$-$y$ curve, decreasing $E$ causes water depth to **drop** ($y_2 < y_1$).
2. **Supercritical Upstream Flow ($Fr_1 > 1$):**
   Decreasing $E$ causes water depth to **rise** ($y_2 > y_1$).
3. **Maximum Allowable Hump Height ($\Delta z_{max}$):**
   The maximum step that can be accommodated without altering upstream conditions occurs when flow over the crest becomes critical ($E_2 = E_{min} = 1.5 y_c$):
   $$\Delta z_{max} = E_1 - E_{min}$$
4. **Choking Condition ($\Delta z > \Delta z_{max}$):**
   If $\Delta z > \Delta z_{max}$, the transition is choked. Flow cannot pass at energy $E_1$. Water backs up, increasing upstream depth ($y_1 \to Y_1$) until new upstream energy satisfies $E_{new} = E_{min} + \Delta z$.

### Example 16.1: Subcritical Flow over a Bed Hump
**Problem:** A rectangular channel $3.0 \text{ m}$ wide carries $Q = 9.0 \text{ m}^3/\text{s}$ at an upstream depth $y_1 = 1.80 \text{ m}$.
1. Find the maximum height of hump $\Delta z_{max}$ without causing choking.
2. If a hump of height $\Delta z = 0.20 \text{ m}$ is installed, calculate the water depth $y_2$ over the hump.

**Solution:**
1. **Upstream State:**
   $$q = \frac{9.0}{3.0} = 3.0 \text{ m}^2/\text{s}, \quad V_1 = \frac{3.0}{1.80} = 1.667 \text{ m/s}$$
   $$E_1 = y_1 + \frac{V_1^2}{2g} = 1.80 + \frac{(1.667)^2}{2 \times 9.81} = 1.80 + 0.1416 = 1.9416 \text{ m}$$
2. **Critical State & Minimum Energy:**
   $$y_c = \left(\frac{q^2}{g}\right)^{1/3} = \left(\frac{9.0}{9.81}\right)^{1/3} = (0.9174)^{1/3} = 0.9717 \text{ m}$$
   $$E_{min} = 1.5 y_c = 1.5 \times 0.9717 = 1.4575 \text{ m}$$
3. **Maximum Hump $\Delta z_{max}$:**
   $$\Delta z_{max} = E_1 - E_{min} = 1.9416 - 1.4575 = 0.484 \text{ m}$$
4. **Depth over Hump for $\Delta z = 0.20 \text{ m}$:**
   Since $\Delta z = 0.20 \text{ m} < \Delta z_{max}$ (no choking occurs):
   $$E_2 = E_1 - \Delta z = 1.9416 - 0.20 = 1.7416 \text{ m}$$
   $$E_2 = y_2 + \frac{q^2}{2g y_2^2} \implies 1.7416 = y_2 + \frac{0.4587}{y_2^2}$$
   $$y_2^3 - 1.7416 y_2^2 + 0.4587 = 0$$
   Solving for the subcritical root: **$y_2 = 1.564 \text{ m}$**.
   *(Water surface drops by $\Delta y = y_1 - (y_2 + \Delta z) = 1.80 - 1.764 = 0.036 \text{ m}$)*

### Key Takeaways
- Transition energy balance: $E_1 = E_2 + \Delta z$.
- Maximum hump without choking: $\Delta z_{max} = E_1 - E_{min}$.
- Choking occurs when $\Delta z > \Delta z_{max}$, causing upstream afflux.
