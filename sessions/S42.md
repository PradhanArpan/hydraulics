> [!note] Core Concept
> A system of partial differential equations has infinite mathematical solutions. A unique physical solution is determined solely by the **Initial Conditions** (state at $t = 0$) and **Boundary Conditions** applied along domain boundaries. Ill-posed boundary conditions are the leading cause of artificial wave reflection and numerical simulation failure.

## 1. Boundary Condition Types in Open Channel CFD
1. **Inflow Boundaries:**
   - **Discharge Hydrograph ($Q(t)$):** Flow rate specified as a function of time. Typical for river upstream cross sections.
   - **Velocity Profile ($u(y, z)$):** Uniform or parabolic velocity distribution specified at inlets.
2. **Outflow Boundaries:**
   - **Stage Hydrograph ($h(t)$):** Water surface elevation specified over time (e.g., tidal cycles in estuaries).
   - **Stage-Discharge Rating Curve ($Q = f(h)$):** Empirical or calibrated power-law relationship $Q = C_r (h - h_0)^b$ at gauging stations.
   - **Normal Depth (Friction Slope):** Downstream boundary assumed to operate at uniform normal depth governed by Manning's equation based on bed slope $S_0$.
   - **Free Overfall / Critical Depth:** At waterfalls and weir crests where $Fr = 1$.
3. **Solid Wall Boundaries:**
   - **No-Slip Condition:** $u = v = w = 0$ for 3D viscous laminar/DNS simulations.
   - **Wall Roughness / Slip with Friction:** In 2D shallow water solvers, shear stress along walls and channel beds is imposed using Manning's $n$ or Nikuradse's $k_s$.

## 2. Initial Conditions
- **Dry Bed Start:** Domain starts completely dry ($h = 0$). Useful for dam-break flood routing, but requires robust wetting-and-drying algorithms to prevent division by zero.
- **Hot Start / Restart File:** Starting from a pre-computed steady-state baseflow water surface elevation to avoid initial transient shockwaves.

### Example 42.1: Downstream Rating Curve Boundary Formulation
**Problem:** At the downstream boundary of a river simulation reach, field gauging data has established the stage-discharge rating curve:
$$Q = 14.5 (h - 98.50)^{1.65}$$
Where $Q$ is in $	ext{m}^3/	ext{s}$ and $h$ is water surface elevation in meters above datum.
During a flood simulation, the upstream hydrograph routes a peak discharge $Q_{peak} = 85.0 \text{ m}^3/\text{s}$ to the downstream boundary.
Calculate the exact water surface elevation $h$ that the CFD solver must impose at the boundary.

**Solution:**
Rearranging the rating equation to solve for stage $h$:
$$\frac{Q}{14.5} = (h - 98.50)^{1.65}$$
$$h - 98.50 = \left(\frac{Q}{14.5}\right)^{1 / 1.65} = \left(\frac{Q}{14.5}\right)^{0.60606}$$
Substituting $Q = 85.0 \text{ m}^3/\text{s}$:
$$\frac{85.0}{14.5} = 5.8621$$
$$h - 98.50 = (5.8621)^{0.60606} = 2.924 \text{ m}$$
$$h = 98.50 + 2.924 = 101.424 \text{ m}$$
The downstream boundary water surface elevation must be set to **$101.42 \text{ m}$**.

<!--tab:Summary-->
### Key Takeaways
- Boundary conditions determine unique physical solutions.
- Inflow: $Q(t)$, stage hydrographs. Outflow: rating curve, normal depth, free weir.
- Subcritical flow requires downstream stage/depth control.
