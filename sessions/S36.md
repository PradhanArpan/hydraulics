> [!note] Core Concept
> A centrifugal pump cannot be analyzed in isolation from the piping system it serves. The **operating duty point** occurs at the exact intersection of the pump's Head-Discharge characteristic ($H_{pump}$) and the pipeline's **System Head curve** ($H_{sys}$). To prevent pump destruction by cavitation, the **Net Positive Suction Head Available ($NPSH_A$)** must strictly exceed that required by the manufacturer ($NPSH_R$).

## 1. System Curve & Operating Point
- **System Resistance Equation:**
  $$H_{sys} = H_{stat} + h_{friction} = (h_s + h_d) + \left(\frac{f L}{2 g D A^2} + \sum \frac{K}{2 g A^2}\right) Q^2 = H_{stat} + k_{pipe} Q^2$$
- **Duty Point ($Q_{duty}, H_{duty}$):** The equilibrium operating condition where $H_{pump}(Q) = H_{sys}(Q)$.

## 2. Pumps in Series vs Parallel
- **Pumps in Series:** Same discharge passes through both pumps; heads add up: $H = H_1 + H_2$ at discharge $Q$. Used for high-lift deep-well pumping.
- **Pumps in Parallel:** Both pumps discharge against the same head; flow rates add up: $Q = Q_1 + Q_2$ at head $H$. Used for high-discharge irrigation and flood control.

## 3. Net Positive Suction Head (NPSH)
$NPSH$ represents the net pressure head available above liquid vapor pressure at the pump suction flange:
$$NPSH_A = \frac{p_{atm}}{\rho g} - h_s - h_{fs} - \frac{p_v}{\rho g}$$
To prevent cavitation:
$$NPSH_A \ge NPSH_R + 0.50 \text{ m (Safety Margin)}$$

### Example 36.1: NPSH and Operating Point Calculation
**Problem:** A centrifugal pump draws water from an open sump with suction lift $h_s = 3.20 \text{ m}$. Suction pipe friction loss is $h_{fs} = 0.85 \text{ m}$. Atmospheric pressure is $10.13 \text{ m}$, water vapor pressure head is $0.28 \text{ m}$.
1. Calculate $NPSH_A$ available at the suction inlet.
2. If manufacturer specifies $NPSH_R = 4.50 \text{ m}$, verify whether the pump will cavitate.
3. Determine the maximum permissible suction lift $h_{s\_max}$ for safe operation.

**Solution:**
1. **Available NPSH ($NPSH_A$):**
   $$NPSH_A = \frac{p_{atm}}{\rho g} - h_s - h_{fs} - \frac{p_v}{\rho g} = 10.13 - 3.20 - 0.85 - 0.28 = 5.80 \text{ m}$$
2. **Cavitation Assessment:**
   $$NPSH_A (5.80 \text{ m}) > NPSH_R (4.50 \text{ m})$$
   The safety margin is $5.80 - 4.50 = 1.30 \text{ m} > 0.50 \text{ m}$. The pump **will operate safely without cavitation**.
3. **Maximum Allowable Suction Lift ($h_{s\_max}$):**
   Setting $NPSH_A = NPSH_R + 0.50 = 5.00 \text{ m}$:
   $$5.00 = 10.13 - h_{s\_max} - 0.85 - 0.28 = 9.00 - h_{s\_max}$$
   $$h_{s\_max} = 9.00 - 5.00 = 4.00 \text{ m}$$

### Key Takeaways
- System curve: $H_{sys} = H_{stat} + k Q^2$.
- Duty point is the intersection: $H_{pump} = H_{sys}$.
- Series = head addition; Parallel = discharge addition.
- Cavitation safety criterion: $NPSH_A > NPSH_R$.
