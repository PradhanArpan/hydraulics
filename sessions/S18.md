> [!note] Hydraulic Jump Classification & Gradually Varied Flow
> Hydraulic jumps and gradually varied flows represent the two fundamental regimes of non-uniform open channel hydraulics:
> 1. **Rapidly Varied Flow (RVF):** The **Hydraulic Jump** dissipates kinetic energy over a short distance, classified into five distinct regimes based on the initial Froude number ($Fr_1$).
> 2. **Gradually Varied Flow (GVF):** Flow depth varies smoothly over several kilometers where the boundary friction slope $S_f$, water surface slope $S_w$, and bed slope $S_0$ diverge. The governing **Dynamic Equation of GVF** yields 12 characteristic water surface profiles ($M_1, M_2, M_3, S_1, S_2, S_3, \dots$).

## 1. Classification of Hydraulic Jumps (USBR & IS: 4997)
Based on the upstream supercritical Froude number ($Fr_1$), the United States Bureau of Reclamation (USBR) categorizes hydraulic jumps into five physical forms:

| Initial Froude ($Fr_1$) | Jump Classification | Physical Flow Characteristics | Energy Dissipation ($\Delta E / E_1$) | Stilling Basin Design |
| :---: | :--- | :--- | :---: | :--- |
| **$1.0 - 1.7$** | **Undular Jump** | Water surface shows gentle standing waves/ripples; no true roller forms; downstream velocity is practically uniform. | **$< 5\%$** | Plain concrete apron; no dissipator blocks needed. |
| **$1.7 - 2.5$** | **Weak Jump** | A series of small surface rollers develop; smooth water surface downstream; uniform velocity. | **$5\% - 18\%$** | **USBR Type I:** Plain horizontal apron. |
| **$2.5 - 4.5$** | **Oscillating Jump** | The incoming jet oscillates irregularly between bed and surface; pulsating waves travel far downstream, damaging earthen canal banks. | **$18\% - 45\%$** | **USBR Type IV:** Requires wave suppressors and deflector sills to arrest downstream wave action. |
| **$4.5 - 9.0$** | **Steady Jump (True Jump)** | The classic, best-performing engineering jump! The roller is stably confined; action is insensitive to tailwater fluctuations. | **$45\% - 70\%$** | **USBR Type III:** Chute blocks, baffle piers, and dentated end sill (shortens basin length by $60\%$). |
| **$> 9.0$** | **Strong / Choppy Jump** | Rough, violent jump with intense spray and turbulence; slugs of water surge periodically downstream. | **$> 70\%$ (up to $85\%$)** | **USBR Type II:** Heavy chute blocks and dentated sills (for high heads $V_1 > 15 \text{ m/s}$). |

## 2. Standard Stilling Basins & Dissipator Appurtenances
To reduce the expensive length of concrete required for stilling basins, hydraulic engineers install standard energy dissipating appurtenances:
1. **Chute Blocks:** Triangular or rectangular teeth installed at the spillway toe to split the incoming high-velocity jet into interlocking sheets, lifting the jump above the bed.
2. **Baffle Piers (Baffle Blocks):** Sturdy reinforced concrete blocks placed in the intermediate basin floor to shatter the jet by direct impact and create stabilizing counter-vortices.
3. **Dentated End Sill:** A toothed wall at the downstream basin exit that lifts remaining bottom currents upward toward the surface, preventing bed scour downstream of the apron.

## 3. The Dynamic Equation of Gradually Varied Flow (GVF)
In GVF, the depth varies gradually along the longitudinal distance $x$ ($dy/dx \ll 1$), so streamlines are essentially parallel and hydrostatic pressure distribution holds:
- Total head: $H = z + y + \frac{\alpha V^2}{2g}$
- Differentiating with respect to channel reach $x$:
  $$\frac{dH}{dx} = \frac{dz}{dx} + \frac{dy}{dx} + \frac{d}{dx}\left(\frac{\alpha V^2}{2g}\right)$$
By definition:
- Total energy line slope: $\frac{dH}{dx} = -S_f$ (frictional slope).
- Channel bed slope: $\frac{dz}{dx} = -S_0$.
- Velocity head gradient:
  $$\frac{d}{dx}\left(\frac{Q^2}{2g A^2}\right) = -\frac{2 Q^2}{2g A^3} \frac{dA}{dx} = -\frac{Q^2}{g A^3} \left(T \frac{dy}{dx}\right) = -Fr^2 \frac{dy}{dx}$$
Substituting these into the energy gradient equation:
$$-S_f = -S_0 + \frac{dy}{dx} - Fr^2 \frac{dy}{dx}$$
$$S_0 - S_f = \frac{dy}{dx} (1 - Fr^2)$$
Rearranging yields the celebrated **Dynamic Equation of Gradually Varied Flow**:
$$\mathbf{\frac{dy}{dx} = \frac{S_0 - S_f}{1 - Fr^2} = \frac{S_0 - S_f}{1 - \frac{Q^2 T}{g A^3}}}$$

## 4. Classification of GVF Water Surface Profiles
The sign of $\frac{dy}{dx}$ dictates whether the water surface is **rising (Backwater curve, $dy/dx > 0$)** or **falling (Drawdown curve, $dy/dx < 0$)**:
- **Zone 1 ($y > y_n$ and $y > y_c$):** $S_f < S_0$ and $Fr < 1 \implies \frac{dy}{dx} > 0$ (**Backwater Curve**).
- **Zone 2 ($y$ between $y_n$ and $y_c$):** $\frac{dy}{dx} < 0$ (**Drawdown Curve**).
- **Zone 3 ($y < y_n$ and $y < y_c$):** $S_f > S_0$ and $Fr > 1 \implies \frac{dy}{dx} > 0$ (**Backwater Curve**).

```
         Zone 1: y > yn > yc  ──► M1 Profile (Backwater curve behind a dam)
      ─── Normal Depth Line (NDL) yn ───────────────────────────────────────
         Zone 2: yn > y > yc  ──► M2 Profile (Drawdown into a waterfall / drop)
      - - - Critical Depth Line (CDL) yc - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - -
         Zone 3: yc > y       ──► M3 Profile (Below a sluice gate on mild slope)
      ══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════ Mild Bed
```

### The 12 Surface Profiles:
1. **Mild Slope ($M_1, M_2, M_3$):** $y_n > y_c$. $M_1$ is the classic dam reservoir backwater; $M_2$ occurs upstream of an abrupt canal drop; $M_3$ forms below a sluice gate.
2. **Steep Slope ($S_1, S_2, S_3$):** $y_c > y_n$. $S_1$ forms behind a weir on a steep chute; $S_2$ is drawdown approaching a break of slope; $S_3$ forms below a sluice gate on steep slope.
3. **Critical Slope ($C_1, C_3$):** $y_n = y_c$.
4. **Horizontal Slope ($H_2, H_3$):** $S_0 = 0, y_n = \infty$.
5. **Adverse Slope ($A_2, A_3$):** $S_0 < 0$.

### Example 18.1: Design and Sizing of a USBR Type III Stilling Basin
**Problem:** A spillway releases a unit discharge of $q = 6.0 \text{ m}^2/\text{s}$ onto a horizontal apron. The flow velocity at the toe of the spillway is $V_1 = 15.0 \text{ m/s}$.
1. Compute initial depth $y_1$ and Froude number $Fr_1$.
2. Classify the hydraulic jump.
3. Determine the sequent subcritical depth $y_2$.
4. Calculate the required length of a USBR Type III stilling basin $L_B$.
5. Determine the standard height of the chute blocks and baffle piers.

:::sol Complete Stilling Basin Design
1. **Initial Depth and Froude Number:**
   $$y_1 = \frac{q}{V_1} = \frac{6.0}{15.0} = \mathbf{0.40 \text{ m}}$$
   $$Fr_1 = \frac{V_1}{\sqrt{g y_1}} = \frac{15.0}{\sqrt{9.81 \times 0.40}} = \frac{15.0}{1.9809} = \mathbf{7.572}$$

2. **Jump Classification:**
   Since $4.5 < Fr_1 = 7.572 \le 9.0$, this is a **Steady Jump** (ideal well-stabilized engineering jump).

3. **Sequent Depth $y_2$:**
   $$y_2 = \frac{y_1}{2} \left[ \sqrt{1 + 8 Fr_1^2} - 1 \right] = \frac{0.40}{2} \left[ \sqrt{1 + 8(7.572)^2} - 1 \right]$$
   $$\sqrt{1 + 8(57.335)} = \sqrt{1 + 458.68} = \sqrt{459.68} = 21.44$$
   $$y_2 = 0.20 [21.44 - 1] = 0.20 \times 20.44 = \mathbf{4.088 \text{ m}}$$

4. **USBR Type III Basin Length ($L_B$):**
   Without appurtenances, a free jump requires $L_j \approx 6.0 (y_2 - y_1) = 6.0 (4.088 - 0.40) = 22.1 \text{ m}$.
   With USBR Type III chute blocks and baffle piers, the required basin length is shortened to:
   $$\mathbf{L_B = 2.7 \times y_2 = 2.7 \times 4.088 = 11.04 \text{ m}}$$
   *Lining savings:* Over **$50\%$ reduction** in expensive concrete apron construction!

5. **Appurtenance Dimensions (IS: 4997 Guidelines):**
   - Chute Block Height: $h_1 = y_1 = \mathbf{0.40 \text{ m}}$
   - Chute Block Width and Spacing: $w_1 = s_1 = y_1 = \mathbf{0.40 \text{ m}}$
   - Baffle Pier Height: $h_3 = 1.5 y_1 \approx 1.5(0.40) = \mathbf{0.60 \text{ m}}$
   - Dentated End Sill Height: $h_4 = 1.25 y_1 \approx 1.25(0.40) = \mathbf{0.50 \text{ m}}$
:::

### Example 18.2: Water Surface Profile Slope Calculation (GVF)
**Problem:** A wide rectangular canal ($S_0 = 0.001$, Manning $n = 0.020$) carries a discharge intensity of $q = 3.0 \text{ m}^2/\text{s}$. At a certain gauging section, a backwater curve creates a flow depth of $y = 2.5 \text{ m}$.
1. Compute normal depth $y_n$ and critical depth $y_c$.
2. Identify the type of GVF profile ($M_1, M_2, \text{etc.}$).
3. Calculate the water surface slope with respect to the bed $\frac{dy}{dx}$ and determine if depth is rising or falling.

:::sol Step-by-Step GVF Calculation
1. **Critical and Normal Depths:**
   - Critical depth:
     $$y_c = \sqrt[3]{\frac{q^2}{g}} = \sqrt[3]{\frac{3.0^2}{9.81}} = \sqrt[3]{0.9174} = \mathbf{0.972 \text{ m}}$$
   - Normal depth in wide channel ($R \approx y_n$):
     $$q = \frac{1}{n} y_n^{5/3} \sqrt{S_0} \implies 3.0 = \frac{1}{0.020} y_n^{5/3} \sqrt{0.001} = 50 \times 0.03162 y_n^{5/3} = 1.5811 y_n^{5/3}$$
     $$y_n^{5/3} = \frac{3.0}{1.5811} = 1.8974 \implies \mathbf{y_n = 1.468 \text{ m}}$$

2. **Profile Identification:**
   - Bed slope classification: Since $y_n = 1.468 \text{ m} > y_c = 0.972 \text{ m}$, the slope is **MILD ($M$)**.
   - Depth comparison: Actual depth $y = 2.50 \text{ m} > y_n = 1.468 \text{ m} > y_c = 0.972 \text{ m}$.
   - Since $y > y_n > y_c$, this is in Zone 1: **Profile Type is $M_1$ (Backwater Curve)**.

3. **Slope Gradient $\frac{dy}{dx}$:**
   - Velocity at $y = 2.5 \text{ m}$: $V = \frac{q}{y} = \frac{3.0}{2.5} = 1.20 \text{ m/s}$.
   - Froude number squared:
     $$Fr^2 = \frac{V^2}{g y} = \frac{1.20^2}{9.81 \times 2.5} = \frac{1.44}{24.525} = 0.0587$$
     $$1 - Fr^2 = 1 - 0.0587 = \mathbf{0.9413}$$
   - Friction slope from Manning equation:
     $$S_f = \frac{n^2 V^2}{R^{4/3}} \approx \frac{(0.020)^2 (1.20)^2}{(2.5)^{4/3}} = \frac{0.0004 \times 1.44}{3.3912} = \frac{0.000576}{3.3912} = \mathbf{0.0001698}$$
   - Dynamic equation gradient:
     $$\mathbf{\frac{dy}{dx} = \frac{S_0 - S_f}{1 - Fr^2} = \frac{0.001 - 0.0001698}{0.9413} = \frac{0.0008302}{0.9413} = \mathbf{+0.000882}}$$
   *Physical Meaning:* $\frac{dy}{dx} > 0$ confirms that water depth **rises by $0.882 \text{ mm}$ per metre distance downstream** (classic reservoir backwater curve).
:::

### Real-World Civil Engineering Standards: HEC-RAS & Dam Safety
1. **Numerical Integration of GVF (Standard Step Method):**
   In natural river channels with irregular cross-sections, the GVF dynamic equation $\frac{dy}{dx} = \frac{S_0 - S_f}{1 - Fr^2}$ cannot be integrated analytically. The US Army Corps of Engineers (USACE) HEC-RAS software uses the **Standard Step Method**, solving the 1D energy balance iteratively from cross-section to cross-section:
   $$WSE_2 + \alpha_2 \frac{V_2^2}{2g} = WSE_1 + \alpha_1 \frac{V_1^2}{2g} + h_e$$
   where $h_e = L \bar{S}_f + C \left|\alpha_2 \frac{V_2^2}{2g} - \alpha_1 \frac{V_1^2}{2g}\right|$ includes contraction/expansion eddy losses.
2. **Central Water Commission (CWC) Dam Safety Guidelines:**
   The Central Water Commission mandates that all major and medium dams in India undergo physical scale-model testing (typically at CWPRS Pune or state research stations) at scales between $1:30$ and $1:60$ to verify that the hydraulic jump remains confined inside the stilling basin during the Maximum Probable Flood (MPF).
