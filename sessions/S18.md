> [!note] Core Concept
> **Gradually Varied Flow (GVF)** is steady non-uniform flow where water depth changes gradually over long longitudinal distances ($x$). Streamlines remain essentially straight and parallel, so hydrostatic pressure distribution prevails. Analyzing GVF water surface profiles allows engineers to predict **backwater curves behind dams, drawdown curves over waterfalls, and flood inundation extents**.

## 1. Dynamic Equation of GVF
Total energy at any section: $H = z + y + \frac{V^2}{2g}$.
Differentiating with respect to longitudinal distance $x$:
$$\frac{dH}{dx} = \frac{dz}{dx} + \frac{dy}{dx} + \frac{d}{dx}\left(\frac{V^2}{2g}\right)$$
Noting that $\frac{dz}{dx} = -S_0$ (bed slope), $\frac{dH}{dx} = -S_f$ (friction slope), and $\frac{d}{dx}(\frac{V^2}{2g}) = -\frac{Q^2 T}{g A^3} \frac{dy}{dx} = -Fr^2 \frac{dy}{dx}$:
$$-S_f = -S_0 + \frac{dy}{dx} - Fr^2 \frac{dy}{dx}$$
Rearranging yields the **Dynamic Equation of Gradually Varied Flow**:
$$\frac{dy}{dx} = \frac{S_0 - S_f}{1 - Fr^2} = \frac{S_0 - S_f}{1 - \frac{Q^2 T}{g A^3}}$$

## 2. Classification of Surface Profiles (12 Profiles)
Channels are classified based on bed slope relative to critical slope $S_c$:
1. **Mild Slope ($M$):** $y_0 > y_c \implies S_0 < S_c$.
   - **$M_1$ Profile ($y > y_0 > y_c$):** $\frac{dy}{dx} > 0$ (Backwater curve behind a dam or weir).
   - **$M_2$ Profile ($y_0 > y > y_c$):** $\frac{dy}{dx} < 0$ (Drawdown curve approaching a free overfall).
   - **$M_3$ Profile ($y_0 > y_c > y$):** $\frac{dy}{dx} > 0$ (Supercritical flow issuing from a sluice gate into a mild channel).
2. **Steep Slope ($S$):** $y_c > y_0 \implies S_0 > S_c$ ($S_1, S_2, S_3$).
3. **Critical Slope ($C$):** $y_0 = y_c$ ($C_1, C_3$).
4. **Horizontal Bed ($H$):** $S_0 = 0$ ($H_2, H_3$).
5. **Adverse Slope ($A$):** $S_0 < 0$ ($A_2, A_3$).

## 3. Direct Step Method for Prismatic Channels
Dividing channel into reaches between depths $y_i$ and $y_{i+1}$:
$$\Delta x = \frac{E_{i+1} - E_i}{S_0 - \bar{S}_f}$$
Where $\bar{S}_f = \frac{S_{f1} + S_{f2}}{2}$ and $S_f = \frac{n^2 V^2}{R^{4/3}}$.

### Example 18.1: Direct Step Computation of M1 Backwater Curve
**Problem:** A rectangular canal $5.0 \text{ m}$ wide carries $Q = 15.0 \text{ m}^3/\text{s}$ on a bed slope $S_0 = 0.0005$ with $n = 0.020$. Normal depth is $y_0 = 1.80 \text{ m}$, critical depth is $y_c = 0.97 \text{ m}$. A weir creates a backwater pool with depth $y = 2.40 \text{ m}$ at the dam. Calculate the distance upstream where depth drops to $y = 2.10 \text{ m}$ in a single direct step.

**Solution:**
1. **Section 1 (At Dam, $y_1 = 2.40 \text{ m}$):**
   - $A_1 = 5.0 \times 2.40 = 12.0 \text{ m}^2, P_1 = 5.0 + 2(2.40) = 9.80 \text{ m}, R_1 = 1.2245 \text{ m}$
   - $V_1 = 15.0 / 12.0 = 1.25 \text{ m/s}$
   - $E_1 = 2.40 + \frac{(1.25)^2}{2 \times 9.81} = 2.40 + 0.0796 = 2.4796 \text{ m}$
   - $S_{f1} = \frac{n^2 V_1^2}{R_1^{4/3}} = \frac{(0.020)^2 (1.25)^2}{(1.2245)^{4/3}} = \frac{0.000625}{1.309} = 0.0004775$
2. **Section 2 (Upstream, $y_2 = 2.10 \text{ m}$):**
   - $A_2 = 5.0 \times 2.10 = 10.50 \text{ m}^2, P_2 = 5.0 + 2(2.10) = 9.20 \text{ m}, R_2 = 1.1413 \text{ m}$
   - $V_2 = 15.0 / 10.50 = 1.4286 \text{ m/s}$
   - $E_2 = 2.10 + \frac{(1.4286)^2}{2 \times 9.81} = 2.10 + 0.1040 = 2.2040 \text{ m}$
   - $S_{f2} = \frac{(0.020)^2 (1.4286)^2}{(1.1413)^{4/3}} = \frac{0.0008163}{1.193} = 0.0006842$
3. **Average Friction Slope & Distance $\Delta x$:**
   $$\bar{S}_f = \frac{S_{f1} + S_{f2}}{2} = \frac{0.0004775 + 0.0006842}{2} = 0.0005809$$
   $$\Delta x = \frac{E_2 - E_1}{S_0 - \bar{S}_f} = \frac{2.2040 - 2.4796}{0.0005 - 0.0005809} = \frac{-0.2756}{-0.0000809} = 3407 \text{ m} = 3.41 \text{ km}$$
   The backwater curve extends **3.41 km** upstream.

### Key Takeaways
- Dynamic equation: $\frac{dy}{dx} = \frac{S_0 - S_f}{1 - Fr^2}$.
- Profiles classified by slope ($M, S, C, H, A$) and depth zone ($1, 2, 3$).
- Direct Step Method: $\Delta x = \frac{\Delta E}{S_0 - \bar{S}_f}$.
- Computations proceed upstream for subcritical flow and downstream for supercritical flow.
