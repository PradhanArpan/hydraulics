> [!note] Core Concept
> Because velocity inside a boundary layer asymptotes smoothly toward $U_\infty$, the outer edge is physically indeterminate. To perform precise engineering calculations, fluid mechanics defines three integral thicknesses based on conservation of mass, momentum, and kinetic energy.

## 1. Boundary Layer Thickness Definitions

1. **Nominal Thickness ($\delta$ or $\delta_{99}$):**
   The arbitrary normal distance from the boundary where velocity reaches 99% of the free-stream value:
   $$u(y = \delta) = 0.99 U_\infty$$

2. **Displacement Thickness ($\delta^*$):**
   The distance by which the external inviscid streamlines are displaced outward away from the boundary due to the deficit in mass flow rate caused by the slow viscous layer:
   $$\delta^* = \int_0^\delta \left(1 - \frac{u}{U_\infty}\right) dy$$

3. **Momentum Thickness ($\theta$):**
   The loss of momentum in the boundary layer expressed as an equivalent thickness of fluid layer moving at free-stream velocity:
   $$\theta = \int_0^\delta \frac{u}{U_\infty} \left(1 - \frac{u}{U_\infty}\right) dy$$

4. **Energy Thickness ($\delta^{**}$):**
   The deficit in kinetic energy transport across the boundary layer:
   $$\delta^{**} = \int_0^\delta \frac{u}{U_\infty} \left(1 - \frac{u^2}{U_\infty^2}\right) dy$$

5. **Shape Factor ($H$):**
   The non-dimensional ratio of displacement thickness to momentum thickness:
   $$H = \frac{\delta^*}{\theta}$$
   - For Blasius laminar flow: $H = 2.59$.
   - For turbulent flow (1/7th power law): $H \approx 1.28 - 1.40$.

### Example 2.1: Evaluation of Integral Thicknesses for a Linear Profile
**Problem:** The velocity profile in a laminar boundary layer is approximated as linear:
$$\frac{u}{U_\infty} = \frac{y}{\delta} \quad \text{for } 0 \le y \le \delta$$
Evaluate: (1) Displacement thickness $\delta^*$, (2) Momentum thickness $\theta$, (3) Energy thickness $\delta^{**}$, and (4) Shape factor $H$ in terms of $\delta$.

**Solution:**
Let $\eta = y / \delta$, so $dy = \delta \, d\eta$.
1. **Displacement Thickness $\delta^*$:**
   $$\delta^* = \int_0^\delta \left(1 - \frac{u}{U_\infty}\right) dy = \delta \int_0^1 (1 - \eta) d\eta = \delta \left[\eta - \frac{\eta^2}{2}\right]_0^1 = \frac{\delta}{2} = 0.500 \delta$$
2. **Momentum Thickness $\theta$:**
   $$\theta = \delta \int_0^1 \eta(1 - \eta) d\eta = \delta \left[\frac{\eta^2}{2} - \frac{\eta^3}{3}\right]_0^1 = \delta \left(\frac{1}{2} - \frac{1}{3}\right) = \frac{\delta}{6} \approx 0.167 \delta$$
3. **Energy Thickness $\delta^{**}$:**
   $$\delta^{**} = \delta \int_0^1 \eta(1 - \eta^2) d\eta = \delta \left[\frac{\eta^2}{2} - \frac{\eta^4}{4}\right]_0^1 = \frac{\delta}{4} = 0.250 \delta$$
4. **Shape Factor $H$:**
   $$H = \frac{\delta^*}{\theta} = \frac{\delta / 2}{\delta / 6} = 3.00$$

### Examination Questions
#### 2-Mark Questions
1. Define momentum thickness $\theta$ and state its physical significance.
   *Solution Outline:* $\theta = \int_0^\delta \frac{u}{U}(1 - \frac{u}{U})dy$. It represents the distance the boundary must be moved to account for total loss of momentum flux.
#### 5-Mark Questions
1. Derive an expression for the energy thickness $\delta^{**}$ of a boundary layer and explain its physical meaning.
   *Solution Outline:* Express deficit of kinetic energy flux $\int_0^\delta \frac{1}{2}\rho u (U_\infty^2 - u^2) dy$ equated to $\frac{1}{2}\rho U_\infty^3 \delta^{**}$.
#### 10-Mark Questions
1. A velocity distribution in a laminar boundary layer is given by $\frac{u}{U_\infty} = \frac{3}{2}\left(\frac{y}{\delta}\right) - \frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{y}{\delta}\right)^3$. Derive expressions for $\delta^*$, $\theta$, $\delta^{**}$, and the shape factor $H$.
   *Solution Outline:*
   - $\delta^* = \frac{3}{8}\delta = 0.375 \delta$
   - $\theta = \frac{39}{280}\delta \approx 0.1393 \delta$
   - $\delta^{**} = \frac{169}{280}\delta$
   - $H = \frac{3/8}{39/280} = \frac{35}{13} \approx 2.692$.

<!--tab:Summary-->
### Key Takeaways
- **Displacement Thickness:** Mass deficit equivalent: $\delta^* = \int_0^\delta (1 - u/U) dy$.
- **Momentum Thickness:** Momentum deficit equivalent: $\theta = \int_0^\delta \frac{u}{U}(1 - u/U) dy$.
- **Energy Thickness:** Kinetic energy deficit: $\delta^{**} = \int_0^\delta \frac{u}{U}(1 - u^2/U^2) dy$.
- **Shape Factor:** $H = \delta^*/\theta$ indicates proximity to separation (separation occurs at $H \approx 3.5 - 4.0$).
