> [!note] Core Concept
> When a jet strikes a curved vane, the fluid is guided smoothly along the curvature. Because the jet can be turned through angles up to $180^\circ$, curved vanes exert **up to double the hydrodynamic force** of flat plates.

## 1. Jet Striking at the Center of a Symmetrical Curved Vane
Let the jet strike the center of a curved vane and deflect symmetrically through an angle $(180^\circ - \theta)$, where $\theta$ is the vane angle at exit:
- Inlet velocity in $x$-direction: $V_{1x} = V$
- Outlet velocity in $x$-direction: $V_{2x} = -V \cos\theta$
$$F_x = \rho a V [V - (-V \cos\theta)] = \rho a V^2 (1 + \cos\theta)$$
For a **semicircular (hemispherical) vane** ($\theta = 0^\circ$):
$$F_x = \rho a V^2 (1 + 1) = 2 \rho a V^2$$
*(Exactly twice the force of a normal flat plate!)*
Due to symmetry, vertical force $F_y = 0$.

## 2. Jet Striking Tangentially at One Tip of a Curved Vane
When the jet enters tangentially at inlet angle $\theta$ and exits at angle $\phi$:
$$F_x = \rho a V (V \cos\theta + V \cos\phi) = \rho a V^2 (\cos\theta + \cos\phi)$$
$$F_y = \rho a V (V \sin\theta - V \sin\phi) = \rho a V^2 (\sin\theta - \sin\phi)$$
If the vane is symmetrical ($\theta = \phi$):
$$F_x = 2 \rho a V^2 \cos\theta, \quad F_y = 0$$

### Example 21.1: Force on a Hemispherical Cup
**Problem:** A jet of water $40 \text{ mm}$ in diameter moving at $25.0 \text{ m/s}$ strikes:
1. A stationary flat vertical plate.
2. A stationary hemispherical cup which deflects the jet through $180^\circ$.
Calculate the force exerted in each case and compare them.

**Solution:**
1. **Jet Parameters:**
   $$a = \frac{\pi}{4}(0.040)^2 = 1.2566 \times 10^{-3} \text{ m}^2$$
   $$\rho a V^2 = 1000 \times (1.2566 \times 10^{-3}) \times (25.0)^2 = 1.2566 \times 625 = 785.4 \text{ N}$$
2. **Case 1: Flat Plate:**
   $$F_{flat} = \rho a V^2 = 785.4 \text{ N}$$
3. **Case 2: Hemispherical Cup:**
   $$F_{hemi} = 2 \rho a V^2 = 2 \times 785.4 = 1570.8 \text{ N}$$
   The hemispherical cup doubles the force because the fluid momentum is completely reversed from $+V$ to $-V$.

### Key Takeaways
- Center impact on curved vane: $F_x = \rho a V^2 (1 + \cos\theta)$.
- Hemispherical cup: $F_x = 2 \rho a V^2$ (maximum force).
- Smooth tangential entry prevents shock and turbulent splashing loss.
