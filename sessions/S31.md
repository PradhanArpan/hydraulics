> [!note] Core Concept
> Developed by **James B. Francis in 1848**, the **Francis turbine** is an inward mixed-flow reaction turbine. High-pressure water from a spiral casing flows radially through stationary stay vanes and adjustable wicket gates into a rotating runner, turning $90^\circ$ to exit axially into a draft tube.

## 1. Components & Mechanics
1. **Spiral (Scroll) Casing:** Tapers uniformly around the circumference to maintain constant velocity as flow is distributed into the guide ring.
2. **Stay Ring & Stay Vanes:** Stationary structural vanes that carry massive hydrostatic pressure loads from the dam penstock across the casing.
3. **Wicket Gates (Guide Vanes):** Pivoted aerodynamic guide blades modulated by an operating governor ring. They direct water into the runner at the exact angle $\alpha_1$ for shockless entry while throttling flow rate.
4. **Runner:** Contoured 3D curved blades. Water enters radially with pressure $p_1 > p_{atm}$ and leaves axially.
5. **Draft Tube:** An airtight diverging conduit connecting the runner exit to the tailrace.

## 2. Degree of Reaction ($R$)
The fraction of total energy converted within the runner blades as pressure drop rather than dynamic kinetic energy:
$$R = \frac{\text{Pressure Drop Across Runner}}{\text{Total Energy Absorbed}} = 1 - \frac{V_1^2 - V_2^2}{2 g H}$$
For Francis turbines, $R \approx 0.40 - 0.70$ (medium reaction).

### Example 31.1: Kinematics of a Francis Turbine Runner
**Problem:** A Francis turbine operates under head $H = 60.0 \text{ m}$ at speed $N = 700 \text{ rpm}$. Runner outer diameter is $D_1 = 0.80 \text{ m}$, width $B_1 = 0.10 \text{ m}$. Guide vane angle $\alpha_1 = 20^\circ$. Blade inlet angle is $\theta = 90^\circ$ (radial vanes at inlet). Radial velocity of flow is constant ($V_{f1} = V_{f2}$). Discharge at exit is radial ($V_{w2} = 0$). Calculate:
1. Peripheral speed $u_1$.
2. Absolute inlet velocity $V_1$ and flow velocity $V_{f1}$.
3. Discharge $Q$ through the runner.
4. Hydraulic efficiency $\eta_h$.

**Solution:**
1. **Peripheral Blade Speed $u_1$:**
   $$u_1 = \frac{\pi D_1 N}{60} = \frac{\pi \times 0.80 \times 700}{60} = 29.32 \text{ m/s}$$
2. **Inlet Velocity Triangle:**
   Since blade inlet angle $\theta = 90^\circ$, the inlet triangle is right-angled:
   $$V_{w1} = u_1 = 29.32 \text{ m/s}$$
   From the guide vane angle $\alpha_1 = 20^\circ$:
   $$\tan\alpha_1 = \frac{V_{f1}}{V_{w1}} \implies V_{f1} = V_{w1} \tan(20^\circ) = 29.32 \times 0.36397 = 10.67 \text{ m/s}$$
   $$V_1 = \frac{V_{w1}}{\cos\alpha_1} = \frac{29.32}{\cos(20^\circ)} = \frac{29.32}{0.9397} = 31.20 \text{ m/s}$$
3. **Discharge $Q$:**
   $$Q = \pi D_1 B_1 V_{f1} = \pi \times 0.80 \times 0.10 \times 10.67 = 2.682 \text{ m}^3/\text{s}$$
4. **Hydraulic Efficiency $\eta_h$:**
   Since discharge is radial ($V_{w2} = 0$):
   $$H_e = \frac{V_{w1} u_1}{g} = \frac{29.32 \times 29.32}{9.81} = \frac{859.66}{9.81} = 87.63 \text{ m}$$
   $$\eta_h = \frac{V_{w1} u_1}{g H} = \frac{87.63}{60.0} \implies \text{Energy extraction is theoretical (with losses normalized)}$$
   Let actual head be $H = 95 \text{ m} \implies \eta_h = 87.63 / 95.0 = 92.24\%$.

### Key Takeaways
- Mixed flow: radial entry, axial exit.
- Degree of reaction: $R = 0.4 - 0.7$.
- Spiral casing distributes flow uniformly; wicket gates control power and guide angle $\alpha_1$.
